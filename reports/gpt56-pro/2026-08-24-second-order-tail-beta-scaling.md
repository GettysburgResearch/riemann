# Second-order Xi centering and the universal critical-tail beta law

The previous high-derivative capacity theorem paid a first-order concentration error against a deliberately crude smallest-eigenvalue bound. Two exact structures improve both sides.

First, choosing the derivative scale by the literal second moment gives `E[V^2]=1`. Therefore `E[V-1]=-(1/2)E[(V-1)^2]`, and the linear Taylor error cancels. The normalized Xi derivative and its first two derivatives differ from sine/cosine by `O((1+Y)^2/(r omega))` on the real-saddle strip.

Second, the unused tangent/cotangent critical tail is a rescaled beta `(1/2,1)` law. Its moment Gram is the Cauchy matrix `[1/(2(i+j+a)+1)]`, and its exact smallest-eigenvalue exponent is `4k+2a-3`.

Combining the two yields the enlarged prefix `J=o((r omega)^(1/(4k-1)))`. It also identifies the natural remote-tail moments: `J^(2n+1) Delta_n`. One scalar zeroth-mass pivot, together with the complete sharp residue sign, prevents endpoint mass escape and forces all fixed remote moments.

This closes neither sharp gate at low derivative order. It produces a much earlier high-derivative finite-packet endpoint and compresses the complete remote-tail uncertainty from a family of isotropic moment assumptions to one endpoint mass coordinate.
