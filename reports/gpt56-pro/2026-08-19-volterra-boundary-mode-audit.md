# Volterra boundary-mode audit

The inverse density in the direct-integral candidate does not by itself fix the
endpoint frame. The operator has the exact nullspace `span{sqrt(x),x}`, and
every activation derivative jump contributes a separate signed atom. The
complete construction is therefore a finite cone problem: certify all smooth
and knot weights, then pay the two boundary modes with two positive anchors.

This is a repair specification and exact reduction, not a proof that the
candidate's unpublished anchor data pass.
