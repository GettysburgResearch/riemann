# Classical inputs and source interfaces

This is an import-scope reconciliation, not a reproduction of external historical proofs or computations and not an originality survey. No external paper or PDF is redistributed.

## Newly checked or reconciled

**Littlewood multiplicity input for LC.** Hall, *On the Zeros of the Riemann Zeta-Function*, J. London Math. Soc. 59 (1999), 65–75, opening text, reports the bound `S(T)=O(log T/log log T)` **assuming RH**. The publisher's primary HTML statement was checked. In LC, the zero-count jumps then bound multiplicities by that order; the `T log T` count in `[T/2,T]` yields a superlinear count of distinct ordinates. Compact-distribution Fourier uniqueness, not simplicity or existence of an ordinary L2 minimizer, proves the conditional strict effective-matrix statement. No numerical ordinate is consumed.

**Brun–Titchmarsh versus Cramér.** The exact interval count used by SSQ is `pi(x+y)-pi(x) <= 2y/log y`, for positive `x` and `y>1`. Montgomery–Vaughan, *The large sieve*, Mathematika 20 (1973), 119–134, is the historical source. Yamada's primary manuscript, arXiv:2312.16090v1, introduction equations (1)–(4), explicitly records the uniform interval `C=2` theorem; its HTML was checked. The newer numerical improvement in that paper is not imported or replayed here. In the square cells, `y=2n+1>1`, so there is no range endpoint problem. This is an unconditional count input, not a prime-between-squares lower bound.

The different CCT converse uses `RH => integral_Y^(2Y)|psi(x)-x|^2 dx <= K Y^2`. Brent–Platt–Trudgian, *The mean square of the error term in the prime number theorem*, arXiv:2008.06140, primary abstract explicitly places its upper bound under RH. This was checked again; no numerical constant, external zero computation or unconditional upper bound is imported. The source's Volterra transfer pays every prime power, the prime-2 endpoint, and the entire time tail. SSQ's unconditional local detail does not supply this global premise.

**Exponential-integral primitives.** DLMF 6.6.1 gives `Ei(x)=gamma+log x+sum_(j>=1)x^j/(j j!)` for positive real `x`. SSQ takes a difference, so gamma cancels. Its two directed logarithms and complete positive series tail were audited; a library li/Ei value is not an accepting input. Reference: https://dlmf.nist.gov/6.6#E1 .

**Causal and Hardy source identification.** CSM's input is the literal positive factorial function `d_b(t)=exp(-bt)[floor(exp t)(1-t)+log(floor(exp t)!)]`, with removable transform `(z+b-1)zeta(z+b)/(z+b)^2`. Analytic continuation through finite boundary arcs excludes singular measure there; real-axis asymptotics exclude delay mass at infinity. The inner factor retains all shifted zeta zeros and multiplicities. The source-generated and maximal causal domains are distinguished after possible common-zero cancellation. The scalar/all-pass Grams and target pairings use the original Lebesgue L2 metric.

**HC to IE and FR.** HC's clipping proof keeps the actual inner factor `B`. Its Sobolev product estimates do not assume `P_+` maps bounded functions to bounded functions. The actual ramp seed belongs to the stated target class with its horizon-dependent bound. HC controls excess above `dist(q,BH2)^2`; FR prices finite ordinary-input realization separately. For IE's unit target, `q=1` meets HC's bounded fractional-Sobolev hypothesis, and the finite-entropy logarithm is Lipschitz on the source's fixed positive initial projection floor. This closes the named dependency reconciliation, not `J=0`.

**CCT, PDS and OEC overlap.** The complex higher-prime-power series has a complete Cauchy-weighted L2 tail. Its square harmonics retain ordered correlations, and the `k>=3` tail is absolutely summable. The fixed logarithmic taper averages both prime and continuum cutoffs with identical weights; it retains the stopped future. Under finite energy the analytic logarithm is constructed before identifying its exponential with `(s-1)zeta(s)/s^2`. The RH-conditional boundary limit is a logarithmic L2 statement, not L2 convergence after exponentiation. OEC's Abel weight has total mass `2^-epsilon`; its exact arithmetic factors are retained.

## Inherited imports, not freshly rerun

The frozen principal reviews retain the qualitative Mertens estimates of Lee–Leong, classical critical-line convexity and its derivative bounds, the Platt–Trudgian finite-height theorem restricted to `3*10^12`, and the quoted Balazard–Saias zero-free-half-plane approximation lemma. Their previous hypothesis qualifications remain. In particular, a zero-free strip strictly to the right of an unknown edge cannot be moved to the edge by continuity of notation. This pass does not rerun an external numerical census or reread the original Balazard–Saias proof.

Standard Mellin/Fourier Plancherel, Hardy factorization, finite projection/Schur algebra, Euler products in their absolute half-plane, and finite divisor identities are classical inputs. Their source-specific uses are reviewed; they are not claimed as new general principles.
