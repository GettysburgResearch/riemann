## 3. Two new issues, with readable repairs

### 3.1 Infinite-reservoir residual certificates need an operator-domain hypothesis

The application in #825 states the Schur-residual identity for any bounded proposed solve and advertises an infinite fixed-prime reservoir extension. On that reservoir the positive self-adjoint graph operator is unbounded. Boundedness of `Y` alone does not make `AY` a Hilbert-space vector or bounded operator.

The repaired strong-residual statement requires `Y(H) subset Dom(A)` and bounded `AY`, with `A >= gamma I`, `B` bounded and the retained block bounded self-adjoint. Then

\[
 R=B-AY,\quad V=Y^*B+B^*Y-Y^*AY,
\]
\[
 B^*A^{-1}B-V=R^*A^{-1}R,
 \qquad H_0-V-\gamma^{-1}R^*R\preceq S\preceq H_0-V.
\]

The proof is direct expansion using `A^{-1}R=A^{-1}B-Y`. The domain restriction is real: in the literal one-prime reservoir, eigenvalues grow like `j`, and `sum j^(-3/2)e_j` is in the form domain but not the operator domain. A bounded rank-one map with that range is therefore not an admissible strong-residual trial.

All finite-matrix statements and the reviewed `N=32` inverse remain unaffected. The graph gap is not invalidated. A different form-domain/dual-residual version would need its own stated contract. [REPAIRS.md](REPAIRS.md) gives the complete current statement and counterexample.
