# Independent review: partial natural selected-row count

**Status:** The stated argument is algebraically sound conditional on the cited sixth-power-free sextic sieve and on a source-faithful character/mask identification. This is a paper review, not an independent verification of De Faveri's preprint or a Lean proof. The count is not the selected-operator estimate required by PR #93, and its exponent does not improve the cited detector count at slope `223/500`.

**Reviewed object:** `research/exploratory/2026-10-29-natural-selected-count/README.md`; dependency [De Faveri, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04045v1), version 1. I checked the displayed theorem's both-index sixth-power-free scope in the cited PDF and the derivation of the four terms, the amplification inequalities, and the stated quantifier dependence. I did not prove that the source's `idealRowHom` and all its local conventions are the same family as the paper's character family.

**Minkowski and masks.** Decompose each good column uniquely as `w v^6`. For fixed `v` the sixth-power factor is a row-dependent coprimality mask of modulus at most one. Minkowski in the row `ℓ²` norm permits removing that mask from the fixed-v term, not from the original combined sum. Cauchy–Schwarz in `v` then multiplies the total column `ℓ²` mass by the sum of the individual sieve constants. Ideal counting gives `O(B^(1/6))` choices of `v`; the powers `Nv^-6`, `Nv^-2` and `Nv^-5` for the other three terms are summable. This recovers the displayed unrestricted-column bound up to the stated small powers. For a fixed vector of exponents at the finitely many excluded primes, the corresponding character factor must be a common row multiplier for the remaining good columns; splitting vectors and applying Cauchy–Schwarz costs at most a fixed power of `log(2B)`. This deduction depends on the explicitly flagged multiplicativity and local-mask identification. Row units and sixth-power-free bad-prime exponents produce only finitely many classes, provided the phase is indeed independent of the good column except for its multiplicative character factor.

**Selection and powers.** The selected hypothesis must explicitly include `prod_i |Q_i(u)|² >= Z^(sigma W)` and the slots must have row-independent coefficient vectors for the stated character expansion. The pointwise `ℓ²` bound, total width `W>=smin`, and the fixed finite slot count yield a slot of width at least a positive `eta` satisfying the weaker `sigma0` lower bound. Once `eta` is fixed, `k=max(1,ceil(log Y/(w log Z)))` is bounded and `Y<=Z^(kw)<=Y Z^mesh`; support enlargement is a fixed `C^k` factor for an arbitrary fixed support constant C, not necessarily `2^k`. The k-fold ideal divisor bound controls collisions in the coefficient `ℓ²` norm. With `B` on this scale, all four sieve terms are `O(B^(7/6))`, yielding `Y^(7/6-sigma0)` and, after absorbing `Y^(sigma-sigma0)<=Z^(A(sigma-sigma0))`, the claimed exponent.

**Quantifiers.** A positive early mesh can be chosen from the requested loss (and fixed ambient constants); `sigma0` can be fixed from the loss and A. Only afterwards are N, slot mass, and `smin` fixed, determining `eta`, the bound on k, and eventual constants. Since k takes only a bounded set of integer values after those choices, the coefficient and sieve constants can be made uniform over the later rows, support data satisfying the fixed bounds, and `1<=Y<=Z^A`. The argument does not provide constants uniform in N, `smin`, or Cslot.

**Scope and remaining gates.** The theorem invocation and its transfer to the exact source family remain conditional, especially for non-squarefree columns, local bad-prime behavior, masks, and any root-dependent or row-dependent slot coefficients. Uniformity through the source's exact early-mesh ordering still requires matching those source definitions, not merely the abstract argument. At slope `223/500`, the resulting exponent is `1081/1500 > 0.6094` and far above the sharp `277/500`; this is neither an optimal selected norm nor a new zero-free region. The general large sieve must not be used to assert the PR #93 all-source operator estimate without a separate proof.
