# Review of short-detector density proposal

**Status:** Conditional scalar and moment-algebra review; not a proof of the proposed short detector or of its cited preprint inputs. The result is a finite-height family-density statement, not a uniform zero-free region.

For `0<delta<1` and fixed `1/2<=t<=1`, the allowed witness range is `max(0,t-1/2)<=r<=t` after taking the smallest admissible `m=t-r`. On `r<=1/2`, the two counting exponents `1-delta*r` and `1-delta*(t-r)` cross at `r=t/2`, giving worst value `1-delta*t/2`. On `r>=1/2`, both relevant expressions are nondecreasing, so the worst value is `min(1/2+(1-delta)*t,1)` at `r=t`. Balancing the first and the unsaturated second yields `t=1/(2-delta)` and exponent `(4-3delta)/(4-2delta)=(7-6a)/(6-4a)`; at `a=4/5` this is `11/14`. The detector lengths must be nonnegative; the proposed source bridge needs to supply this as well as the simultaneous lower witnesses.

For the ordinary second moment, unique factorization `j=q r²` with squarefree q gives a common row factor for fixed r; a shared zero mask when q and r have common prime factors does not invalidate multiplicativity. Assuming the source's finite-class reciprocity and the companion rectangular sieve, the q-column has squared mass `O(Q/D)`, and since `Q<=C sqrt(X)` the sieve contributes `O(X^(1+epsilon))`. Minkowski costs `sum_{Nr<=sqrt D}1/Nr=O(log D)`, with a convergent fixed-S factor. The argument needs uniform row-independent coefficient vectors for each fixed decomposition, not arbitrary row-dependent coefficients.

The decisive open step is the stated extension of the buffered saturated detector to `t<1`, including both witnesses for the same zero/frequency, finite binning, derivative/Sobolev control for rowwise evaluations, fixed-type conductor and removed-Euler-factor conventions, and height-loss orders fixed before choosing τ. These are not established by the scalar calculation or by the printed t≥1 theorem. The density and fixed-conductor conclusions remain conditional on this bridge and on the companion general-order sieve; no conclusion is made about their independent correctness.
