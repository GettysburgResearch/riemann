# Error allowance for the original Platt interpolation on the audited range

The historical source initializes `intererr` in `arb_zeta.c`, and its `inter.c` forms a finite Gaussian-windowed sinc sum. That function does not add `intererr` to the output. Here an unused part of the **existing real Fourier coefficient enclosure** pays the interpolation truncation. The argument applies to the unmodified executable on the audited range, with its actual numeric parameters. It does not require any property of the zeros being counted. Take $L=3\cdot10^{12}$ and $U=3000200000000$. The ordinate centers and grid and interpolation targets used in this archive belong to $[L,U]$, including Turing padding. Here $B=5376$, $A=512/21$, $h=116$, $H=13/64$, $J=768000$, $K=23$ and $N_s=70$. The auxiliary frequency grid has Nyquist point $A/8$ and the final grid has Nyquist point $A/2$. ### A direct bound for the shifted gamma integral

In the notation of Platt's thesis, Lemma 5.5.1, $C(\sigma,T,h,k)$ is $(2\pi)^k$ times the integral of
$$
 |\Gamma((\sigma+i(T+t))/2)|e^{\pi(T+t)/4}
       e^{-t^2/(2h^2)}|\sigma+1/2-it|^k .
$$
For each positive odd $\sigma$ write $m=(\sigma-1)/2$. The gamma recurrence and the reflection formula at $1/2+iy/2$ give
$$
 |\Gamma((\sigma+iy)/2)|e^{\pi y/4}
 \le \sqrt{2\pi}\,2^{-m}(|y|+\sigma)^m .
$$
Indeed each of the $m$ recurrence factors has modulus at most $(|y|+\sigma)/2$ and $|\Gamma(1/2+iy/2)|^2=\pi/\cosh(\pi y/2)$. For $T\in[L,U]$ put $a=\sigma+1/2$ and $q=m/(L+\sigma)+k/a$. The two polynomial factors are bounded by their values at $U+\sigma$ and $a$, times $e^{q|t|}$. Integration of this against the Gaussian gives
$$
 C(\sigma,T,h,k)\le4\pi h(2\pi a)^k((U+\sigma)/2)^m e^{q^2 h^2/2}.
$$
This bound permits direct interval evaluation with no fit to a computed Hardy value.

### Spectral and time-domain estimates

The checker uses Lemmas 5.4.1, 5.5.2–3, 5.6.2–3, 5.7.1–2 and 5.10.1 of the thesis, or the corresponding Fourier and sampling bounds in Platt's Math. Comp. paper. The numerical checks compare against the binary64 versions of the header decimals. [Published algorithm](https://doi.org/10.1090/mcom/3198)

- For the Dirichlet tail use $\sigma=1431$. The bound uniform on positive frequencies, including the DC input, is $2.015\cdot10^{-33}$ against the source allowance $3.26\cdot10^{-33}$. The residue of the shifted pole is smaller than $10^{-1500}$.
- The [historical bin check](check_buckets.c) reproduces the source's own positive-magnitude conversion and nearest-bucket arithmetic for every $1\le n\le768000$ at each supported precision. It proves $|B\xi|<0.500021$ for the **actual** bin residual $\xi$, including binary conversion. The Taylor remainder with this inflated spacing is $<6.728\cdot10^{-33}$ against the source allowance $6.73\cdot10^{-33}$. The finite $g$-lattice error including its $1/k!$ factor is $<1.823\cdot10^{-44}$ for all $k<K$.
- For the $G^{(k)}/k!$ frequency alias sum use $\sigma=511$, sampling frequency $A/4$. It costs $<10^{-1070}$ for all $k<K$, within the $10^{-307}$ source allowance. The same majorant bounds every single $G^{(k)}/k!$ omitted by hard zeroing beyond $A/8$. There is no full cyclic wrap because the positive bin index and original frequency index sum to less than $N_1$. For each frequency the omitted-bin weights total at most $2K\sqrt J$, since $|\xi|<1$ and $\sum_{n\le J}n^{-1/2}<2\sqrt J$. All resulting omissions together cost less than $2K\sqrt J\cdot10^{-307}$ per low input bin. For the final $F$ frequency aliases and padded high $F$ bins use $\sigma=201$; they fit their separate source allowances. In the residual terms of the referenced inequalities, the factor $e^{-L^2/(2h^2)}$ bounds a finite polynomial times at most $e^{(2\sigma+1)^2/(8h^2)}$. Even bounding every factorial denominator below by one and all polynomial factors by $(100U)^{K+5}$ gives $<10^{-1500}$ in these applications.
- The scaled completed zeta on the real line has modulus bounded by $P(y)=10^6+32(1+|y|)^{1/3}$. For $y\ge2$ put $s=1/2+iy$, $N=\lceil y\rceil$. Euler summation bounds $|\zeta(s)|$ by $2\sqrt N+\sqrt N/|s-1|+2|s|/\sqrt N<6\sqrt y$. The first Stirling remainder on $z=1/4+iy/2$ is at most $\sec^2(\arg z/2)/(12|z|)\le1/(6|z|)$. Since $(y/2)\arctan(1/(2y))\le1/4$, it gives $|\Gamma(z)|e^{\pi y/4}\le\sqrt{2\pi}(y/2)^{-1/4}e^{1/(3y)}<4y^{-1/4}$. Their product is $<24y^{1/4}$. [Stirling remainder](https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.11#iii) For $0\le y\le2$ the alternating zeta series and partial summation give $|\zeta(1/2+iy)|\le 2|1/2+iy|/(\sqrt 2-1)$, and $|\Gamma(1/4+iy/2)|\le\Gamma(1/4)$. Conjugation with the real exponential factor covers $y<0$. Thus the time alias is bounded by $4P(U+2B)e^{-B^2/(8h^2)}<1.5\cdot10^{-110}$ even on the entire time grid. The two tails can each be bounded by twice their first-term envelope: successive Gaussian exponents differ by at least $B^2/h^2$, while successive $P$ factors increase by at most two.
- In the actual sinc sum the closest omitted grid node is at least $69$ lattice spacings away. The search index belongs to the original grid of size $131072$; the 30-bit positive-magnitude rounding of the index changes it by less than $1/1024$, and either branch in `arb_inter_t` takes $70$ successive grid points on each side. Both nearest neighbors are included. At distance at least $n$ spacings use $|\operatorname{sinc}(\pi z)|\le 1/(\pi n)$. With $n=69$ the two Gaussian tails are bounded by $4P(U+(n+1)/A)e^{-n^2/(2A^2H^2)}/(\pi n)$; the ratio of successive envelopes is less than $2e^{-(2n+1)/(2A^2H^2)}<1/2$. Add the Fourier tail of Lemma 5.10.1 at $\sigma=3$ from the displayed direct gamma bound. **The complete analytic interpolation error is $<2.05\cdot10^{-38}$.**

### The DC reserve survives normalization and interpolation

After accounting for its Dirichlet tail and all omitted $G$ bins, every low spectral input still contains the true value enlarged by an arbitrary **real** increment of modulus $r>1.24\cdot10^{-33}$. We use only the real DC increment. The exact nonnormalized Hermitian inverse DFT adds this increment to every real time-grid sample. The original subsequently divides by $B$, compensates the time alias, and removes the Gaussian with a factor at least one. Thus each independently stored time-grid ball contains the true scaled completed zeta value enlarged by an interval of radius at least $r/B-1.5\cdot10^{-110}>2.31\cdot10^{-37}$. This argument follows by feeding every allowed DC increment through the exact transform; enclosure arithmetic contains all the resulting values. It needs no lower bound on any individual floating-point rounding radius.

For interpolation, vary only the nearest time-grid value within its now-certified ball. The target lies within one half lattice spacing of some such included sample, whose real sinc-Gaussian coefficient is at least $(2/\pi)e^{-1/(8A^2H^2)}>0.63$. Interval evaluation of the linear form therefore encloses its exact finite value enlarged by more than $1.46\cdot10^{-37}$. This exceeds the complete analytic interpolation error above. No derivative enclosure enters a sign test in the original program; the stationary-point resolver uses values only. Grid points already have certified enclosures. A ball containing zero supplies no definite sign.
