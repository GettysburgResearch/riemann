We present a source-grounded account of recent arithmetic and approximation results in the Gettysburg Research Riemann repository. The common objective is to preserve the contribution of a fixed zeta zero while changing the arithmetic representation in which it must be estimated. We give a conductor-uniform comparison between primitive quadratic-field reciprocal coefficients and ordinary Möbius coefficients, with an explicit finite-family error. For actual quadratic characters, a mean-square approximation above the critical line yields quantitative full-residual transfer and an exact optimization that removes auxiliary local variance without removing the ordinary zeta factor. We also present a two-mollifier transfer theorem for capped reciprocal moments, complete native denominator and frequency-sector bounds, and positive cycle determinants with explicitly controlled regularization. On the approximation side, the frozen computational sources provide five directed certificates of real-zero entire functions matching the normalized theta expansion through orders 22, 24, and 26. A positive weighted canonical-moment criterion removes several unnecessary restrictions from the remaining all-order construction. We explain a compactness obstruction for strip-confined probability laws, give a corrected strip-transfer theorem and a finite-graph extension admitting controlled negative couplings, and preserve an exact failed comparison for the proposed boundary 0.87. The manuscript distinguishes proved component arguments, source-qualified analytic deductions, finite certificates, and unproved sufficient estimates. It claims neither the Riemann hypothesis nor a new global zero-free region for the ordinary zeta function.
