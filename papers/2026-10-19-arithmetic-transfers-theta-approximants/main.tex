\begin{abstract}
We describe recent work on zero-free regions for zeta functions and on two related approaches to the Riemann hypothesis. The first part follows the proposed improvements for Dedekind zeta functions from $31/32$ to $271/300$, explaining the role of zero-density estimates, averages of numerator $L$-values, shorter theta sums, and an enlarged domain for the local correction factors. These all-number-field results are kept separate from the stronger reported bounds for ordinary zeta and Dirichlet $L$-functions. The second part develops quadratic-character averages and mollifiers that retain the ordinary zeta factor; it identifies the rational error that remains after the auxiliary variance is removed. The third part presents finite real-zero approximants matching the theta expansion through order $26$, together with the convergence and positivity conditions needed to pass to all orders. We also discuss signed M\"obius sums, positive determinants, and precise failures of several attempted extensions. Proofs of the component results are included. Analytic proposals that depend on unreviewed adaptations and finite computer-assisted certificates are identified separately. The Riemann hypothesis is not proved here.
\end{abstract}
