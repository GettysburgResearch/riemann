## How to read the tables

Each row gives a year, one precise statement, the corpus works that carry it, and a status label. The bracketed tag after a PAPER id is the Observatory audit level of that record: L0 and L0+ mean an index-level record (metadata, abstract or review, not a reading of the paper), L1 means the full text was read for its statements, and L1-partial means part of it was. None of these levels means that a proof was checked.

Status labels:

- THEOREM: unconditional, refereed (or classical) result.
- THEOREM-COND (hypothesis): theorem whose hypothesis (RH, GRH, GLH, a conjecture) is not known.
- EQUIVALENCE: theorem that RH or a named variant is equivalent to the stated condition.
- COMPUTATION-RIGOROUS: finite computation with interval or error-controlled arithmetic. COMPUTATION-HEURISTIC: floating-point or local computation, not a verification.
- CONJECTURE and HEURISTIC: unproved statement or non-rigorous argument.
- REFUTED, ERROR, WITHDRAWN: a claim shown false, flawed or withdrawn.
- UNREFEREED: human preprint not refereed as of 2026-11-17. AI-UNREFEREED: AI-generated or AI-discovered claim, not refereed. Observatory audits are not refereeing.
- RECORD (quantity): the entry is a step in a record progression for that quantity.
- EVENT: a publication, announcement or structural event with no theorem content.

Finite or local results are never global evidence here: verification of RH to height $H$ proves RH only for $|\gamma|\le H$, and a proportion theorem, even at 100%, would not prove RH.

## S0. Pre-Riemann antecedents (before 1859)

Four selected works precede Riemann's memoir. Euler's product, Dirichlet's character L-series and Chebyshev's elementary prime counting supply the three ingredients that Riemann later fuses: an Euler product for a zeta-type series, L-functions attached to characters, and a quantitative theory of primes. No zero of zeta is yet in view. Everything proved here uses real arguments $s>1$ only, so none of it can see the critical line.

## S1. 1859 to 1900: the memoir, the product formula and the prime number theorem

Riemann's 1859 memoir creates the field: continuation and functional equation of $\zeta$, an explicit formula for primes in terms of its zeros, and a remark that it is "very probable" that all nontrivial zeros are real on the critical line. The next four decades turn the memoir's sketches into proofs. Hadamard's factorisation of entire functions of finite order and von Mangoldt's explicit formula make the zeros rigorous objects, and non-vanishing of $\zeta(1+it)$ gives the prime number theorem in 1896. RH itself is a side remark in this stratum, and it is von Koch's equivalence (1900) and Hilbert's problem list that turn it into a stated target. The 16 corpus works here are mostly L0 index records, with L1 reading only for Hadamard 1896, von Koch and de la Vallée Poussin.

## S2. 1901 to 1950: zero counting, the first computations, Lindelöf, and RH over finite fields

The first half of the century is the Landau-Hardy-Littlewood era. Von Mangoldt's zero count is made explicit, Hardy proves that infinitely many zeros lie on the line, Hardy and Littlewood prove a linear lower bound for their number, and Selberg proves a positive proportion; the Lindelöf hypothesis and the convexity bound open the growth problem. In parallel, hand computation (Gram, Backlund, Hutchinson, Titchmarsh) and then the first machine (Turing) test RH numerically. The function-field analogue is proved by Hasse for elliptic curves and Weil for all curves, which becomes the model of what a proof of RH should look like. Equivalent formulations (Riesz, Franel-Landau, Nyman, Jensen-Polya) and the first systematic false lead (Turan's partial-sum criterion) also appear.

## S3. 1951 to 1980: computers, large sieves, pair correlation and Deligne

The computer arrives: Lehmer, Lehman, Rosser and Brent push verification from $10^4$ to $7.5\times10^7$ zeros and reveal that zeros can nearly collide. Analytic technique advances along three lines: the large sieve and zero-density theorems (Bombieri, Huxley, Jutila), exponent-pair bounds for $\zeta(\tfrac12+it)$, and Levinson's method, which raises the critical-line proportion to one third. The decisive conceptual events are Weil's explicit formula and positivity criterion (1952), Deligne's proof of RH over finite fields (1974), and Montgomery's pair-correlation theorem (1973), which opens the random-matrix link. Newman's 1976 conjecture $\Lambda\ge0$ gives RH a quantitative one-sided companion, while the Polya conjecture falls.

## S4. 1981 to 2000: random matrices, Mertens falls, Robin, Li, Connes, moments

This is the stratum in which RH acquires its modern three-sided shape. First, numerics (Odlyzko) and theory (Katz-Sarnak, Keating-Snaith) tie zero statistics and moments to random matrices. Second, arithmetic and analytic reformulations appear: Robin's inequality, Nicolas's primorial criterion, Li's positivity, Weil positivity in new dress, and the Nyman-Beurling programme. Third, the zero-verification ladder reaches $1.5\times10^9$ zeros and Odlyzko and Schonhage's fast evaluation makes local computation at very large heights accessible. The Mertens conjecture is disproved, the Selberg class is formulated, Connes starts the noncommutative-geometry approach, and de Branges's proof announcements fail, before Clay lists RH as a Millennium Problem in 2000.

## S5. 2001 to 2015: conjectured moments, conditional sharpness, mollifier ceilings, explicit methods

Moment theory is reorganised by the CFKRS recipe and by Soundararajan's and Harper's conditional upper bounds, which together make the conjectured shape of the zeta moments the working hypothesis of the field. Explicit analysis matures: Ford's constants, Kadiri's and Mossinghoff-Trudgian's zero-free regions, Trudgian's $S(T)$ bounds, and rigorous computation (Gourdon's $10^{13}$ zeros, unrefereed). The critical-line proportion creeps from $0.4088$ to $0.4173$ by ever longer mollifiers, while the pair-correlation method pins down the simple-zero proportion under RH at $19/27$. Heat-flow results (Ki-Kim-Lee, Saouter-Gourdon-Demichel) squeeze $\Lambda$ from both sides, and Li's coefficients and Weil positivity are computed. A claimed proof by X.-J. Li (2008) is withdrawn within days.

## S6. 2016 to 2023: decoupling, de Bruijn-Newman, $10^{12}$ zeros, prolate spectra, failures

Three large results define this period: Bourgain's decoupling bound $\mu(\tfrac12)\le13/84$, Rodgers and Tao's proof of Newman's conjecture $\Lambda\ge0$, and Platt and Trudgian's verification of RH to height $3\cdot10^{12}$. Polymath15 makes the upper bound for $\Lambda$ effective ($\le0.22$) and ties RH to a finite-dimensional, computable heat-flow problem. In the other direction, Connes and Moscovici revive spectral approaches (prolate operators, zeta cycles) and the Jensen-polynomial programme produces rigorous hyperbolicity for huge degrees. Matomaki and Radziwill's short-interval multiplicative-function results shift the analytic method away from zeros altogether. The stratum also holds a run of failed or withdrawn proof attempts, now documented in the failure-anatomy campaign.

## S7. 2024 to 2026-11-17: Guth-Maynard, explicit regions, and AI-generated claims

After half a century in which the best zero-density exponent was Huxley's $12/5$ (1972), Guth and Maynard prove $30/13$ and a short-interval prime theorem for $\theta>17/30$. Explicit methods continue (Mossinghoff-Trudgian-Yang, Bellotti, Hiary-Patel-Yang, Bellotti-Trudgian-Yang), the pair-correlation programme is rebuilt (unconditional Montgomery theorem, then pair correlation alone implying 100% simple zeros), and finite computations (Lu-Zaman-Zhao, Weil positivity windows) are certified. In August to November 2026 two new kinds of claim enter: an AI-discovered proof that at least $2/3$ of the zeros are simple and critical (Claude, with Alpöge and Furman, independently re-proved by Lamzouri) and AI-generated manuscripts from OpenAI asserting zero-free half-planes $\operatorname{Re}s>7/8$ and $>11/12$ for all Dirichlet $L$-functions, the quasi-Riemann hypothesis. None of the 2026 claims is refereed as of 2026-11-17, none bears on $\operatorname{Re}s=\tfrac12$, and RH is open.

- Zero-free region constant $R$ in $\sigma>1-1/(R\log t)$ (ledger ZFR-4, ZFR-5): $30.4679$ (de la Vallée Poussin 1899); $17.537$ (Westphal 1938); $17.51631$ (Rosser-Schoenfeld 1962); $9.65$ (Stechkin 1970); $9.64591$ (Rosser-Schoenfeld 1975); $9.547897$ (Kondrat'ev 1977); $8.463$ (Ford 2002); $5.69693$ (Kadiri 2005); $5.68371$ (Jang-Kwon 2014); $5.573412$ (Mossinghoff-Trudgian 2015); $5.558691$ (Mossinghoff-Trudgian-Yang 2024, refereed record); $4.896$ (Bellotti-Trudgian-Yang 2026, unrefereed). ## What this timeline does not say

- RH is open. No entry above, including the 2026 entries, proves or disproves it, and nothing here should be read as evidence that RH is true or false beyond the stated finite or conditional content.
- The quasi-Riemann hypothesis ($\operatorname{Re}s>\theta$ with $\theta<1$ for all Dirichlet $L$-functions) concerns a half-plane away from the critical line; it is AI-generated and unrefereed, and the 7/8 manuscript and its Lean file were not audited or replayed by the Observatory.
- Proportion theorems (for instance $2/3$ simple and critical) are lower-density statements. Even $100\%$ would not prove RH.
- Verification to height $3\cdot10^{12}$, Weil positivity on bounded windows, and upper bounds for $\Lambda$ are finite or conditional statements. A "Lean-checked" claim attests only that a formal statement was checked by its producer, not by the Observatory.
- Where this timeline gives a priority, a constant or a date from the ledger marked "from memory", "unverified", or "not checked against the original", that qualification is attached to the entry and should be carried with it.
