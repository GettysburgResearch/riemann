## Mathematical content (Level 1: full text read)

**Research question.** Hecke's converse theorem (Mellin transform links Dirichlet series with functional equation to automorphic forms) requires Gamma(s) or Gamma(s/2)Gamma((s+1)/2) factors, so it does not cover zeta functions of real quadratic fields. Which Dirichlet-series systems with functional equations with Gamma((s+-ir)/2) factors correspond to a new class of automorphic functions (Maass wave forms), and can these be used to treat Hecke L-series with Grossencharacters of real quadratic fields (and Euler products via Hecke operators)?

**Historical motivation.** Hecke's theory characterizes Dirichlet series with functional equation (Gamma(s) or Gamma(s/2)Gamma((s+1)/2) factors) through modular forms, covering Q and imaginary quadratic fields, but not real quadratic fields, whose Gamma-factors are Gamma(s/2)^2-type. Maass (dedicated to Hecke's memory) introduces the wave equation on the hyperbolic plane to supply the missing automorphic objects, extending Hecke's Euler-product programme (Hecke operators) to this setting.

### Main results

- **THM-PAPER-0150-1** (Satz 1, theorem (converse theorem, equivalence), scope: global; Section Einleitung, p.143 (formulas (5)-(14)); expansion (15)-(17) on p.144; proof of equivalence in section 1, pp.150-152 (direction Dirichlet series -> wave function proved via (51)-(59))). Fix real $\lambda>0$, $r\ge0$, $q\in\mathbb N$, an $N\times N$ matrix $(c_{kl})$ with $(c_{kl})^2=I$, and integers $b_1,\dots,b_N$. Consider $2N$ functions $\varphi_k,\psi_k$ with: (1) $(s-1-ir)(s-1+ir)\varphi_k(s)$ and $(s-1-ir)(s-1+ir)\psi_k(s)$ entire of finite order; (2) $\xi_k(1-s)=\sum_l c_{kl}\xi_l(s)$, $\eta_k(1-s)=-\sum_l c_{kl}\eta_l(s)$ with $\xi_k(s)=(\lambda/\pi)^s\Gamma(\tfrac{s-ir}{2})\Gamma(\tfrac{s+ir}{2})\varphi_k(s)$, $\eta_k(s)=(\lambda/\pi)^{s+1}\Gamma(\tfrac{s+1-ir}{2})\Gamma(\tfrac{s+1+ir}{2})\psi_k(s)$; (3) $\varphi_k=\sum_{n\ne0, n\equiv b_k(q)}a_n^{(k)}|n|^{-s}$, $\psi_k=\sum(\operatorname{sgn}n)a_n^{(k)}|n|^{-s}$ converging somewhere; (4) the principal-part conditions with $\varrho_k=\sum c_{kl}\alpha_l$, $\sigma_k=\sum c_{kl}\beta_l$, $\varrho_k(1-e^{2\pi i b_k/q})=\sigma_k(1-e^{2\pi i b_k/q})=0$, and $\varphi_k-\frac{\alpha_k}{s-1-ir}-\frac{\beta_k}{s-1+ir}$ (resp. $\varphi_k-\frac{\alpha_k}{s-1}-\frac{\beta_k}{(s-1)^2}$ for $r=0$) entire. Such systems correspond bijectively to systems $g_k(\tau)$, $\tau=x+iy$, with: (1) $(\partial_x^2+\partial_y^2+\frac{r^2+1/4}{y^2})g_k=0$, regular in $y>0$; (2) $g_k=O(y^{\kappa_1})$ as $y\to\infty$, $O(y^{-\kappa_2})$ as $y\to0$ uniformly in $x$; (3) $g_k(\tau+\lambda/q)=e^{2\pi i b_k/q}g_k(\tau)$; (4) $g_k(-1/\tau)=\sum_l c_{kl}g_l(\tau)$. The correspondence is via Mellin transform and Fourier expansion $g_k=u_k(y)+\sum_{n\neq0}a_n^{(k)}y^{1/2}K_{ir}(2\pi|n|y/\lambda)e^{2\pi i n x/\lambda}$ with $u_k$ explicit in (16).
- **THM-PAPER-0150-2** (Satz 2, theorem, scope: global; p.145 (statement of Satz 2); the proof is in later sections (not read)). All solutions $\varphi(s)$ of $\xi(s)=(\lambda/\pi)^s(\Gamma(s/2))^2\varphi(s)=\xi(1-s)$ that are Dirichlet series with $(s-1)^2\varphi(s)$ entire of finite order form a linear family, generated for $\lambda=1$ by $\zeta^2(s)$ and for $\lambda=2$ by $2^{-s}\zeta^2(s)$ and $(1+2^{1-2s})\zeta^2(s)$. There is for neither $\lambda=1$ nor $\lambda=2$ a non-identically-zero Dirichlet series entire function of finite order $\psi$ with $\eta(s)=(\lambda/\pi)^{s+1}(\Gamma(\tfrac{s+1}{2}))^2\psi(s)=-\eta(1-s)$.
- **THM-PAPER-0150-3** (Satz 3, theorem (uniqueness for a concrete system), scope: global; p.147, (30)-(31)). The system of Hecke zeta functions of $\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)$ $\varphi_1=\zeta_0(s,0,(1),1,\sqrt5)$, $\psi_1=\zeta_1(s,0,(1),1,\sqrt5)$, $\varphi_2=\zeta_0(s,1,\dots)$, $\psi_2=\zeta_1(s,1,\dots)$, $\varphi_3=\zeta_0(s,2,\dots)$, $\psi_3=\zeta_1(s,2,\dots)$ is, up to a constant factor, uniquely determined by requirements I.1-I.4 of Satz 1 with $N=3$, $\lambda=q=5$, $r=0$, $b_1=0,b_2=1,b_3=-1$ and $(c_{kl})=\tfrac1{\sqrt5}\begin{pmatrix}1&2&2\\1&\zeta^2&\zeta+\zeta^{-1}\\1&\zeta+\zeta^{-1}&\zeta^{-2}\end{pmatrix}$, $\zeta=e^{2\pi i/5}$ (as printed; matrix entries should be checked against the scan).
- **THM-PAPER-0150-4** (Einleitung (23)-(29), theorem/observation (application), scope: global; pp.145-146, (23)-(29)). For a real quadratic field $R(\sqrt D)$, the partial Hecke zeta functions $\zeta_\nu(s,\varrho,\mathfrak a,\lambda_1^n,Q\sqrt D)$ with Grossencharacter $\lambda_1(\mu)=|\mu/\mu'|^{\pi i/\log\varepsilon}$ satisfy the functional equations (25),(26) and form a system of type Satz 1 with $N=Q^2D$, $\lambda=q=QD$, $r=\pi n/\log\varepsilon=cn$. The associated wave functions are $g(\tau,\varrho,\mathfrak a,\lambda_1^n,Q\sqrt D)$ in (29), invariant under the principal congruence group $M(QD)$ (Hecke).
- **THM-PAPER-0150-5** (Einleitung p.145, theorem (finiteness), scope: global; p.145). If the normal subgroup $N\subset G(\lambda/q)$ (kernel of the representation on the matrices (20)) has finite index in $G(\lambda/q)$ (e.g. $\lambda=q$ or $2q$), then by Siegel's method there are only finitely many linearly independent automorphic wave functions for $N$ having prescribed behaviour at the parabolic cusps like $g_k$ as $y\to\infty$; the dimension of the linear classes of function systems I and II is finite.
- **THM-PAPER-0150-6** (Einleitung pp.147-150, theorem (outlined, proofs deferred), scope: global; pp.147-150, formulas (32)-(44); proofs referenced to Hecke T_n I,II and Petersson K I-III; the paper's full proofs lie in pages not read). For the congruence group of level $Q$, a wave function $g$ with expansion (32) is assigned the pair of Dirichlet series (33). Wave functions are reduced to cusp-form-like ones by subtracting Eisenstein series $E(\tau,1+2ir;(a_1,a_2),Q)$ of (34); for $r>0$ one gets a decomposition $\mathfrak E(Q)=\mathfrak E^{+1}\oplus\mathfrak E^{-1}$ (eigenvalue of the reflection $K:g(\tau)\mapsto g(-\bar\tau)$) with Dirichlet series linearly equivalent to products $L(s+ir,\chi_1)L(s-ir,\chi_2)$ (40). Hecke operators $T_m^t$ act, and for primes $p\nmid Q$ the matrices $\lambda(p)$ can be simultaneously diagonalized (via Petersson metrization), yielding Euler product (44) $\Phi(s)=t^{-s}\prod_p(\lambda(1)-\lambda(p)p^{-s}+\lambda(1)\chi(p)p^{-2s})^{-1}$.

### Key definitions

- Wave function: solution g(tau) of (partial_x^2+partial_y^2+(r^2+1/4)/y^2)g=0 on upper half plane, i.e. eigenfunction of the hyperbolic Laplacian with eigenvalue 1/4+r^2 (Maass form, nonanalytic automorphic function)
- Group G(lambda/q) generated by tau->tau+lambda/q and tau->-1/tau; normal subgroup N = kernel of the representation on (c_kl), (delta_kl e^{2 pi i b_k/q})
- Function systems I (Dirichlet series) and II (wave functions) in Satz 1
- Hecke zeta functions zeta_0, zeta_1 of a real quadratic field with Grossencharacter lambda_1^n (even and odd), with sgn(N mu) twist for zeta_1

### Principal lemmas

- **THM-PAPER-0150-L1** ((47)-(50); p.150 (section 1)). $K_{ir}(z)\sqrt{2z/\pi}\,e^{z}\to1$ as $z\to\infty$, and $|K_{ir}(z)|\le C_1 z^{-1/2}e^{-z}$ for all $z>0$ with $C_1=\pi/(\sqrt2\,|\Gamma(\tfrac12+ir)|)$; for $r=0$ the quantity $K_0(z)\sqrt{2z/\pi}e^z$ is monotonically increasing in $z$. — *Role:* Uniform control of K-Bessel to pass from Dirichlet series convergence to Fourier expansion bounds (12) and to shift contours.
- **THM-PAPER-0150-L2** ((51)-(52); p.151). Convergence of the Dirichlet series (7) at some point is equivalent to the polynomial bound $|a_n^{(k)}|\le C_2|n|^\kappa$; then $|g_k(\tau)-u_k(y)|=O(y^{-(\kappa+1/2)})$ as $y\to0$, giving growth condition (12) with $\kappa_1>1/2,\ \kappa_2=\kappa+1/2$. — *Role:* Verifies growth conditions II.2 and justifies termwise differentiation of the Fourier expansion.
- **THM-PAPER-0150-L3** ((52)-(56); pp.151-152). Mellin-Barnes integral for $y^{(1+\nu_1+\nu_2)/2}K_{(\nu_2-\nu_1)/2}(2\pi|n|y/\lambda)$ gives $F_k(y)=\sum a_n^{(k)}y^{1/2}K_{ir}(\cdot)=\frac{1}{8\pi i}\int\xi_k(s)y^{1/2-s}ds$ (and analogous $G_k$); replacing $s\to1-s$, using functional equations (5) and shifting the line gives $F^*_k(1/y)=\sum c_{kl}F^*_l(y)$, $G_k(1/y)=-\sum c_{kl}G_l(y)$ with $F^*_k=u_k+F_k$. — *Role:* Heart of the Dirichlet series -> wave function direction; residues produce the terms $u_k(y)$ of (16).
- **THM-PAPER-0150-L4** ((58)-(59) + recursion; p.152). The functions $g_k(-1/\tau)-\sum c_{kl}g_l(\tau)$ solve the wave equation and vanish with $\partial_x$ on $x=0$; expanding in powers of $x$, the recursion $(n+2)(n+1)c_{n+2}+c_n''+\frac{r^2+1/4}{y^2}c_n=0$ forces the solution to vanish identically (uniqueness for Cauchy data on $x=0$). — *Role:* Replaces the analytic-case identity theorem; shows transformation formula (14) from the functional equations (56). Uses the assumption (I.1) that the entire functions have finite order only via the justified contour shifts.

### Proof architecture

The equivalence of Satz 1 is proved by the Mellin transform and its inversion applied to the Fourier coefficients. (a) Dirichlet series to wave functions: define g_k by the Fourier expansion (15) with K-Bessel functions; bound the coefficients to get convergence and (12),(13); represent F_k, G_k as Mellin-Barnes integrals, shift contours using (5) and the principal parts (10), obtaining the inversion relations (56). Since the wave equation has no analytic initial-value uniqueness in the usual sense, the key new step is a Cauchy-problem uniqueness argument on the line x=0 (the Dirichlet series determine g only on x=0, so both g and d g/d x must be given, which is why each wave function corresponds to a PAIR of Dirichlet series phi_k, psi_k with Gamma((s+ir)/2)Gamma((s-ir)/2) and Gamma((s+1+ir)/2)Gamma((s+1-ir)/2) factors respectively). (b) Converse: from wave functions with (11)-(14) take the Fourier expansion in x (period lambda/q twisted by (13)), identify coefficients with K_{ir}, define Dirichlet series by Mellin transform and derive functional equations from (14). Applications Satz 2 and 3 use Siegel's finiteness argument and the explicit parabolic behaviour. Sections 2 onward (not read) apparently contain the proofs of finiteness, Eisenstein series, and the Hecke operator theory.

## Context and interpretation

**Importance and obstacle overcome.** Founding paper of the theory of Maass wave forms. The obstacle was the non-analytic character: the Dirichlet series only give g on the line x=0, so a pair of series (even and odd, phi and psi) is needed, and uniqueness comes from Cauchy data for the wave equation rather than analyticity. It extends converse theorems to Gamma((s+ir)/2)Gamma((s-ir)/2) factors, includes zeta functions of real quadratic fields with Grossencharacter, and sets up Hecke operators/Euler products for them. Also relevant to RH-type generalized L-functions (Selberg-class models: Maass L-functions) as the first examples of automorphic L-functions whose zeros/RH one may study.

**Relation to prior work.** Direct nonanalytic analogue of Hecke 1936/1937 (converse theorem + T_n) and uses Hecke's 1927 papers (principal congruence subgroups, Eisenstein series) and Petersson's Euler product method. Also invoked later by Selberg (trace formula for Maass forms), Weil (converse theorem for GL(2) 1967), and Jacquet-Langlands.

**Limitations and open questions.** Beyond the read pages: the statements of subsequent theorems on Eisenstein series, Hecke operators and the 'Hauptachsentheorem' are described only in outline in the introduction; proofs are in sections not read. Satz 1 gives a converse theorem for the specific group G(lambda/q), and does not cover general level and nebentypus in full generality; no Ramanujan-Petersson or RH statement for Maass L-functions is made. Existence of non-Eisenstein Maass cusp forms is not established in the portion read.

**Ideas in remarks/appendices/abandoned paths.** Remark on p.148 that the Eisenstein series of level QD and the wave functions g(tau,rho,a,lambda,Q sqrt D) are not independent: summation of g over a complete system of ideal classes gives Eisenstein series of level D, giving as a by-product a new proof of Dirichlet's class number formula for real quadratic fields (stated on p.148 in the introduction). Also the claim that for Q=1,2,3,4,6 (and 5) the number of independent Eisenstein series equals sigma (number of cusps) only for r=0 (p.148).

## Research potential

- **Hypotheses that might be weakened.** Hypothesis (1) of Satz 1 that (s-1-ir)(s-1+ir)phi_k is entire of finite order; the matrix condition (c_kl)^2=I (functional equation is an involution); the fixed Gamma-factor shape. Weakening to Selberg-class type degree-2 factors Gamma(s/2+mu_j) is the route to a converse theorem for degree-2 Selberg class members.
- **Generalizations.** Converse theorem for degree 2 in the Selberg class (Kaczorowski-Perelli: classification of degree <=2) is the direct descendant; Weil-type twist hypotheses could replace the 'pair of series' trick; vector-valued/Hilbert-modular analogues for number fields beyond real quadratic.
- **Candidate connections.** Maass L-functions are test cases for RH-style questions (zero statistics, GRH) and for Selberg-class axioms; the functional-equation-with-pairs (phi,psi) structure is exactly the object studied in signed native/Mobius or L-function family programmes of the archive (family D/E).
- **Reusable lemmas.** Bounds (47)-(50) on K_{ir} (uniform in z>0, explicit constant C_1) and the Cauchy-uniqueness step (58)-(59) are reusable as standalone lemmas for any converse theorem on the hyperbolic plane.
- **Possible experiments.** Numerically verify Satz 3 for Q(sqrt5): compute zeta_0, zeta_1 for the three classes, check functional equations with the printed matrix (c_kl) and check the associated wave function g of (29) satisfies Delta g = (1/4) g (r=0); test r>0 Grossencharacters n>=1 for evaluation of zeros of those L-functions.
- **Risks / barriers.** No new information on zero location; the correspondence is soft (Mellin) and gives no handle on critical-line vs off-line zeros. Deep part is the analytic theory of Maass cusp forms (Selberg), not in this paper.
