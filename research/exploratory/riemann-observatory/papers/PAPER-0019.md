## Mathematical content (Level 1: full text read)

**Research question.** For families of GL_2 holomorphic L-functions (weight aspect F_K, level aspect F_N with N prime) and the family F_{sym^2} of symmetric square L-functions, determine the 1-level density of zeros near s=1/2 (scaled by (\log c_F)/2\pi), test the Katz-Sarnak Density Conjecture (symmetry type SO(even), SO(odd), O, Sp) beyond the 'diagonal' support range, and draw consequences for non-vanishing of central values L(1/2,f).

**Historical motivation.** Katz-Sarnak conjectured that low-lying zeros of families follow eigenvalue distributions of classical compact groups (derived in function-field cases). ILS tests the conjecture for genuine GL2/GL3 families, going beyond the diagonal range reachable for zeta (Montgomery, Hejhal, Rudnick-Sarnak) and leveraging it for non-vanishing at the central point (cf. Iwaniec-Sarnak, where >1/2 is tied to Landau-Siegel zeros).

### Main results

- **THM-PAPER-0019-1** (Theorem 1, theorem (conditional on GRH), scope: asymptotic; Sect. 1, Theorem 1 and eq. (4)). Assume GRH for all relevant L-functions. Let \phi\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R) be even with \mathrm{supp}\,\hat\phi\subset(-2,2). Then \lim_{K\to\infty}\frac1{M^+(K)}\sum_{k\equiv0(4),k\le K}\sum_{f\in H_k^+(\Gamma)}D(f,\phi)=\int\phi\,W(SO(even)), \lim\frac1{M^-(K)}\sum_{k\equiv2(4),k\le K}\sum_{f\in H_k^-(\Gamma)}D(f,\phi)=\int\phi\,W(SO(odd)); and for N\to\infty (N prime, fixed weight k) \frac1{M_k^\pm(N)}\sum_{f\in H_k^\pm(\Gamma_0(N))}D(f,\phi)\to\int\phi\,W(SO(even/odd)). Moreover for \mathrm{supp}\,\hat\phi\subset(-4/3,4/3): \lim_{K\to\infty}\frac1{M(K)}\sum_{k\equiv0(2),k\le K}\sum_{f\in H_k(\Gamma)}D(\mathrm{sym}^2(f),\phi)=\int\phi\,W(Sp). Here D(F,\phi)=\sum_{\gamma_F}\phi(\gamma_F\log c_F/2\pi), c_f=k^2 (F_K), c_f=N (F_N), c_{sym^2 f}=k^2; W(SO(even))=1+\frac{\sin2\pi x}{2\pi x}, W(SO(odd))=\delta_0+1-\frac{\sin2\pi x}{2\pi x}, W(O)=\tfrac12\delta_0+\tfrac12 (stated as \tfrac12\delta_0+dx? see audit notes), W(Sp)=1-\frac{\sin2\pi x}{2\pi x}. *Conditional on:* GRH.
- **THM-PAPER-0019-2** (Remark B after Theorem 1, theorem (unconditional range), scope: asymptotic; Sect. 1 Remark (B)). Theorem 1 holds unconditionally for \mathrm{supp}\,\hat\phi\subset(-1,1) (only the diagonal term of Petersson's formula contributes). The extension to (-2,2) (resp. (-4/3,4/3)) is where Kloosterman-sum terms enter; \hat W(Sp),\hat W(SO(even)),\hat W(SO(odd)) are discontinuous at \xi=\pm1, so the beyond-diagonal range distinguishes the symmetry types.
- **THM-PAPER-0019-3** (Corollary 2, theorem (conditional on GRH for GL2 and Dirichlet L-functions), scope: asymptotic; Sect. 1, Corollary 2; Sect. 4; Appendix). \liminf_{K\to\infty}\#\{f\in H_k:k\le K,k\equiv0(4),L(1/2,f)\ne0\}/\#\{\ldots\}>9/16; for k\equiv2(4): \liminf\#\{L'(1/2,f)\ne0\}/\#>15/16; same bounds 9/16 and 15/16 for H_k^\pm(\Gamma_0(N)) as N\to\infty; \limsup_{N\to\infty}M_k^*(N)^{-1}\sum_{f\in H_k^*(N)}\mathrm{ord}(1/2,f)<1; for sym^2: \liminf\#\{L(1/2,\mathrm{sym}^2f)\ne0\}/\#>55/64. *Conditional on:* GRH.
- **THM-PAPER-0019-4** (Theorem 3, theorem (unconditional), scope: asymptotic; Sect. 1, Theorem 3). With the weighted average A_K[X_f]=\sum_{k\equiv0(2)}h(k/K)\sum_{f\in H_k(\Gamma)}L(1,\mathrm{sym}^2f)^{-1}X_f (h\in C_0^\infty(0,\infty), h\ge0), for \phi\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R) even with \mathrm{supp}\,\hat\phi\subset(-2,2): \lim_{K\to\infty}A_K[D_k(f,\phi)]/A_K[1]=\int\phi(x)W(O)(x)dx. (Parity not broken; D_k approximation (20).)
- **THM-PAPER-0019-5** (Hypothesis 4 / Theorem 5, conditional theorem, scope: asymptotic; Sect. 1 Hypothesis 4, Theorem 5; Sect. 2.3 Prop. 4). Hypothesis 4: for every \epsilon>0, X\ge2, c\ge1, (a,c)=1, \sum_{p\le X,p\equiv a(c)}e(2\sqrt p/c)\ll_\epsilon X^{1/2+\epsilon}. Theorem 5: assuming Hypothesis 4, Theorem 3 holds for \mathrm{supp}\,\hat\phi\subset(-7/3,7/3) (Prop. 4 of Sect. 2). Only a bound of the form X^\delta c^A with \delta<3/4 is needed for the extension; \delta=7/8 is essentially Vinogradov, \delta=3/4 follows from the density hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions. *Conditional on:* Hypothesis 4.
- **THM-PAPER-0019-6** (Corollary 6, conditional theorem (quasi-GRH for GL2 in weight aspect), scope: asymptotic; Sect. 1 Corollary 6; Sect. 2 eqs. (36)-(39)). Assume Hypothesis 4 and that the zeros of L(s,f), f\in H_k^*(\Gamma), lie either on \mathrm{Re}\,s=1/2 or on the real axis. Then for k sufficiently large (effectively), every such f satisfies L(\beta,f)\ne0 for \beta>13/14. (Proof: test function \phi=(\sin(2\pi\nu x)/(2\pi\nu x))^2, \nu<7/6, positive on the line and real axis zeros give k^{4\nu\mu_f}\ll k^{2+\epsilon}, hence \mu_f\le(2\nu)^{-1}\to3/7.) The remark states the assumption on zero locations can be removed by a different method [ILS] to get a quasi-RH for large weight. *Conditional on:* Hypothesis 4.
- **THM-PAPER-0019-7** (Appendix, Prop. 1 and Cor. 2, theorem (extremal problem), scope: finite; Sect. 5 (Appendix), Prop. 1, Cor. 2). In the nonsingular case \inf_{g\in L^2[-1,1]}R(g)=1/A with A=\langle1,f_0\rangle>0 where (I+K)f_0=1 (Fredholm equation of the second kind), the infimum being attained at f_0 (unique if \lambda_1>-1). Corollary 2 determines when \phi=(\sin 2\pi x/2\pi x)^2 is optimal for the non-vanishing proportion; yields the constants 9/16, 15/16, 55/64 up to explicit tiny improvements.

### Proof architecture

Reduce 1-level density to prime sums via the Rudnick-Sarnak explicit formula (Prop. 3), with GRH used to ensure zeros are \frac12+i\gamma, \gamma real, so \phi (Schwartz on R) can be applied to \gamma(\log c)/2\pi. Average Hecke eigenvalue sums with the Petersson formula: the diagonal term gives the 'identity' part of the density; for support in (-1,1) only diagonal contributes (unconditional). For supports beyond 1, Kloosterman sums with Bessel functions contribute; summing over weight (F_K) with Prop. 1 kills most terms, and the remainder is evaluated via exponential sums over primes in progressions (needing GRH for Dirichlet L-functions, or Hypothesis 4 for support beyond 2). In the level aspect, Petersson over Gamma_0(N) with Atkin-Lehner-type input separates signs of functional equation. The symmetric square case requires its own treatment because the Hecke eigenvalues of sym^2 involve \lambda_f(p^2) and new non-diagonal contributions appear. Positive test functions \phi=(\sin(2\pi\nu x)/2\pi\nu x)^2 give non-vanishing proportions, with the optimal choice obtained by solving a Fredholm extremal problem (Appendix).

## Context and interpretation

**Importance and obstacle overcome.** First demonstration, in natural automorphic families, that the symmetry type is detectable beyond the diagonal support (-1,1) (the transition at \xi=\pm1 distinguishes SO(even), SO(odd), Sp, O), giving unconditional evidence for the Density Conjecture in a sense that pair-correlation results for zeta do not. The obstacle is controlling Kloosterman terms; overcome by summing over k and using prime sums. Nonvanishing proportions 9/16, 15/16, 55/64 under GRH exceed unconditional 1/2 (which cannot be exceeded without handling Landau-Siegel zeros).

**Relation to prior work.** 

**Limitations and open questions.** Support is restricted ((-2,2), (-4/3,4/3), or (-7/3,7/3) under Hypothesis 4), well short of the full conjecture; results are in measure/average over families (cannot decide RH for any single f); Corollary 6 gives only a zero-free region \beta>13/14 conditional on Hypothesis 4 plus zeros-on-line-or-real assumption; the Density Conjecture itself remains open for arbitrary support. For the sym^2 family the range (-4/3,4/3) is smaller than for GL2.

## Research potential

- **Hypotheses that might be weakened.** Hypothesis 4 is needed only with a bound X^\delta c^A, \delta<3/4; a proof for some \delta<3/4 uniformly in c would suffice, and \delta=3/4 follows from the density hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions, so any unconditional improvement of density estimates would unconditionalize Theorem 5/Corollary 6 in the quasi-GRH form. The GRH assumption in Theorem 1 is used only to apply \phi to real \gamma.
- **Generalizations.** Higher-level densities (Hughes-Rudnick, Miller); other families (twists, GL_3 Hecke families, Rankin-Selberg); the same architecture gives the Sp/O/SO(even)/SO(odd) discrimination; replacing the explicit-formula + Petersson route with the ratios conjecture to include lower-order terms.
- **Candidate connections.** Quasi-RH from Hypothesis 4 (Corollary 6, 13/14) is a rare conditional 'GL1 exponential sum implies GL2 zero-free region' statement; direct relevance to quasi-RH programs and to the Lang-Weil analogy; connection to Landau-Siegel zero (Iwaniec-Sarnak). Positive-test-function extremal problem connects to Selberg sieve / Montgomery-Vaughan-type quadratic forms and Beurling-Selberg extremal functions.
- **Reusable lemmas.** Proposition 4 (average of D_K(f,phi) with harmonic weights, support up to 7/3 under Hypothesis 4); the Appendix extremal-problem Proposition 1 (explicit minimiser via Fredholm equation) for optimizing non-vanishing proportions in any family where the density is known on a supported range.
- **Possible experiments.** Numerically compute low-lying zeros of L(s,f) for all f in H_k(1) with k up to a few hundred and check the transition of the 1-level density at \xi=\pm1 (SO(even) vs SO(odd)); test Hypothesis 4 numerically for moderate X, c (check size of the exponential sum against X^{1/2}); verify Corollary 2 constants by computing the optimal positive test function from the Appendix integral equation.
- **Risks / barriers.** Extending beyond support 2 for the weight aspect essentially hits GL2 RH (the authors note this); any improvement beyond (-7/3,7/3) would need new input on Kloosterman sums or exponential sums over primes; unconditional access to Hypothesis 4 is as hard as strong density estimates.
