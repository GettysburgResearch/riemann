## Mathematical content (Level 1: full text read)

**Research question.** Give a uniform recipe producing the complete main terms (all lower-order terms, not only the leading coefficient) of integral moments and shifted moments of $L$-functions in $t$-aspect and in families (Dirichlet, quadratic twists, modular forms), and show the resulting formulas parallel the exact random-matrix formulas for $U(N)$, $USp(2N)$, $O(2N)$.

### Main results

- **THM-PAPER-0011-1** (Conjecture 1.5.1.1 (with 1.5.2-1.5.7), conjecture, scope: conditional; Section 1.5, p.13; general form Conjecture 2.5.2.4, p.30). For a suitable weight $g$, $\int_{-\infty}^\infty|\zeta(\tfrac12+it)|^{2k}g(t)\,dt=\int P_k\bigl(\log\tfrac t{2\pi}\bigr)(1+O(t^{-1/2+\epsilon}))g(t)\,dt$, where $P_k$ is a polynomial of degree $k^2$ given by $P_k(x)=\frac{(-1)^k}{k!^2(2\pi i)^{2k}}\oint\cdots\oint G(z_1,\dots,z_{2k})\frac{\Delta^2(z_1,\dots,z_{2k})}{\prod_j z_j^{2k}}e^{\frac x2\sum_{j=1}^k(z_j-z_{k+j})}dz$, with $G=A_k(z)\prod_{i,j=1}^k\zeta(1+z_i-z_{k+j})$ and $A_k(z)=\prod_p\prod_{i,j}\bigl(1-p^{-1-z_i+z_{k+j}}\bigr)\int_0^1\prod_{j=1}^k(1-e(\theta)p^{-1/2-z_j})^{-1}(1-e(-\theta)p^{-1/2+z_{k+j}})^{-1}d\theta$ (the extract prints the signs of the shifts partly garbled). Shifted version (1.5.6)-(1.5.7) has $\prod_{j,i}(z_j-\alpha_i)$ in the denominator. *Conditional on:* conjecture (proved only for k=1,2, with O(T^{1/2+eps}) error for k=2 only in smooth-weight form).
- **THM-PAPER-0011-2** (Theorem 1.5.1.2, theorem (quoted from [CFKRS] random matrix paper, CAND-01369), scope: finite; Section 1.5, p.13, eqs. (1.5.8)-(1.5.9)). $J_k(U(N),0)=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{j!}{(k+j)!}\prod_{i=1}^k(N+i+j)$, and for general shifts $J_k(U(N),\alpha)=\frac{(-1)^k}{k!^2(2\pi i)^{2k}}\oint\cdots\oint G(z)\frac{\Delta^2(z)}{\prod_{i,j}(z_j-\alpha_i)}e^{\frac N2\sum(z_j-z_{k+j})}dz$ with $G(z)=\prod_{i,j=1}^k(1-e^{-z_i+z_{k+j}})^{-1}$.
- **THM-PAPER-0011-3** (Conjecture 1.5.1.3, conjecture, scope: conditional; Section 1.5, pp.14-16, eqs. (1.5.10)-(1.5.19)). For fundamental discriminants $d$ and a weight $g$ supported in $(0,\infty)$ or $(-\infty,0)$: $\sum^*_dL(\tfrac12,\chi_d)^kg(|d|)=\sum^*_dQ_k(\log|d|)(1+O(|d|^{-1/2+\epsilon}))g(|d|)$, where $Q_k$ is a polynomial of degree $k(k+1)/2$ given by the $k$-fold residue (1.5.12) with $G=A_k\prod_{i\le j}\zeta(1+z_i+z_j)$ and $\Delta(z_1^2,\dots,z_k^2)^2$. Equating log-conductors gives $2N=\log(|d|/\pi)+\frac{\Gamma'}{\Gamma}(\tfrac14+a)$. *Conditional on:* conjecture.
- **THM-PAPER-0011-4** (Theorem 1.5.1.4 / 1.5.1.6, theorem (quoted, [CFKRS] CAND-01369), scope: finite; Section 1.5, eqs. (1.5.17), (1.5.26)-(1.5.29)). $J_k(USp(2N),0)=2^{k(k+1)/2}\prod_{j=1}^k\frac{j!}{(2j)!}\prod_{1\le i\le j\le k}\bigl(N+\tfrac{i+j}2\bigr)$, $J_k(SO(2N),0)=2^{k(k+1)/2}\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\frac{j!}{(2j)!}\prod_{0\le i<j\le k-1}\bigl(N+\tfrac{i+j}2\bigr)$ (the printed factor in 1.5.26 uses the index range $0\le i<j\le k-1$ as extracted), with shifted contour-integral forms (1.5.18), (1.5.28)-(1.5.29).
- **THM-PAPER-0011-5** (Conjecture 1.5.1.5, conjecture, scope: conditional; Section 1.5, eqs. (1.5.21)-(1.5.25), (1.5.30)). For $q$ squarefree, $H_n(q)$ the newforms in $S_n(\Gamma_0(q))$, $\sum_{f\in H_n(q)}L_f(\tfrac12)^k\langle f,f\rangle^{-1}=\sum_f R_k(n,q)\langle f,f\rangle^{-1}(1+O((nq)^{-1/2+\epsilon}))$ as $nq\to\infty$, where $R_k$ is the $k$-fold residue (1.5.23) with $G=A_k\prod_{i<j}\zeta(1+z_i+z_j)$ and $A_k$ of (1.5.25) (integral over $\sin^2\theta$ for orthogonal symmetry); $2N=\log(q/4\pi^2)+\frac{\Gamma'}\Gamma(n/2)$. *Conditional on:* conjecture.
- **THM-PAPER-0011-6** (Section 2.1 (the recipe, six steps), heuristic method, scope: conditional; Section 2.1, pp.21-22; Section 4.1 for families). (1) Start with the product of $2k$ shifted $L$-functions. (2) Replace each by the two terms of its approximate functional equation, giving $2^{2k}$ terms. (3) Keep the $\binom{2k}{k}$ terms for which the product of $\chi$-factors is not oscillating. (4) Keep only the diagonal of the Dirichlet-series sums. (5) Complete the sums to infinity; call the total $M(s,\alpha)$. (6) Conjecture that $L$ and $M$ have the same average, with error $O(t^{-1/2+\epsilon})$. The authors state that the recipe 'is not a heuristic', that steps can discard terms of main-term size, and that other steps add terms back, with the conjecture being that these cancel. *Conditional on:* unproved cancellation of the discarded and added terms.
- **THM-PAPER-0011-7** (Theorem 1.6.1.1 and Corollaries 1.6.1.2-1.6.1.3, theorem (unconditional, new), scope: asymptotic; Section 1.6, pp.18-19, eqs. (1.6.4)-(1.6.8)). For $I(\alpha,\beta,\delta)=\int_0^\infty\zeta(\tfrac12+it+\alpha)\zeta(\tfrac12-it-\beta)e^{-\delta t}dt$, for every $\eta>0$, $|\alpha|,|\beta|\le\tfrac12-\eta$, $|\arg\delta|\le\tfrac\pi2-\eta$: $I=\int_0^\infty\bigl(\zeta(1+\alpha-\beta)+(\tfrac t{2\pi})^{\alpha-\beta}\zeta(1-\alpha+\beta)\bigr)e^{-\delta t}dt+C_\delta(\alpha,\beta)+O(\delta\log1/\delta)$ uniformly, with $C_\delta(\alpha,\beta)\ll\log1/\delta$ and $C_\delta(\alpha,-\alpha)=-2\pi\zeta(2\alpha)$. Consequently $\lim_{\delta\to0}\bigl(\int_0^\infty|\zeta(\tfrac12+it)|^2e^{-\delta t}dt-\int_0^\infty(2\gamma+\log\tfrac t{2\pi})e^{-\delta t}dt\bigr)=\pi$.
- **THM-PAPER-0011-8** (Section 1.6-1.7 consistency, consistency statement (partly proved by others), scope: asymptotic; Sections 1.6-1.7, 5.1). For $k=1$, $P_1(x)=x+2\gamma$, matching Ingham's theorem $\int_0^T|\zeta(\tfrac12+it)|^2dt=\int_0^T(\log\tfrac t{2\pi}+2\gamma)dt+O(T^{1/2+\epsilon})$. For $k=2$ the recipe agrees with Motohashi's main term $L_r$ and gives the Ingham/Heath-Brown polynomial, with leading coefficient $\frac1{2\pi^2}$ (so $P_2(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi^2}x^4+\cdots$ in (5.1.4); numerically $0.0507x^4+0.6989x^3+2.426x^2+3.228x+1.312$). A residue computation yields the restated 4th-moment form (1.7.13) with $\frac{\zeta(1+x)^4\zeta(1+y)^4}{\zeta(1+x-y)\zeta(1+y-x)\zeta(2+2x+2y)}$.

### Principal lemmas

- **THM-PAPER-0011-L1** (Theorem 2.4.2.1; Section 2.4, p.27). For $|\alpha_j|<\delta$, the Dirichlet series $R(s;\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_{2k})$ converges absolutely for $\sigma>\tfrac12+\delta$ and has meromorphic continuation to $\sigma>\tfrac14+\delta$, with $R=\prod_{i,j=1}^k\zeta(2s+\alpha_i-\alpha_{k+j})A_k(s;\alpha)$ where $A_k$ is an Euler product of local factors $B_p=\int_0^1\prod_jL_p(e(\theta)/p^{s+\alpha_j})L_p(e(-\theta)/p^{s-\alpha_{k+j}})d\theta$ times the correction $\prod(1-p^{-2s-\alpha_i+\alpha_{k+j}})$. — *Role:* identifies the arithmetic factor $A_k$ and its analyticity region, which is needed for the residue evaluation
- **THM-PAPER-0011-L2** (Lemma 2.5.2.1; Section 2.5, p.29). Let $F(a;b)$ be symmetric in each group of $k$ variables, regular near $0$, $f$ with a simple pole of residue 1 at 0, and $K=F\prod_{i,j=1}^kf(a_i-b_j)$. Then $\sum_{\sigma\in\Xi}K(\alpha_{\sigma(1)},\dots;\alpha_{\sigma(k+1)},\dots)=\frac{(-1)^k}{k!^2(2\pi i)^{2k}}\oint\cdots\oint K(z)\frac{\Delta(z_1,\dots,z_{2k})^2}{\prod_{i,j=1}^{2k}(z_i-\alpha_j)}dz$ over small circles about the $\alpha_j$. — *Role:* converts the sum over $\binom{2k}{k}$ shuffles in the recipe to the contour integral of Conjecture 1.5.1.1
- **THM-PAPER-0011-L3** (Lemma 2.5.2.2; Section 2.5, p.30). For symmetric regular $F$, simple-pole $f$ of residue 1, and $K=F\prod_{i\le j}f(a_i+a_j)$ or $F\prod_{i<j}f(a_i+a_j)$: $\sum_{\epsilon_j=\pm1}K(\epsilon\alpha)$ and the sign-weighted version equal the $k$-fold contour integrals (2.5.5)-(2.5.6) with kernel $\Delta(z^2)^2\prod z_j$ (resp. $\prod\alpha_j$) over $\prod(z_i-\alpha_j)(z_i+\alpha_j)$. — *Role:* symplectic/orthogonal analogue of Lemma 2.5.2.1
- **THM-PAPER-0011-L4** (Lemma 2.5.2.3; Section 2.5, p.30). General statement for $F(a;b)$ with $m$ $a$-variables and $n$ $b$-variables, $G=F\prod f(a_i-b_j)$, $f=1/s+c+\cdots$: the sum over $\Xi_{m,n}$ equals $\frac{(-1)^{m+n}}{m!n!}$ times the sum of residues of $G\Delta^2/\prod(z_i-\alpha_j)$ at $z=\alpha_\sigma$ (printed form partially garbled in extract). — *Role:* master lemma from which 2.5.2.1 and 2.5.2.2 follow
- **THM-PAPER-0011-L5** (Lemma 2.6.2.1; Section 2.6, p.32 (statement not fully read)). For $L_p(x)=(1-\gamma_px)^{-1}$ with $|\gamma_p|=1$ an explicit evaluation (the extract cuts off the statement) shows the local factor of $A_k$ is a polynomial in $p^{-1}$ and $p^{-\alpha_i}$, as noted in comment 3 after Theorem 1.5.1.2. — *Role:* explicit form of the arithmetic factor; shows $A_k$ is polynomial in $p^{\pm\alpha_i}$
- **THM-PAPER-0011-L6** (Section 2.7 (leading term recovery); Section 2.7, pp.34-39 (only beginning read)). $P_k(x)$ has leading term $\frac{g_ka_k}{k^2!}x^{k^2}$ with $a_k=A_k(0,\dots,0)$ and $g_k=k^2!\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}\frac{j!}{(k+j)!}$ (eq. 1.3.3). This recovers the Keating-Snaith leading-order conjecture, which is a special case of Conjecture 1.5.1.1 (derivation re-done in Section 2.7). — *Role:* consistency with Conrey-Ghosh/Keating-Snaith
- **THM-PAPER-0011-L7** (Comment 4 after Thm 1.5.1.2; Section 1.5, p.14, comment 4). $P_k$ is a polynomial of degree $k^2$: the poles from $\zeta(1+z_i-z_{k+j})$ cancel $k^2$ of the $2k(2k-1)$ degrees of $\Delta^2$, so the residue requires Taylor terms of $\exp(\tfrac x2\sum z_j-z_{k+j})$ only up to degree $k^2$. — *Role:* degree count for $P_k$

### Proof architecture

There is no proof of the main statements: they are conjectures. The apparatus: (a) the recipe (2.1.1-2.1.2) applied to $\zeta$ in 2.2, to a general primitive $L$ in 2.3, and to families in Section 4 via approximate functional equations plus averaging orthogonality $\langle\chi(n_1)\cdots\rangle=\delta(\ldots)$ (e.g. (4.3.4)); (b) analysis of the arithmetic factor $A_k$ (Thm 2.4.2.1, Lemma 2.6.2.1) giving the Euler product with an $\int_0^1d\theta$ local average; (c) conversion of the shuffle sums to $2k$-fold (unitary) or $k$-fold (symplectic/orthogonal) residue integrals via Lemmas 2.5.2.1-2.5.2.3; (d) comparison with the exact RMT formulas (Thms 1.5.1.2/4/6, from CAND-01369) under conductor-matching $N=\log(t/2\pi)$ resp. $2N=$ conductor; (e) checks: $k=1,2$ against Ingham, Heath-Brown, Motohashi; new Theorem 1.6.1.1; leading terms against Keating-Snaith; numerics for $\zeta$ ($k$ up to 4), quadratic Dirichlet $L$ ($k\le8$) and a $GL(2)$ family.

Dependency edges: CAND-01200 → CAND-01369 (RMT side (Thms 1.5.1.2/4/6) is quoted from CAND-01369)

## Context and interpretation

**Importance and obstacle overcome.** Earlier conjectures (Conrey-Ghosh, Keating-Snaith) only gave the leading coefficient of $\int|\zeta|^{2k}$. This paper conjectures the full polynomial $P_k$ of degree $k^2$ with all lower-order coefficients, and the analogous statements for families, by a recipe whose output is identical in structure to the exact RMT formulas. It confirmed known cases ($k=1,2$) and provided numerically testable predictions for $k=3,4$ (e.g. the explicit $P_3$ and $P_4$-type polynomials) and Dirichlet/modular families. It is the basis of the 'CFKRS conjectures'.

**Relation to prior work.** 

**Limitations and open questions.** All main statements for $k\ge3$ (zeta), $k\ge2$ (families, with few exceptions) are conjectural. The authors say the recipe is not a heuristic and 'the underlying cause for [the cancellation of errors] remains a mystery' (Section 4.1). The error exponent $-1/2+\epsilon$ is only supported by examples and numerics. 'Suitable weight function' is undefined. Moments at the critical line for large $k$ ($k>2$) remain open; the conjectures also concern only the main term, not individual shifts' error terms. The authors note no conjecture covers families without a clean symmetry type here.

## Research potential

- **Hypotheses that might be weakened.** 'Suitable weight function': try to prove Conjecture 1.5.1.1 for $k=2$ with sharp-cutoff $O(T^{1/2+\epsilon})$ (the paper notes only smooth weights are known). The $k=3$ case with a power-saving error under GRH-type hypotheses. The extension past $|\alpha_j|<\delta$ to larger shifts.
- **Generalizations.** Ratios conjecture (later work) is a direct generalization; also moments of derivatives and of $|\zeta|^{2k}$ with non-integer $k$ (Gonek-Hughes-Keating hybrid); GL(3) and higher families via $A_k$ with $L_p$ for degree-3 Euler factors.
- **Candidate connections.** (i) The RMT counterpart (CAND-01369) gives exact finite-$N$ formulas to test conductor-matching beyond $N=\log t/2\pi$. (ii) Montgomery-Soundararajan (CAND-01265) shows lower-order arithmetic terms matter numerically. The same 'diagonal plus Hardy-Littlewood singular series' structure underlies $A_k$. (iii) Odlyzko's high-zero data (CAND-01225) can be used for moments of $\zeta$ near $t\sim10^{22}$.
- **Reusable lemmas.** Lemmas 2.5.2.1-2.5.2.3 (shuffle sums as contour integrals) are model-independent and reusable for any 'sum over $\binom{2k}{k}$ splittings' structure. Theorem 2.4.2.1 (analytic continuation of $R$ to $\sigma>1/4+\delta$) is a clean unconditional statement. Theorem 1.6.1.1 is a rigorous new smoothed second-moment result with constants.
- **Possible experiments.** Compute $\int_C^D|\zeta(\tfrac12+it)|^6dt$ on windows near $t\sim10^{22}$ (Odlyzko-Schonhage evaluation) and compare to $\int P_3(\log\tfrac t{2\pi})dt$ with $P_3$ of (5.1.6), checking the claimed error $|D-C|^{1/2}D^\epsilon$. Tabulate the coefficient sign pattern of $P_k$ for $k\ge5$ (the paper observes negative coefficients first appear at $k=5$, Table 5.1.1). Test the shifted conjecture with $\alpha\neq0$ numerically for $k=3$.
- **Risks / barriers.** The recipe's unproved cancellation of discarded diagonal/off-diagonal terms is the central gap. The conjectures concern moments and do not imply RH, nor do they follow from it. Numerical agreement at moderate heights does not decide the lower-order terms asymptotically (small leading coefficients make lower terms dominate).
