## Mathematical content (Level 1: full text read)

**Research question.** Given a signature {\lambda,k,\gamma} (\lambda>0,k>0 real, \gamma=\pm1), determine all Dirichlet series \varphi(s)=\sum a_n n^{-s} (converging somewhere) such that R(s)=(2\pi/\lambda)^{-s}\Gamma(s)\varphi(s) satisfies: (I) (s-k)\varphi(s) is entire of finite genus, (II) R(s)=\gamma R(k-s). How many linearly independent solutions exist, and can zeta/L-functions be characterised by their functional equation? Extended to systems (3) R_l(k-s)=\sum c_{lr}R_r(s) with residue-class support condition (IV).

**Historical motivation.** Hamburger (1921-22) showed zeta is determined by its functional equation plus regularity; Hecke generalises to signatures and to L-series of arithmetic origin (zeta of imaginary quadratic fields, Grossencharacters, sums of r squares).

### Main results

- **THM-PAPER-0151-1** (Satz 1 (announced SS1, proved SS3), theorem, scope: global; p. 666 (statement); proof SS3 not read). If \lambda>2, then for every k>0, \gamma=\pm1 there exist infinitely many linearly independent functions \varphi of signature \{\lambda,k,\gamma\}.
- **THM-PAPER-0151-2** (Satz 2 (SS4), theorem, scope: global; p. 666 (statement); proof SS4 not read). For \lambda=2 the number of linearly independent \varphi of signature \{2,k,\gamma\} is finite: for \gamma=1 it equals \lfloor k/4\rfloor+1; for \gamma=-1 it equals \lfloor (k-2)/4\rfloor+1 if k\ge2 and 0 otherwise. In particular \{2,1/2,1\} has only \zeta(2s) (up to constant) and \{2,1,1\} only the zeta function of \mathbb{Q}(i).
- **THM-PAPER-0151-3** (Satz 3,5,6 (SS5), theorem, scope: global; p. 666 (statement); proofs SS5 not read). For 0<\lambda<2 solutions of signature \{\lambda,k,\gamma\} exist only if \lambda=2\cos(\pi/q), k=\frac{4K}{q-2}+1-\gamma with positive integers q\ge3, K. The number of solutions is \le \lfloor (K+(\gamma-1)/2)/q\rfloor+1, and the number regular at s=k is exactly \lfloor (K+(\gamma-1)/2)/q\rfloor. For 0<\lambda<1 there are none. Consequently the zeta function of \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3}) is determined up to constant by signature \{\sqrt3,1,1\}.
- **THM-PAPER-0151-4** (Remark for \lambda=1 (SS5), theorem, scope: global; p. 666). Signature \{1,k,\gamma\} (modular group G(1), q=3) has no solutions unless k is even and \gamma=(-1)^{k/2}; then the number of independent solutions is \lfloor k/12\rfloor if k\equiv2 \pmod{12}, and 1+\lfloor k/12\rfloor otherwise (dimension of modular forms of weight k, via f(\tau) with group G(\lambda)).
- **THM-PAPER-0151-5** (Satz 9 (SS6) and SS7 (announced), theorem, scope: global; p. 668-669 (overview); SS6, SS7 not read). For L-series modulo q / zeta functions of \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-q}) the system (3)+(IV) has only finitely many linearly independent solution systems (the group \mathfrak{M} is finite, isomorphic to a binary modular group mod q or 2q); for integer k\ge2 the number of solution systems explicitly involves the class number of \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-q}); for prime D, zeta functions with Grossencharacters are singled out as eigen-objects (characteristic roots) of a matrix given by the data. Statement paraphrased from the overview, exact form not read.

### Key definitions

- Signature \{\lambda,k,\gamma\}: \varphi with (s-k)\varphi entire of finite genus, R(s)=(2\pi/\lambda)^{-s}\Gamma(s)\varphi(s)=\gamma R(k-s), \varphi a Dirichlet series (p. 664-665)
- Type k: \varphi such that (s-k)\varphi(s) is entire of finite genus
- Group G(\lambda) generated by \tau\mapsto\tau+\lambda and \tau\mapsto-1/\tau
- System (3)+(IV): N series with R_l(k-s)=\sum c_{lr}R_r(s) and coefficients supported on residue class b_l mod q (p. 667-668)

### Principal lemmas

- **THM-PAPER-0151-L1** (SS2 reduction (Mellin transform); pp. 665, 669 (SS2 beginning)). Under I-III, \varphi has finite order of growth in vertical strips: \varphi(\sigma+it)=O(|t|^A) uniformly for \beta_1\le\sigma\le\beta_2 (Phragmen-Lindelof). With F(x)=\sum a_n e^{-2\pi n x/\lambda}, \mathrm{Re}\,x>0, Mellin inversion converts the functional equation into a modular-type transformation of f(\tau) under the group G(\lambda) generated by \tau\mapsto\tau+\lambda and \tau\mapsto-1/\tau (weight -k, multiplier \gamma). — *Role:* Translates the Dirichlet-series problem to automorphic functions on G(\lambda); G(\lambda) is Schottky type for \lambda>2, modular-type (triangle group) for \lambda\le2.

### Proof architecture

Dictionary: signature solutions correspond to functions f(\tau) with weight -k and multiplier \gamma for the group G(\lambda); the condition 'Dirichlet series' is behaviour at \tau\to\tau+\lambda and the functional equation is behaviour under -1/\tau. Existence for \lambda>2 and \lambda<2 (SS3, SS5) uses only the Riemann mapping theorem and reflection principle (triangle functions), explicitly without general automorphic function theory (p. 669). Finiteness for \lambda=2 in SS4; discreteness forces \lambda=2\cos(\pi/q) in SS5. SS6-SS7 treat systems with finite group \mathfrak{M}. Only the overview and the beginning of SS2 were read; the proofs themselves were not.

## Context and interpretation

**Importance and obstacle overcome.** Shows that functional equation alone does determine Dirichlet series when \lambda\le2 (finite-dimensional), recovering Hamburger's result for \zeta(s) under a weaker regularity (finite genus rather than Hamburger's double-Dirichlet condition), and introduces the dictionary between functional equations and Hecke groups that underlies the later converse theorems (Weil 1967).

**Relation to prior work.** Hamburger, Math. Z. 10 (1921), 11 (1922), 13 (1922); Hecke, Math. Z. 1 (1919), 6 (1920) (Grossencharacter zeta functions); Hecke 1935 Danske Vid. Selsk. note on prime numbers and elliptic modular functions.

**Limitations and open questions.** Hecke notes that for \lambda>2 uniqueness fails (infinitely many solutions), so characterisation of zeta/L needs additional input such as the multiplicative or Euler-product structure; his SS1 stresses that this is the content of the residue-class condition (IV) rather than Hamburger's stronger requirement that \varphi(s/2) also be a Dirichlet series.

**Ideas in remarks/appendices/abandoned paths.** Overview (p. 668-669) remarks that adjoining the residue-class-split series to the Dedekind zeta gives a new viewpoint on the Riemann zeta function with L-series added; the 'eigen-system' idea (zeta functions as characteristic roots of a matrix from functional-equation data, for prime D) is not developed in the portion read.

## Research potential

- **Hypotheses that might be weakened.** Condition I (entire of finite genus) could be replaced by growth/regularity assumptions; test whether Satz 2 dimension count survives without finite genus. Condition III 'converges somewhere' could be weakened to Dirichlet series with polynomial growth coefficient.
- **Generalizations.** Weil's converse theorem (twists by characters, level N) and Selberg-class axiomatics; consider Satz 5/6 for degree-2 Selberg-class elements with \lambda<2 as a rigidity statement.
- **Candidate connections.** Selberg class: Hecke's finite-dimensionality for \lambda=2 vs the conjecture that degree-2 elements are automorphic; cross-reference with the RH-for-L-functions programme (family C) as a uniqueness statement for the object whose zeros are studied.
- **Reusable lemmas.** The Mellin/Phragmen-Lindelof reduction in SS2; the dimension count formula in Satz 2 as a check for finite computations.
- **Possible experiments.** Numerically verify Satz 2 count by solving for linear dependence in truncated Dirichlet-series bases satisfying \gamma R(k-s)=R(s), e.g. \{2,k,\gamma\} with k=4,6,8; compare with \lfloor k/4\rfloor+1.
- **Risks / barriers.** Proofs (SS3-SS7) were not read, so no part of this is audited; infinite families for \lambda>2 do not give any RH-relevant constraint.
