### Known obstructions

- **BRG-MELLIN_WAVELET-O1** Ingham's linear-relations theorem. If M(x)/sqrt x or L(x)/sqrt x is bounded on one side, then RH and simple zeros hold, and the positive ordinates satisfy infinitely many integer linear relations. Under LI, liminf x^{-1/2}M(x) = -inf and limsup = +inf. — *A. E. Ingham, 'On two conjectures in the theory of numbers', Amer. J. Math. 64 (1942) 313-319 (zbMATH 0063.02974 review). Restated in Mossinghoff-Trudgian arXiv:2007.14537 section 1.*. How it constrains: It rules out, under LI, pointwise eventual sign for 'Pólya-class' critical detectors: those with only a constant main term and zero coefficients r_gamma ~ 1/(gamma zeta'(rho)) that are not absolutely summable. The repo's SHARP critical H^{[1]} with jump kernel T(y)=4sqrt y-3 is in this class. Its Mellin transform B(s+1/2)(4/(s-1/2)-3/s) gives bias -3B(1/2)=1.80332 and r_gamma ~ (1-67^{-rho})/(i gamma zeta'(rho)). Analyst computation: sum over the first 200 zeros of 2\|r_gamma\| is 1.426 and still growing, tracking the Mertens analogue (1.393). Under RH plus Gonek's second-moment asymptotic, Hölder gives sum 1/\|rho zeta'(rho)\| = inf. So 'H^{[1]}(x)>0 for all large x' would force linear relations among zeta ordinates. Only the negative-mass/weighted-variation form (OPEN.ARITH.CV) is viable. The obstruction does NOT apply to detectors with log drift (rows 2,3, W_X) or with absolutely summable gamma^{-2} coefficients and a large bias (G_1 with S=4(sqrt y-1)_+: bias 2.40443 vs sum 2\|r\| ~ 0.066 over 200 zeros; C_m). [abstract_only (zbMATH review) + secondary_source (Mossinghoff-Trudgian intro). The application to H^{[1]} is analyst derivation plus non-directed mpmath numerics and is not reviewed.; verifier: not verified]
- **BRG-MELLIN_WAVELET-O2** Pólya-, Turán- and Mertens-type fixed sign conjectures, each implying RH through a Landau consumer, are false. — *C. B. Haselgrove, Mathematika 5 (1958) 141-145 (Zbl 0085.27102). Odlyzko-te Riele, J. reine angew. Math. 357 (1985). Tanaka, Tokyo J. Math. 3 (1980). Borwein-Ferguson-Mossinghoff, Math. Comp. 77 (2008). Kim-Nguyen, arXiv:2502.21021 (first Mertens counterexample below exp(1.96e19)). Humphries, arXiv:1108.1524 (under RH+LI, positive log density of Pólya counterexamples).*. How it constrains: Positivity observed up to large X, such as the repo's row scans or historical L(x)<=0 up to 9e8, is no evidence for a fixed detector's eventual sign when it lies in the Pólya class. The repo's preference for the subpower negative-mass premise over pointwise sign is consistent with this history. [abstract_only / secondary_source; verifier: not verified]
- **BRG-MELLIN_WAVELET-O3** The negative-mass premise is RH-equivalent for every fixed detector in the repo's class, so OPEN_CUTS section 1 cannot be weaker than RH. Graded form: for theta in (0,1/2), int_1^Y F_-dx/x << Y^{theta+eps} for a fixed noncancelling detector excludes zeros with Re rho > 1/2+theta. Conversely, a zero-free half-plane Re s>1/2+theta gives \|F(X)\| << X^{theta+eps} for 1/s^2-smoothed rows, via Titchmarsh 14.2-type reciprocal bounds and Mellin inversion. — *Littlewood 1912. Titchmarsh sections 14.2 and 14.25. Landau / MV Lemma 15.1. Repo R17 (reviews/D-final/REPAIRS.md) and R15 part 1 for the reciprocal bound.*. How it constrains: Any unconditional partial result with theta<1/2 would be a zeta zero-free strip, which no one has ever established (the best known regions are of Vinogradov-Korobov type). Partial 'Y^theta negative mass' results in the Q4, half-divisor, XD or wavelet coordinates are therefore at least as hard as quasi-RH. [memory_unverified (classical); the graded equivalence is an analyst derivation to be reviewed; verifier: not verified]
- **BRG-MELLIN_WAVELET-O4** Sign-change counts are Theta(log Y) for no-main-term detectors. Kaczorowski-Pintz show V(f,Y) >= c log Y whenever the Mellin transform has a nonreal boundary singularity but is regular on the real axis. Under RH the logarithmic horizon caps the count at O(log Y). — *Kaczorowski-Pintz 1986/87 (Zbl 0613.10036, 0631.10026). Morrill-Platt-Trudgian arXiv:1910.14203. Grześkowiak et al. arXiv:2408.10399.*. How it constrains: This supports the OPEN_CUTS firewall: counting negative intervals of the wavelet or XD detectors cannot distinguish RH from not-RH. L(Y) <= log Y is automatic, as R7 already notes. [abstract_only; verifier: not verified]
- **BRG-MELLIN_WAVELET-O5** Finite truncations of L^2 RH criteria have unconditional logarithmic defects: for N-term Nyman-Beurling approximants, \|\|F-chi\|\| >= C/sqrt(log N), with Burnol's sharper constant. — *Báez-Duarte arXiv:math/0202141 eq. (1.2), citing Balazard-Saias and Burnol (Adv. Math. 170 (2002)).*. How it constrains: Any fixed-cutoff or finite-window positive or energy certificate in the wavelet suffix-field Hilbert space can approach a criterion at best logarithmically. This is consistent with the repo's REFUTATION.WAVELET.CRITICAL_DESMOOTHING and finite-scope firewalls. [fulltext_read (as quoted by Báez-Duarte); verifier: not verified]
- **BRG-MELLIN_WAVELET-O6** The Landau-Pintz finite-to-zero-free method cannot improve knowledge for zeta. Johnston-Trudgian call it 'doomed to fail' for zeta, because RH is verified to height 3e12 and explicit zero-free regions are already strong. — *D. R. Johnston, T. Trudgian, 'A round of Pintz to celebrate oscillations in sums', arXiv:2511.09978 (v4 2026), section 1. Pintz, Math. Pannonica 28 (2022) 58-64 (Zbl 1513.11159).*. - **BRG-MELLIN_WAVELET-O7** Family averages of sign criteria can be proved unconditionally while the principal member stays RH-hard. Suzuki proves unconditional negativity of character-averaged weighted Chebyshev Riesz sums for Q >= x, while the individual sign is equivalent to (G)RH. — *M. Suzuki arXiv:2411.07436, Thm 7 versus Thms 1 and 3.*. [fulltext_read; verifier: not verified]
- **BRG-MELLIN_WAVELET-O8** Selberg's parity phenomenon: sieve or positive-weight (Type I) information alone cannot produce cancellation in mu or lambda sums. — *A. Selberg, Proc. Sympos. Pure Math. 20 (1971). Friedlander-Iwaniec, Opera de Cribro (2010).*. ### Novelty assessment

Classical, with prior art: - The analytic consumer is classical: M2-M5, the specialized Landau theorem, the holomorphic-defect transfer and the negative-mass relaxation. M3 is Landau's theorem in the Widder Thm II.5b / Montgomery-Vaughan Lemma 15.1 form, proved the standard way. M4 and M7 are routine corollaries of it. The repo's real contributions here are careful bookkeeping: the punctured-germ predicate, the entire-case handling and the zeta(1) normalization gate. These are useful for formalization, but they are not new theorems. - The fixed-row and 5:3 detectors fit a known pattern: first-order logarithmic Riesz means at critical normalization, with a log-drift main term. Suzuki (arXiv:2411.07436, 2024) and Han (arXiv:2608.27130, Aug 2026) prove the same pattern for Lambda and lambda. For the 1/zeta-type rows, the Landau direction is classical, and the converse follows Han's method under simple zeros plus a zeta' summability hypothesis. - The specific numerator algebra appears to be new as specific instances, but it is elementary. This covers the two-row common-zero exclusion -3(a-1)(a-2) and the 5:3 factorization. - Wavelet: the energy identity is finite summation by parts. The spectral-abscissa theorem is a compactly smoothed version of the classical result that the M(x) growth/convergence abscissa is Theta, and its reciprocal-zeta growth step is essentially Titchmarsh section 14.2. - The R4 suffix-field criterion uses Nyman-Beurling-style L^2 dilation duality but is not equivalent to it. - The minimal causal dyadic annihilator (I-sqrt2 S_2)(I-S_2)^2 and its ratio-eight compact wavelet: no prior art was found. Apparently new (with uncertainty): the specific coordinate systems and their exact RH-equivalent or sufficient gates (UOSACF, SACF, HCNC, XD, carry-hinge weighted response, same-K1 translation). No literature equivalents were found. They are reformulations. Each open gate is RH-equivalent or at least zero-free-strip-hard, so none is a weakening. ### Known obstructions

- **BRG-ROBIN-O1** If RH is false, Robin's inequality fails for infinitely many n, by a secondary-term margin. There exist 0<beta<1/2 (any 1-b<beta<1/2, with b=Re(rho)>1/2) and C>0 such that sigma(n) >= e^g n loglog n + C n loglog n/(log n)^beta infinitely often. — *Robin 1984, Section 4, Proposition 1, as restated in Lagarias, arXiv:math/0008177, Proposition 3.2.*. How it constrains: A theorem covering the whole unbounded canonical tail is RH itself. No 'complete infinite pruning argument' weaker than RH can exist. Any proposed unconditional tail theorem must contain RH-strength analytic input, i.e. a bound on prime counts at the scale x^{1/2+o(1)}. [fulltext_read (Lagarias restatement; Robin original not accessed); verifier: not verified]
- **BRG-ROBIN-O2** Under RH the Robin margin on the extremal (SA/CA) numbers tends to zero: limsup (sigma(n)/n - e^g loglog n) sqrt(log n) <= -e^g(2sqrt2 - 4 - g + log 4pi) ~ -1.393 (Ramanujan, assuming RH). The explicit form is sigma(n)/n <= e^g(loglog n - 2(sqrt2-1)/sqrt(log n) + S_1(log n) + O(...)), where S_1 is a sum over zeta zeros. — *S. Ramanujan, 'Highly composite numbers', annotated by J.-L. Nicolas and G. Robin, Ramanujan J. 1 (1997) 119-153 (zbMATH 0917.11043), sections 59-71; restated in Nicolas-Sondow arXiv:1211.6944 section 1, CNS arXiv:1110.5078 Proposition 1, Axler arXiv:2110.13478 final remark.*. How it constrains: Finite pruning and envelopes with a fixed relative margin cannot close the tail. The margin shrinks like (log n)^{-1/2} and oscillates with the zeros through S_1. [secondary_source; verifier: not verified]
- **BRG-ROBIN-O3** For every fixed n0, limsup_{a->inf} G(a n0) = e^g. Any subtree with a fixed prefix and an unrestricted continuation has no margin. A margin exists only once continuation exponents are capped, and with cap A the asymptotic margin is about a factor prod(1-p^{-A-1}), which tends to 1 as A grows. — *Caveney-Nicolas-Sondow, Ramanujan J. 29 (2012) 359-384 (arXiv:1112.6010), Fact 2 (citing CNS 2011, Lemma 10).*. How it constrains: A subtree tail lemma must be stated per (prefix, cap A). Uniformity in A is equivalent to RH, because every n is t-free for some t. [fulltext_read; verifier: not verified]
- **BRG-ROBIN-O4** The t-free (Sole-Planat Psi_t) method stalls with current explicit inputs. Morrill-Platt report that both their finite-range and asymptotic bounds exceed 1 at t=21. Axler reached t=21 only with improved Mertens-product estimates. The method's margin e^g(1-1/zeta(t)) ~ e^g 2^{-t} must dominate explicit prime-counting errors. — *Morrill-Platt 2021, Section 5 'Comments'. Axler 2023, Theorem 1.2.*. How it constrains: Exponent-uniform 'tail lemmas' are capped by the precision of explicit PNT and Mertens bounds. Pushing t up by one requires better explicit inputs; no t-free argument reaches unbounded exponents. [fulltext_read; verifier: not verified]
- **BRG-ROBIN-O5** Size of the canonical search domain: HR integers <= x number exp((1+o(1)) 2pi sqrt(log x/(3 loglog x))) (Hardy-Ramanujan 1917). SA numbers <= x number between (log x)^{1+5/48} for large x (Erdos-Nicolas 1975) and exp(O((loglog x)^2)) (Alaoglu-Erdos). CA numbers form a one-parameter family. — *Hardy-Ramanujan, Proc. LMS 16 (1917) 112-132 (via CLMS 2007 Proposition 1). Erdos-Nicolas, Bull. SMF 103 (1975) 65-90. Akbary-Friggstad 2009, p. 2 (quoting Alaoglu-Erdos).*. How it constrains: Not an obstruction to truth, but to the computational route. Canonical-tree traversal (the frozen 10^54/10^100 deposit) is super-polylogarithmically larger than SA or CA certification of the same range. Published CA-based certification already reaches 10^(10^13.11) (Morrill-Platt Theorem 13). [fulltext_read (CLMS, AF statements); originals secondary; verifier: not verified]

### Novelty assessment

Classical or known, with high confidence after full-text reading: (i) The canonical transform and the 'complete canonical reduction' (packet sections 2-3) match the Hardy-Ramanujan-integer reduction in Choie-Lichiardopol-Moree-Sole 2007, section 5 (Proposition 1, Lemmas 7-10), including the name. They also match Vojak 2020, Theorems 2.3-2.4, which even uses the same letter H. The exchange lemma goes back to Ramanujan 1915 and Alaoglu-Erdos 1944, Theorem 1. (ii) Stronger classical reductions exist: Akbary-Friggstad 2009 (the least counterexample is superabundant, using a 5041..10080 barrier) and Robin 1984, Section 3, Proposition 1 (a CA counterexample exists if any counterexample does). The repo correctly says canonical is not CA, but does not record that these stronger reductions are standard. (iii) The nested prefix-level encoding is the conjugate-partition / product-of-primorials representation (CNS 2012, eqs. 10-11). (iv) The powered envelope is a localized, capped, exact-rational form of the classical CA/Lagrangian bound sigma(n)/n^{1+eps} <= sigma(N_eps)/N_eps^{1+eps}. Its DP-with-cap-vector organization parallels Robin 1983 (RAIRO) for highly composite numbers. Its DP collapses to the classical separable closed form; that closed form is my derivation, checked exactly. (v) The finite barrier lies far inside known verified ranges: Robin 1984 numerics to 55440, Briggs 2006, and Morrill-Platt 2021 Theorem 13 to 10^(10^13.11485).  Possibly new, with moderate or low confidence: (a) a fully directed, exact-integer/dyadic self-contained certificate of the small barrier; (b) explicit identification of 5583 as the exact threshold where e^g loglog n first exceeds I(5040). This is implicit in AF-style arguments, but I did not find it stated; (c) the explicit treatment of the boundary case H(n)<=5040, which the printed proofs of CLMS Proposition 1 and Vojak Theorem 2.4 leave implicit (a rigor improvement, not a new theorem); (d) the specific fixed-prefix, capped, budgeted subtree envelope with rational eps, as a formulation.  The open cut is not new. 'Control the unbounded canonical tail' is equivalent to RH (Robin + CLMS), and the parameter-restricted versions it requests already have a published baseline: t-free for t<=21 (Axler 2023); valuation floors (Hertlein 2018, Axler 2023 Theorem 1.4); odd, squarefree and squarefull numbers (CLMS); and least-counterexample structure (Vojak 2020, Theorem 1.6).  Uncertainty and access limits: Robin 1984, Alaoglu-Erdos 1944, Briggs 2006 and Nicolas 1983 were not read in the original; their statements come from consistent secondary sources. Vojak 2020 and Wu 2019 are unrefereed preprints.  What I actually ran: - an exact-rational recomputation of both barrier maxima and their uniqueness; - double-precision (not directed) threshold values; - an exact DP-versus-closed-form comparison on 300 random instances plus the R6 fixture (0 mismatches; fixture U^{1/64}~3.8985); - floating-point support-size bounds.  No repository file was modified.

### Known obstructions

- **BRG-SHARP_NATIVE-O1** Ingham's theorem (1942) applies to critical Möbius-type sums with a constant bias. If M(x)/sqrt x or L(x)/sqrt x is bounded on one side, then RH holds, the zeros are simple, and the zero ordinates satisfy infinitely many Z-linear relations. If there are no, or only finitely many, such relations, liminf = -infinity and limsup = +infinity. Mossinghoff-Trudgian and Humphries adapt the argument to L_1 (alpha=1, Turán's function). — *A. E. Ingham, Amer. J. Math. 64 (1942) 313-319 (zbMATH 0063.02974 review); the adaptation to alpha in [0,1/2) and alpha=1 is summarised in P. Humphries, arXiv:1108.1524, §1, and M. J. Mossinghoff and T. S. Trudgian, arXiv:2007.14537, §1.*. How it constrains: H_1 has constant apparent bias b_H ≈ 1.8033. Its zero coefficients have size about 1/(gamma\|zeta'(rho)\|), and their absolute sum appears to diverge like the Mertens sum (1.674 after 400 zeros). Eventual nonnegativity of H_1 would imply RH (L-99943) and, by the Ingham argument, Q-linear dependence of the ordinates; it is therefore expected false under LI. The adaptation to H_1 has not been checked line by line. The repo's negative-mass formulation of OPEN.ARITH.CV avoids this obstruction. Any route needing pointwise H_1>=0 (or a fixed-p deep-root sign with constant bias) should be treated as LI-obstructed. [abstract_only (Ingham review) + fulltext_read (summaries in Humphries and Mossinghoff-Trudgian) + analyst computation (h1coef.py); verifier: not verified]
- **BRG-SHARP_NATIVE-O2** Finite positivity is unreliable at critical scale. Turán's T(x)=sum lambda(n)/n has positive apparent bias (sqrt x T(x) has bias -1/zeta(1/2)=0.685), yet it is first negative at x=72,185,376,951,205. Comp. 77 (2008), as quoted in Angelo-Xu, arXiv:2205.05822, eq. (1.1); Tanaka, as quoted in Humphries §1.*. How it constrains: H_1>=1 on [1,1e7] (analyst exploratory computation) is not evidence for the critical estimate. H_1's bias is 2.6 times Turán's, so any negative values are expected only at astronomically large x. [fulltext_read (Angelo-Xu introduction) + abstract_only (Haselgrove review); verifier: not verified]
- **BRG-SHARP_NATIVE-O3** Parity problem. Sieve axioms based on level of distribution (Type I, axiom R) cannot determine signed Möbius/prime sums, even at level x^{1-eps} (Selberg; Bombieri's asymptotic sieve). A bilinear hypothesis exploiting sign changes of mu(mn) is required. — *J. Friedlander and H. Iwaniec, 'Asymptotic sieve for primes', Ann. of Math. 148 (1998) 1041-1065, arXiv:math/9811186, §1 (pp.1041, 1044-1045); E. Bombieri, 'The asymptotic sieve', Mem. Accad. Naz. XL 1/2 (1976) 243-269.*. How it constrains: The dynamic critical Bellman block (fixed/small least prime) cannot be closed by propagating positivity through Buchstab/Bellman recursions fed only with rough-number densities, Dickman/Buchstab profiles or sieve bounds. - **BRG-SHARP_NATIVE-O4** RH-equivalence of the smooth-number asymptotic at y ≈ (log x)^{2+eps} (Hildebrand 1984). — *A. Hildebrand, Mathematika 31 (1984) 258-271 (zbMATH 0544.10042).*. ### Novelty assessment

Classical or known in substance, though stated here for new specific objects: (a) The exact identities: the distributional descent L-99940/42 (the Riesz-means mechanism), the Buchstab/first-owner decomposition L-99601, and the Stieltjes transfer L-98040 (integration by parts). (c) The fixed-P61 bias (§8). It is a fixed finite-modulus Legendre sieve of a near-constant ramp sequence; R25 itself disclaims novelty. Via the Legendre/Tao identity it is the limiting density of integers free of primes in (p,p^a), so its positivity is immediate.  Apparently new as a stated theorem: L-99613 (all-x positivity of H^{[m]} for every real m>=2, with the 67-doubled Möbius source and the kernel T^m). No identical statement was found. The technique is the elementary Bonferroni/Brun inclusion-exclusion with subunit prime mass, in the absolutely convergent regime sigma=(m+1)/2>=3/2. Eventual positivity for each fixed m>1 is soft (dominated convergence). The literature already has a stronger-in-sigma all-X positivity of log-smoothed Möbius sums at sigma=1 (Pólya 1919, Turán 1948, Ramaré-Zuniga Alterman Thm 1.2), so L-99613 is a sound but modest result whose distance to RH is structural. Genuinely RH-strength and open: OPEN.ARITH.CV (RH-equivalent by the repo's own proof), FCHD67, ROWS23_NATIVE, the deep fixed-least-prime Bellman root, and top-range growing-P control. The literature supplies no tool that reaches these. ### Novelty assessment

Classical or known (high confidence): (i) Every RH-necessary inequality in research/integrated/xi/derivative-free-pick-loewner.md follows from three standard facts once the resolvent representation is accepted. Those facts are: under RH, xi'/xi is a Caratheodory function on Re s>1/2 (Hinkkanen 1997; Lagarias 1999); its Poisson/real part makes J_T complete Bernstein and H_T Stieltjes; and Cauchy-kernel total positivity applies (Karlin). This covers the secant, alternating divided differences, cross-Loewner total nonnegativity and the barycentric localizer. (ii) The scalar base R_T>=0 is Sondow-Dumitrescu horizontal monotonicity. (iii) The all-order real-node safe-axis criterion (L-91904, OPEN.OPERATOR.XI.PICK_ORDER4_PLUS taken over all orders) is an instance of Audenaert's 2011 characterization of anti-Loewner functions plus the identity theorem. It is equivalent to Sra 2026 Prop A.1(vi) (log h complete Bernstein), to Polson 2026 (complete monotonicity of the heat trace) and to Zhang 2023. The repo's H_ij is literally the anti-Loewner/Kwong matrix of F. (iv) The reverse-Rolle identity is a one-step form of classical Fourier-Polya critical-point counting. (v) The fixed-rectangle high-derivative entry is a special case of Kim 1996. (vi) The Conrey trial sits inside Conrey 1983.  Probably new, with honest uncertainty: (a) The specific finite disproof architecture (two-channel localizer, exact matched-pole annihilator) and the two directed 8x8 complex Pick boxes at T about 4.7e12. I found no literature computing certified complex Pick matrices for xi'/xi; Grigutis et al. work only at the scalar level. (b) Order-3 safe-axis Kwong positivity for actual xi via reciprocal concavity of p and the 3x3 determinant factorization. I found no prior statement. However, the order-2 part and the tp-curvature part follow, by a short Laplace-transform argument (mine, not stated in the source), from H>0 and H'<0. Sra 2026 and Polson 2026 prove that unconditionally using zeros only up to height 100, after the repo's August 2026 integration. Reciprocal concavity (1/p concave) is not implied by H>0, H'<0, so that component and the order-3 assembly remain the repo's distinctive contribution as far as I can tell. The critical-reserve budget mechanism is the same as Sra's Lemma 2.2 and eq. (2.2), down to the identical bound 2(log U+1)/U. It was found independently and earlier by the repo. (c) The growing high-derivative window c sqrt(m/log m) with frozen-lambda safe-ray sector control is a natural saddle-point strengthening of Kim 1996 and Ki 2006 for Xi. It may already be implicit in O'Sullivan 2020 or Coffey 2009 asymptotics, which I did not read in full.  Context that affects how to read the frontier: neighbouring hierarchies of the same function G(t)=xi(1/2+sqrt t) are unconditionally known far beyond order 3. These are Jensen degree <=8 for all shifts (GORZ), Jensen degree up to 2*10^17 at the origin (Chasse), and PF_{1.8*10^12} for the reciprocal laws (Sra). By analogy, fixed-order N>=4 safe-axis Pick positivity is very likely provable from Platt-Trudgian with the same signed-perturbation ideas. It should not be presented as an RH-strength frontier; only the all-order statement is RH-equivalent. Whether the Pick hierarchy disperses or concentrates zero information (Farmer's criterion) is open and is the key question for its usefulness.  I did not find an explicit error in any repository statement. Points I would put to contributors as questions, not defects: whether T-91002 (radial curvature) is a reparametrization of Jensen's 1913 / Polya 1927 convexity criterion; and whether Hinkkanen's 1997 matrices A_n coincide with the repo's Pick matrices.
