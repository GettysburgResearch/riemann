## CRIT

**1. Best established theorem.** REFEREED (classical zeta, unconditional): kappa >= 0.417293962 (> 5/12) and kappa* >= 0.407511457, PRZZ, Res. Math. Sci. 7 (2020) Paper 2, Theorem 1.2 and §8. NEWEST (unconditional, unrefereed, but independently proved twice): liminf N0^s/N >= C_MT = 3/2 - (1/sqrt2)cot(1/sqrt2) = 0.6725007... by Alpöge-Furman/Claude (arXiv:2608.13637, Theorem A) and by Lamzouri (arXiv:2609.02882, Theorem 1.1). Hence kappa >= kappa* >= 0.6725, distinct >= 0.83625, and simple-or-on-line >= 0.88762. Arias de Reyna (arXiv:2610.24612) reconstructs the Claude proof, repairs one boundary step he says fails as written in v2, and concludes it is correct. COMPUTER-ASSISTED, unrefereed: kappa* >= 0.67353820, distinct >= 0.83900460, simple (anywhere) >= 0.67800919 (Knausgård, arXiv:2610.08965); simple-or-on-line > 8/9 (Cobeli-Zaharescu) and 0.889315 (Knausgård); at least 0.6552 of zeros are c-isolated (Lamzouri, arXiv:2610.26841). CONDITIONAL: under RH, simple >= 19/27 (Bui-Heath-Brown 2013) and distinct >= 0.8477 (Chirre-Gonçalves-de Laat 2020). The Pair Correlation Conjecture alone implies 100% simple and on the line (Goldston-Lee-Schettler-Suriajaya, arXiv). Mollifier length: 4/7 for structured mollifiers (Conrey 1989; PRZZ for Feng-type), 17/33 for arbitrary coefficients (Bettin-Chandee-Radziwill 2017).

**2. Historical improvement.** Mollifier era. kappa: Selberg 1942 (positive), Levinson 1974 0.3474, Conrey 1983 0.3658, Conrey 1989 0.4088 (+0.061 from theta 1/2 to 4/7), BCY 2011 0.4105, Feng 2012 0.4128 (justified only in 2018), Pratt-Robles 2018 0.41492, PRZZ 2020 0.41729. Total gain after 1989 was +0.0085 in 31 years. kappa*: 0.3474, then 0.4013, 0.4058, 0.4075 (+0.0062 after 1989). Pair-correlation era (Aug-Oct 2026, unrefereed): kappa* jumped from 0.4075 to 2/3, then 0.6725 (+0.265, about 65% relative, by a change of method), then 0.67354 (+0.001, computer-assisted). Distinct: Farmer 1995 0.639, Wu 0.660 (or Ki-Lee >0.70, unverified), 2026 0.83625 then 0.83900. Under RH, simple zeros rose from 2/3 (1973) to 19/27 (1998 with GLH; 2013 RH only). Pair-correlation-only RH bounds rose from 2/3 to 0.6792 (2020).

**3. Exact bottleneck.** (a) New method. Montgomery's form factor F(alpha) is known (unconditionally, in the BGST sum over all complex zeros) only for \|alpha\| <= 1. The prime-side diagonal evaluation needs Dirichlet polynomials of length X <= T; for X >> T the off-diagonal needs prime-pair asymptotics. Every certificate built from the first two trace moments at bandwidth one is capped at about 0.6818 (Alpöge-Furman §7.2), and the local-inequality refinements in use are capped at 0.6736-0.6738 (Knausgård §7). Higher traces tr G^k are unconditionally unavailable at the required bandwidth (Rudnick-Sarnak range X^k <= T^{2-eps}); the third trace is available only under RH. (b) Mollifier method. The twisted second moment off-diagonal (Kloosterman-sum bounds, Deshouillers-Iwaniec) has fixed theta at 4/7 since 1989. Even the conditional theta = 1 gives only 0.6107 (Feng, Cor. 1), below the new 0.6725, and for every finite theta the mollified error is >= c/theta (Radziwill). So mollifiers become competitive only with theta well beyond 1, which again needs off-diagonal or prime-pair information. (c) RH-conditional discrete moments over zeros (CGG/BHB, 19/27 > 0.6818) use RH to treat sum_rho \|B zeta'(rho)\|^2 as a sum over real ordinates. No RH-free inertia-type replacement for these discrete moments exists yet.

**4. Bottleneck type.** mixed

**5. What negative results rule out.** (1) Bandwidth-one ceiling: any certificate depending only on the first two trace moments with Fourier support in [-1,1] and the on-line/off-line partition gives at most p0 <= 0.6818287 (Alpöge-Furman §7.2, interval-certified 256-periodic law). Within that information the inequality behind 2/3 is sharp: Montgomery's extremal configuration has 2N/3 simple zeros plus N/6 doubles, or off-line pairs of vanishing depth (§7.2(b)). (2) The Montgomery-Taylor window is optimal among windows of the form 2 - R(psi) [Carneiro-Chandee-Littmann-Milinovich 2017, Cor. 14, as cited by Alpöge-Furman]. (3) Knausgård §7: 8-point inequalities give at most 0.67357; pair-energy bounds at most 0.67383; any regular window at most 0.68185 (kappa*). For distinct zeros the caps are 0.83916, 0.83998 and 0.84093. (4) The inputs are insensitive to o(N) off-line zeros and hold for Davenport-Heilbronn and Epstein zeta, which violate RH, so the method cannot prove RH or 100% without new arithmetic (Alpöge-Furman §1.4, §7.2(f)). (5) Radziwill (arXiv:1207.6583, Theorem 1): I(M_theta) >= c/theta for any Dirichlet polynomial of length T^theta; under RH + PCC, >= 1/(0.5 + theta). (6) Feng 2012, §1(a): a positive proportion of zeros of zeta + zeta'/log T lie near the line (under the small-gap conjecture), so 100% via Levinson needs R -> 0 or a new f(s). (7) Farmer's theta = infinity conjecture implies 100% (Mathematika 1993), and Bettin-Gonek show it implies RH (Mathematika 2017), so arbitrarily long mollifiers are at least as hard as RH. (8) The mollifier-length gains of 1989-2020 (4/7, 17/33, 6/11, 4/7-Feng) produced only +0.0085 in kappa, a sign of diminishing returns at fixed theta (PRZZ §9(4) calls further pieces 'a painful calculation').

**6. Possible external ingredients.** (i) Prime-pair correlations (Hardy-Littlewood) or pair correlation beyond Fourier support 1. Even a one-sided or averaged lower bound for F(alpha) on 1 < \|alpha\| <= 1 + delta raises the bandwidth, and Hua-Yang's formula 2 - 1/c*_lambda increases with the bandwidth lambda (our check). (ii) Triple and n-level correlation (Hejhal; Rudnick-Sarnak) made RH-free in the BGST manner for complex zeros. This would make tr G^3 available unconditionally (Alpöge-Furman §7.3 uses it under RH to reach distinct >= 0.85082). (iii) Spectral theory of automorphic forms and Kloosterman-sum technology (Deshouillers-Iwaniec, Bettin-Chandee trilinear, Blomer-Harcos shifted convolution) for longer mollifiers and for GL(2) pair correlation. (iv) Asymptotic large sieve and family averaging, where theta = 1 and bandwidth > 1 are available (CIS; Sono; Alpöge-Furman Remark 7.2: 0.811). (v) Operator and quadratic-form theory: Weil-form positivity, Bombieri's observation that the negative index of Weil-form truncations counts off-line zeros, Connes' truncations. The inertia device is what made RH removable here. (vi) Fourier optimisation, semidefinite programming and dissipative-systems storage functions (CGdL; Knausgård) for window and certificate design. (vii) Zero-density estimates near the line (Selberg, Jutila, Conrey) to control off-line zeros in discrete-moment methods. (viii) Formal verification (Lean) and interval arithmetic, to certify constants in a field now flooded with AI-generated claims.

**7. Meaningful incremental breakthrough.** (1) Refereed publication, or a complete independent formal audit with the paper-to-Lean correspondence checked, of liminf N0^s/N >= C_MT = 0.6725007 for zeta. Arias de Reyna's report that the printed v2 contains a false step makes this the first gate. (2) An unconditional proof that liminf N0^s(T)/N(T) > 0.6819. This beats the bandwidth-one ceiling, and so necessarily brings in a genuinely new input: an RH-free third trace, pair correlation beyond support 1 on average, or an RH-free CGG/BHB discrete-moment bound. (3) The unconditional statement liminf N0^s/N >= 19/27, matching the best RH-conditional result. (4) Under RH, simple zeros > 19/27 (unchanged since 1998/2013). (5) For the mollifier method on its own terms, an admissible theta > 4/7 for Conrey- or Feng-type coefficients. It would no longer improve the record unless theta exceeds about 1, but it would break a barrier that has stood since 1989.

**8. Next concrete step.** Experiment first, cheap and decisive. Extend the certified ceiling computation of Alpöge-Furman §7.2 and Knausgård §7 (LP/SDP over periodic configurations with an on-line/off-line partition) to certificates that also use a third trace moment at reduced bandwidth, X = T^{2/3 - eps}, inside the Rudnick-Sarnak range. Report the new ceiling with interval enclosures. If the gain over 0.6818 is below 1e-3, abandon this direction. If it is larger, attempt the lemma: an RH-free asymptotic for sum over complex zeros rho1, rho2, rho3 with T < gamma_i <= 2T of f1(gamma1 - gamma2) f2(gamma2 - gamma3), for f_i of restricted Fourier support, following BGST's treatment of complex gamma, with off-line contributions bounded via the positive and negative indices of the compressed Weil form. Parallel lemma: an RH-free version of the Conrey-Ghosh-Gonek / Bui-Heath-Brown discrete moments sum_{T<gamma<=2T} B(rho) zeta'(rho) and sum \|B(rho) zeta'(rho)\|^2 over all complex zeros, in a form compatible with the inertia argument. That would transfer 19/27 from RH to unconditional for simple zeros, and possibly for simple on-line zeros. ## DBN

**1. Best established theorem.** Peer-reviewed and unconditional: 0 ≤ Λ ≤ 0.2 for the classical ζ in the normalization H_0(z) = ξ(1/2+iz/2)/8. The lower bound is Rodgers–Tao (Forum Math. Pi 8 (2020) e6, Thm 1), with an independent proof by Dobner (Acta Arith. 2021). The upper bound is Platt–Trudgian (BLMS 2021, Cor. 2), which combines Polymath's criterion (Thm 1.2), Polymath Table 1 row 2, and an interval-arithmetic verification of RH to 3,000,175,332,800. It follows that RH ⇔ Λ = 0. For every F in the extended Selberg class, Λ_F ≥ 0 (Dobner Thm 2). Function-field analogues are settled for specific families: sup Λ_D = max Λ_D = 0. As of 2026-11-17 there is a cluster of unrefereed computer-assisted claims. Unconditional in form: Λ < 0.149396 (Raymond, Zenodo, with erratum), < 0.156821 (Planat–Raymond, arXiv:2611.06245), < 0.158 (Gordon), < 0.1595 and < 0.16 (Mosaic Intelligence), ≤ 0.1732 (Planat), < 0.172422 (Kornaus), ≤ 0.1787854 (Gomila), ≤ 0.1875 (Mosaic). Conditional on extra analytic premises: ≤ 0.148612 (Planat, under (A1)–(A3)). Conditional on the unrefereed 7/8 zero-free half-plane: ≤ 0.13963 (Hakimoglu-Brown) and ≤ 0.107965 (Raymond 2026b). None of these is established. For Dirichlet L-functions there is an unrefereed claim Λ_L ≤ 0.2978125 for real primitive χ with conductor 3 ≤ m ≤ 400 (Freire et al.).

**2. Historical improvement.** Upper side: 1/2 (de Bruijn 1950), then < 1/2 with no explicit margin (Ki–Kim–Lee 2009), then 0.22 (Polymath 2019, a drop of 0.28), then 0.2 (Platt–Trudgian 2021, a further 0.02 that came only from extending RH verification from 3.06×10¹⁰ to 3.0×10¹²). That is a total drop of 0.30 in about 70 years. If the smallest unrefereed unconditional claim (0.149396) holds up, 2026 would add about 0.05 at the same RH height by better canopy and barrier certificates. Lower side: −∞ (1976), −50 (1988), −5 (1991), −0.385 (1992), −0.0991 (1991), −4.379×10⁻⁶ (1994), −5.895×10⁻⁹ (1993), −2.63×10⁻⁹ or −2.7×10⁻⁹ (2000; the secondary sources disagree), −1.14541×10⁻¹¹ (2011), and finally 0 (Rodgers–Tao 2018/2020). The lower side is closed. Generalizations: Stopple's quadratic-family bound Λ_Kr > −1.12929×10⁻⁷ (2014) was superseded by Dobner's Λ_F ≥ 0 (2021).

**3. Exact bottleneck.** Λ ≤ 0 is exactly RH, so the upper side can only reach 0 with an RH-strength input. Every quantitative upper bound so far goes through a criterion of Polymath type: Λ ≤ t0 + y0²/2 given (i) RH verified to height X/2, (ii) a zero-free canopy for H_{t0} beyond X, and (iii) a barrier at x ≈ X for 0 ≤ t ≤ t0. Polymath's §10 heuristic (pp.62–63) says that RH verified to height T yields at best Λ ≲ C/log T. A hypothetical off-line zero at height T with y ≍ 1 moves at speed ≍ log T and needs time ≍ 1/log T to reach the real axis. Polymath's own table implies C ≈ 5–6, so Λ ≤ 0.1 already needs RH to about 4.5×10²¹, roughly 10⁹ times the current height. The 2026 work (collision exclusion, translated probes, logarithmic-derivative barriers, Euler-product mollifiers) improves the constant C at the fixed height 3·10¹², roughly from 5.9 to about 4.4 if the 0.149396 claim holds, but it does not change the 1/log T scaling. An initial strip narrower than \|Im z\| ≤ 1 at t = 0, which is equivalent to a zero-free half-plane Re s > σ with σ < 1, enters linearly through de Bruijn's Δ²/2. That is why the unrefereed 7/8 claim feeds directly into bounds of 0.108–0.14. A secondary bottleneck is certification: none of the 2026 numerical certificates has been independently replayed or refereed.

**4. Bottleneck type.** mixed

**5. What negative results rule out.** (1) Rodgers–Tao 2020 Thm 1 and Dobner 2021 Thm 2: Λ ≥ 0, and Λ_F ≥ 0 on S♯. RH can only hold 'barely', so no heat-flow argument can prove RH by establishing real-rootedness at a negative time. Dobner further shows that for every t < 0, ξ_t has infinitely many off-line zeros. (2) Polymath 2019 §10 (a heuristic, not a theorem): finite RH verification to height T is expected to give only Λ = O(1/log T), and big improvements need either exclusion of RH violations at height T or much larger zero-free regions. (3) Rodgers–Tao pp.3–4: proving Newman's conjecture purely through Lehmer pairs would need GUE-type input, because known small-gap results alone do not suffice. Andrade–Chang–Miller 2014 show the Lehmer-pair technique is insufficient for linear combinations of L-functions. (4) Function fields (Andrade–Chang–Miller Thm 1.9): individual Λ_D can be negative or −∞, so Newman-type statements hold only as family suprema. (5) Lagarias–Montague 2011: a functional-equation family (the integral of ξ) has an infinite Λ-analogue, so an initial zero strip is essential. (6) Michalowski 2026 (unrefereed, certified): the kernel Φ(\|u\|) is not PF_5, which rules out total-positivity arguments of order ≥ 5. (7) Planat–Raymond 2026 (unrefereed): a Cauchy–Schwarz obstruction intrinsic to the four-prime source family limits their collision-exclusion method near its endpoint. This limits the method, not Λ itself.

**6. Possible external ingredients.** (a) Zero-free regions and zero-density estimates for ζ: any proven half-plane Re s > σ with σ < 1 gives a narrower initial strip. This is exactly where the unrefereed OpenAI 7/8 claim would enter, so an independent verification or refutation of it, including a replay of its Lean formalization, matters directly here. (b) Large-scale rigorous RH and GRH verification (Platt-style interval arithmetic, GPU ball arithmetic): each extra factor e in height is worth roughly ΔΛ ≈ C/log² T. (c) Value distribution of Dirichlet polynomials and Euler products (Bohr–Jessen almost periodicity, Turán-type power sums, mollifiers): these drive both the canopy certificates and Dobner's off-line-zero construction. (d) Random matrix theory, pair correlation and small-gap theorems, which control the lower side and any refinement near t = 0. (e) Statistical mechanics and heat flows of polynomials and entire functions: Lee–Yang theory (Newman–Wu, Bull. AMS 57 (2020) 595–614), Calogero–Moser and Dyson-type gradient flows (the Rodgers–Tao Hamiltonian), and Kabluchko's backward heat flow for unitary Hermite polynomials (arXiv:2203.05533). (f) Formal verification (Lean 4) and independent interval-arithmetic replays, to turn the 2026 certificate cluster into established results. (g) Laguerre–Pólya class and Jensen-polynomial hyperbolicity (Griffin–Ono–Rolen–Zagier type results), which give finite-degree shadows of real-rootedness.

**7. Meaningful incremental breakthrough.** In order of difficulty: (1) A refereed, independently replayed unconditional theorem Λ < 0.2 that uses only published inputs (Polymath Thms 1.2, 1.3, 1.5 and Platt–Trudgian's height). For example, independent confirmation of any one of Λ < 0.158 (Gordon), < 0.156821 (Planat–Raymond) or < 0.149396 (Raymond) would be the first peer-reviewed improvement since 2021. (2) An effective, explicit-constant theorem that makes the Polymath heuristic rigorous: if ζ has no zeros off the line with 0 < γ ≤ T, then Λ ≤ A/log T for all T ≥ T0, with explicit A (around 5) and T0. Any future verification height would then translate automatically into a bound on Λ, with no new barrier computation. (3) A uniform-in-conductor result: Λ_χ ≤ c < 1/2 for all primitive Dirichlet characters, or all real ones, of every conductor. (4) A rigorous converse: if Λ ≤ ε, then ζ has no zeros off the line below height exp(c/ε). As far as I can tell this is not established, and it would turn bounds on Λ into partial RH verification.

**8. Next concrete step.** Lemma: make Polymath Thm 1.5(i) explicit. Find a numerical C such that for 0 < t ≤ 0.2 and x ≥ exp(C/t), H_t(x+iy) ≠ 0 for y ≥ y0(t, x) with an explicit y0 = O(1/√log x). This is the missing piece that would turn the 1/log T heuristic into a theorem and remove the expensive canopy computation (ii). A concrete experiment to run alongside it: with outward-rounded ball arithmetic (Arb/FLINT), replay from scratch one Polymath-style criterion instance at the largest admissible site, X ≤ 2 × 3,000,175,332,800, for example the X = 6000000185827 used by Planat and by Planat–Raymond. Optimize (t0, y0) to produce an independently certified baseline below 0.2, which recovers the 'extra decimals' Platt–Trudgian did not pursue. Then use that code as an independent checker for the published certificate of one 2026 claim, e.g. Planat–Raymond commit 18dbc01 or Gordon's 12,666-row barrier with its Lean companion. Report exactly which inputs were reproduced from primitive sources and which were trusted. Separately, completing the blocked Lean replay of the OpenAI 7/8 claim would immediately decide the status of the conditional bounds 0.13963 and 0.107965.

**Open questions.**
- Is Λ = 0? This is equivalent to RH, given Rodgers–Tao.
- Can the Polymath heuristic 'RH verified to T ⇒ Λ = O(1/log T)' be proved with explicit constants? What is the best constant achievable by barrier and canopy methods at a fixed height?
- Conversely, does Λ ≤ ε imply that ζ has no off-line zeros below height exp(c/ε) for some explicit c? I found no source establishing this.
- Which of the 2026 unrefereed bounds (0.1875 down to 0.149396 unconditional in form; 0.148612 under (A1)–(A3); 0.13963 and 0.107965 under H7/8) survive independent refereeing and certificate replay?
- Is the OpenAI zero-free half-plane Re s > 7/8 correct and correctly formalized? If so, H7/8 becomes unconditional, and with it at least Λ ≤ 9/32 by de Bruijn alone, plus the stronger comparison bounds.
- Were Polymath's §10 conditional-table runs (including row 2, on which Platt–Trudgian's Λ ≤ 0.2 depends) carried out with outward-rounded enclosures? The paper describes 20-digit target accuracy and does not explicitly say so.
- Uniform upper bounds for Λ_χ below 1/2 for all primitive Dirichlet characters, including complex characters and all conductors, and for higher-degree L-functions in S♯.
- Can Rodgers–Tao's equilibrium mechanism be made quantitative in the opposite direction, so that the local statistics of zeros at t = 0 give information about how fast non-real zeros appear for t slightly negative?
- Discrepancy to resolve from the primary source: Odlyzko 2000 lower bound −2.63×10⁻⁹ (Rodgers–Tao table) versus −2.7×10⁻⁹ (zbMATH review).

**3. Exact bottleneck.** (1) mu(1/2): Bourgain's argument (§3, eqs. 3.12-3.13) gives \|S\|^6 << M^{9/2+eps} T^{1/2} N^{-3/2}. The block length is set to N = M T^{-2/7}, the largest compatible with the second-spacing term B_1 << M^2 R^2 Q^2/N^6 and with R <= N <= R^2. The first-spacing input (the A_6 mean value) is already essentially sharp. So the binding obstruction is Huxley's second spacing problem: counting near-coincidences of the vectors x(I) of the local cubic approximations (Huxley's 'resonance curves'), for sums of length M = T^alpha with 17/42 <= alpha <= 1/2. My reading, not a theorem: formally N = M T^{-theta} would give exponent 1/12 + theta/4 (13/84 at theta = 2/7). My bookkeeping with Bourgain's §4 inputs, Huxley's Theorem 3 (4.1) and the exponent pair (1/9,13/18) (4.2): these still cover the middle range for any target mu' > 43/279 = 0.154122. ## MOM

**1. Best established theorem.** (i) Asymptotics are known only for k=1 and k=2. For k=1, Hardy–Littlewood 1918 and Ingham 1926, with error E(T) ≪ T^{1515/4816+ε} (Bourgain–Watt, IMRN 2018). For k=2, Ingham 1926 and Heath-Brown 1979, with E_2(T) ≪ T^{2/3}(log T)^{C} (Ivić–Motohashi; log-power 10/3 in a 2025/26 preprint). (ii) Order of magnitude T(log T)^{k^2}. Unconditional lower bounds hold for every real k > 0 (Radziwiłł–Soundararajan 2013 for k ≥ 1; Heap–Soundararajan 2022 for 0 < k ≤ 1). Unconditional upper bounds hold only for 0 ≤ k ≤ 2 (Heap–Radziwiłł–Soundararajan 2019). Under RH upper bounds hold for all k ≥ 0 (Harper 2013, constant e^{e^{O(k)}}; e^{e^{18.63k}} in Tao's 2024 preprint). (iii) Above the fourth moment, the refereed unconditional upper bounds are only power-type: ∫\|ζ\|^6 ≪ T^{5/4+ε} and ∫\|ζ\|^{12} ≪ T^2(log T)^{17} (Heath-Brown 1978). An unrefereed October 2026 preprint (de Faveri–Pandey) gives T^{37/30+ε} and T^{2−520/445121+ε}. (iv) Twisted moments with arbitrary coefficients: second moment with θ < 17/33 (BCR 2017); fourth moment with ϑ < 1/4 (BBLR 2020). (v) The constants 42 and 24024 are unproven. The best unconditional sixth-moment lower constant is 34.4 c_3 vs. the conjectured 42 c_3 (Durkan–Page 2026 preprint).

**2. Historical improvement.** E(T) exponent: 1/2 (Ingham 1926) → 131/416 ≈ 0.314903 (Watt 2010) → 1515/4816 ≈ 0.314576 (Bourgain–Watt 2018). The last step gained about 3.3·10^{−4}, and the conjectured endpoint is 0.25. The withdrawn 2017 claims of 517/1648 are not counted. E_2(T): 7/8 (1979) → 2/3 (about 1989–1995). Since then only log powers have improved (C_1: 8 → 10/3). Sixth-moment power: 5/4 (1978) → 5/4 − 1/60 (2026 preprint), the first change in 48 years. Twelfth moment: T^2 log^{17} (1978) → T^2 log^{15} (2025/26 preprint) → T^{2−0.00117} (2026 preprint). Twisted fourth-moment asymptotic: ϑ < 4/589 (Gaggero Jara) → 1/11 (Hughes–Young 2010) → 1/4 (BBLR 2020), the conjectured natural barrier. Twisted second moment, arbitrary coefficients: 1/2 (BCHB 1985) → 17/33 (BCR 2017), δ = 1/66. The 2026 claim δ = 1/46 was withdrawn. Unconditional sharp upper bounds: k ∈ {1/n} (1981) and k ∈ {1+1/n} (2017) → all k ∈ [0,2] (2019). Conditional: (log T)^{k^2+ε} (2009) → sharp (2013) → explicit constant (2024) and large-deviation form (2026). Lower bounds: rational k (1981) → k ≥ 1 (2013) → all k > 0 (2022). Sixth-moment lower constant: 2c_3-type bounds → 34.4 c_3 (2026), about 82% of the conjecture.

**3. Exact bottleneck.** For upper bounds of the correct order with k > 2, there is no anchor. The Radziwiłł–Soundararajan/HRS upper-bound principle needs a higher moment computed with room to spare, for example a twisted sixth moment bounded by T^{1+o(1)}. Yet even ∫\|ζ\|^6 ≪ T^{1+ε} is a Lindelöf-type statement and wide open. Under RH, Soundararajan's inequality (log\|ζ(1/2+it)\| ≤ Re Σ_{n≤x} Λ(n)n^{−1/2−1/log x−it} log(x/n)/(log x log n) + log T/log x + O(1/log x)) replaces the anchor. Unconditionally, no pointwise upper bound for log\|ζ\| by a short prime sum exists. For the sixth-moment asymptotic, the bottleneck is the off-diagonal: shifted convolution sums Σ_{n∼X} d_3(n)d_3(n+h) for h up to X^{1/3}, with power savings uniform in h. This is the ternary additive divisor problem, a GL(3)-type shifted convolution beyond current spectral technology; Ng shows it suffices. For power bounds on M_k with 4 < k ≤ 12, the bottleneck is large values of \|ζ\| at V ≈ T^{1/8}. These reduce to large-value estimates at V ≈ N^{3/4} for exponential sums with perturbed square-root phases x^{1/2} + ηx^{3/2}. For twisted moments, the bottleneck is trilinear Kloosterman-fraction sums (second moment) and the spectral large sieve with Watt's bound and its Burgess-type 1/4 barrier (fourth moment).

**4. Bottleneck type.** mixed

**5. What negative results rule out.** (1) BCR (Crelle 2017, p.2–3): the general twisted second-moment formula fails for θ > 1. The θ < 1 version for arbitrary coefficients (the 'θ = 1 conjecture') implies Lindelöf. BCR Corollary 1 shows that square-root cancellation in trilinear Kloosterman-fraction sums (their Conjecture 1, best possible by their appendix) implies Lindelöf, so the full twisted range is at least Lindelöf-hard. (2) BBLR (JEMS 2020, pp.4–5): ϑ < 1/4 in the twisted fourth moment is optimal even assuming Selberg's eigenvalue conjecture. Such an amplifier recovers only the Burgess-type 3/16 bound, a barrier in other families, so ζ-specific input is required. (3) de Faveri–Pandey (2026, §1.2): for ℓ^2-normalized coefficients the mean-value bound for square-root-phase sums is sharp (counterexample a_n = N^{1/4}1_square(n); Guth survey arXiv 2503.07410, Cohen, Fu–Guth–Maldague 2023). Any large-value improvement must use ℓ^∞ structure. (4) Harper (§6) and Arguin–Bailey–Roberts 2026: all known conditional methods give doubly exponential constants (e^{e^{O(k)}}, exp(e^{ck})), far from the RMT-predicted e^{−k^2 log k}. (5) dFP p.1 and Durkan–Page p.2: ∫\|ζ\|^{2k} ≪ T^{1+ε} for all k is equivalent to Lindelöf, so unconditional sharp upper bounds for all k are at least as hard as Lindelöf. (6) Pseudomoments (Bondarenko–Brevig–Saksman–Seip–Zhao, BLMS 2018; Gerspach arXiv 1909.10224; abstracts only): moments of partial sums Σ_{n≤N} n^{−1/2−it} show three growth regimes and deviate from (log N)^{k^2} for low k. Naive truncated Dirichlet-series proxies therefore fail for small moments. (7) Radziwiłł, 'Limitations to mollifying ζ(s)' (arXiv 1207.6583, abstract only): a non-trivial lower bound on off-diagonal contributions to mollified moments for mollifiers of arbitrary length. (8) Withdrawn or retracted claims: Bourgain–Watt arXiv 1709.04340 (517/1648 and short-interval mean square, withdrawn as theorems in 2023); Dong–Robles–Zeindler arXiv 2601.00292 (θ = 1/2+1/46, withdrawn for a missing L^2 factor in (2.53)). ## VERIF

**1. Best established theorem.** Zeta, rigorous (ball arithmetic, refereed): RH holds for every nontrivial zero with 0<gamma<=3,000,175,332,800, i.e. the lowest 12,363,153,437,138 zeros (Platt-Trudgian, BLMS 53 (2021), Theorem 1). Rigorous simplicity and high-precision isolation (+-2^-102) are certified only to 3.0610046e10, covering 103,800,788,359 zeros (Platt 2017, Theorem 5.1). Floating-point and unrefereed: Gourdon's 10^13 zeros (H about 2.44e12), local windows of 2e9 zeros at zero #10^24 (Gourdon), and more than 50,000 zeros in 200+ windows up to zero #10^36 (Bober-Hiary). A window of about 1.4e6 zeros near t about 1e28 is certified by Hiary-Ireland-Kyi, conditional on the accuracy of the input data. Dirichlet L-functions: GRH for all primitive chi mod q<=400,000 to height max(1e8/q, c/q+200), about 3.8e13 zeros (Platt 2016, Theorem 7.1). Central values L(1/2,chi) != 0 for q<=2e6 (Theorem 7.2). No real zeros with sigma>=1-1/(5 log q) for quadratic chi with q<=1e10 (Lu-Zaman-Zhao 2026). Higher degree: rigorous only at small heights, e.g. Maass L-functions for SL(2,Z) with r<=178 to t=30000 (Booker-Then 2018) and S5/A5 Dedekind zeta to \|Im s\|<=100 (Booker 2006).

**2. Historical improvement.** Verified count or height for zeta: Gram 1903 (10-15 zeros) -> Backlund 1914 (79) -> Hutchinson 1925 (138) -> Titchmarsh-Comrie 1936 (1,041; t<=1468) -> Turing 1953 (1,104) -> Lehmer 1956 (25,000) -> Lehman 1966 (250,000) -> Rosser-Yohe-Schoenfeld 1969 (3.5e6) -> Brent 1979 (75,000,001; t<32,585,736.4) -> 1982/1983 (2e8/3e8) -> van de Lune-te Riele-Winter 1986 (1.5e9; H=5.454e8). After that come unrefereed floating-point runs: van de Lune 2001 (1e10), ZetaGrid 2003-04 (claims 2e11-9e11 zeros, inconsistent), Gourdon 2004 (1e13 zeros, H about 2.44e12). Then rigorous runs: Platt 2017 (H=3.061e10) and Platt-Trudgian 2021 (H=3.0002e12). Factors: rigorous H grew about 98x from 2017 to 2021 and about 5500x from 1986 to 2021 (35 years, about 25%/year compounded). Platt-Trudgian exceed Gourdon's unrefereed H by 22%. No larger rigorous H was found from 2021 to Nov 2026 (5+ years without a record). GRH (Dirichlet): Rumely 1993 (q<=13 to height 1e4, about 1e7 zeros) -> Platt 2016 (q<=4e5 to 1e8/q, about 3.8e13 zeros), about 6 orders of magnitude in zeros. Turing's constant b: 0.128 (Turing) -> 0.114 (Lehman 1970) -> 0.059 (Trudgian 2011) -> 0.049 plus a 0.183 log log term (Trudgian 2016). Single-point evaluation exponent: 1/2 (Riemann-Siegel) -> 3/8 (Schoenhage) -> 1/3 (Heath-Brown/Hiary) -> 4/13 (Hiary 2011).

**3. Exact bottleneck.** For the verified height H, the bottleneck is cost that grows linearly with the number of zeros, plus the overhead of rigor. Any zero-by-zero method must detect about N(H) ~ (H/2pi)log(H/2pi) sign changes. With Odlyzko-Schoenhage/Booker FFT amortization the cost per zero is only H^{o(1)}, so total cost is about H^{1+o(1)}, and faster single-point algorithms (t^{4/13}) do not help. The rigorous run is about 725x slower per unit height than Gourdon's floating-point run, because of about 25 samples per zero instead of 1.2 and multiprecision ball arithmetic instead of hardware floats (Platt-Trudgian Section 2). The rigorous run used 7.5e6 core hours to reach 3e12. Linear extrapolation (my estimate, not from a source) gives roughly 2.6e7 core hours to reach 1e13 (about 4.31e13 zeros). Second, certification and trust: there is no certificate cheaper to check than the computation (Gourdon Section 1.1), and the 3e12 code is not permanently archived (zbMATH review). An independent rigorous replication would therefore cost as much again, and none exists. Third, a structural limit: finite verification says nothing about RH globally. S(t) is unbounded (Selberg's Omega result), and the regime where \|S(t)\| is large, which might plausibly host anomalies, lies at heights accessible only through isolated non-rigorous windows (largest observed S about 3.35 at t about 7.75e27). For GRH the cost is O_eps(q^{1+eps}) per modulus for all characters (Platt 2016, Lemma 6.1, as cited by Lu-Zaman-Zhao). The total over q<=Q at fixed height therefore grows like Q^2, which limits the modulus-height trade-off.

**4. Bottleneck type.** computational

**5. What negative results rule out.** (1) Gram-point methods alone cannot work. Gram's law and the Weak Gram law fail for a positive proportion of Gram intervals (Trudgian, Acta Arith. 148 (2011)). Backlund's \|S\|<1/2 approach must eventually fail because S(T)=Omega_{+-}((log T)^{1/3}(log log T)^{-7/3}) (Selberg, as cited in Trudgian 2011 Section 1). Rosser's rule fails infinitely often (Lehman 1970), and its empirical failure rate rises with height: 20.0M violations in the first 1e12 zeros versus 37.6M in zeros 9e12-1e13 (Gourdon 2004). So completeness must rest on Turing-type integrated-S(t) bounds or explicit-formula tests. (2) Limits of Turing's constants: Trudgian 2016 states 'it seems difficult to improve substantially' on his Theorem 1, and his 2011 method cannot push b below 0.0353; gains in a or b only reduce the number of Rosser blocks needed (already about 6 at 2pi*1e12), not the dominant cost. (3) De Bruijn-Newman: Rodgers-Tao's Lambda>=0 means further Lehmer-pair discoveries cannot improve the lower bound. Close pairs occur at GUE-predicted rates (13 pairs with delta<1e-4 against 10.96 expected to 1e13; Gourdon), so near-coincidences are not evidence against RH. (4) Complexity: no published single-point algorithm beats t^{4/13+o(1)} (Hiary 2011). Any complete verification to height H needs at least about N(H) evaluations, so the cost is linear in H up to logarithmic factors. (5) No finite H, however large, implies RH or can be turned into global evidence without a separate locality theorem. RH-dependent explicit results (e.g. Johnston 2021, Buethe 2016) become valid only for x up to about a constant times H^2 (e.g. x<=2.169e25 from H=3e12).

**6. Possible external ingredients.** (a) Certified and formally verified numerics: Lean/Coq-verified interval and ball kernels, or proof-carrying checkers, to turn 'trust the implementation' into a checkable artifact; verified FFT error bounds of Whittaker-Shannon/Weiss type. (b) Hardware: GPU or vectorized double-double interval arithmetic. Platt's GRH run already used SSE2 double-precision intervals, which suggests much of the 725x rigor overhead is avoidable. (c) Explicit analytic number theory: sharper explicit bounds for \|zeta(sigma+it)\| near sigma=1/2 and 1 (e.g. Hiary-Patel-Yang and later work) and a reduction of the log 4 term of Booker's Lemma 4.4, to tighten Turing windows. (d) Explicit-formula and Hadamard-product completeness tests (Buethe 2015; Hiary-Ireland-Kyi 2027) as an independent second check that needs only cheap evaluations of L'/L in the region of absolute convergence. (e) Random-matrix theory and LLL lattice reduction, to target stress regions (large \|Z\|, large \|S\|, close pairs) where floating-point methods are most fragile (Bober-Hiary). (f) Trace formulas (Booker-Stroembergsson) and Selberg-class Turing bounds (Palojarvi-Zhao) for higher-degree L-functions. (g) Prime-number-theory consumers (explicit PNT, Goldbach-type results, Brun constant) to fix which heights and moduli matter.

**7. Meaningful incremental breakthrough.** Option 1: a rigorous verification that every zero with 0<gamma<=H, H>=1.0e13 (about 4.31e13 zeros), has beta=1/2, with archived source code, artifact hashes and an explicit acceptance rule. By Platt-Trudgian's reading of Polymath15 Table 1, this would immediately give Lambda<0.19. Option 2 (less costly but equally valuable): an independent rigorous replication of RH(3e12) with a different algorithm or library, e.g. an Odlyzko-Schoenhage-type pipeline in double-interval arithmetic, cross-checked by the Hiary-Ireland-Kyi window test, making the current record no longer single-source. For GRH: verification for all primitive chi mod q<=Q to a uniform height T0 that strictly enlarges Platt's region, e.g. Q>=1e6 at height 1e8/q, or T0>=1e4 for all q<=1e5. Such an extension feeds directly into explicit prime-number theorems in arithmetic progressions.

**8. Next concrete step.** Experiment: re-implement the Platt-Trudgian windowed Booker/FFT sweep with two tiers. Tier (i): a fast floating-point pass locating sign changes at about 1.2-2 Z-evaluations per zero (Gourdon-style Gram-block heuristics). Tier (ii): a rigorous certification pass in IEEE double-interval (not multiprecision) arithmetic that re-evaluates the completed zeta only at stored sign-change abscissae and Turing endpoints, escalating to Arb only for unresolved Gram blocks (Platt's GRH run used the same escalation ladder). Apply Turing's method with Trudgian's 2016 constants per block, plus the Hiary-Ireland-Kyi explicit-formula test on randomly sampled windows as an independent completeness check. Benchmark on [3.0e12, 3.0e12+1e8] and report GHz-hours per unit height against Platt-Trudgian's about 110,000 per GHz-hour. A 10x gain would bring H=1e13 within about 2-3e6 core hours (my extrapolation). The lemma to prove first: an explicit rounding- and truncation-error bound for the windowed Booker/Odlyzko-Schoenhage evaluation at t of about 1e13 in 53-bit interval arithmetic, the analogue of Platt 2017 Appendix A with working precision about 53 instead of 300 bits. The bound must show that the certified error is below the minimum \|Z\| at the chosen sample points in all but a quantified fraction of Gram blocks.

It comes from the T\|W\|^2N^{3/2−σ} part of the S3 bound (Prop. 11.2), i.e. the \|W\|^3N^{1−2σ} 'random-set' term of the additive-energy bound (Prop. 11.1) fed into Prop. 10.1. It equals the MVT term T·N^{1−2σ} exactly at σ = 7/10 for every T. - T^{4/3}N^{2−4σ}. It comes from the low-energy bound S3 ≲ T^2\|W\|^{3/2} (Prop. 10.1). It is tangent at σ = 7/10 when T = N^{6/5} and worse than the MVT for T > N^{6/5}. This is why GM fix T = N^{6/5} (Prop. 3.1) and reach larger T by Huxley subdivision, which loses a full factor of T/N^{6/5}.  So lowering sup A needs both a better energy bound for large-value sets at V ≈ N^{7/10} and a better low-energy S3 bound, or a non-subdivision route to T ≈ N^{13/10}.  The DH threshold 25/32 is set by Bourgain's dichotomy at τ ≈ 3/2. TTY's exhaustive machine optimisation of all recorded inputs could not move it.

**3. Exact bottleneck.** Three different bottlenecks. (1) Explicit classical constant: the zero detector is nearly saturated.
