## ARITH

**1. Best established theorem.** Robin and Lagarias: Robin's inequality is verified for 5040 < n ≤ 10^(10^13.11485), with certified interval arithmetic (Morrill–Platt 2021, Thm 13). Lagarias's inequality holds on the same range. Robin is proved for every 21-free n ≥ 5041 (Axler 2023, re-proved by Assani–Chester–Paschal 2026). Unconditionally, σ(n)/n < (1 + 3.15367·10^{-7}) e^γ log log n off an explicit finite set (Axler 2023). Mertens: \|M(x)\| < √x exhaustively for 1 < x ≤ 10^16 (Hurst 2018, exact integer sieve); limsup M/√x > 1.826054 and liminf < −1.837625 (Hurst 2018, uncertified high precision); first counterexample < exp(1.96·10^19) (Kim–Nguyen 2025, Arb-certified). Skewes: 10^19 < x_s < exp(727.951336108) ≈ 1.397167·10^316 (Büthe 2018; Saouter–Trudgian–Demichel 2015). Explicit PNT: \|π(x) − Li(x)\| ≤ 9.2211 x √(log x) exp(−0.84768√(log x)) for x ≥ 2 (Fiori–Kadiri–Swidinsky 2023). Schoenfeld's RH-strength bound \|π(x) − li(x)\| < √x log x/(8π) holds unconditionally on [2657, 1.101·10^26] (Johnston 2022, via RH to 3·10^12). Nyman–Beurling: unconditional liminf d_N^2 log N ≥ Σ m_ρ^2/\|ρ\|^2 (Burnol 2002), sharp under RH plus a moment hypothesis (Bettin–Conrey–Farmer).

**2. Historical improvement.** Robin range: 10^8576 (Robin 1984) → 10^(10^10) (Briggs 2006) → 10^(10^13.11485) (Morrill–Platt 2021), a ×1300 gain in log10 log10 N. No extension in 5 years. t-free Robin: 5 (2007) → 7 (2012) → 11 (2015) → 20 (2021) → 21 (2023). Unconditional Robin factor: 5.645·10^{-7} → 3.15367·10^{-7}. Mertens exhaustive range: 10^8 (1963) → 10^12 → 10^13 → 10^14 (2003/04) → 10^16 (2018), with no extension since; isolated values reach M(10^25) (2026 preprint). Mertens limsup: 1.06 (1985) → 1.218 (2006) → 1.6383 (2015) → 1.826054 (2018), +73% overall and stalled for 8 years. First Mertens counterexample: exp(3.21·10^64) (1987) → exp(1.59·10^40) (2006) → exp(1.004·10^33) (2014) → exp(1.017·10^29) (2023 preprint) → exp(1.96·10^19) (2025), 45 orders of magnitude off the exponent, mostly from better lattice algorithms. Skewes upper bound: ≈1.65·10^1165 (1966) → ≈6.66·10^370 (1987) → 1.39822·10^316 (2000) → 1.397167·10^316 (2015), only 0.075% since 2000. Skewes lower bound: ~10^14 (2008) → 1.2·10^17 (2016) → 10^19 (2018). Explicit PNT constant c in exp(−c√log x): 0.8 (2021) → 0.8274 (2023) → 0.84768 (2023). Korobov–Vinogradov-shape constant: 0.1853 (2023) → 0.1982767 (2026 preprint, ψ only).

**3. Exact bottleneck.** The bottleneck is structural first. Every quantity here is RH-equivalent or RH-adjacent, so finite verification cannot move toward RH. Johnston–Trudgian (2025) quantify the locality barrier: arithmetic verification up to Y yields zero information only for \|γ\| ≲ Y^{1/5}. Hurst's \|M(x)\| ≤ 0.571√x to 10^16 excludes simple zeros only with β > 0.99, γ < 5. In the other direction, zero verification to height T controls prime counting up to about T². Each sub-frontier then has its own rate-limiting step. (a) Robin/Lagarias range: certified enumeration of colossally abundant numbers needs primes up to p_max ≈ log N, now about 3·10^13. (b) t-free Robin: one needs an unconditional explicit bound, below about 2^{-t}, on e^{-γ}∏_{p≤x} p/(p−1)/log θ(x) − 1 for all x ≥ p_{k0}. The binding regions are the bottom of the range and the middle range x ∈ [10^26, e^{10^4}], beyond partial-RH reach (Morrill–Platt's g_B, g_∞ > 1 at t = 21). A heuristic float extrapolation (mine, not certified) puts the true excess near p ≈ 3·10^13 at about (0.3–1.1)/(√x log x) ≈ 2–7·10^{-9}. So the method has an intrinsic ceiling near t ≈ 27 at the current range, rising by 1 per 4× in p_max. (c) Mertens limsup and first counterexample, and the Skewes upper bound: simultaneous Diophantine approximation of (γ_i y mod 2π) over hundreds to about a thousand zeros. This is lattice-reduction or enumeration quality in dimension ~100–1000 with zeros known to ≥ 5000 digits (y ≈ 10^5097). (d) Mertens and Skewes lower ranges: linear-time sieving for M(x) (about a week per 10^16 on 2026 hardware) and O(x^{1/2+ε}) analytic algorithms with certified zero tables (zeros to 10^11 used for 10^19). (e) Explicit PNT constants: explicit zero-free constant R (5.558691 refereed; 4.896 preprint), explicit zero-density N(σ,T), and RH height H = 3.0·10^12, plus the large engineering cost of re-propagating improved inputs through chains of papers. Some of those links are themselves broken (the Ramaré 2016 → Dusart 2018 → downstream erratum chain).

**4. Bottleneck type.** mixed

**5. What negative results rule out.** (1) The Mertens conjecture is false (Odlyzko–te Riele 1985). If the linear-independence hypothesis LI holds, M(x)/√x is unbounded (Ingham 1942). Best–Trudgian (Math. Comp. 2015) prove there is no integer relation Σc_iγ_i = 0 among the 500 heaviest zeros with \|c_i\| ≤ 4976. (2) Kim–Nguyen's rigorous bound exp(1.96·10^19) contradicts the Kotnik–van de Lune heuristic that the first Mertens counterexample is ≈ exp(5.15·10^23). Kim–Nguyen note that no disproof avoiding lattice reduction is known. Surviving growth conjectures have \|q(x)\| = Ω((log log log x)^θ) with θ = 1/2 (Kotnik–van de Lune), 1 (Kaczorowski), or 5/4 (Ng/Gonek). (3) Johnston–Trudgian (arXiv 2511.09978): the Landau–Pintz arithmetic-to-zero-free route for ζ 'is doomed to fail' to improve known zero-free regions, and verifying Mertens up to 10^80 would give only no simple zeros with β > 0.99, γ < 10^13. (4) Robin's t-free method: Morrill–Platt §5 show that their inputs fail at t = 21. Heuristically (my analysis) any t-free method has a ceiling growing like log2(√p_max) and cannot cover all integers. If RH fails, Robin showed G(n) > e^γ(1 + c/(log n)^b) for infinitely many n with b < 1/2 (as quoted by Zimov, arXiv 2510.23889), but nothing bounds where. (5) Nicolas criterion: proving it via strict monotonicity of the natural sequence would contradict Cramér's conjecture (Choie–Planat–Solé, arXiv 1012.3613). (6) Nyman–Beurling: unconditional d_N^2 ≳ 0.046/log N (Burnol), so finite d_N computations cannot certify RH. (7) Explicit PNT: Broucke (arXiv 2507.13780) builds Beurling zeta functions with zeros on prescribed contours, showing that the zero-free-region → error-term refinement is close to sharp. Abstract zero-free-region input alone cannot do much better. Johnston (2024) and Bellotti (2026) give essentially optimal exp(−ω(x)) forms. (8) Skewes: Stoll–Demichel's 'lower bound 3.17·10^114' is heuristic only. The location of the first crossing is unknown.

**6. Possible external ingredients.** (a) Lattice reduction and enumeration from post-quantum cryptanalysis (BKZ 2.0, extreme-pruned enumeration, lattice sieving such as G6K-type implementations) for the Ingham/Pintz/Lehman kernels: Mertens limsup, the first Mertens counterexample, and finding new or earlier Skewes regions. Kim–Nguyen report million-fold speedups from orthogonality-aware enumeration. (b) Certified arithmetic and formal verification: Arb/FLINT ball arithmetic and directed rounding, plus Lean or Coq replay of checkers, to convert uncertified records (Hurst's Theorem 6.1, Nicolas's 30-digit Maple run) into certified ones. (c) Zero verification at larger heights (Platt–Trudgian-style Turing-method computations) and higher-precision zero tables: these feed partial-RH prime bounds directly. (d) Analytic zero-density progress (Guth–Maynard large values; the Tao–Trudgian–Yang systematic exponent-pair and zero-density framework, arXiv 2501.16779) and explicit zero-free regions (Bellotti–Trudgian–Yang 2026), for the middle-range explicit bounds that block t-free Robin. (e) High-throughput prime and Möbius sieving (Hurst's 2026 bucket-scheduled segmented sieve; Helfgott–Thompson O(x^{3/5}) isolated M(x)) for range extensions. (f) Chirre–Helfgott optimal bounds for sums of non-negative arithmetic functions (arXiv 2512.15709), already used by Fiori–Jaskari. (g) An infrastructure ingredient: a machine-readable dependency graph of explicit-number-theory constants, as the IEANTN effort aims for, so that erratum chains like Ramaré → Dusart → Morrill–Platt are caught automatically.

**7. Meaningful incremental breakthrough.** Any one of the following, each precisely stated and achievable in principle. (i) A certified proof that limsup_{x→∞} M(x)/√x ≥ 2 and liminf ≤ −2: an Arb-enclosed value \|h(y,N)\| ≥ 2 in Ingham's inequality. (ii) A rigorous proof that the first Mertens counterexample is below exp(10^18). (iii) Robin's inequality for every 25-free n > 5040, or a certified extension of the Robin range to 10^(10^15) (p_max ≈ 2.3·10^15), which also lowers Axler's unconditional ε to about 0.0094243/(log p_max)^3 ≈ 2.4·10^{-7}. (iv) π(x) < li(x) for all 2 ≤ x ≤ 10^20 (a ×10 gain on Büthe). (v) A refereed explicit bound \|π(x) − li(x)\| ≤ A x exp(−0.198 (log x)^{3/5} (log log x)^{−1/5}), the π-analogue of Fiori–Jaskari, with the improved inputs R = 4.896 and current zero-density propagated. (vi) A refereed, fully re-derived replacement for every Dusart 2018 Mertens-product estimate used in the Robin and Nicolas literature, closing the documented proof gap.

**8. Next concrete step.** Two cheap, high-value steps for an ambitious researcher. 1. Certify, then beat, the Mertens oscillation record. First re-evaluate Hurst's h(y±, 14400) in Arb ball arithmetic, using the exact y± from the published notebook (wolfr.am/mertens) and zeros/ζ'(ρ) recomputed with ≥ 5,200 correct digits (needed since y ≈ 10^5097). This turns Theorem 6.1 into a certified result. Then port Kim–Nguyen's BKZ-plus-pruned-enumeration pipeline (Zenodo 10775723) from the Pintz/Saouter–te Riele kernels to Ingham's kernel f(t) = (1 − t)cos πt + sin(πt)/π, with N ≈ 1000–1200 zeros and target \|h(y,N)\| ≥ 2. Hurst's own LLL extrapolation (≈10 months for 2.00) is an upper bound on the effort. 2. Robin side: prove the lemma 'for all k ≥ k0 = 999,999,476,056, e^{−γ}R_22(N_k) < 1', i.e. ∏_{p≤p_k}(1 − p^{−22}) p/(p−1) < e^γ log θ(p_k). Combine BFKNW Theorem 6 or Axler 2024 product bounds on [p_{k0}, ∞) with Johnston's partial-RH range on [p_{k0}, 1.1·10^26]. In the same pass, re-derive Morrill–Platt Theorem 11 from a valid source to close the Dusart-dependency gap. A float pre-check here (arith_scripts/mertens_product.py) suggests the true margin at p ≈ 3·10^13 is about 100× smaller than 2^{-22} ≈ 2.4·10^{-7}, so the lemma should be true. The work is purely in making rigorous explicit bounds sharp enough.

*Independent critique of this frontier analysis:* Overall: the records are mostly accurate and well sourced. I opened and checked every 'current-best' claim against the full text except Saouter–Trudgian–Demichel, for which I used the zbMATH review. Errors are concentrated in the drafter's own heuristics and wording, not in the quoted records.  (1) The t-free Robin bottleneck is mis-located and mis-quantified. - (a) The heuristic script uses log x where the criterion needs log θ(x). With the correct normalisation, Nicolas 2012 (under RH; numerically to 10^9) gives a true excess e^{-γ}∏p/(p−1)/log θ − 1 ≈ (1.24–2.28)/(√x log x) ≈ 0.7–1.3·10^{-8} at p_k0. The draft's 2–7·10^{-9} is too small. The perfect-information ceiling is therefore t ≈ 26, not 27, and the margin against 2^{-22} is about 20–30×, not 100×. - (b) The 'middle range [10^26, e^{10^4}]' is not binding. Published ε-type bounds there (Büthe 2016 as corrected by Bhattacharjee: 1.24·10^{-11} for x ≥ e^60; Chirre–Helfgott preprint: 1.05·10^{-12}) contribute ≲ 10^{-12} to the Mertens-product error. The binding region is x ≈ p_k0, where Axler's uniform closed-form product bound (relative error ≈ 3.05·10^{-7} at p_k0) is the actual obstruction for t = 22. - (c) The proposed next step would fail as stated. Johnston's RH-strength bound \|θ(x) − x\| < √x log²x/(8π), integrated in the Mertens-product formula, gives an error ≈ log x/(4π√x) ≈ 4.5·10^{-7} at p_k0. That exceeds 2^{-22} ≈ 2.4·10^{-7}, and it is the same mechanism that makes MP's g_B(·;21) > 1. One must instead use Büthe 2018's computed bounds (0.05√x < x − θ(x) ≤ 1.95√x on [1423, 10^19]). In my uncertified arithmetic, the boundary terms cancel against log θ(x) and the integral term gives a rigorous excess ≲ 3.9/(√x log x) ≈ 2.3·10^{-8}. That would in principle suffice up to t ≈ 25. If so, '25-free' is a near-term target rather than a breakthrough. This needs a careful explicit check.  (2) The Johnston–Trudgian 'locality barrier' is overstated. Their \|γ\| ≲ Y^{1/5} describes their explicit Landau–Pintz method with B_G = 7/2 ('approximately'). It is not a theorem bounding what any arithmetic verification can yield, and it concerns M(x), not Robin or Lagarias. The quoted numbers (β > 0.99, γ < 5 from 10^16; γ < 10^13 from 10^80; β > 0.51, γ < exp(10^16) from exp(10^19); 'doomed to fail') are all verbatim-correct.  (3) The explicit-PNT frontier omits the intermediate range. For x < e^{2394}, ε-type bounds dominate the de la Vallée Poussin-shape record: Chirre–Helfgott (arXiv 2512.15709, unrefereed) give \|ψ(x) − x\| ≤ πx/(3·10^12) + 113.67√x for all x ≥ 1, and 34.409x/log⁴x for x ≥ 69991. (5) Negative results. These are real and correctly stated: Odlyzko–te Riele; Ingham under LI; Best–Trudgian (500 heaviest zeros, \|c_i\| ≤ 4976); Kotnik–van de Lune / Kaczorowski / Ng–Gonek exponents 1/2, 1, 5/4 (confirmed via Kim–Nguyen §4.2); Choie–Planat–Solé (Cramér); Broucke (abstract); and Rozmarynowycz–Kim's omission of StR14. Arias de Reyna (arXiv:2610.24612) reconstructs the Claude proof, repairs one boundary step he says fails as written in v2, and concludes it is correct. **2. Historical improvement.** Mollifier era. kappa: Selberg 1942 (positive), Levinson 1974 0.3474, Conrey 1983 0.3658, Conrey 1989 0.4088 (+0.061 from theta 1/2 to 4/7), BCY 2011 0.4105, Feng 2012 0.4128 (justified only in 2018), Pratt-Robles 2018 0.41492, PRZZ 2020 0.41729. Pair-correlation-only RH bounds rose from 2/3 to 0.6792 (2020).

**3. Exact bottleneck.** (a) New method. Montgomery's form factor F(alpha) is known (unconditionally, in the BGST sum over all complex zeros) only for \|alpha\| <= 1. The prime-side diagonal evaluation needs Dirichlet polynomials of length X <= T; for X >> T the off-diagonal needs prime-pair asymptotics. Every certificate built from the first two trace moments at bandwidth one is capped at about 0.6818 (Alpöge-Furman §7.2), and the local-inequality refinements in use are capped at 0.6736-0.6738 (Knausgård §7). Higher traces tr G^k are unconditionally unavailable at the required bandwidth (Rudnick-Sarnak range X^k <= T^{2-eps}); the third trace is available only under RH. (b) Mollifier method. The twisted second moment off-diagonal (Kloosterman-sum bounds, Deshouillers-Iwaniec) has fixed theta at 4/7 since 1989. Even the conditional theta = 1 gives only 0.6107 (Feng, Cor. 1), below the new 0.6725, and for every finite theta the mollified error is >= c/theta (Radziwill). So mollifiers become competitive only with theta well beyond 1, which again needs off-diagonal or prime-pair information. (c) RH-conditional discrete moments over zeros (CGG/BHB, 19/27 > 0.6818) use RH to treat sum_rho \|B zeta'(rho)\|^2 as a sum over real ordinates. No RH-free inertia-type replacement for these discrete moments exists yet.

## DBN

**1. Best established theorem.** Peer-reviewed and unconditional: 0 ≤ Λ ≤ 0.2 for the classical ζ in the normalization H_0(z) = ξ(1/2+iz/2)/8. The lower bound is Rodgers–Tao (Forum Math. Pi 8 (2020) e6, Thm 1), with an independent proof by Dobner (Acta Arith. 2021). The upper bound is Platt–Trudgian (BLMS 2021, Cor. 2), which combines Polymath's criterion (Thm 1.2), Polymath Table 1 row 2, and an interval-arithmetic verification of RH to 3,000,175,332,800. It follows that RH ⇔ Λ = 0. For every F in the extended Selberg class, Λ_F ≥ 0 (Dobner Thm 2). Function-field analogues are settled for specific families: sup Λ_D = max Λ_D = 0. As of 2026-11-17 there is a cluster of unrefereed computer-assisted claims. Unconditional in form: Λ < 0.149396 (Raymond, Zenodo, with erratum), < 0.156821 (Planat–Raymond, arXiv:2611.06245), < 0.158 (Gordon), < 0.1595 and < 0.16 (Mosaic Intelligence), ≤ 0.1732 (Planat), < 0.172422 (Kornaus), ≤ 0.1787854 (Gomila), ≤ 0.1875 (Mosaic). Conditional on extra analytic premises: ≤ 0.148612 (Planat, under (A1)–(A3)). Conditional on the unrefereed 7/8 zero-free half-plane: ≤ 0.13963 (Hakimoglu-Brown) and ≤ 0.107965 (Raymond 2026b). None of these is established. For Dirichlet L-functions there is an unrefereed claim Λ_L ≤ 0.2978125 for real primitive χ with conductor 3 ≤ m ≤ 400 (Freire et al.).

**2. Historical improvement.** Upper side: 1/2 (de Bruijn 1950), then < 1/2 with no explicit margin (Ki–Kim–Lee 2009), then 0.22 (Polymath 2019, a drop of 0.28), then 0.2 (Platt–Trudgian 2021, a further 0.02 that came only from extending RH verification from 3.06×10¹⁰ to 3.0×10¹²). That is a total drop of 0.30 in about 70 years. If the smallest unrefereed unconditional claim (0.149396) holds up, 2026 would add about 0.05 at the same RH height by better canopy and barrier certificates. Lower side: −∞ (1976), −50 (1988), −5 (1991), −0.385 (1992), −0.0991 (1991), −4.379×10⁻⁶ (1994), −5.895×10⁻⁹ (1993), −2.63×10⁻⁹ or −2.7×10⁻⁹ (2000; the secondary sources disagree), −1.14541×10⁻¹¹ (2011), and finally 0 (Rodgers–Tao 2018/2020). The lower side is closed. Generalizations: Stopple's quadratic-family bound Λ_Kr > −1.12929×10⁻⁷ (2014) was superseded by Dobner's Λ_F ≥ 0 (2021).

**3. Exact bottleneck.** Λ ≤ 0 is exactly RH, so the upper side can only reach 0 with an RH-strength input. Every quantitative upper bound so far goes through a criterion of Polymath type: Λ ≤ t0 + y0²/2 given (i) RH verified to height X/2, (ii) a zero-free canopy for H_{t0} beyond X, and (iii) a barrier at x ≈ X for 0 ≤ t ≤ t0. Polymath's §10 heuristic (pp.62–63) says that RH verified to height T yields at best Λ ≲ C/log T. A hypothetical off-line zero at height T with y ≍ 1 moves at speed ≍ log T and needs time ≍ 1/log T to reach the real axis. Polymath's own table implies C ≈ 5–6, so Λ ≤ 0.1 already needs RH to about 4.5×10²¹, roughly 10⁹ times the current height. The 2026 work (collision exclusion, translated probes, logarithmic-derivative barriers, Euler-product mollifiers) improves the constant C at the fixed height 3·10¹², roughly from 5.9 to about 4.4 if the 0.149396 claim holds, but it does not change the 1/log T scaling. An initial strip narrower than \|Im z\| ≤ 1 at t = 0, which is equivalent to a zero-free half-plane Re s > σ with σ < 1, enters linearly through de Bruijn's Δ²/2. That is why the unrefereed 7/8 claim feeds directly into bounds of 0.108–0.14. A secondary bottleneck is certification: none of the 2026 numerical certificates has been independently replayed or refereed.

**4. Bottleneck type.** mixed

**5. What negative results rule out.** (1) Rodgers–Tao 2020 Thm 1 and Dobner 2021 Thm 2: Λ ≥ 0, and Λ_F ≥ 0 on S♯. RH can only hold 'barely', so no heat-flow argument can prove RH by establishing real-rootedness at a negative time. Dobner further shows that for every t < 0, ξ_t has infinitely many off-line zeros. (2) Polymath 2019 §10 (a heuristic, not a theorem): finite RH verification to height T is expected to give only Λ = O(1/log T), and big improvements need either exclusion of RH violations at height T or much larger zero-free regions. (3) Rodgers–Tao pp.3–4: proving Newman's conjecture purely through Lehmer pairs would need GUE-type input, because known small-gap results alone do not suffice. Andrade–Chang–Miller 2014 show the Lehmer-pair technique is insufficient for linear combinations of L-functions. (4) Function fields (Andrade–Chang–Miller Thm 1.9): individual Λ_D can be negative or −∞, so Newman-type statements hold only as family suprema. (5) Lagarias–Montague 2011: a functional-equation family (the integral of ξ) has an infinite Λ-analogue, so an initial zero strip is essential. (6) Michalowski 2026 (unrefereed, certified): the kernel Φ(\|u\|) is not PF_5, which rules out total-positivity arguments of order ≥ 5. (7) Planat–Raymond 2026 (unrefereed): a Cauchy–Schwarz obstruction intrinsic to the four-prime source family limits their collision-exclusion method near its endpoint. This limits the method, not Λ itself.

**6. Possible external ingredients.** (a) Zero-free regions and zero-density estimates for ζ: any proven half-plane Re s > σ with σ < 1 gives a narrower initial strip. This is exactly where the unrefereed OpenAI 7/8 claim would enter, so an independent verification or refutation of it, including a replay of its Lean formalization, matters directly here. (b) Large-scale rigorous RH and GRH verification (Platt-style interval arithmetic, GPU ball arithmetic): each extra factor e in height is worth roughly ΔΛ ≈ C/log² T. (c) Value distribution of Dirichlet polynomials and Euler products (Bohr–Jessen almost periodicity, Turán-type power sums, mollifiers): these drive both the canopy certificates and Dobner's off-line-zero construction. (d) Random matrix theory, pair correlation and small-gap theorems, which control the lower side and any refinement near t = 0. (e) Statistical mechanics and heat flows of polynomials and entire functions: Lee–Yang theory (Newman–Wu, Bull. AMS 57 (2020) 595–614), Calogero–Moser and Dyson-type gradient flows (the Rodgers–Tao Hamiltonian), and Kabluchko's backward heat flow for unitary Hermite polynomials (arXiv:2203.05533). (f) Formal verification (Lean 4) and independent interval-arithmetic replays, to turn the 2026 certificate cluster into established results. (g) Laguerre–Pólya class and Jensen-polynomial hyperbolicity (Griffin–Ono–Rolen–Zagier type results), which give finite-degree shadows of real-rootedness.

## LFUN

**1. Best established theorem.** Refereed and unconditional: for Dirichlet L-functions the standard region has explicit constant R = 9.645908801 for all q (McCurley 1984) and R = 5.60 for q <= 4*10^5 (Kadiri 2018, using Platt's rigorous GRH check up to q <= 4*10^5). **3. Exact bottleneck.** (a) Verification. The two largest advances (Siegel-zero exclusion, QRH) are AI-generated and unrefereed. The Siegel argument is short and has several independent proofs plus a Lean formalization. For QRH 11/12, the load-bearing mean-square estimate (Prop. 5.1, Secs. 5-7) has not been independently audited here, and the 7/8 paper reportedly has not been digested. Under a fixed zero-free half-plane, Heath-Brown's remark (1992, p.269) reduces L to the single-modulus density exponent, so the bottleneck becomes large values of Dirichlet polynomials with characters (Huxley 12/5; claimed 7/3).  (d) Congruence spectral gap. Progress beyond 7/64 needs functoriality of higher symmetric powers (Sym^n for n >= 5) or a new cancellation mechanism.

**5. What negative results rule out.** 1. No Euler product, no RH. The Davenport-Heilbronn function and Epstein zeta functions of class number > 1 satisfy Riemann-type functional equations but have a positive density of zeros off the line, N(sigma1,sigma2,T) asymptotic to T, including zeros in sigma > 1 (Davenport-Heilbronn 1936; summarized in Rezvyakova arXiv 2411.18492, Sec. 1). Explicit strip zeros such as 0.8085 + 85.699i were recomputed here non-rigorously. Any GRH proof must use multiplicativity, not only analytic continuation and the functional equation.  2. Spectral analogues. General compact hyperbolic surfaces can have small eigenvalues, so the Selberg-zeta RH fails without arithmeticity. Random-surface results (Hide-Magee; Anantharaman-Monk) say nothing about congruence surfaces, which have WP-measure zero (Monk 2026, Remark 1.2). For graphs, Ramanujan is typical but not universal: about 31% of random regular graphs fail it (Huang-McKenzie-Yau).  4. Classical methods. The standard de la Vallee Poussin / Landau method cannot remove one real zero of one real character for an intrinsic reason (Zhang 2022, Sec. 1; Page's theorem). Heath-Brown (1983) and Friedlander-Iwaniec showed Siegel zeros would imply twin-prime-type statements; this indicated the Siegel-zero world was consistent with all known sieve tools and needed non-sieve input. That input came from transcendence-style interpolation determinants and Masser's zero estimate.  5. Linnik lower bounds. Lower-bound constructions show L = 1 is the limit (Chowla conjecture L = 1 + eps); details from memory, not verified.  6. Finite verifications (Platt; Lu-Zaman-Zhao) cannot settle infinite statements.

**1. Best established theorem.** (i) Asymptotics are known only for k=1 and k=2. For k=1, Hardy–Littlewood 1918 and Ingham 1926, with error E(T) ≪ T^{1515/4816+ε} (Bourgain–Watt, IMRN 2018). For k=2, Ingham 1926 and Heath-Brown 1979, with E_2(T) ≪ T^{2/3}(log T)^{C} (Ivić–Motohashi; log-power 10/3 in a 2025/26 preprint). (ii) Order of magnitude T(log T)^{k^2}. Unconditional lower bounds hold for every real k > 0 (Radziwiłł–Soundararajan 2013 for k ≥ 1; Heap–Soundararajan 2022 for 0 < k ≤ 1). Unconditional upper bounds hold only for 0 ≤ k ≤ 2 (Heap–Radziwiłł–Soundararajan 2019). Under RH upper bounds hold for all k ≥ 0 (Harper 2013, constant e^{e^{O(k)}}; e^{e^{18.63k}} in Tao's 2024 preprint). (iii) Above the fourth moment, the refereed unconditional upper bounds are only power-type: ∫\|ζ\|^6 ≪ T^{5/4+ε} and ∫\|ζ\|^{12} ≪ T^2(log T)^{17} (Heath-Brown 1978). An unrefereed October 2026 preprint (de Faveri–Pandey) gives T^{37/30+ε} and T^{2−520/445121+ε}. (iv) Twisted moments with arbitrary coefficients: second moment with θ < 17/33 (BCR 2017); fourth moment with ϑ < 1/4 (BBLR 2020). (v) The constants 42 and 24024 are unproven. The best unconditional sixth-moment lower constant is 34.4 c_3 vs. the conjectured 42 c_3 (Durkan–Page 2026 preprint).

**3. Exact bottleneck.** For upper bounds of the correct order with k > 2, there is no anchor. The Radziwiłł–Soundararajan/HRS upper-bound principle needs a higher moment computed with room to spare, for example a twisted sixth moment bounded by T^{1+o(1)}. Yet even ∫\|ζ\|^6 ≪ T^{1+ε} is a Lindelöf-type statement and wide open. Under RH, Soundararajan's inequality (log\|ζ(1/2+it)\| ≤ Re Σ_{n≤x} Λ(n)n^{−1/2−1/log x−it} log(x/n)/(log x log n) + log T/log x + O(1/log x)) replaces the anchor. Unconditionally, no pointwise upper bound for log\|ζ\| by a short prime sum exists. For the sixth-moment asymptotic, the bottleneck is the off-diagonal: shifted convolution sums Σ_{n∼X} d_3(n)d_3(n+h) for h up to X^{1/3}, with power savings uniform in h. This is the ternary additive divisor problem, a GL(3)-type shifted convolution beyond current spectral technology; Ng shows it suffices. For power bounds on M_k with 4 < k ≤ 12, the bottleneck is large values of \|ζ\| at V ≈ T^{1/8}. These reduce to large-value estimates at V ≈ N^{3/4} for exponential sums with perturbed square-root phases x^{1/2} + ηx^{3/2}. For twisted moments, the bottleneck is trilinear Kloosterman-fraction sums (second moment) and the spectral large sieve with Watt's bound and its Burgess-type 1/4 barrier (fourth moment).

## OPS

**1. Best established theorem.** Several things are proved unconditionally. (i) Spectral realizations exist, but none has RH content. Connes' delta-Sobolev and Connes–Consani's zeta-cycle constructions see only the zeros already on the critical line (LED-OPS-1, LED-OPS-4). Meyer's virtual representation realizes all zeros but in non-Hilbert spaces (LED-OPS-3). (ii) Connes' S-local trace formula holds for every finite set of places, and the global trace formula is equivalent to RH; that equivalence is proved only in positive characteristic and sketched for Q (LED-OPS-2). (iii) Weil positivity is proved only for test functions supported in (1/2, 2), i.e. with no primes involved: Yoshida 1992, then Connes–Consani 2021 with the operator inequality W_inf(g*g^*) >= Tr(theta(g)S theta(g)^*) - c\|g^(0)\|^2, 13 < c < 17 (LED-OPS-5). (iv) The strongest new structural theorem is Connes–van Suijlekom (CMP 2025). For every finite-prime window, if the lowest eigenvalue of the (truncated) semilocal Weil form is simple, isolated and iota-even, then the Mellin transform of the ground state has ONLY real zeros (LED-OPS-7). The same holds for Suzuki's unconditional self-adjoint differentiation operators D_{a,theta}, which have real simple spectrum, and for which a simple, positive, even ground state is proved for small windows (LED-OPS-11; unrefereed). (v) In de Branges theory, xi(1/2 + h - iz) is Hermite–Biehler for h >= 1/2 unconditionally (Lagarias 2005), and Suzuki's explicit Hamiltonians cover omega > 1 (LED-OPS-14, LED-OPS-15). Under RH, Suzuki (Oct 2026) proves that the localized-Weil spectra decrease monotonically to the distinct ordinates gamma_n (LED-OPS-12).

**2. Historical improvement.** Spectral realization: the 1998-99 version needed an ad hoc Sobolev weight delta > 1; zeta-cycles (2021/2023) use the canonical L^2 of a circle, with no gain in RH content. The Weil-positivity window has not been extended since Yoshida 1992 (lambda^2 = 2): Connes–Consani 2021 explained the same window conceptually and did not enlarge it. Numerical reproduction of zeros by the finite-prime Weil ground state: lambda^2 = 13 (6 primes), N = 120 gave 50 zeros, the first to 2.4e-55 (CCM Nov 2025; Connes Letter Feb 2026). Groskin's c = 67 gave about 1.5e-168, and c = 100 gave 10 zeros to 307-329 digits (2026). lambda^2 = 250 (53 primes), N = 4000 gives the first zero to 1341 digits, 1000 zeros to > 500 digits, and all 2469 zeros up to height 3000 with a certified count (Andrews–Connes–La Gatta, Nov 2026): an improvement of about 1287 orders of magnitude in the first-zero error between 2025 and 2026. Analytic control of the near-radical spectrum: Kastoris' bound eps_lambda <= lambda^{O(1)} delta_4(lambda) was improved by Connes–Kastoris to C(log lambda)^3 delta_4(lambda), with delta_4 ~ c lambda^9 e^{-4 pi lambda^2}; this upper-bound direction only is proved. CCM's 'missing step 1' (simple-even ground state) went from unproved (Nov 2025) to proved for sufficiently small windows (Suzuki, Jun 2026), which do not yet contain a prime as far as stated. On the physics side there has been no progress in proven content since 1999: xp models reproduce only N_bar(E), and SRL-type spectra deviate by about (theta - pi/4)/(1 + W(n/e)) ~ 1/ln n (2026 numerics).

**3. Exact bottleneck.** Every route ends in the same RH-equivalent positivity, appearing in five forms: Weil positivity QW_lambda >= 0 for all lambda; de Branges/Hermite–Biehler membership of xi + xi' (equivalently E_h for 0 < h < 1/2); the boundary behaviour at infinity of the canonical system; the Lax–Phillips decay or causality condition; and W > 0 in pseudo-Hermitian intertwiner schemes. In the most advanced (CCM/Connes) programme the concrete gaps are three. (1) The lowest eigenvalue of QW_lambda must be simple with an iota-even eigenvector for all large lambda, in windows that contain primes. (2) The normalized ground states must converge to tau = theta/theta(1), with M theta = (1/4) xi(s + 1/2), in the weighted L^1 norms on u and u^{-1} that control the Mellin transform on the closed strip; then Hurwitz' theorem gives RH (Proposition 7.3 of 2611.13129). (3) The existing convergence estimates assume QW_lambda >= 0 (circular); replacing that assumption needs the LOWER-bound direction of the Fuchs-ladder correspondence. That is, nothing in the spectrum of QW_lambda may lie below what the uncertainty operator U_lambda = 2 - P_lambda - P^_lambda permits, uniformly in lambda and including the prime terms. Galerkin truncations give upper sections only and can never certify (3).

**4. Bottleneck type.** mixed

**5. What negative results rule out.** Conrey–Li (IMRN 2000) show that de Branges' sufficient positivity conditions fail for E(z) = xi(1 - iz): the witness is -Re{xi'(rho) xi(1 + rho)} = -5.389e-69 < 0 at the 34th zero. They fail likewise for L(s, chi_{-4}), and Sarnak gave a computation-free proof of the F(W) failure, so de Branges' specific route is closed. Bellissard (2017): the Bender–Brody–Müller momentum operator with a Dirichlet condition has deficiency indices (1, 0), hence no self-adjoint extension, and psi_z is not in L^2 for Re z = 1/2. Endres–Steiner (2010): the Berry–Keating operator on L^2(R_+) has purely continuous spectrum. Meyer (2005) and Connes (1999)/Connes–Consani (2023): spectral realizations (of all zeros, or of critical zeros) exist unconditionally, so a 'spectral realization' carries no RH information without positivity. Lax–Phillips (1973-76) themselves judged that their RH-equivalent decay criterion is thrown back onto number theory. At fixed lambda the Weil-ground-state agreement breaks down at height t ~ 3100 (lambda^2 = 250), independently of N (Andrews–Connes–La Gatta 2026, Section 7.3), so no single finite window suffices. The authors state that their conditional estimates assume QW_lambda >= 0 and 'do not give a proof' (Remark 7.4). Suzuki (2026) notes that his limit-formula route needs lambda_a > 0 for all a, which is equivalent to RH. Yakaboylu's 2025 self-adjointness claim was withdrawn into a tautological W > 0 <=> RH (v17, Aug 2026), with an independent critique by Feldmann (Zenodo 2026). Unverified or disputed numerics: Śliwiński's claimed lower bound 1/(4 ln lambda) on the mean error has a gap in its proof. Groskin's negative even-sector eigenvalues at c = 100 are disputed as quadrature artifacts (de Andrade Silva, Zenodo 2026).

**6. Possible external ingredients.** (1) Non-asymptotic time-frequency concentration theory for prolate spheroidal functions: two-sided bounds on the eigenvalues 1 - \|chi_n(lambda)\| in the 'plunge' region (Slepian, Landau–Widom, Fuchs; Osipov–Rokhlin–Xiao; Bonami–Jaming–Karoui-type estimates). These could supply the lower-bound direction of the Fuchs ladder at least for the archimedean block. (2) Two-projection (Halmos) theory and Toeplitz/Carathéodory–Fejér/Szegő theory for quantitative gap and simplicity estimates of corank-one positive forms. (3) Inverse spectral theory of canonical systems and Krein strings (de Branges, Remling, Makarov–Poltoratski Toeplitz-kernel uncertainty principles), to control the a -> infinity limit in Suzuki's framework. (4) Schur-complement and perturbation theory with certified interval/ball arithmetic (Arb/FLINT) to turn finite-window numerics into lower certificates. (5) Function-field analogues, where Connes' Theorem 5 and RH are theorems, as a test bed for the semilocal positivity mechanism (Sonin space, near-radical block). (6) NCG spectral-action expansions (divided-difference matrices), which give the exact algebraic form of the truncated Weil matrices.

**7. Meaningful incremental breakthrough.** A rigorous LOWER certificate for one window that contains primes. Concretely: prove unconditionally that for some lambda with lambda^2 > 2, for example lambda^2 = 3 (S = {inf, 2, 3}) or lambda^2 = 5, the semilocal Weil form QW_lambda on L^2([1/lambda, lambda], d*u) is positive, with a simple, isolated, iota-even ground state. Better, prove this for every lambda^2 in an interval [2, 2 + eta]. By Connes–van Suijlekom this would give the first rigorous family of finite-prime entire functions with only real zeros, built from genuine prime data with no truncation hypothesis. It would also extend Weil positivity beyond Yoshida's 1992 window for the first time, as far as found here. An analytic version would be a two-sided Fuchs-ladder estimate c_1 delta_4(lambda)/lambda^A <= eps_lambda(1) <= C delta_4(lambda)(log lambda)^3, together with a gap eps_lambda(2)/eps_lambda(1) >= c lambda^4, for all lambda in some unbounded range. That range would have to include the prime terms, which is not RH-equivalent at any fixed lambda.

## POS

**1. Best established theorem.** (a) Weil positivity. The only published unconditional window is the prime-free archimedean one: test functions supported in [2^{-1/2}, 2^{1/2}], log half-width (log 2)/2 (Yoshida 1992; Connes–Consani, Selecta 2021, Thm 6.11). An unrefereed certified computation extends this to half-width 0.8, the first window where the primes 2 and 3 enter, with λ*(0.8) ∈ [8.9e-18, 2.27e-17] (Zhu 2026). (b) Li coefficients. λ_n ≥ 0 is known for n ≤ T0^2 ≈ 9·10^24 through the semi-local implication from zero verification; Oesterlé's statement is unpublished, and the 9·10^24 figure is derived. Direct rigorous computation reaches n ≤ 10^5 (Johansson 2015). Asymptotics under GRH are λ_n = (N/2) n log n + C_1 n + O(√n log n) (Lagarias 2007), and RH ⟺ (y_n) ∈ ℓ^2 (Arias de Reyna 2011). (c) Jensen polynomials. Hyperbolic for d ≤ 9·10^24 and all n (Chasse-type criterion plus Platt–Trudgian), and for n ≫_ε d^{1/2+ε} and all d (Kim–Lee 2022). The unshifted J_d has at least d − C√d log d real zeros (Ono 2026, unrefereed). (d) Turán (d=2) and higher-order Turán (d=3) hold for all indices. Toeplitz minors are positive for k ≥ 10^18 r^3 (unrefereed). The coefficient sequence is PF_r for r ≲ 9.4·10^12 via zero verification, and infinitely log-concave (unrefereed). (e) Nyman–Beurling. Unconditionally liminf d_N^2 log N ≥ Σ m_ρ^2/\|ρ\|^2. The matching asymptotic d_N^2 ~ (2+γ−log 4π)/log N is known only under RH plus a ζ'(ρ) moment hypothesis. (f) Monotonicity is known only on σ ≤ 0 (\|t\| ≥ 8). (g) Λ ≥ 0 (Rodgers–Tao).

**2. Historical improvement.** Weil window half-width: 'sufficiently small' (Yoshida, Lemma 2) → (log 2)/2 ≈ 0.347 (Yoshida/Connes–Consani) → 0.8 (Zhu 2026, about ×2.3, unrefereed). A 2026 claim of 1.19 was retracted. Jensen degree, all shifts: 2 (1986) → 3 (2011) → 10^17 or 2·10^17 (Chasse 2013) → 9.36·10^20 (2022) → 9·10^24 (2026). This only tracks the square of the verified RH height. Shift threshold: ineffective (2019) → c·e^{d/2} (2022) → O(d^{1/2+ε}) (2022), from exponential to polynomial. Li direct computation: 7000 (Keiper 1992) → 10^5 (Johansson 2013/15), about ×14, with no change since. The semi-local Li reach grows as T0^2. Li asymptotic error: o(n) (Oesterlé, unpublished) → O(√n log n) (Lagarias 2007) → ℓ^2-type (Arias de Reyna 2011). Nyman–Beurling constant: Σ 1/\|ρ\|^2 (2000) → Σ m^2/\|ρ\|^2 (Burnol 2002), and conditional matching (2012). Toeplitz minors: fixed order (Katkova 2007) → uniform k ≥ 10^18 r^3 (2026).

**3. Exact bottleneck.** Every unconditional result falls into one of three regimes that miss RH. (i) Archimedean-only windows, where no prime enters (Weil (log 2)/2). (ii) Results that are corollaries of finite zero verification and scale like T^2 or πT: Jensen d ≤ T^2, Li n ≤ T^2, PF_r with r ≤ πT, Laguerre on \|x\| ≤ T. (iii) Asymptotic tail regimes where the object has forgotten the zeros: large shift n, index k ≫ r^3, or Hermite and cosine universality, which also hold for functions violating RH (Ki; Kim–Lee; Farmer). The RH-critical regimes are untouched. These are n = 0 for all d, k ~ r for Toeplitz minors, Weil windows containing many primes, Li indices n ≳ T0^2, and the strip 0 < σ < 1/2 for monotonicity. Each requires controlling cancellation in the prime comb Σ Λ(n) n^{-1/2} ĝ(log n) uniformly in frequency, which is as hard as zero information. Quantitatively, Zhu's Theorem 1.4 shows that pointwise-envelope certificates need frequency resolution T1 = 2π e^{A_L} with A_L ~ 4e^L (doubly exponential). Meanwhile the margin λ*(L) collapses empirically like exp(−2π^2 N(T*)/log N(T*)) with T* = 2πe^{2L}.

**4. Bottleneck type.** structural

**5. What negative results rule out.** (1) Rodgers–Tao (Forum Math. Pi 2020, arXiv:1801.05914): Λ ≥ 0, so there is no positive heat-flow or universal-factor slack. (2) Farmer (Adv. Math. 411, 2022, arXiv:2008.07206): Jensen polynomials disperse zero information. A function with an off-line zero at height ≈10 has real-rooted J^{d,0} up to d = 118, and Ki's cosine universality holds in the extended Selberg class, which contains RH-violating functions. (3) Kim–Lee (J. Korean Math. Soc. 59, 2022): large-shift hyperbolicity holds for every real entire function of order < 2 with zeros in a strip, so it cannot imply RH. (4) GORTTW Remark (3) and Ono 2026 Remark 4: the partial Jensen results imply no zero-free region or density estimate. (5) Voros (Exp. Math. 2020, eqs. (17)–(18)): by an uncertainty-principle argument, λ_n can detect an off-line zero 1/2+t+iT only for n ≳ T^2/\|t\| ≳ 2T0^2 ≈ 10^25, so direct Li computations are void as RH tests. (6) Zhu 2026, Theorem 1.4: pointwise-envelope Weil certificates cost doubly exponential in the window, and no pointwise comb bound can lower the threshold (Lemma 3.2). (7) Burnol 2002: Nyman–Beurling approximations converge no faster than (C/log N)^{1/2}. (8) Michalowski 2026: the Φ kernel is not PF_5, so total-positivity shortcuts on Φ itself fail. (9) Bombieri 2000: if RH fails with finitely many off-line zeros, truncated Weil matrices show exactly half that many negative eigenvalues, so violations are visible only at large truncation. (10) Davenport–Heilbronn-type functions satisfy the functional equation but not RH. Any criterion that ignores the Euler product (Jensen/Hermite universality, Toeplitz tails) therefore cannot prove RH. Voros's deformed Keiper–Li tests react correctly to Davenport–Heilbronn zeros.

**6. Possible external ingredients.** (a) Diophantine approximation and linear forms in logarithms (Baker–Wüstholz-type lower bounds for \|Σ k_p log p − t·2π m\|). These would show that the phases (t log p)_{p ≤ e^{2L}} cannot align, which beats the pointwise prime-comb envelope (Zhu, Remark 1.6). (b) Time–frequency analysis: prolate spheroidal functions, Landau–Widom eigenvalue plunge, Slepian concentration (Connes–Consani–Moscovici; Andrews–Connes–La Gatta; Zhu). (c) de Branges spaces and screw functions (Suzuki 2023–2026), which give continuous-function and operator models of the localized Weil form. (d) Finite free probability and multiplicative convolution (Holland 2026; Campbell–O'Rourke–Renfrew) for joint degree–shift control of Jensen polynomials. (e) Total-positivity theory (Schoenberg, Aissen–Schoenberg–Whitney–Edrei, Brändén). (f) Certified numerics: Arb ball arithmetic, verified Cholesky/LDL^T, interval quadrature, plus independent replay infrastructure. (g) Large-sieve and mean-value theorems for Dirichlet polynomials, for averaged rather than pointwise comb control. (h) Semi-local trace formula and noncommutative geometry (Connes–Consani). (i) Proof assistants (Lean), as claimed for Ono 2026.

**7. Meaningful incremental breakthrough.** Any one of the following would be a precise, non-trivial advance short of RH. (1) A refereed and independently replayed certificate of Weil positivity on a window with half-width L ≥ 1.19 (autocorrelation support ≥ 2.38, so primes up to 7 and prime powers up to 9 enter). It should use an arithmetic comb-cancellation lemma rather than a pointwise envelope, so that certificate cost grows sub-doubly-exponentially in L. Alternatively, a computer-free proof of positivity for any window containing the prime 2. (2) A published, explicit semi-local Li theorem in both directions. One direction: RH up to T ⟹ λ_n ≥ 0 for n ≤ T^2 (Oesterlé), with complete proof. The other: λ_1..λ_N ≥ 0 ⟹ no zeros off the line with \|γ\| ≤ c√N, repairing Brown's Theorem 2. This would make Li positivity a two-way finite zero detector. (3) A converse 'semi-local Jensen' theorem: hyperbolicity of J^{d,0} for all d ≤ D implies RH up to height f(D) for explicit f(D) → ∞. Farmer's examples suggest f(D) ≈ c√D at best. (4) Toeplitz-minor positivity D_{r,k} > 0 for k ≥ C r^{1+δ} with δ < 2, closing part of the gap to the RH-critical regime k ~ r without zero input. (5) An unconditional proof of the global Laguerre inequality L_1[Ξ](x) ≥ 0 on some explicit interval longer than the verified-zero interval, or for \|x\| ≥ X0, without new zero input.

**8. Next concrete step.** Weil windows. Take Zhu's one-stroke reduction at L ∈ [0.8, 1.2]. Replace the pointwise prime-comb bound A_L = Σ_{n ≤ e^{2L}} Λ(n) n^{-1/2} by a certified bound on sup_{t ∈ [T_a, T_b]} of the actual trigonometric sum Σ Λ(n) n^{-1/2} w(log n) cos(t log n). For the few primes involved (2, 3, 5, 7) this is a finite problem. Partition the t-range and use interval arithmetic with explicit lower bounds for \|k_1 log 2 + k_2 log 3 + ... − 2π m\| from linear forms in logarithms to exclude near-alignment. This should lower the required resolution T♯ below T1 = 2πe^{A_L} and make an L = 1.19 certificate feasible. In parallel, replay Zhu's L = 0.8 Cholesky certificate independently in Arb, so that the first window with primes is authenticated by two checkers. Li coefficients. Write out and referee a complete proof of Oesterlé's semi-local proposition with explicit constants, and test it numerically against Johansson's λ_n for n ≤ 10^5 and against Davenport–Heilbronn models, where the first sign failure of λ_n can be compared with the predicted n ≈ T^2/\|t\|. Jensen polynomials. Experimentally tabulate d_max(T) = max{d : J^{d,0} hyperbolic} for Farmer-type X_j and Davenport–Heilbronn functions, in certified arithmetic, and fit d_max against the height and displacement of the first off-line zero. This would yield a conjectural converse law to prove.

Unconditional simple-and-on-line: 1/3 (1974) -> 2/5 (1989) -> 0.4075 (2020) -> 2/3 and 0.6725 (Aug 2026) -> 0.67354 (Oct 2026), a 64% relative jump in one step. Unconditional distinct: 0.6603 (2015) -> 0.83625 -> 0.83900 (2026). F(alpha) beyond alpha = 1: no asymptotic progress since 1973; only GRH one-sided bounds (2000) and long-interval averages 1/3..2 -> 0.9278..1.3302 (2022) -> 0.9303..1.3208 (2023).

**3. Exact bottleneck.** (1) Small gaps: crossing mu = 1/2. Every unconditional or RH input in use (pair correlation on \|alpha\| <= 1, n-level correlations with sum \|xi\| < 2, mean values of Dirichlet polynomials of length <= T in the diagonal regime) is provably consistent with the Alternative Hypothesis (Lagarias-Rodgers Thm 3.1). So mu < 1/2 needs information outside this band. Bondarenko-Heap identify the missing ingredient: long Dirichlet polynomials (length > T) with coefficients f where f(p) = -1 on many primes. That requires power-saving asymptotics for sum f(n)f(n+h) and for shifted-prime correlations sum Lambda(k)f(m)f(km+h), which for f = mu is a Chowla-type problem out of reach. Equivalently, one would need F(alpha) for alpha > 1, i.e. Hardy-Littlewood-strength variance of primes in short intervals. (2) Positive proportion below 1/2 - eps is exactly what Siegel zeros forbid in the Conrey-Iwaniec range, so it is tied to the Landau-Siegel problem. (3) Simple zeros: the unconditional bandwidth-one ceiling is about 0.6818 (Alpoge-Furman Sect. 7.2). Going past it needs the form factor beyond support 1 or higher trace moments, which are unavailable unconditionally beyond the Rudnick-Sarnak range X^k <= T^{2-eps}. Under RH the mollifier record 19/27 has not moved since 1998, limited by mollifier length (theta < 1/2 type constraints). (4) Large gaps: no structural barrier is known. Progress is technical, limited by twisted mean-value theorems for weights of length about sqrt(T) and by the coefficient optimization. (5) F(alpha) for \|alpha\| > 1 is equivalent (Goldston-Montgomery) to an asymptotic for the variance of primes in short intervals, a prime-pair-correlation problem.

**4. Bottleneck type.** mixed

**5. What negative results rule out.** (a) Lagarias-Rodgers (Q. J. Math. 71 (2020), Thm 3.1): an explicit half-integer-gap sequence reproduces all band-limited n-level GUE correlations, so the known correlation information cannot prove mu < 1/2. (b) Conrey-Ghosh-Gonek (1984): the Montgomery-Odlyzko criterion cannot give mu < 1/2. Goldston-Trudgian-Turnage-Butterbaugh (JMAA 527 (2023), Thm 1): it cannot give mu < 0.5042 with y = T^{1-delta}, nor lambda >= 3.6747. Inoue-Kobayashi-Toma (arXiv 2510.14309, Sect. 7): with L <= T it is limited to mu >= 0.508 and lambda <= 3.022. (c) Conrey-Iwaniec (Acta Arith. 103 (2002)): a Siegel zero forces at most O(T(log T)^{4/5}) normalized gaps < 1/2 - eps in the relevant range, so positive-proportion small-gap results below 1/2 must defeat Siegel zeros. (d) Alpoge-Furman (2608.13637, Sects. 1.4 and 7.2): any 'bandwidth-one certificate' gives at most about 0.6818 for simple-and-on-line zeros. Unconditionally, higher trace moments add nothing (prime side exact only for X^k <= T^{2-eps}). The method is insensitive to o(N) off-line zeros and works for Davenport-Heilbronn and Epstein zeta functions, so it cannot reach RH. (e) Knausgard (2610.08965) proves upper limits for what his local inequalities can give with his two main test functions. (f) Bui-Milinovich note that all Mueller-method (discrete vs continuous mean value) results are superseded and bounded by the Wirtinger approach; no proof is known that lambda = infinity follows from any finite-support correlation data. The AH construction is consistent with large gaps of half-integer size.

Power-saving shifted correlations for a multiplicative f with f(p) = -1 on a dense set of primes would enable long resonators beyond length T. (2) Zero-density estimates for Dirichlet L-functions (Guth-Maynard, Chen-Gupta-Li's T^{17/14}). (3) Fourier optimization and the Cohn-Elkies sphere-packing LP/SDP machinery, and reproducing-kernel Hilbert spaces of entire functions (de Branges), which drove CCLM, CGdL, CCCM, CMR and Lamzouri. (4) Matrix analysis (Sylvester inertia, rank-trace and Schur-Horn inequalities, Christoffel functions, Chebyshev-Markov-Stieltjes bounds) applied to compressions of Weil's explicit-formula form, which gave the 2026 unconditional simple-zero jump. (5) Random matrix theory and determinantal point processes (ratios conjecture, sine-process moments, discrete sine processes) to calibrate what correlation data can and cannot imply. (6) Hyperbolic geometry and conformal mapping (Hall-Hayman) for unconditional gap upper bounds. **7. Meaningful incremental breakthrough.** Prove, under RH alone (or unconditionally), that mu <= 1/2 - delta for an explicit delta > 0. That is, infinitely many normalized gaps are below 1/2 - delta. This would refute every 'strong' Alternative Hypothesis of the form gaps in (1/2)Z_{>=1} + o(1) without assuming Siegel zeros. A stronger, arithmetically decisive version: under RH (or GRH), #{gamma_n <= T : normalized gap < 1/2 - eps} >> T log T for some eps > 0. Via Conrey-Iwaniec this gives an effective class-number bound h(-q) >> sqrt(q)(log q)^{-A}. Secondary targets: (i) an unconditional proportion > 0.6819 of simple zeros on the line, which must go beyond Fourier support 1; (ii) more than 19/27 simple zeros under RH, for example by feeding Hejhal/Rudnick-Sarnak third-trace information into the Alpoge-Furman rank-trace certificate for simple (not merely distinct) zeros; (iii) lambda > 4 under RH; (iv) an asymptotic for the average of F(alpha) over any fixed interval [b,b+ell] with b >= 1.

## VERIF

**1. Best established theorem.** Zeta, rigorous (ball arithmetic, refereed): RH holds for every nontrivial zero with 0<gamma<=3,000,175,332,800, i.e. the lowest 12,363,153,437,138 zeros (Platt-Trudgian, BLMS 53 (2021), Theorem 1). Rigorous simplicity and high-precision isolation (+-2^-102) are certified only to 3.0610046e10, covering 103,800,788,359 zeros (Platt 2017, Theorem 5.1). Floating-point and unrefereed: Gourdon's 10^13 zeros (H about 2.44e12), local windows of 2e9 zeros at zero #10^24 (Gourdon), and more than 50,000 zeros in 200+ windows up to zero #10^36 (Bober-Hiary). A window of about 1.4e6 zeros near t about 1e28 is certified by Hiary-Ireland-Kyi, conditional on the accuracy of the input data. Dirichlet L-functions: GRH for all primitive chi mod q<=400,000 to height max(1e8/q, c/q+200), about 3.8e13 zeros (Platt 2016, Theorem 7.1). Central values L(1/2,chi) != 0 for q<=2e6 (Theorem 7.2). No real zeros with sigma>=1-1/(5 log q) for quadratic chi with q<=1e10 (Lu-Zaman-Zhao 2026). Higher degree: rigorous only at small heights, e.g. Maass L-functions for SL(2,Z) with r<=178 to t=30000 (Booker-Then 2018) and S5/A5 Dedekind zeta to \|Im s\|<=100 (Booker 2006).

**2. Historical improvement.** Verified count or height for zeta: Gram 1903 (10-15 zeros) -> Backlund 1914 (79) -> Hutchinson 1925 (138) -> Titchmarsh-Comrie 1936 (1,041; t<=1468) -> Turing 1953 (1,104) -> Lehmer 1956 (25,000) -> Lehman 1966 (250,000) -> Rosser-Yohe-Schoenfeld 1969 (3.5e6) -> Brent 1979 (75,000,001; t<32,585,736.4) -> 1982/1983 (2e8/3e8) -> van de Lune-te Riele-Winter 1986 (1.5e9; H=5.454e8). After that come unrefereed floating-point runs: van de Lune 2001 (1e10), ZetaGrid 2003-04 (claims 2e11-9e11 zeros, inconsistent), Gourdon 2004 (1e13 zeros, H about 2.44e12). Then rigorous runs: Platt 2017 (H=3.061e10) and Platt-Trudgian 2021 (H=3.0002e12). Factors: rigorous H grew about 98x from 2017 to 2021 and about 5500x from 1986 to 2021 (35 years, about 25%/year compounded). Platt-Trudgian exceed Gourdon's unrefereed H by 22%. No larger rigorous H was found from 2021 to Nov 2026 (5+ years without a record). GRH (Dirichlet): Rumely 1993 (q<=13 to height 1e4, about 1e7 zeros) -> Platt 2016 (q<=4e5 to 1e8/q, about 3.8e13 zeros), about 6 orders of magnitude in zeros. Turing's constant b: 0.128 (Turing) -> 0.114 (Lehman 1970) -> 0.059 (Trudgian 2011) -> 0.049 plus a 0.183 log log term (Trudgian 2016). Single-point evaluation exponent: 1/2 (Riemann-Siegel) -> 3/8 (Schoenhage) -> 1/3 (Heath-Brown/Hiary) -> 4/13 (Hiary 2011).

**3. Exact bottleneck.** For the verified height H, the bottleneck is cost that grows linearly with the number of zeros, plus the overhead of rigor. Any zero-by-zero method must detect about N(H) ~ (H/2pi)log(H/2pi) sign changes. With Odlyzko-Schoenhage/Booker FFT amortization the cost per zero is only H^{o(1)}, so total cost is about H^{1+o(1)}, and faster single-point algorithms (t^{4/13}) do not help. The rigorous run is about 725x slower per unit height than Gourdon's floating-point run, because of about 25 samples per zero instead of 1.2 and multiprecision ball arithmetic instead of hardware floats (Platt-Trudgian Section 2). The rigorous run used 7.5e6 core hours to reach 3e12. Linear extrapolation (my estimate, not from a source) gives roughly 2.6e7 core hours to reach 1e13 (about 4.31e13 zeros). Second, certification and trust: there is no certificate cheaper to check than the computation (Gourdon Section 1.1), and the 3e12 code is not permanently archived (zbMATH review). An independent rigorous replication would therefore cost as much again, and none exists. Third, a structural limit: finite verification says nothing about RH globally. S(t) is unbounded (Selberg's Omega result), and the regime where \|S(t)\| is large, which might plausibly host anomalies, lies at heights accessible only through isolated non-rigorous windows (largest observed S about 3.35 at t about 7.75e27). For GRH the cost is O_eps(q^{1+eps}) per modulus for all characters (Platt 2016, Lemma 6.1, as cited by Lu-Zaman-Zhao). The total over q<=Q at fixed height therefore grows like Q^2, which limits the modulus-height trade-off.

**4. Bottleneck type.** computational

**5. What negative results rule out.** (1) Gram-point methods alone cannot work. Gram's law and the Weak Gram law fail for a positive proportion of Gram intervals (Trudgian, Acta Arith. 148 (2011)). Backlund's \|S\|<1/2 approach must eventually fail because S(T)=Omega_{+-}((log T)^{1/3}(log log T)^{-7/3}) (Selberg, as cited in Trudgian 2011 Section 1). Rosser's rule fails infinitely often (Lehman 1970), and its empirical failure rate rises with height: 20.0M violations in the first 1e12 zeros versus 37.6M in zeros 9e12-1e13 (Gourdon 2004). So completeness must rest on Turing-type integrated-S(t) bounds or explicit-formula tests. (2) Limits of Turing's constants: Trudgian 2016 states 'it seems difficult to improve substantially' on his Theorem 1, and his 2011 method cannot push b below 0.0353; gains in a or b only reduce the number of Rosser blocks needed (already about 6 at 2pi*1e12), not the dominant cost. (3) De Bruijn-Newman: Rodgers-Tao's Lambda>=0 means further Lehmer-pair discoveries cannot improve the lower bound. Close pairs occur at GUE-predicted rates (13 pairs with delta<1e-4 against 10.96 expected to 1e13; Gourdon), so near-coincidences are not evidence against RH. (4) Complexity: no published single-point algorithm beats t^{4/13+o(1)} (Hiary 2011). Any complete verification to height H needs at least about N(H) evaluations, so the cost is linear in H up to logarithmic factors. (5) No finite H, however large, implies RH or can be turned into global evidence without a separate locality theorem. RH-dependent explicit results (e.g. Johnston 2021, Buethe 2016) become valid only for x up to about a constant times H^2 (e.g. x<=2.169e25 from H=3e12).

(b) Hardware: GPU or vectorized double-double interval arithmetic. Platt's GRH run already used SSE2 double-precision intervals, which suggests much of the 725x rigor overhead is avoidable. (c) Explicit analytic number theory: sharper explicit bounds for \|zeta(sigma+it)\| near sigma=1/2 and 1 (e.g. Hiary-Patel-Yang and later work) and a reduction of the log 4 term of Booker's Lemma 4.4, to tighten Turing windows. (d) Explicit-formula and Hadamard-product completeness tests (Buethe 2015; Hiary-Ireland-Kyi 2027) as an independent second check that needs only cheap evaluations of L'/L in the region of absolute convergence. (e) Random-matrix theory and LLL lattice reduction, to target stress regions (large \|Z\|, large \|S\|, close pairs) where floating-point methods are most fragile (Bober-Hiary). ## ZDE

**1. Best established theorem.** Classical ζ, unconditional: N(σ,T) ≤ T^{A(σ)(1−σ)+o(1)} with A(σ) given by the piecewise table in ZDE-04, unchanged in the live ANTEDB as of 2026-11-17. The pieces are Ingham 3/(2−σ) on [1/2, 7/10]; Guth–Maynard 15/(3+5σ) on [7/10, 19/25); then Ivić, TTY, Bourgain, Heath-Brown and Chen–Debruyne–Vindas pieces up to 9/10; then TTY and Pintz pieces up to 1. Consequences: - sup_σ A(σ) ≤ 30/13, attained at σ = 7/10 (GM, Ann. Math. 2026). - A(3/4) ≤ 20/9. - DH (A ≤ 2) holds for σ ≥ 25/32 (Bourgain 2000; the endpoint follows from TTY Theorem 51). - A(1−η) ≤ (3√2 + o(1))η^{1/2} as η→0 (Pintz 2023). - Arithmetic: asymptotic PNT in [x, x+x^θ] for all x when θ > 17/30, and for almost all x when θ > 2/15. Existence of primes in [x − x^{0.525}, x] (BHP 2001) is a separate sieve result.  Other L-functions: - Σ_{χ mod q} N(σ,T,χ) ≤ (qT)^{7(1−σ)/3+o(1)} (Chen–Gupta–Li, unrefereed). - GL2: T^{(5/2)(1−σ)+o(1)} (Cossaboom 2026, unrefereed). - DH for holomorphic cusp forms when σ ≥ 1407/1601 (CDV 2024, refereed).  Explicit bounds: - Ingham-1940 shape with constants, e.g. B1 = 21.77 on [0.625, 0.875] (Chourasiya–Simonič, unrefereed). - 8/3-shape KLN constants, as recomputed by Johnston–Yang. - Log-free B = 1.448 near σ = 1 (Bellotti 2025). - No explicit version of Guth–Maynard exists.

**3. Exact bottleneck.** After Guth–Maynard, the binding point is σ = 7/10, where Ingham's mean-value bound and GM's bound coincide at 30/13.  Under the TTY heuristic (Heuristic 54: A(σ) ≤ 3/τ0 needs LV(σ,τ0) ≤ 3−3σ), going below 30/13 at σ = 7/10 requires the following for 1-bounded Dirichlet polynomials of length N with T = N^{13/10} and V = N^{7/10}: a large-values bound below the mean-value bound R ≲ T N^{1−2σ} = N^{9/10}. GM's Theorem 1.1 is exactly tangent there (its terms are N^{0.6}, N^{0.8} and N^{0.9}). For σ < 7/10 nobody has ever beaten the mean value theorem.  My own reading of GM's master inequality (12.1) traces the tangency to two terms: - T·N^{9/2−7σ}. It comes from the T\|W\|^2N^{3/2−σ} part of the S3 bound (Prop. 11.2), i.e. the \|W\|^3N^{1−2σ} 'random-set' term of the additive-energy bound (Prop. 11.1) fed into Prop. 10.1. It equals the MVT term T·N^{1−2σ} exactly at σ = 7/10 for every T. - T^{4/3}N^{2−4σ}. It comes from the low-energy bound S3 ≲ T^2\|W\|^{3/2} (Prop. 10.1). It is tangent at σ = 7/10 when T = N^{6/5} and worse than the MVT for T > N^{6/5}. This is why GM fix T = N^{6/5} (Prop. 3.1) and reach larger T by Huxley subdivision, which loses a full factor of T/N^{6/5}.  So lowering sup A needs both a better energy bound for large-value sets at V ≈ N^{7/10} and a better low-energy S3 bound, or a non-subdivision route to T ≈ N^{13/10}.  The DH threshold 25/32 is set by Bourgain's dichotomy at τ ≈ 3/2. TTY's exhaustive machine optimisation of all recorded inputs could not move it.

**5. What negative results rule out.** - A(1/2) = 2 (Riemann–von Mangoldt; TTY eq. ## ZFR

**1. Best established theorem.** REFEREED, human-verified for zeta (as of 2026-11-17). (a) Classical: sigma >= 1 - 1/(5.558691 log\|t\|) for \|t\| >= 2 (Mossinghoff-Trudgian-Yang 2024, Theorem 1.3). (b) Littlewood: sigma > 1 - log log\|t\|/(21.233 log\|t\|) for \|t\| >= 3 (Yang 2024, Corollary 1.2). (c) VK: sigma >= 1 - 1/(53.989 (log\|t\|)^{2/3}(log log\|t\|)^{1/3}) for \|t\| >= 3 (Bellotti 2024, published version; arXiv v1 says 54.004). (d) Asymptotically, the constant is 48.0718 (Bellotti 2024, Theorem 1.3). (e) RH holds for \|t\| <= 3*10^12 (Platt-Trudgian 2021). The VK shape (log t)^{2/3}(log log t)^{1/3} has not been improved in any refereed work since 1958. UNREFEREED but complete specialist preprint: classical R = 4.896 for t >= 3 (Bellotti-Trudgian-Yang, arXiv:2603.21490, Theorem 1). It is partly computer-assisted, with one step only numerically checked. UNREFEREED, AI-generated claims, potentially decisive: (i) zeta and every Dirichlet L-function is zero-free in Re s > 7/8 (OpenAI, 30 Sep 2026, 199 pp.), with a Lean Comparator challenge 'riemannZeta s != 0 for 7/8 < Re s' under standard axioms. The catalogue review status is 'unchecked' and it was not replayed here. (ii) Re s > 11/12 (OpenAI, 5 Oct 2026, 49 pp., human-edited, not formalized). (iii) Parameter refinements down to 0.874886647... (Liu; Xu-Sang), conditional on OpenAI's intermediate estimates. If (i) or (ii) is correct, it subsumes every dlVP, Littlewood and VK region for zeta at all heights above the RH-verified range. It would be the quasi-Riemann hypothesis, NOT RH; RH remains open.

**2. Historical improvement.** Classical R: 30.4679 (de la Vallee Poussin 1899) -> 17.537 (1938) -> 9.65 (Stechkin 1970) -> 9.645908801 (Rosser-Schoenfeld 1975) -> 9.547897 (1977) -> 8.463 (Ford 2002; 8.464 after 2025 correction) -> 5.69693 (Kadiri 2005) -> 5.68371 (2014) -> 5.573412 (2015) -> 5.558691 (MTY 2024, refereed) -> 4.896 (BTY 2026 preprint). Overall a 5.48x wider region (refereed) or 6.22x (preprint). The last refereed step, 2015 to 2024, gained only 0.26%; the 2026 preprint step gained 11.9% in R by combining Stechkin pairing with a near-optimal Heath-Brown-type smoothing weight. VK all-\|t\| constant: 990 (Cheng 2000) -> 57.54 (Ford 2002) -> 55.241 (MTY 2024) -> 53.989 (Bellotti 2024), i.e. 6.2% since 2002 (thesis claim 51.34 unverified). Asymptotic VK constant: 49.13 (2002) -> 49.08 (2022) -> 48.1588 (2024) -> 48.0718 (2024), i.e. 2.2% in 22 years. Littlewood: first explicit constant 21.233 (2024); thesis claim 19.62. Shape: dlVP 1899 -> Littlewood 1922 -> Chudakov 1938 ((log t)^{3/4+eps}) -> VK 1958; frozen for 68 years. 2026: claimed jump to a fixed half-plane (11/12, then 7/8, refined to about 0.87489), a qualitative change if verified.

**3. Exact bottleneck.** Three different bottlenecks. (1) Explicit classical constant: the zero detector is nearly saturated. BTY report efficiency c = 1.98 against a hypothetical maximum of 2 for their smoothing family, and more RH verification height helps very little (MTY Table 4: 5.5587 -> 5.5473 for T0 from 3*10^12 to 10^15). Remaining gains come from the crossover with the Littlewood region (BTY Theorem 2: 4.8594 if Littlewood 19.62) and from trigonometric-polynomial degree. (2) VK constants and shape: everything flows from Richert's bound \|zeta(sigma+it)\| <= A t^{B(1-sigma)^{3/2}} log^{2/3} t. The exponent 3/2 (hence the 2/3 in the region) comes from Vinogradov's mean value method. Explicit B (4.43795) is limited by explicit Vinogradov-integral constants: optimal-exponent VMVT (efficient congruencing, decoupling) has constants far too large to insert (Bellotti pp.8-9). A better shape needs cancellation in sum_{n~N} n^{-it} for N = exp((log t)^{2/3+delta}), which no exponential-sum method provides. (3) Quasi-RH: if OpenAI's route stands, the whole question becomes verification. For the 11/12 argument the single load-bearing statement is Proposition 3.1 (p.11): sum_{N(u) <= D^{1+theta}} \|A_u(D)\|^2 << D^{2+theta+eps}, i.e. square-root cancellation on average for sextically twisted Mobius sums over Z[omega]. The exponent itself is limited structurally: the trivializing rows u = p^6 number only about H^{1/6}, and Liu proves 0.874957... optimal within the 'exponent comparison system' of the 7/8 method.

**4. Bottleneck type.** mixed

**5. What negative results rule out.** (a) Diamond-Montgomery-Vorhauer (Math. Ann. 334, 2006) and Broucke (arXiv:2507.13780, Theorem 1.6): Beurling zeta functions with N(x) = Ax + O(x^{1/2+eps}) can have infinitely many zeros on sigma = 1 - f(log\|t\|), with none to the right, for any strictly convex f of index -alpha (0 < alpha <= 1) or slowly varying (e.g. 1/log u). So Euler product plus integer regularity gives nothing beyond the dlVP region. VK needs additive structure of n^{it}; any quasi-RH proof needs more again (OpenAI uses cubic/sextic reciprocity and metaplectic theta series). (b) Landau's extremal constant V (Arestov-Kondrat'ev 1990; Mossinghoff-Trudgian 2015 Theorem 2: 34.468305 < V < 34.4889920009) caps the Landau/Stechkin polynomial method at R0 of about 9.5. Smoothing (Heath-Brown 1992, Kadiri 2005) was needed to go below, and BTY place their smoothing within 1% of the hypothetical limit of that framework. (c) Bellotti 2024: B cannot be improved substantially by parameter choices alone, and current optimal-exponent VMVT constants (Steiner) cannot be inserted into Ford's argument. (d) Ford 2002: improving Stechkin's region for all \|t\| >= 3 via the midline method is impossible at T0 = 5.45*10^8, because 6 log log t is about 0.895 log t there. (e) MTY Section 10: an inductive union-of-regions scheme would reach VK constant 52.74, but only if zeros could be excluded from small boxes at heights exp(4*10^4) to exp(5*10^7), and raw computation and zero-density estimates cannot do this. (f) Liu (arXiv:2610.12234, Section 34): 0.874957... is optimal within the comparison system of the OpenAI 7/8 method, so going lower needs a genuinely new estimate. (g) Errata to track: Cheng-Graham Lemma 3 is false and unfixable (Ford v5, footnote 1). It invalidated the 3 t^{1/6} log t midline bound and the claimed Trudgian and Hiary improvements; the repair is Hiary-Patel-Yang's 0.618 t^{1/6} log t. Ford's own Theorems 2-4 were renumerated in 2025 (Theorem 4: 8.463 -> 8.464), while MTY and Yang quote the older Theorem 3 numerics.
