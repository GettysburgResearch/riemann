## M1. The full rank-three reflection group has a short exact normal form

Let

\[
 C=3I_3-\delta\delta^{\mathsf T},\qquad
 \delta=(1,1,1)^{\mathsf T},\qquad
 S_i x=x-(Cx)_i e_i.
\]

Thus

\[
S_1=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1&1\\0&1&0\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
S_2=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\1&-1&1\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad
S_3=\begin{pmatrix}1&0&0\\0&1&0\\1&1&-1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

For a row vector \(v\), write \(T_v=I+\delta v\). Define

\[
 \Lambda=\{v\in\mathbb Z^3:\ v\delta=0,
                    \ v_1\equiv v_2\equiv v_3\pmod3\}.
\]

Then every element of \(G=\langle S_1,S_2,S_3\rangle\) has the unique form

\[
 \boxed{T_v h,\qquad v\in\Lambda,\quad
 h\in G_0=\{I,S_1,S_2,S_1S_2,S_2S_1,S_1S_2S_1\}.}       \tag{M1}
\]

In particular \(G\cong\Lambda\rtimes S_3\), and the translation lattice is
generated by \(a=(2,-1,-1)\) and \(b=(-1,2,-1)\). It has index three in
\(\{v\in\mathbb Z^3:v\delta=0\}\).

**Proof.** Each \(S_i\delta=\delta\), \(S_i^2=I\), and
\((S_1S_2)^3=I\). Direct multiplication gives the six distinct elements of
\(G_0\); their action on \(\mathbb R^3/\mathbb R\delta\) is faithful.
Furthermore, with \(c=(-1,-1,2)=-a-b\),

\[
 S_3=T_c(S_1S_2S_1),\qquad
 S_1S_2S_3S_2=T_a.                                    \tag{M2}
\]

The translations add: \(T_vT_z=T_{v+z}\), since \(v\delta=z\delta=0\).
Conjugation by \(h\) sends \(T_v\) to \(T_{vh^{-1}}\). The lattice is
invariant under right multiplication by \(S_1,S_2\): the distinguished entry
changes from \(v_i\) to \(-v_i\), and each other entry to \(v_j+v_i\).
The entries still sum to zero and are congruent modulo three.
Conjugating \(T_c\) by \(S_1\) and \(S_2\) supplies \(T_{-b}\) and
\(T_{-a}\), respectively. Consequently all of \(\Lambda\) occurs, and
the displayed normal forms are closed under the three generators.
The quotient action determines \(h\) uniquely, after which \(v\) is unique.
Finally the determinant of the coordinates of \(a,b\) in the basis
\((1,0,-1),(0,1,-1)\) is three. This proves all claims. \(\square\)

This identifies the entire kernel of the finite shape action. The already
known Coxeter square is a longer translation:

\[
 (S_1S_2S_3)^2=T_{(3,0,-3)}=T_{2a+b}.
\]

## M2. Shape is finite; the common scale is sheared only by shape

Write \(x=c_0\delta+y\), where \(\delta^{\mathsf T}y=0\) and
\(c_0=(x_1+x_2+x_3)/3\). A simple reflection gives

\[
 c_0'=c_0-y_i,\qquad y_i'=-y_i,\qquad
 y_j'=y_j+y_i\quad(j\ne i).                           \tag{M3}
\]

The induced shape transformations form the six-element positive \(A_2\)
reflection group. A translation has the particularly simple formula

\[
 T_v x=x+(vy)\delta,\qquad c_0'=c_0+vy,\qquad y'=y.    \tag{M4}
\]

The form \(x^{\mathsf T}Cx=3\|y\|^2\) is preserved, so the common-scale
shear has no cost in this semidefinite quadratic form. On the balanced ray
\(x=\ell\delta\), every reflection and every translation fixes the point.

Therefore interpreting \(x_i=\log A_i\), as a prospective physical scale
coordinate, gives

\[
 (A_1,A_2,A_3)=(D,D,D)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 T_v\text{ and every }S_i\text{ leave all three lengths equal to }D. \tag{M5}
\]

No long affine word reduces a balanced physical length. This is an exact
obstruction to paying the sixth-moment core solely through a decreasing
common-scale orbit.

## M3. The transpose convention exposes a finite critical Mellin slice

The two-axis source map is
\(R_1(w_1,w_2)=(1-w_1,w_1+w_2-1/2)\).
After centering \(u_i=w_i-1/2\), this is the transpose of the root-coordinate
reflection, rather than the reflection itself. Accordingly the compatible
prospective rank-three spectral action is

\[
 u\longmapsto S_i^{\mathsf T}u,\qquad
 u_i'=-u_i,\quad u_j'=u_j+u_i\ (j\ne i).              \tag{M6}
\]

The common spectral coordinate
\(\eta=u_1+u_2+u_3=\sum_iw_i-3/2\) is invariant. A translation acts as

\[
 \boxed{T_v^{\mathsf T}u=u+\eta v^{\mathsf T}.}       \tag{M7}
\]

There is a word-order convention here: transposition reverses a word.
The same ordered word in the spectral generators \(S_i^{\mathsf T}\)
represents \(g^{-\mathsf T}\), because each root generator is an
involution. Thus a physical word \(T_v\) corresponds to the spectral
matrix \(T_{-v}^{\mathsf T}\). In (M7)–(M8), \(v\) labels the displayed
spectral matrix \(T_v^{\mathsf T}\) independently. Both signs occur in
\(\Lambda\), and the conclusions below do not depend on this sign choice.

Hence on the plane \(\eta=0\), all translations act identically and the
entire matrix action has only six transformations. This is a genuine finite
algebraic mechanism available specifically on the critical common-Mellin
slice \(\sum_iw_i=3/2\).

For a physical Mellin factor \(\prod_i A_i^{w_i}\), the exact translation
multiplier is

\[
 \boxed{\prod_i A_i^{\eta v_i}
       =\exp\left(\eta\sum_i v_i\log A_i\right).}    \tag{M8}
\]

At balanced lengths this is one for every complex \(\eta\), since
\(\sum_i v_i=0\). At \(\eta=0\) it is one at every collection of lengths.
For an unbalanced rectangle and real \(\eta\), the translations \(v\)
and \(-v\) have reciprocal multipliers. Thus the whole translation group
cannot have a uniform strict contraction from this scalar either.

**Analytic boundary.** These statements do not shift a Dirichlet-series
contour onto \(\eta=0\). They do not show that a completed affine series
exists, that it is regular there, or that its local scattering operators act
trivially when their spectral coordinate action is trivial. Nontrivial
scattering can survive a matrix action with a kernel. A finite critical-slice
adapter would have to supply continuation, residues, moving-conductor bounds,
and the operator action on that slice. None of them is supplied by (M7).

## M4. The actual long-dual row cannot be paid by a missing scale change

The inspected two-axis A2 packet explicitly has the leading fourth-moment

\[
 L=D^2,\quad H_{\rm original}=D^{1+\theta},\quad
 \mathcal H\asymp D^{3-\theta},\quad F=1,
 \qquad\frac{\mathcal H}{LF}\asymp D^{1-\theta}.      \tag{M9}
\]

That primitive-conductor calculation is independent of the prospective
rank-three geometry. An arithmetic completion or a scale transformation
which fixes the balanced lengths and does not prove a change of row conductor
leaves the excess \(D^{1-\theta}\) unpaid. In particular there is no scalar
geometric factor here which changes it to a strict positive gap.

Equation (M9) is the source's two-axis initialization. It is not being asserted
as a derived first-Poisson row length for a new rank-three completion. Obtaining
the latter would require the literal higher-order row-Poisson calculation.

There is a second source-specific warning. The PR #915 reunited theta Euler

 v=w+r-1=\tfrac12+z+t,

and scalar
\(A^{v-1}[(Nk)^2/(cAB)]^t\). Its principal pole is \(v=1\), while the

## M5. Exact block fusion preserves every triangle edge

There is nevertheless a different exact reduction which does not use the
infinite reflection group. Let \(k\ge2\), partition the factor axes into two
nonempty blocks \(I,J\), and define, on squarefree ideals,

\[
 d_I(R)=\sum_{\prod_{i\in I}n_i=R}
                \prod_{i\in I}W_i(Nn_i/X_i),\qquad
 d_J(S)=\sum_{\prod_{j\in J}n_j=S}
                \prod_{j\in J}W_j(Nn_j/X_j).
\]

For pairwise coprime squarefree original factor axes outside a fixed bad-prime
set and a moving exclusion \(q_0\), the exact Gauss row polynomial is

\[
\boxed{
 \sum_{n_1\cdots n_k\ {m squarefree}\atop(n_1\cdots n_k,q_0)=1}
 a_\xi(n_1\cdots n_k)\chi_{n_1\cdots n_k}(h)
 \chi_{n_1\cdots n_k}(f)^4\prod_iW_i(Nn_i/X_i)
 =\sum_{RS\ {m squarefree}\atop(RS,q_0)=1}
 a_\xi(RS)\chi_{RS}(h)\chi_{RS}(f)^4 d_I(R)d_J(S).
}                                                               \tag{M10}
\]

The two aggregate variables retain the exact cubic A2 edge, because

\[
 a_\xi(RS)=a_\xi(R)a_\xi(S)\kappa_S(R)^{-1},\qquad
 \prod_{i\in I,j\in J}\kappa_{n_j}(n_i)^{-1}
       =\kappa_S(R)^{-1}.                              \tag{M11}
\]

All within-block edges are already absorbed into \(a_\xi(R)\) and
\(a_\xi(S)\). Both row and auxiliary nonunit zeros remain in (M10).

**Proof.** Group the finite sum by \(R=\prod_{i\in I}n_i\) and
\(S=\prod_{j\in J}n_j\). Their squarefreeness makes the within-block
factorizations pairwise coprime automatically. The complete coefficient and
the two characters depend only on \(RS\), so the remaining weights factor
into the two displayed divisor sums. The Gauss CRT identity gives (M11).
This is a bijective finite regrouping, not an inequality. \(\square\)

For fixed \(k\) and bounded smooth tests, the divisor bound gives
\(|d_I(R)d_J(S)|\ll_\epsilon (NRS)^\epsilon\) and coefficient square mass
\(\ll_\epsilon (\prod_iX_i)D^\epsilon\) on polynomially bounded scales.
Thus block fusion introduces no new power loss in coefficient size. For a
balanced three-axis core, one may use aggregate lengths \((D^2,D)\).

**What remains unpaid.** The aggregate weights are arithmetic divisor
convolutions, not a fixed smooth function of \((NR/D^2,NS/D)\). The inspected
classical A2 continuation theorem does not automatically permit these weights
inside its coefficient family. Completing or projecting the two aggregate
variables also produces shifted divisor-colored weights, which must be
carried through the actual analytic transformation. An A2 centered signed
bilinear estimate stable under this precise divisor-coloring would control
the fused higher moment; a theorem for one fixed smooth two-axis profile
does not. Fusion relocates the triangle interaction into an explicit weight
class and preserves it exactly.

## M6. Result and a focused next contract

The infinite affine orbit can be separated completely into a rank-two
translation lattice and six shapes. Its critical common-Mellin plane has a
finite coordinate action, but balanced scale gives exactly unit translation
multiplier and no conductor savings. This rules out the proposed contraction
shortcut while identifying a precise critical-slice analytic question.

An independent exact block-fusion identity reduces any fixed-order coprime
Gauss core to a two-axis A2 coefficient with explicit divisor-colored weights.
Two concrete remaining contracts are therefore available:

1. A moving-twist affine completion with controlled scattering and residues
   on \(\sum_iw_i=3/2\), including the source's centered signed off diagonal.
2. A two-axis centered signed A2 estimate uniform under the divisor-coloring
   and shifted weights in (M10), at the actual primitive-conductor row range.

Neither contract is established here. The existing generalized moment and RH
gaps remain open.
