# All-row quadratic Möbius moments: source comparison

This is a targeted literature check, not an independent verification of the [density-assisted bootstrap](2026-10-29-quadratic-inverse-bootstrap/README.md) and not a priority claim. Its displayed conditional target uses all integer rows, including squares and imprimitive quadratic characters, whereas much of the inverse-moment literature averages only primitive quadratic characters.

* Gao and Zhao, [*Mean squares of quadratic twists of the Möbius function*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09241), J. Number Theory 247 (2023), Theorem 1.1, assume GRH and study a smoothed second moment of μ(n)χ_{8d}(n), where d is positive, odd and squarefree. Their asymptotic has main order XY and error O(X^(1/2+ε)Y^(3/2+ε)+XY^(1/2+ε)), giving an asymptotic when Y≪X^(1−ε). The row d is restricted to be squarefree and χ_{8d} is primitive; it omits the square-label component of the all-integer family. The smoothed inverse polynomial is otherwise very close to the one at issue here.
  Their Lemma 2.6 states, under GRH, a uniform reciprocal bound `|L(1/2+δ+it,χ)|^(−1)≪_{δ,ε}(q(1+|t|))^ε` for Dirichlet χ modulo q. This **does** imply an all-integer bound as a straightforward deduction, although it is not their stated moment theorem: for each 1≤u≤Y, the character `(u/·)` on odd integers is induced by a character of modulus O(Y), and its finite missing Euler factors cost (YD)^ε. Mellin inversion of a fixed smooth compact-annular W on Re s=1/2+δ then gives `M_u(D)≪(YD)^ε`, after choosing δ and the preliminary loss in terms of ε and using rapid Mellin decay. Thus GRH yields `A(Y,D)≪_ε Y(YD)^ε`, with no restriction D≤Y in this expression. This is the θ=1/2 endpoint of the proposed kind of all-row upper bound; the density-assisted statement at θ>1/2 and the fixed-point obstruction are separate questions.
* Toma, [*On the moments of averages of quadratic twists of the Möbius function*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18778v1), Research in Number Theory 11 (2025), Theorem 1.2, treats the same positive odd squarefree d family with a **sharp** n-cutoff and obtains unconditionally an XY main term with error O(XY^(7/12))+O(X^(1/2+ε)Y^(2+ε))+O(X^(1/2)Y^(5/2)(log Y)^2), for Y≪X^(1/3−ε). Theorem 1.3 treats higher moments. Neither theorem sums over d k² as physical rows.
* Čech, [*The ratios conjecture for real Dirichlet characters and multiple Dirichlet series*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.04409), Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 377 (2024), Theorem 1.2 and §§3, 6, 9, explicitly averages L^(2)(1/2+α,χ_u)/L^(2)(1/2+β,χ_u) over **all odd integers u**, with χ_u=(·/u), including nonprimitive and square rows, under GRH and the paper's fixed-shift restrictions. Its §9 expands u into a squarefree kernel and a square and keeps deleted Euler factors. For target characters (u/n), reciprocity relates the two orientations after splitting fixed classes modulo 4, introducing a fixed twist in the n-variable when needed. This is important direct prior art on enlarging the same type of quadratic family and handling imprimitive Euler factors. It is a one-over-one L-ratio at prescribed shifts, not a two-denominator second moment of a smoothed reciprocal Dirichlet polynomial, and it does not state the θ-dependent bootstrap bound.
* Gao and Zhao, [*Ratios conjecture for quadratic twists of modular L-functions*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40993-024-00544-3), §3.1, similarly average over all odd indices and use the decomposition n=d h² and the finite products at primes dividing h; their Theorem 1.1 is GRH-conditional and applies to a one-over-one ratio of GL₂ twists. It is an imprimitive-family method comparison, not the Möbius second moment here.
* Corrigan and Zhao, [Theorem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.00260), bound the aggregate zero count over primitive **quadratic** characters of conductor at most Q by `(QT)^ε(Q³T⁴)^((1−σ)/(2−σ))` for T sufficiently large. This supplies the Q exponent `H(σ)=3(1−σ)/(2−σ)` and height exponent `4(1−σ)/(2−σ)` used in the conditional bootstrap. It counts primitive characters, and the k² multiplicity and deleted Euler factors must still be handled separately. In particular, the source does not state the resulting all-row inverse bound.

The square sector already contains an ordinary Mertens obstruction: for u=k², the χ_u sum is the odd Möbius sum with primes dividing k deleted. An upper bound of order Y^(1+ε) when D=Y^r has the zeta-zero implication stated in [the power-row note](2026-10-29-power-row-spectral-boundary/README.md). Classical Mertens/RH equivalences therefore give the conceptual obstruction, although the exact single-test, all-row scale threshold needs the separate square-sector argument. The target `Y+sqrt(Y)D^(2θ−1)` remains a proposed derivation with its own uniform reciprocal-L, height and zero-bin hypotheses. Searches so far have not identified it as a quoted theorem; this is not proof of novelty.

## Caution about a frequently repeated uniformity claim

The introductions of Gao–Zhao and Toma's thesis attribute to Ye a GRH bound `M(x,χ)≪sqrt(x) exp((log x)^{3/5+o(1)})` uniformly for q and χ. If read with no restriction whatsoever on q relative to x, this statement is false: select a prime q≡5 mod 8, larger than x, such that (q/p)=−1 for every odd prime p≤x, using the Chinese remainder theorem and Dirichlet's theorem. The primitive quadratic character χ_q then has χ_q(p)=−1 for each p≤x, including 2, and `Σ_{n≤x} μ(n)χ_q(n)=Σ_{n≤x} μ(n)^2≫x`. The construction is unconditional and remains valid under GRH.

Ye's primary source [*Bounding sums of the Möbius function over arithmetic progressions*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.7326), abstract and Theorem 1.1, concerns a **single progression** M(x;q,a) uniformly in q,a. Its Lemma 5.3 bounds a sum over characters; it does not imply the displayed pointwise bound for every χ and unrestricted q. This observation does not refute Gao–Zhao's separate GRH reciprocal-L lemma or their squarefree-family moment theorem. For the bootstrap, use an explicitly stated reciprocal-L estimate in the needed conductor/height range, not the overbroad introductory paraphrase.
