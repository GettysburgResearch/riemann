# ChainF50 count-face reconnaissance

**Status:** Floating-point exploration of candidate scalar guards plus a separate exact rational comparison at one point. The scan does not prove a new window, transport theorem, or zero-free region. The source triples are parsed from `ChainF50/Chain.lean` in the locally pinned PR94 checkout; formulas and the 15 admission guards are transcribed from `Return/Formulas.lean`. The baseline uses the five original branch formulas only on their 31 existing windows.

On the 301×201 grid `0≤δ≤3/4`, `0≤p≤1/2`, evaluate all 130 faces from `boot` and `source_01` through `source_129`. For the new uncapped branches, require every guard to exceed `10^-12`. For capped branches, choose the largest numerically feasible marked cap by intersecting the affine guard inequalities, with a `10^-5` margin below the supplied global source cap and the source's own safety losses. Existing branch admission uses the original windows and floating tolerance `10^-12`. Each proposed new face is tested **globally** under its pointwise guards; applying it would require establishing the corresponding new windows and the generic source interface in the formal return, not merely copying existing window certificates.

With θ=0.8738702 for the old count's κ, 25,372 of 60,501 grid points improve numerically over the window-restricted baseline. The largest found count-exponent gain is about 0.00284328 at `(δ,p)=(0.47,0.435)`, from `source_129_C`; the gain there reflects extending the **existing final face beyond its current windows**. The boot capped branch wins at 7,607 grid points after cap retuning, and `source_121_cap` improves the count by about 0.00005066 at `(0.4,0.48)`. These are representative points, not certified regions, and improvements in count need not yield an improved final return.

At the fixed-return obstruction `(δ,p)=(223/500,1/2)`, the baseline count is `137478360780643/225606975000000 ≈ 0.6093710568153`, and the full grid/face scan finds no gain; it also finds no gain at `(0.446,0.499)`, `(0.446,0.49)`, `(0.445,0.5)` or `(0.45,0.5)`. In the finer fixed-cap scan of `[0.44,0.45]×[0.49,0.5]` there was no gain at any of 201² sampled points; the retuned-cap statement at that resolution has not been separately run.

At the obstruction point, `check_chain_point.py` uses exact fractions for all 130 source triples. The uncapped count is minimized by `source_129`; `source_128` is next and is larger by `2919281589474229/1022756395347637500000 ≈ 2.854327387×10^-6`. For each source independently, allow its capped parameter z to range over **all real numbers** and discard all admission guards except the weaker necessary inequality `MN≤(B−Λz)DJ/2`. The exact minimum of the capped count under that relaxed inequality equals that source's uncapped count. Thus no one of these 130 faces, even under this generous cap relaxation and pointwise global availability, beats the current final-face C count at this one point. The exact arithmetic check is not itself a Lean theorem; independently verifying source transcription remains a separate task. The conclusion does not cover another family of source faces, a different count formula, or a new detector.
