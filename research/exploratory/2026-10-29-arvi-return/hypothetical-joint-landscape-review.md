The note and `hypothetical_joint_scan.py` were checked against PR #94's `Return/Formulas.lean`, `Return/Constants.lean`, and `ReturnD/TransportGeometry.lean`, and against the PR #93 joint-count formula and its source gates. The result is floating-point reconnaissance of an explicitly hypothetical estimate, not a new estimate, complete-domain certificate or zero-free theorem.

The hypothetical branch `3(1−s)(1−δ)/(3−δ−2s)` is the PR #93 algebraic `jointCountExponent` after mapping its zero-location parameter ν to δ and its selected squared-response slope to `s=2δp`. The count at small s is **not** supplied by that source: the apparent bottleneck has s≈0.03687 and a≈0.64881, below both the actual selected-slope and zero-location gates. Physical compatibility with PR #94's row subsets, masks and early mesh is a further hypothesis. The old and admitted C/capC/weakC7/capP branch formulas, guard arithmetic, window ranges, `Ebase` and physical conductor line match the source definitions. The code uses floating-point guard tolerance 10^(−12), so it cannot certify exact admission or strict inequalities.

The active row term is the old/joint crossing on the new top edge; no source C-window is active at such a small p. At the nearby exact rational point δ=297617/10^6, p=61939/10^6, the two counts are approximately 0.7720559259 and 0.7720559355, and s=0.036868198726. This is consistent with the reported optimizer, not an independent global-max proof.
