# Cyclic subgroups and possible real factors

Status: proposed classical-input transfer; independent review needed. This is a derivation from cited theorems, not a priority claim.

Scope: arbitrary finite Galois extensions of number fields and cyclic subgroups of order at least three. It gives a low-height region with at most one real linear-character exception; it does not resolve that exception or prove a fixed half-plane.

Inputs: [Ahn–Kwon's](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jnt.2014.06.008) published `15.10` nonreal Hecke zero-free theorem and real simple-zero theorem; [Das–Gaba–Lee–Savalia–Wong, Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.19319) for the fixed field below; Artin factorization, conductor–discriminant and [Stark's low-order holomorphy theorem](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00605-025-02060-7). The Ahn–Kwon statement was checked in the published abstract, not reproved.

## Transfer

Let `K/k` be finite Galois, let `H` be cyclic of order `m>=3`, put `g=gcd(m,2)`, `M=K^H`, `M0=K^(H^2)`, and `D=|disc K|`. Assume `[M0:Q]>=7`. Then `[M0:M]=g`, `[K:M0]=m/g`, and `[K:M]=m`.

In the region

    Re(s)>1-phi(m)/(15.10 log D),    |Im(s)|<(1/3)tan(pi/8),

every irreducible Artin L-function of dimension at least two is holomorphic and nonzero. So is every one-dimensional Artin L-function whose character is nonreal. Away from `s=1`, at most one simple real zero of the remaining real one-dimensional factors is possible. At `s=1` the trivial factor has a pole, while all nontrivial irreducible factors are holomorphic and nonzero by the classical Artin nonvanishing theorem.

For each character `chi` of `H` with order `d>=3`, write `Q_chi` for the norm of the finite conductor of its associated **primitive** Hecke character over `M` and `A_chi=d_M Q_chi`. The Hecke character corresponds by class field theory to the finite Galois character itself, so has exact order `d`. The `phi(d)` characters with kernel `ker chi` are its powers with exponents coprime to `d`; they have the same finite conductor, since they have identical kernels on each local ramification group. The conductor–discriminant formula applied to `K^(ker chi)/M`, followed by the tower formula, gives `A_chi^(m phi(d)/d) <= D`. Since `d|m`, `phi(d)/d >= phi(m)/m`, hence `A_chi <= D^(1/phi(m))`.

Artin factorization gives

    zeta_K/zeta_M0 = product_(chi in H^, ord chi>2) L_M(s,chi).

All factors on the right are entire, nonreal finite-order Hecke L-functions and are zero-free in the displayed region by Ahn–Kwon. Thus `ord_s zeta_K = ord_s zeta_M0` there away from `s=1`, with signed orders.

As `d_M0^(m/g)<=D` and `phi(m)<=m/g`, the displayed region is contained in the degree-at-least-seven Das et al. region `Re(s)>=1-1/(11.51910 log d_M0), |Im(s)|<=1`. That theorem permits only one real zero. Such a zero is simple by Ahn–Kwon's separate real-zero theorem for `zeta_M0`, whose `1/(2 log d_M0)` horizontal width contains the Das region and whose height condition is automatic on the real line. Consequently `ord_s zeta_K<=1` everywhere in the displayed region except its pole at one.

Stark's theorem makes every irreducible Artin factor holomorphic at each `s!=1` with `ord_s zeta_K<=1`. In the Galois zeta factorization, orders are therefore nonnegative integers, weighted by representation dimension. A zero of a factor of dimension at least two would force `ord_s zeta_K>=2`, impossible. A zero of a nonreal linear factor would have to occur at the single real zero allowed for `zeta_K`, where its distinct conjugate factor has a zero as well; again the total order is at least two. This proves the stated exception structure.

The case `m` odd has `M0=M`; the even case removes the quadratic real character from the quotient automatically, so a hypothetical common zero of `zeta_M` and that quadratic factor cannot create a double zero of `zeta_M0` in the low-order rectangle. The argument does not assert that either real factor is separately zero-free. If `G` has no quotient of order two, every nontrivial irreducible Artin factor is zero-free throughout the region; the remaining possible real carrier is the trivial character of `G`.

## Symmetric groups

Let `G=S_n` for `n>=5`, let `m=g(n)` be the maximum order of a permutation, and choose `H` generated by a permutation of that order. Every permutation is a product of disjoint cycles of lengths `l_i`, so `m<=product l_i<=2^(n-1)`. Therefore

    [M0:Q] >= [G:H] = n!/m >= n!/2^(n-1) >= 7,

and the theorem applies. The only real one-dimensional characters of `S_n` are the trivial and sign characters. Thus every nontrivial irreducible other than sign is zero-free in the region with width `phi(g(n))/(15.10 log D)`; the sign factor has at most the one simple real exception. In the case `k=Q`, the trivial factor `zeta(s)` has no real zeros in `(0,1)`, so any exceptional point must lie in the sign factor.

The coefficient `phi(g(n))` grows rapidly: [Landau's asymptotic](https://www.numdam.org/item/JTNB_2008__20_3_625_0/) gives `log g(n) ~ sqrt(n log n)`, and the standard lower bound `phi(q) >> q/log log q` gives

    log phi(g(n)) = (1+o(1)) sqrt(n log n).

This concerns the coefficient in a bound measured against the full Galois-field discriminant `D`; it does not by itself imply a wide region as `n` grows, because the discriminant also changes with the field and degree.

## Optional field-specific width

Every character of `H` is a power of a faithful character `lambda`, so its finite conductor divides that of `lambda`. All `phi(m)` faithful characters have the same finite conductor. The conductor–discriminant formula therefore gives

    A_chi^phi(m) <= D/d_M    for every chi of H.

One may replace the displayed width by the minimum of `phi(m)/(15.10 log(D/d_M))` and `1/(11.51910 log d_M0)`, retaining the height restriction. The intersection with the second term is essential; the first term alone need not be contained in the cited low-order region, especially for even `m=4`.
