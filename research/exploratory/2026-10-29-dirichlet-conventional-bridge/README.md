The precise Lean statement quantifies over every natural q with `[NeZero q]`, every `DirichletCharacter ℂ q`, and every complex s with `θ<s.re`, subject to `¬(χ=1 ∧ s=1)`. Its conclusion is `DirichletCharacter.LFunction χ s ≠ 0`. Since q is natural, `NeZero q` means exactly q≥1. It includes both primitive and imprimitive characters; there is no conductor, parity, reality, or height restriction.

Mathematically this is the usual all-positive-moduli Dirichlet zero-free half-plane, with the principal pole omitted. In particular the conventional Riemann zeta function has no zeros in `Re s>θ`, apart from the fact that s=1 is its pole and is not a point of its domain as a meromorphic function. ## Source definitions and conventional comparison

* In mathlib [`DirichletCharacter/Basic.lean`](/workspace/scratch/qproject/.lake/packages/mathlib/Mathlib/NumberTheory/DirichletCharacter/Basic.lean), `DirichletCharacter ℂ q` is `MulChar (ZMod q) ℂ`: a character of the residue-class units, extended by zero at nonunits. `apply_eq_zero_iff` identifies this with the usual gcd condition. Every conventional character modulo q corresponds to such a finite unit-group character, and conversely.
* In [`LSeries/DirichletContinuation.lean`](/workspace/scratch/qproject/.lake/packages/mathlib/Mathlib/NumberTheory/LSeries/DirichletContinuation.lean), `LFunction χ` is `ZMod.LFunction χ`. The latter is the finite Hurwitz-zeta combination in [`LSeries/ZMod.lean`](/workspace/scratch/qproject/.lake/packages/mathlib/Mathlib/NumberTheory/LSeries/ZMod.lean). The theorem `LFunction_eq_LSeries` identifies it with `Σ_{n≥1}χ(n)n^(−s)` for `Re s>1`. `differentiableAt_LFunction` gives holomorphy away from the principal pole and `differentiable_LFunction` gives an entire function for nonprincipal χ. The usual uniqueness of analytic continuation therefore identifies it with the conventional Dirichlet L-function on the whole relevant pole-deleted half-plane. The raw `LSeries` is a separate totalized definition and should not be substituted for the analytic continuation below Re s=1.
* The candidate itself ranges over all levels, so no primitive reduction is needed to interpret its conclusion. For comparison, mathlib has `primitiveCharacter`, `conductor_dvd_level`, and `changeLevel_primitiveCharacter` in `DirichletCharacter/Basic.lean`; `LFunction_changeLevel` in `DirichletContinuation.lean` identifies the imprimitive function with its inducing primitive function times the usual finitely many Euler deletions, including s=1 for nonprincipal χ. Each factor `1−χ*(p)p^(−s)` is nonzero for Re s>0 because |χ*(p)|≤1 and p>1. Thus the primitive and imprimitive formulations have the same zeros in the strip.
* For a principal character modulo q, `LFunctionTrivChar_eq_mul_riemannZeta` gives ζ(s) times `∏_{p|q}(1−p^(−s))` for s≠1. `LFunctionTrivChar_residue_one` gives its actual simple-pole residue `∏_{p|q}(1−p^(−1))`, which is positive. The candidate's `hpole` explicitly excludes exactly this pole case and does not exclude s=1 for nonprincipal χ.

## Modulus one and the pole's totalized value

Mathlib's `LFunction_modOne_eq` states equality of functions `LFunction χ = riemannZeta` for every character modulo one. The one-element ring ZMod 1 has 0=1, so the unique character takes the value 1 on its sole residue class, including the residue of the integer 0. This is harmless: `LSeries.term` separately defines its n=0 term to be zero. Thus the remaining positive terms give the standard zeta series.

The pole needs particular care. Lean functions `ℂ→ℂ` are total; their value at a meromorphic pole is an arbitrary convention, not the value of the conventional meromorphic function. In fact mathlib's [`Harmonic/ZetaAsymp.lean`](/workspace/scratch/qproject/.lake/packages/mathlib/Mathlib/NumberTheory/Harmonic/ZetaAsymp.lean) proves `riemannZeta_one = (γ−log(4π))/2` and `riemannZeta_one_ne_zero`. This finite *totalized value* is not the conventional pole and is not the Laurent constant of ζ at 1. The candidate avoids any ambiguity by excluding s=1 when χ=1; mathlib's `riemannZeta_residue_one` separately records the genuine residue-one pole.

The accompanying [RiemannCorollary.lean](RiemannCorollary.lean) specializes `Candidate.bound` to q=1 and proves the zeta nonvanishing assertion with `s≠1`, using `LFunction_modOne_eq`. It was checked in the current qproject environment with `lake env lean` against the available dependency artifacts; `#print axioms Candidate.bound` printed only `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`. This verifies the small semantic specialization in that environment, but is not a fresh independent rebuild or source-level validation of the full imported analytical dependency chain and does not establish tracker admission.
