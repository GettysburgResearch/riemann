## 1. Anatomy of Lemma 18.1: which steps are specific to TWO plain factors

Recall the object (9341–9345): rows k, 0 < qk ≪ Z^m, ψ_k(n) = τ(n)χ_n(k), effective width M = m + q (q = moving-radical length of τ), A = n1 + n2 + z. The assertion (18.2) is Σ_{k∈R_z} |S(n1)S(n2)Q|^2 ≪ Z^{M+ε} in two cases: (1) z = 0, **no restriction on n1, n2** (9387–9389); (2) z > 0 and A + (6κ−1)z ≤ M (18.3).

The product S(n1)S(n2)Q is one polynomial Σ_u c(u)ψ_k(u) of length Z^A with divisor-type coefficient (the allocated convolution A_C, A_D of (18.21)). Listing the steps and their k-dependence (k = number of plain factors):

**k-agnostic steps (everything uses only the total length A and the "product of the same kind" coefficient class):**
* first row Poisson (18.21), 9843–9900: zero frequency = powerful products of the two full index products, count O(X^{1+ε}) for norm O(X^2) → O(Z^{m+ε}) for any k (9843–9850);
* Gauss-row norms N_C, N_D and the Cauchy–Schwarz in h (9913–9927); target bound (18.23) and the common-support ledger (18.24) (prime-by-prime, independent of k);
* amplifier h ↦ hp^6 (18.28)–(18.29), 10020–10060;
* second transform (18.30), diagonal (18.31), nominal widths (18.32), child envelope (18.33), the excess ledger (18.38), greedy slot removal (18.39) — all affine in the aggregate length list (18.51), none mentions the number of plain factors;
* exceptional-row count (18.41) Z^{(m'−f)/6} and the volume identity (18.42)–(18.44): use only the total length a0 − b2 of the product;
* Lemma 18.3 (masked rectangle cancellation): generalizes verbatim to k factors with the comparison condition ∏X_i = ∏Y_i (the k main terms c^k ϑ T^{1+it} ∏I_i(t) cancel);
* prime estimate (18.12) (pointwise κz, zero-free input) — independent of the plain factors.

**k = 2-specific steps:**
1. **Reflection reset, (18.10), lines 9573–9580** — "For z = 0, reflect each original factor whose length exceeds M/2 once … n*_i ≤ M/2 + ξ, A* = n*_1 + n*_2 ≤ M + 2ξ ≤ M + δ." With k plain factors this gives A* ≤ kM/2 + kξ, which exceeds M for k ≥ 3. This is THE k=2 identity: two Hecke reflections bring any pair of lengths to total ≤ M, i.e. to the Lindelöf-on-average range.
2. **Induction order, 9617–9640**: "Prove all zero-slot bands first… Within a zero-slot band, first prove the uncentered assertion for A ≤ 5M/6, and then the padded core (18.10). Reflection then supplies all zero-slot lengths in that band." The unrestricted zero-slot assertion is proved by reflection from the padded core (also 10616–10618: "The unrestricted zero-slot assertion follows once from the padded core by reflecting at most its two plains").
3. **Where the unrestricted zero-slot assertion is consumed**: (a) zero-slot children of every stage: the row enlargement g = J_+ + σ (10059, 10062) buys the strict width decrease M' ≤ M − σ (18.32) at the price of an excess growth (A'−M') − (A−M) ≤ σ + δ_fr,1 per stage (specialize (18.38) to w = w_o = ℓ = 0); this excess is reset only because the child is estimated by the *unrestricted* zero-slot assertion at width M' (an already completed band). (b) The greedy fallback, 10536–10541: "If the slots disappear before the affine boundary is reached, the remaining plain lengths may be arbitrary; this is exactly why the completed smaller-width zero-slot assertion includes all lengths."
4. **Comparison/centering arithmetic, 9652–9672**: L = M/4, "If one original plain length is b < L, reflect the other factor … A_comp ≤ 3M/2 − A + 2z + ξ ≤ 23M/30 + ξ"; margin M/15. The centering saving (18.50): A − 5M/6 − 2v/3 − (L−v)_+ ≤ (A−M)_+ with maximum at v = L = M/4. For k = 3 one can take comparison lengths (L', L', A−z−2L') with the comparison reflected into the proven range only if L' ≤ (A − M/6 − 2z)/4 ≤ 5M/24; the exceptional deficit then is A − 5M/6 − 2L'/3 ≥ A − 35M/36 > A − M. So for three factors the centering closes the uncentered gap only up to A ≤ 29M/30 (z = 0), not up to M.
5. **The diagonal (18.31) and (18.50) make "A ≤ M at every node" a hard wall**: the second-transform diagonal (Gauss-sum orthogonality u = v) costs exactly (A − M) + 5σ/3; the exceptional rows cost (A−M)_+ + 3σ. No node of the induction may carry an excess above o(1). Since the two Poisson transforms are essentially involutive (c = d = c2 = d2 = w = 0 gives M' = M, same lengths), the only progress mechanism is "give away σ of width per stage"; for k = 2 the σ is refunded by reflection, for k ≥ 3 it accumulates over D ≈ 2M_max/σ stages.

Everything else in Sections 18.3–18.8 goes through for k factors. So: the k=2 restriction is not spread through the proof; it is concentrated in the AFE symmetry n ↔ M − n, which is what converts "Lindelöf for total length ≤ M" into "Lindelöf for all lengths", and that unrestricted statement is the backbone (reset) of the entire finite induction.

## 2. Lemma 18.1(k): statement, what the proof gives, and the precise obstruction

**Lemma 18.1(k) (conjectural).** Same hypotheses as Lemma 18.1 with k plain factors S_{ψ_k}(n_i; W_i), A = Σ_{i≤k} n_i + z. For every ε > 0 there is a slot mesh η such that Σ_{k∈R_z} |∏_{i≤k} S(n_i) Q|^2 ≪ Z^{M+ε} whenever
   (z = 0 and Σ n_i ≤ M)  or  (z > 0 and Σ n_i + 6κ z ≤ M, β* ≤ (1+κ)/2).
(The zero-slot case can no longer be unrestricted: at n_i = M/2 it would contain Σ_k |L(1/2+it, ψ_k)|^{2k} ≪ Z^{M+ε}, the 2k-th moment of the family on the critical line, which for k ≥ 3 is not known for any family of this shape; the family has size Z^M and the critical-line polynomial length is Z^{M/2}, so km ≤ M fails already at k = 3. Coordinator's observation, recorded here: Lemma 18.1 at m = 1/2, z = 0 is itself a Lindelöf-on-average FOURTH moment of the sextic Kummer family (rows inducing in Θ excluded), beyond the large sieve's U^{4/3}; a sixth moment would be new even for Dirichlet characters.)

**Ledger for k factors.** Redoing (18.33), (18.38), (18.39) with k plain factors changes nothing: A enters only as a total; the extracted common supports c, d, c2, d2, w are allocated among k plain variables and the slots exactly as in 18.4/18.6 (the allocation identity 1_{p|∏n_i} = Σ_{J≠∅}(−1)^{|J|+1}∏_{i∈J}1_{p|n_i}, line 10300, is for any number of factors); the capacity coefficient 6κ is still forced by [pointwise cost κ per unit slot] × [ledger rate 6 per unit of width gain in (18.38)] (note B §1). So the positive-slot induction for k factors runs **as long as slots remain**, with the same mesh and same loss envelope.

**Where it breaks.** Every branch of the positive-slot induction ends either at the width floor (A ≤ M + δ by (18.3)) or in the fallback of 10536–10541: all slots removed, remaining excess e = F_act − 6κ z_act ∈ (0, 6(w+ℓ)+O(ξ)] = O(σ), remaining budget Δ_child − κ z_act ≥ e/6. The child is then a zero-slot k-fold product at width M' with A − z_act = M' + e. Needed: the zero-slot k-fold assertion with excess e at loss ≤ e/6, i.e.

   (Z_k)   Σ_{rows, width M'} |∏_{i≤k} S(n_i)|^2 ≪ Z^{M' + (Σn_i − M')_+/6 + ε}   for Σ n_i ≤ M' + O(σ).

For k = 2, (Z_2) holds with loss 0 for all lengths (reflection). For k ≥ 3 the same two-transform induction cannot prove (Z_k): its zero-slot children have excess growing by σ per stage (item 3a above) and the diagonal (18.31) forbids any node with positive excess; the centering handles only A ≤ 29M/30 (item 4). Nor can (Z_k) be obtained from the sextic large sieve: Lemma 9.1 gives Lindelöf only for Σ n_i ≤ M'/2 and loses 2e/3 (from (KD)^{2/3}) at M'/2 + e, against a budget of e/6. There is no k-dependence of the kind "works for k = 3 but not k = 4": the obstruction is the same for every k ≥ 3, and it is the reset, not the ledger.

**Minimal additional input.** Any ONE of:
 (i) the k-fold zero-slot assertion (Z_k) (Lindelöf-on-average for k-fold plain products of total length ≤ M, at all smaller widths, uniformly in masks/twists) — a 2k-th-moment statement; for k = 3 equivalent in strength (via AFE at n_i = M/2 and the M/2-reflection) to the sixth moment of the sextic family, which no method gives (the only known sixth moments, Conrey–Iwaniec–Soundararajan 2012 for Dirichlet L-functions, need a family of size Q^2 ≥ (length)^{…} and an extra t-average; Diaconu–Ion–Paşol–Popa arXiv:2607.27131 (Jul 2026), via multiple Dirichlet series, obtain only the first and second twisted moments for r-th order Hecke families, with error X^{5/6+ε} for r = 3 over Q(√−3) that they attribute to Heath-Brown's (AB)^{2/3} cubic large sieve, now known sharp (de Faveri–Dunn–Hoffstein 2026); no 2k-th moment with k ≥ 2 asymptotically);
 (ii) a second "free" reset for k-fold products, i.e. a functional equation for S^k other than Hecke's — none exists for a product of smooth partial sums of unequal lengths;
 (iii) a half-capacity variant (Σ n_i + 6κz ≤ M/2) using the large sieve for the zero-slot base: still fails at the fallback (loss 2e/3 > e/6) and is in any case useless for the application (k m ≤ 1/2 forces m ≤ 1/6).

**Why none of this matters for B (even if Lemma 18.1(k) held).** Proposition 19.2 counts each row by min(count_M(r), count_S(m)) and the rows choose (r, m) adversarially subject to r+m = t+s, s ∈ [0,1/2], m ≤ 1/2, r ≤ t (Prop 8.3 (8.3)–(8.4)). At the critical class the paper's optimum is t* = 1.124, r* = 0.716, m* = 0.409 (note B). A k-th plain moment is usable only when k m ≤ 1, i.e. m ≤ 1/k; the saving F_S(m) = max_{k: km ≤ 1}[km + 2x(1−km)/(6κ)] jumps UP as m decreases through 1/3 and the adversary simply keeps m ∈ (1/3, 1/2]. At t = 1 the min-max is attained at r = 0.62, m = 0.38 (saving 0.811, k = 2 regime); at t* it is at (0.716, 0.409). Model runs: R* = 0.667 for plain k ≤ 3, k ≤ 6, overshoot form, inverse k ≤ 3 (r* > 1/2), all combined. Coordinator's note H reaches the same conclusion. The Montgomery mechanism "raise the short witness until its length is the family size" needs moments of ALL orders (fractional k), i.e. a large-values inequality — treated next.

## 3. Large values for the inverse witness (Halász–Montgomery route)

Set D = U^r (r* ≈ 0.72), V^2 = U^{δr} = U^{0.28}, G = ‖c‖² ≍ 1 (central normalization), B = {u ≍ U sixth-power-free: |M_r(u)|² ≥ U^{δr}}, R = #B.

**(a) Duality/large-sieve bound.** R V² ≤ Σ_{u∈B}|M_r(u)|² ≤ (U + D + (UD)^{2/3})U^ε (Lemma 9.1 with a fixed row restriction, 3700–3708) ⇒ R ≪ U^{2(1+r)/3 − δr} = U^{0.867} at the critical class, and Prop 9.2's R(δ) = max(1−δ/2, 4/3−δ) = 0.944 after maximizing over r ∈ [1/2,1]. Far worse than the 2/3 the structured moments give; the (KD)^{2/3} term is sharp for general squarefree-supported coefficients, so this route is capped.

**(b) Halász–Montgomery inequality in this family.** R V² ≤ ‖c‖²(‖φ‖² + R·E) with φ_u(n) = W(n/D)χ_n(u), ‖φ‖² ≍ D, E = max_{u≠u'∈B}|Σ_n W(n/D)χ_n(u)χ̄_n(u')|. By sextic reciprocity the inner sum is a character sum of length D for n ↦ χ_n(u ū'^5), conductor ≍ N(uu') ≍ U². Pólya–Vinogradov gives E ≪ U^{1+ε} > D (useless); Burgess-type bounds for Hecke characters give E ≪ D^{1−1/k'}U^{(k'+1)/(2k'²)} which at r = 0.72 saves at most U^{0.02} (k' = 3) — negligible; so E ≈ D and the inequality reads R V² ≪ D(1 + R): nothing. Even with GRH-quality correlations E ≈ D^{1/2}U^ε the condition V² ≥ 2GE is U^{δr} ≥ U^{r/2}, i.e. **δ ≥ 1/2, a ≥ 3/4** — the classical fact that Halász–Montgomery lives in σ ≥ 3/4. Powers M_r^k (length U^{kr}, spike U^{kδr}) satisfy the same threshold δ ≥ 1/2. The critical class a_c = (1+δ_c)/2 = 0.694 < 3/4.

**(c) Huxley subdivision has no analogue.** Huxley's V^{−6} term comes from boxes of t-length T_0 = V⁴/4G² inside which correlations are ≪ T_0^{1/2}. The multiplicative analogue — boxes C_w = {wb : N(b) ≍ U/N(w)} of rows sharing a common factor w, inside which χ_n(u)χ̄_n(u') = χ_n(ab⁵)1_{(n,w)=1} has conductor N(ab) ≤ (U/N(w))² — needs N(w) ≥ U^{1−δr} = U^{0.72} for V² ≥ E_B, but a row u with no divisor of norm ≍ U^{0.72} (e.g. a prime element) is isolated, so the number of boxes is ≫ U. Dead.

**(d) Numbers.** Taking the most optimistic Huxley form R ≪ U^ε(N V^{−2} + N U V^{−6}) literally: count exponent max(r(1−δ), 1 + r − 3δr) = max(0.44, 0.88) = 0.88 vs the paper's 0.667; the Halász term beats the mean-value term 1 − δr iff δ > 1/2. In the Section-20 model (G_2k_model_fast.py) adding this bound as an extra option leaves R* = 0.667 at the critical class and B unchanged (0.87515 on the coarse grid = baseline on the same grid). Guth–Maynard-type large-values improvements (2024) begin at σ = 0.7 (where 30/13 = 3/(2−σ)); a = 0.694 is just below that too.

**Conclusion for part 3:** note B's suggestion (iii) ("a large-values version of Lemma 17.1") is retracted. At the binding class the spike δ_c r*/2 = 0.14 is too small relative to the length (δ_c < 1/2 is what matters, not δr), so no large-values inequality — even an ideal one — improves on the mean-value count U^{1−δr} there. Large values only help classes with a > 3/4, which are not binding (they are counted at ≤ 1 − δ + … already and sit far below the E = 0 surface).

## 4. Deliverables

**(a) Statements.**
* Lemma 18.1(k): as in §2 (capacity Σn_i + 6κz ≤ M; zero-slot case restricted to Σn_i ≤ M). Proof: §1/§2 — all ledgers go through; (Z_k) is the missing base.
* Lemma HM (hypothetical): #{u ≍ U: |M_r(u)|² ≥ V²} ≪ U^ε(U^r V^{−2} + U^{1+r}V^{−6}). Status: not provable (needs sub-Burgess pointwise cancellation in sextic Hecke character sums of length U^r, conductor U²), and in any case idle at a < 3/4.
* **Lemma 17.1×18.1 (hybrid, the one that matters):** with ψ_u = νχ_·(u), Σ_{u≍U, 6th-power-free} |M_u(U^r;W_1) S_u(U^m;W_2) Q_u|² ≪ U^{1+ε} for r ≤ 1, m ≤ 1/2, r + m + c z ≤ 1 + θ. With θ = 0 (only the pairs with s = 0) nothing changes; θ = 1/8 → B ≈ 0.8745; θ = 1/4 → B = 0.87236 (floor barrier); θ = 1/2 (all detector pairs) → R* = 1 − δ for every class = "ideal counts" of note E → 13/15 once the floor rows are handled. Reason it works: at t = 1 the detector gives |M_r S_m|² ≥ U^{δ(r+m)} with r + m = 1 + s (Prop 8.3, (8.3)), so the hybrid count is 1 − δ(1+s) ≤ 1 − δ directly, no primes needed, no r/m balancing.

**(b) Obstruction (precise).** For Lemma 18.1(k): the reflection reset (18.10) is the only k=2-specific identity, but (18.31)+(18.38) make the induction unable to carry any positive excess, so the unrestricted zero-slot assertion is needed at every width; for k ≥ 3 it is the 2k-th moment of the family (k = 3: sixth moment), beyond all current technology including the 2026 MDS results. For large values: δ_c < 1/2.

**(c) Predicted bounds.** Table in §0. All runs: κ = 3/4 (7/8 known), d_max = 3/4, floor bin d0 = 0.02, Section-20 side constraints as in stage3_closed.py. Baseline on the coarse (t,r,s) grid of G_2k_model_fast.py is 0.87515 vs Liu's 0.874957 on the closed form; the scenario differences are robust (identical numbers for all idle scenarios, floor saturation 0.872356 = note B's value for the hybrid θ ≥ 1/4).

**(d) Creative idea: prove the hybrid, not a higher moment.** The product M_r S_m is the dyadic block of the tail of 1 = L(s,ψ_u)^{−1}L(s,ψ_u) truncated at U^t — the classical zero-detecting polynomial with coefficient μ_{≤U^t} * 1. Its second moment over the lattice rows u is a *second* moment of total length up to U^{3/2}, i.e. a mollified second moment with a mollifier of conductor length. Ingredients already in the paper and the specific places where it fails today:
  1. The Möbius factor is handled in Lemma 17.2 through the cubic-theta completion (14.2): the squarefree index n is completed by the cube variable b³ with Gauss-sum weight γ₂(n), and the reflection (Prop 5.1, Lemma 5.2, Cor. 14.2) is the metaplectic automorphy of Patterson's theta function — a genuinely second functional equation available for Möbius/Gauss-weighted sums but not for plain sums. The plain factor is handled in Lemma 18.1 by Hecke's functional equation. The hybrid needs both resets in one induction. The precise blocker in Lemma 17.2 is its coefficient hypothesis (lines 6997–7003): "No other row-dependent column coefficient or puncture is allowed. There is no additional residual coefficient b(n)… arbitrary bounded weights are permitted only as the stated product of individual prime-slot coefficients." A plain factor S_m is not a product-form mark d(n) of (14.1). The slots of Lemma 17.1 are allowed precisely because they are short and multiplicatively separable; S_m is long (U^{0.41}) and additive.
  2. Alternatively start from Lemma 18.1 and replace one plain factor by M_r. What changes: (i) the reflection reset applies only to the plain factor, so the unrestricted zero-slot range becomes r + min(m, M−m) ≤ M — this covers r + m ≤ 1 (s = 0) and m ≥ r, but NOT the adversarial region r ∈ ((1+s)/2, 1], m = 1 − r + s; (ii) on exceptional rows (inducing character in Θ, finitely many fixed characters) the Möbius factor has no main term to centre (Lemma 18.3 is not needed) but needs a power saving: the paper's own Theorem (zero-free Re s > 7/8 for the finitely many fixed ϑ ∈ Θ, all finite-order Hecke characters of Q(√−3)) gives Σ_l μ(l)ϑ(l)W(l/X) ≪ X^{7/8+ε}, a saving X^{−1/8}, which closes the exceptional deficit A − 5M/6 only when 3r/4 + m ≤ 5M/6; at r + m = M that is m ≤ M/3 — again short of m* = 0.41 but the bootstrap improves it (a zero-free region Re s > β for Θ gives saving (1−β)r). This is the first place in the whole architecture where the proven half-plane would feed back into a moment lemma with a visible gain. (iii) all ledgers (18.33),(18.38),(18.39) and the amplifier are untouched since they see only total lengths.
  3. The additive structure of the rows (u ∈ Z[ω] lattice; Poisson in u is the first transform (18.21)) is exactly what the k=2 proof already exploits; it is not an unused resource. What is unused is the *cubic-theta reset for the Möbius factor inside the plain-factor induction*. Concretely the proposal: run the Section-18 two-transform induction on (M_r, S_m); when the S_m-reflection cannot reset the excess, apply the marked completion/reflection of Section 14 to the M_r factor (as Lemma 17.2 does at its terminal nodes) — the completed sum has length ≈ (row width) − r on the theta side, so the pair (r, m) ↦ (M − r, m) is available as a second reset, and the two resets together cover r + m ≤ M + min(r, m, M−r, M−m)... whether the Gauss-sum weights γ₂ on the completed index are compatible with the Gauss-row norms G(u,h) of 18.5 (they are both cubic Gauss sums; the amplifier identity (13.5) G(p^i u, h) is what must be redone with the γ₂ weight present) is the first thing to check, and it is a finite computation (Lemma 13.2/13.3 with an extra γ₂(n) factor).
  4. Payoff ordering: hybrid with θ = 1/4 already saturates the floor barrier (0.87236); beyond that the floor rows (a ≤ 51/100, counted trivially) are the next wall, and only then 13/15 and the C_0 ≤ 0 constraint. So a hybrid lemma with r + m ≤ 5/4 is the correct next target, with a mean-value-over-rows saving on the floor (note E §3) as the one after.
