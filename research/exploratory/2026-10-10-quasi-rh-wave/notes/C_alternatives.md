## 1. Mechanism: why a cubic theta series makes 1/L(s,η) appear

### 1.1 The three identities that drive the proof

Fix F = Q(√−3), O = Z[ω], primary generators c ≡ 1 (mod 3), sextic symbol χ_c(·) = (·/c)_6, normalised Gauss sums γ_j(c) = q_c^{−1/2} Σ_{d mod c} χ_c(d)^j e(d/c). Three facts, and only these three, make the argument close:

**(A) Hasse–Davenport / Jacobi-sum sign (main paper eq. (4.7); companion §2.3, after Hasse pp. 443–445 and Heath-Brown [HB00, (2)]).** For a primary prime p,
  γ_1(p) γ_2(p) = −α(p) χ_p(4) γ_3(p),   α(p) = p/|p|.
Equivalently g(χ)g(χ²) = J(χ,χ²) g(χ³) with J(χ,χ²) = −χ(4)·p for the sextic χ = χ_p. Here γ_3 is the *quadratic* Gauss sum, which is explicit (|γ_3| = 1 and its phase is a fixed ray-class function). Multiplying over p | c (c squarefree) gives
  γ_1(c) γ_2(c) = µ(c) · (explicit ray-class phase) · α(c).
**The Möbius function is manufactured by the sign −1 per prime in the Jacobi sum.** (Conceptually: J(ψ,ψ) = π for the *cubic* character in the Ireland–Rosen normalisation π ≡ 2 mod 3; switching to the paper's primary normalisation π ≡ 1 mod 3 costs exactly (−1)^{ω(c)} = µ(c).) Companion paper, §2.3: "µ(n)γ_{−1}(n) = χ_n(−1)G(n)^{−1}α(n)γ_2(n)".

**(B) Patterson's theorem (companion (2.10), DR Appendix A):** the cubic Gauss sums γ_2(c) (c squarefree) are the Fourier coefficients of Kubota's cubic theta function θ on the 3-fold cover of GL(2, F), with c_θ(c b³) = 3^{5/2}|b| χ_c(λ)² γ_2(c) and c_θ = 0 at indices that are not squarefree×cube. This gives automorphy, hence a reflection formula (Prop. 5.1) for the *completed* sum over A = c n³.

**(C) Reflection twists by a quadratic character (companion (2.14)):** at the dual cusp the Fourier/theta phases combine as χ_p^{−1}·χ_p^{−2} = χ_p^{−3}, which is quadratic. Hence the reflected rows are *quadratic* Hecke twists of theta coefficients and the perfectly orthogonal Goldmakher–Louvel quadratic large sieve (M+N) applies to the direct bound, avoiding the (MN)^{2/3} of the higher-order sieve.

### 1.2 How 1/L is produced (main paper §7)

The probe (6.1) averages the completed theta coefficients γ_2(c) α(cn³) η(cn³)·(phase corrections) against the sextic residue character χ_{cn³}(m) over m, with an outer average over auxiliary s of norm ≍ Y. Poisson in m (modulus s b* A, A = cn³) produces the sextic Gauss sum of χ_A at frequency H = u a⁶ (u sixth-power-free): g_{χ_A}(A, H) = χ̄_A(H) q_A^{1/2} γ_1(A). The row-u coefficient is thus
  γ_1(A) · γ_2(c) · χ̄_A(u) · η(A) · phases,
and by (A) the squarefree part contributes µ(c) η(c) ψ̄_u(c) q_c^{−x}, ψ_u = Kummer character c ↦ χ_c(u). The cube part n³ and the mixed valuations are tabulated in Lemma 7.1 (six cases j = 0..5 of v_p(A) mod 6), and everything sums to (7.13):
  F_{η,u}(x,w,z) = ζ_F^S(6z) · L^S(w, χ_•(u)) / L^S(x, η χ_•(u)) · H_{η,u},
with H_{η,u} a holomorphic Euler product, = 1 + O(P_0^{−4/5}) for u = 1. So **the u = 1 row carries 1/L(x,η)** exactly because the Gauss sum coming out of Poisson is the *inverse* (= conjugate) of the theta coefficient, up to an explicit quadratic Gauss sum and a Möbius sign. The ζ_F(6z) factor is Σ_a q_a^{−6z}, the sum over the sixth-power parts a⁶ of the frequency.

### 1.3 The exponent arithmetic, and what each number is

Mellin weight (7.5): W = X^{1/2−z} Z^{x+z−1} Y^{w−1}. Principal residues at w = 1 and z = 1/6 give (Lemma 10.5):
  X^{1/3} Z^{s−5/6} Z^{ℓ/6} = Z^{C(s)}.
- **Shift 5/6 = 1 − 1/6**: the pole of ζ_F(6z) at z = 1/6, i.e. the sixth powers in the Poisson frequency H = u a⁶. It is 1 − 1/N with N = 6 the order of the residue-symbol family.
- **Amplification slope α = 5/6** (eq. (17.88): Σ_u |M_u|² ≪ U^{1/6} H^{5/6}): each sixth-power-free u of norm ≍ U has ≍ (H/U)^{1/6} companions ua⁶ ≤ H with the same character, so the mean square over rows is divided by (H/U)^{1/6}. Again α = 1 − 1/N.
- **Part I** (X = Y = Z^{1/2}): C_I(s) = s − 5/6 + 1/6 = s − 2/3; direct bound Z^{1/4} (Kintali §3: Z^{−1/2}·Z^{1/4}·Z^{1/2}); boundary σ_0 = 2/3 + 1/4 = 11/12.
- **Part II** (Liu §0.1): C_b(s) = s − (4+b)/6, |J| ≪ Z^{l_x/2 + b/12}, l_x = (1−b−ℓ)/2, giving B(ℓ) = 11/12 − ℓ/4 independent of b; ℓ = total exponent of selected primes, constrained to ≈1/6 by the nonprincipal rows ⇒ 7/8. The dynamic κ = 2β_* − 1 ∈ (3/4, 5/6] (eq. (12.1)) is the zero-class ceiling; the moment lemmas (Lemma 18.1) are stated for κ ∈ [3/4, 1], i.e. precisely for β_* ≥ 7/8.

**General-N bookkeeping (heuristic).** If every input had the same shape for a family of N-th order characters (Poisson frequencies u a^N, pole of ζ(Nz) at 1/N, prime weights q_p^{−1+1/N}):
  C(s) = s − 1 + 1/N + l_x(1/2 − 1/N) + ℓ/N,  |J| ≪ Z^{l_x/2 + b/(2N)},
  ⇒ B_N(ℓ) = 1 − 1/(2N) − 3ℓ/(2N).
Check: N = 6 gives 11/12 − ℓ/4 = Liu's formula exactly, including b-independence. So **smaller N is better**: N = 4 gives Part-I boundary 7/8 with no Part-II machinery, and 7/8 − 3ℓ/8 (= 13/16 at ℓ = 1/6) with it. The formal N → 2 limit of the Part-I boundary is 3/4 — plausibly the origin of the "barrier at 3/4" remark, though N = 2 is exactly the degenerate case where the mechanism does not exist (next paragraph).

### 1.4 Why Jacobi theta over Q gives nothing, and the classification of admissible pairings

The engine is a *pairing* (character of order N, theta coefficient g(χ^b)) such that g(χ)·g(χ^b) = µ-sign × explicit × g(χ^{1+b}) with χ^{1+b} quadratic, i.e. 1 + b = N/2, AND the n-fold cover theta carrying g(χ^b) (n = order of χ^b) has fully known coefficients at non-squarefree indices (needed for the completion over n-th powers and the Möbius inversion of companion §2.5).
- N = 2 (Jacobi theta over Q): the only partner is g(χ)·g(χ) = χ(−1)p, explicit with **no sign per prime**, so no µ; and the theta coefficients are themselves explicit (ε_c), so the Poisson dual contains L(x, η χ_h χ_{−1}) in the *numerator*. Putting µ into the probe by hand makes the direct bound a Möbius sum again — circular.
- N = 2n, b = n − 1: χ^{n−1} has order n (n odd) or 2n (n even). Fully known theta coefficients require n ≤ 3 (unique Whittaker model; Patterson 1977 for n = 3; Patterson 2014: "known completely only for double covers and up to a constant for triple covers"). Hence **N = 6 with the cubic theta is the unique unconditional pairing**. This is the precise content behind Lichtman's "the cubic family appears essential".
- N = 4, b = 1 (quartic character over Q(i), quartic theta): g(χ)² = J(χ,χ) g(χ²), J(χ_π,χ_π) = −χ_π(−1)·π̄ (Ireland–Rosen Ch. 9), so γ_1(c)² = µ(c)·(explicit)·α(c)^{±1}: **the Möbius sign survives.** The pairing exists; what is missing is (B): the 4-fold-cover theta θ_4 has a 2-dimensional Whittaker space, its coefficients at c_1 c_2² (c_2 squarefree ≠ 1) are not forced by Hecke theory (Suzuki 1983 evaluates them at squares; Eckhardt–Patterson 1992 conjecture the rest). (C) still holds: χ_p^{−1}χ_p^{−1} = χ_p^{−2} is quadratic, so the direct bound would again use the quadratic large sieve over Q(i) (Goldmakher–Louvel; Onodera), exactly as David–Dunn–Hamieh–Lin do for quartic Gauss sums over primes.
- N = 8 over Q(ζ_8), N = 12 over Q(ζ_12): the pairing reduces to the embedded quartic/quartic or sextic/cubic pairings; no new structure. Higher Kazhdan–Patterson covers (n ≥ 5) and BBFH-type multiple Dirichlet series provide automorphy but not coefficient control, and the theta on the n-fold cover of GL(2) has non-unique Whittaker model for n ≥ 4; the GL(3) cubic theta of Bump–Hoffstein is a different kind of object (see §3(ii)).

**Predicted exponents.** Quartic over Q(i), if a completed quartic reflection with L²-control of θ_4 coefficients were available: Part-I-type argument ⇒ 7/8 for finite-order Hecke L-functions over Q(i) and (via L(s,χ∘N) = L(s,χ)L(s,χχ_{−4})) for all Dirichlet L-functions; Part-II-type ⇒ ≈13/16 at the same ℓ, with the row counts unconditionally as good as the cubic ones (BGL Theorem 1.3 holds for all n ≥ 3 with the same (MN)^{2/3}) and conjecturally better (DFDH Conjecture 1.3: A^{3/4}B^{1/2} + A^{1/2}B^{3/4}, i.e. X^{5/4} vs X^{4/3} at A ≍ B ≍ X). The amplification slope α = 3/4 is *more* favourable than 5/6. A sextic-theta (6-fold cover) version with cubic characters (N = 3) would formally give 5/6 at Part I but requires the 6-fold cover coefficients, which are unknown.

## 2. Survey with sources

**(a) Sharpness of Heath-Brown's cubic large sieve.** Heath-Brown, *Kummer's conjecture for cubic Gauss sums*, Israel J. Math. 120 (2000), Theorem 2: for β supported on squarefree b ≡ 1 (3),
  Σ_{N a ≤ A, a ≡ 1(3)} µ²(a) |Σ_{N b ≤ B} β_b (b/a)_3|² ≪_ε (A + B + (AB)^{2/3})(AB)^ε Σ|β_b|².
Heath-Brown wrote that the (AB)^{2/3} "could be removed with further effort", and BGL14 repeated that it "can most likely be replaced with (AB)^{1/2}". Dunn–Radziwiłł, *Bias in cubic Gauss sums: Patterson's conjecture*, Ann. of Math. 200 (2024) 967–1057, arXiv:2109.07463 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.07463), **Theorem 1.4**: under GRH for Hecke L-functions over Q(ω) the operator norm satisfies B(A,B) ≍ (AB)^{o(1)}(A + B + (AB)^{2/3}); the extremal sequence is β_b = g̃(b)W(N b/B) (normalised cubic Gauss sums), for which Voronoi summation gives Σ ≫ A^{2/3}B^{5/3} when A ≤ (AB)^{1/2−ξ} (their (9.3)–(9.5)). Their §9 also gives an "improved cubic large sieve" with an explicit asymptotic main-term correction (the Patterson 5/6 bias). **Made unconditional in July 2026**: de Faveri–Dunn–Hoffstein, *Non-orthogonality of the cubic and quartic large sieves via Rankin–Selberg*, arXiv:2607.07911 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.07911), Theorem 1.1: Ξ_3(A,B) ≫_ε (AB)^{−ε}(A + B + (AB)^{2/3}) unconditionally (Zagier regularised Rankin–Selberg; inputs: Lindelöf-on-average for the second moment of Kubota's Dirichlet series and an average lower bound for coefficients of θ⊗θ̄). Critical point for us: **the extremal sequence is exactly the Gauss-sum sequence that the OpenAI/companion argument produces after its own Poisson steps** (companion §2.3: sextic Gauss sums absorb µ and become cubic Gauss sums), so the row envelope R(δ) = 4/3 − δ on 1/3 ≤ δ ≤ 2/3 (main paper §9, from the (KD)^{2/3} term of Lemma 9.1) cannot be improved by a better *inequality*, only by subtracting the bias main term.

**(b) Quartic large sieve.** Note: Baier–Young (JNT 130 (2010) 879–903, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022314X10000119) is the *cubic* large sieve for Dirichlet characters over Z; the quartic analogue is Gao–Zhao, *Large sieve inequalities for quartic characters*, Q. J. Math. 63 (2012) 891–917, arXiv:1101.3646 (https://arxiv.org/abs/1101.3646), Theorem 1.1: for squarefree m, n ≡ 1 mod (1+i)³ in Z[i],
  Σ*_{N m ≤ M} |Σ*_{N n ≤ N} a_n (n/m)_4|² ≪_ε (M + N + (MN)^{3/4})(MN)^ε Σ|a_n|²,
and Theorem 1.2 for quartic Dirichlet characters over Z with a min{Q^{7/4}+M, Q^{11/8}+Q^{1/2}M, Q^{5/4}+Q^{2/3}M, Q+Q^{1/2}M+M^{17/7}} bound. BGL supersede the (MN)^{3/4} by (MN)^{2/3}. DFDH Theorem 1.2: Ξ_4(A,B) ≫ (AB)^{−ε}(A + B + A^{3/4}B^{1/2} + A^{1/2}B^{3/4}); Conjecture 1.3: this is also the upper bound; and (1.8): for general n, Ξ_n = (AB)^{o(1)}(A + B + A^{1−1/n}B^{2/n} + A^{2/n}B^{1−1/n}). They add: "Any power-saving improvement on the (AB)^{2/3+ε} term in (1.5) for any n ≥ 4 would be a substantial advance."

**(c) Goldmakher–Louvel and Blomer–Goldmakher–Louvel.** GL, *A quadratic large sieve inequality over number fields*, Math. Proc. Cambridge 2013, arXiv:1112.1642 (https://arxiv.org/abs/1112.1642), Theorem 1.1: for a quadratic Hecke family {χ_a} w.r.t. c, Σ*_{N a ≤ M}|Σ*_{N b ≤ N} λ_b χ_b(a)|² ≪ (MN)^ε (M+N) Σ|λ_b|²; Corollary 1.2: same bound for χ_b^{n/2} in an n-th order Hecke family (n even). This is the input the OpenAI direct bound uses (Kintali (11)). BGL, *L-functions with n-th order twists*, IMRN 2014, arXiv:1112.1650 (https://arxiv.org/abs/1112.1650), Theorem 1.3: for K ⊇ µ_n, n ≥ 3, Σ*_{N a ≤ M}|Σ*_{N b ≤ N} λ_b χ_a(b)|² ≪ (MN)^ε (M + N + (MN)^{2/3}) Σ|λ_b|², proved by Heath-Brown's recursion (§3); Corollary 1.6: zero-density for the n-th order family; the text repeats "(MN)^{2/3} is not optimal and can most likely be removed" — now known false for n = 3 and conjecturally false for every n (DFDH (1.8)). The OpenAI Lemma 9.1 is the sextic specialisation with zeros retained.

**(d) David–de Faveri–Dunn–Stucky**, *Non-vanishing for cubic Hecke L-functions*, arXiv:2410.03048 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.03048): unconditionally a positive proportion (>14% per a secondary citation, arXiv:2507.06609) of L(1/2, χ_q), q squarefree ≡ 1 mod 9 in Z[ω], are nonzero, via mollified first and second moments; the main new input is an asymptotic for the mollified second moment with power saving, using Patterson's coefficients and the cubic large sieve; they stress the family is unitary and "does not satisfy a perfectly orthogonal large sieve bound". Related: Hamdar, arXiv:2509.06152 (first moment of cubic Hecke L-functions across the strip, phase transition at s = 1/3).

## 3. Genuinely different routes to a power saving in BOTH representations

Where the slack is: by the Poisson identity the probe's true size is Z^{C(β_*)} up to the nonprincipal rows; under RH that is Z^{C(1/2)} = Z^{−1/6} (Part I), whereas the direct bound is Z^{1/4}. The direct side therefore loses up to Z^{5/12}, all of it in the Cauchy–Schwarz over the ≍Y rows s before the quadratic large sieve (the sieve's diagonal term Y is then unavoidable). The Poisson side loses in the row counts, whose central range is governed by the sharp (KD)^{2/3}.

**(i) Spectral/Kuznetsov bound on the direct side, or a double Dirichlet series.** The reflected probe is Σ_s ξ(s)W_1(q_s/Y) Σ_µ d(µ)α(µ)(µ/s)_2 V^♯(…): a cubic-theta coefficient sequence twisted by moving quadratic characters, summed *with signs* over s. Cauchy–Schwarz in s is the only lossy step. Define Z_η(s,w) = Σ*_s Σ_c γ_2(c)α(c)η(c)(c/s)_2 q_c^{−s} q_s^{−w} = Σ_s D(s, η·χ_s^{(2)}) q_s^{−w} (quadratic twists of Kubota's Dirichlet series). Each D(s,ψ) has Patterson's functional equation; the s-sum should be a two-variable multiple Dirichlet series with a finite group of functional equations (a Weyl-group MDS attached to the 6-fold cover; cf. BBFH and Brubaker–Bump residues giving Kubota theta). A convexity bound in w then replaces the large-sieve diagonal. Sketch: direct bound becomes |Z_η| on the line Re w = 1 − l_y/… ; any saving Y^{−κ'} over the diagonal moves σ_0 to 2/3 + 1/4 − κ'l_y (Part I). Patterson's residual-spectrum caveat: θ is a residue of the metaplectic Eisenstein series, so the Kuznetsov route must handle the continuous spectrum of the 3-fold cover; the existence/continuation of Z_η is the real question. Predicted: if even half-square-root cancellation in s is realised (κ' = l_y/4 ≈ 1/8 at balance), σ_0 ≈ 0.79. Highly uncertain.

**(ii) GL(3) cubic theta (Bump–Hoffstein, Invent. Math. 84 (1986); Duke 53 (1986)).** The Eisenstein series on the cubic cover of GL(3) induced from θ_{GL(2)} has Whittaker coefficients that are essentially the double series Σ_c g(c) L(s, χ_c) q_c^{−w} (cubic double Dirichlet series; see Chinta–Friedberg–Hoffstein's survey, https://chinta.ccny.cuny.edu/publ/patterson.pdf). The nonprincipal rows of the OpenAI Poisson side are quotients L(w, ψ_u)/L(x, ηψ_u) over the sextic Kummer family; a GL(3) object supplies a *second functional equation in the row variable u*, which is what a large sieve lacks. Sketch: replace Prop. 9.2's row count by a moment of the numerator L(w, ψ_u) weighted by the detector's inverse polynomial, evaluated through the GL(3) Eisenstein series' Mellin transform; the (KD)^{2/3} would be replaced by the bias main term (which is explicit, Patterson's conjecture-type) plus a Lindelöf-on-average error (now unconditional via DFDH's Rankin–Selberg input). Predicted exponent: tomoto0's model says density-hypothesis-strength counts give at best ≈13/15 = 0.8667; so this is a 7/8 → ~0.87 route unless combined with (i). Honest assessment: real but modest.

**(iii) Self-improvement.** Already built in: Part II uses β_* ≤ 11/12 to set κ ≤ 5/6, and the moment lemmas need κ ≥ 3/4 ⇔ β_* ≥ 7/8; the three parameter re-optimisations above and tomoto0's "bootstrap fixed point near 0.8745" show the fixed point of feeding the output back into the zero-class ceiling is ≈0.87496. Feeding 7/8 into the large-sieve/moment inputs does not help because those inputs are sharp inequalities (not zero-dependent); feeding it into the zero detector only shrinks the bins [δ ≤ κ], already optimised. Verdict: exhausted; expect no more than 10^{−4}.

**(iv) Two-family cubic+quartic cancellation.** The two probes live on different metaplectic groups over different fields; their representations are *bounds*, not identities, so nothing cancels. What is available instead is a *max*: a quartic argument over Q(i) (§1.4) would give its own boundary for Hecke L-functions over Q(i) and, through the norm pullback, for all Dirichlet L-functions, independently of the cubic one; the best of the two then applies to Dirichlet L-functions, but not to non-norm Hecke characters over Q(√−3). A single-field two-pairing version exists over Q(ζ_12) ⊇ µ_12 (χ² sextic / χ⁴ cubic and χ³ quartic simultaneously), but it inherits the quartic coefficient problem. Verdict: fails as a cancellation; survives only as "run the quartic version".

**(v) (not asked, but relevant) Bias-corrected sextic large sieve.** DR §9 gives a cubic large sieve with a non-trivial main term (conditional there); DFDH's unconditional Lindelöf-on-average for Kubota's series is the missing input to make the *upper* bound with explicit main term unconditional. In the OpenAI row count the (KD)^{2/3} enters only through Lemma 9.1 applied to coefficients that are themselves Möbius×Hecke polynomials; after the paper's own two Poisson transformations these become Gauss-sum sequences — exactly where the main term is explicit and could be subtracted and treated as a second "principal-type" row.

## 4. Ranked directions and the biggest obstruction

**1. Quartic analogue over Q(i) (target: 7/8 by a Part-I argument, ≈13/16 with Part II).**
Target Lemma Q1 (completed quartic reflection): for the Patterson quartic theta θ_4 on the 4-fold cover of GL(2, Q(i)), with Ψ a finite-ray character times Π_p χ_p^{j_p}, the completed sum Σ_{c sf, b} γ_1(c) α(c b⁴) Ψ(c b⁴) V(q_{cb⁴}/X)/√q_{cb⁴} equals a finite combination of dual sums Σ_m d_{θ_4}(m) α(m) ϑ(m) χ_R(m)² V^♯(q_m X/q_R²…) with the twist quadratic, where the cusp coefficients d_{θ_4} are known at squarefree×fourth-power indices and satisfy the mean-square bound Σ_{N m ≤ M} |d_{θ_4}(m)|² ≪ M^{1+ε} (only an L²-bound at indices c_1c_2² is needed, since the next step is Cauchy–Schwarz + quadratic large sieve). DFDH's Rankin–Selberg control of θ_4 coefficients is the natural source. Target Lemma Q2 (local identity): the quartic analogue of Lemma 7.1 with four valuation cases, giving ζ_{Q(i)}(4z)L(w, ψ_u)/L(x, ηψ_u)·H, from g(χ_π)² = −χ_π(−1) π̄ g(χ_π²).

**2. Bias-corrected sextic row count (target: replace R(δ) = 4/3 − δ by 1 − δ + explicit bias row on 1/3 ≤ δ ≤ 2/3).**
Target Lemma B: unconditional version of DR's Theorem 9.x — for c_b = g̃(b)β_b supported on squarefree b, Σ_a µ²(a)|Σ_b c_b (b/a)_3|² = (explicit main term from the 5/6 residue of the metaplectic Eisenstein series) + O((A + B)(AB)^ε Σ|β|²) in the range A ≍ B, using DFDH's Lindelöf-on-average for the second moment of Kubota's series in place of GRH. Then re-run §19 with the bias row treated like the principal row (it carries ζ-type factors, not 1/L). **3. Signed row sum on the direct side via a two-variable Dirichlet series (target: break the Cauchy–Schwarz diagonal).**
