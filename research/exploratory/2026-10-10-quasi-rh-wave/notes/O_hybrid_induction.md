## 1. The hybrid object and what the first Poisson transform does to it

**1.1 The object.** At a node, the paper's product S(n_1)S(n_2)Q is one polynomial Σ_u c(u)ψ_k(u) with c the allocated convolution of the two profiles and the slot coefficients (9841–9870). The hybrid replaces c by
    c_hyb = (μ·W_1(·/Z^r)) * (W_2(·/Z^m)) * (slot coefficients),
one polynomial of length A with divisor-bounded coefficients, the Möbius variable l_1 squarefree. Nothing in the first-transform derivation uses the shape of c: the zero frequency (9843–9850: "a character mean can be nonzero only if its exponent at every prime is zero modulo six … powerful … O(X^{1+ε})") uses only |c(u)| ≪ q_u^ε; the common-support extraction "fix its exact valuation in each plain variable" (9862–9869) applies with the single modification that the Möbius variable admits valuation 0 or 1 ("An impossible allocation is a zero term", 9869). So (18.21) holds verbatim:
    H/(X√(q_e q_r)) · Σ_{h≠0} G_{ξ_r}(r,h) Σ_{(a,b)=1} A_C(a)A_D(b)/√(q_a q_b) · τ_C(a)τ_D(b) R(a,b) G(a,h)G(b,−h) Φ̂_1(Hq_h/(q_e q_r q_a q_b)),
with A_C containing μ on the l_1-divisor. Rows h run to Z^K, K = K_0 + O(ξ), K_0 = 2A − c − d + R + E − ρ (9877); the target for the Gauss-row norm is (18.23), N_C ≪ Z^{A − c + q̃ + B_c − s_0 + ε_G}, and the ledger (18.24) is prime-by-prime and unchanged.

**1.2 The Möbius-dual column is a canonical theta sum.** For squarefree a = l_1·l_2Π with (l_1, l_2Π h) = 1, CRT and sextic reciprocity (870: γ_j(ab) = χ_a(b)^jχ_b(a)^jγ_j(a)γ_j(b); Lemma 4.4) give
    G(l_1 l_2Π, h) = χ_{l_1}(l_2Π)χ_{l_2Π}(l_1) G(l_1,h)G(l_2Π,h) = R(l_1, l_2Π) χ_{l_1}(l_2Π)^2 · χ̄_{l_1}(h)γ_1(l_1) · G(l_2Π,h),
and by (4.7) (814) and the paper's own line 8115, μ(l_1)γ_1(l_1) = ᾱ(l_1)γ_2(l_1)G(l_1) = θ(l_1)·G(l_1) (θ := ᾱγ_2 the canonical coefficient of Prop. 5.1, line 1416; the direct computation from (4.7), γ_1 = μαGγ̄_2, gives the conjugate θ̄·G — the orientation χ_n(k) vs χ_k(n) of the Poisson Gauss sum decides which; both are reflectable, the conjugate by conjugating the whole polynomial inside |·|² as at 8787). Hence the l_1-part of the Gauss-row column is
    Σ_{l_1 sf} θ(l_1) G(l_1) · χ_{l_1}(h)^{∓1} χ_{l_1}(l_2Π)^{4 or 2} R(l_1,l_2Π) W_1(q_{l_1}/Z^r),
which is exactly the Section 14 shape a(n)Ψ_k(n), Ψ_k(n) = ν(n)ρ(n)χ_n(k)χ_n(f)^4 (line 5507) with row k = h and the "f⁴-twist" f = l_2Π (the paper's f is a squarefree ideal of length V; here V = m + z). Context item (iv) is thus confirmed with the explicit cross phases: Poisson in the row turns μ into θ, and the plain variable rides along as an f⁴-twist of length m on the theta character.

**1.3 The row-count wall at the Gauss-row stage (coefficient-independent).** In a clean stage (c = d = R = E = 0) the Gauss-row norm has Z^K rows, K = 2A − ρ, columns of length A, central normalization, and target Z^{A+q}. Its diagonal alone is Z^K, so N_C ≪ Z^{A+q} forces K ≤ A + q, i.e. A ≤ M. This is the same count that appears as the j = 0 term of the second transform, (18.31): "(K + g − ℓ − s_0) − (a_0 + q̃ + w_o + w + B_c − s_0) = (A − M) − … ≤ A − M + 5σ/3 + δ_fr,1" (10116–10122). No coefficient class and no reflection applied to the columns can change the number of rows; so **at every node A ≤ M + o(1) is forced before anything is done to the columns**. Resets therefore have to act on the primal side, before (18.21).

## 2. The finite check: Gauss-row norms (18.5) and the amplifier (13.5) with the Möbius/γ₂ weight

**2.1 What is coefficient-agnostic.** Section 18.5 bounds the Gauss-row norm of a polynomial Σ_u B(u)τ_1(u)q_u^{it}G(u,h) and explicitly allows B arbitrary: "Let B(u) denote the full allocated convolution coefficient … It contains the whole difference when centering is used" (10076–10078). The amplifier identity
    G(p^i u, h) = R(p,u)^i χ_p(u)^{2i} G(p^i,h)G(u,h),   G(u, hp^6) = G(u,h)   (10004–10006)
and (13.5) |G(p^a,k)| = P^{(a−1)/2}1_{v_p(k)=a−1} (6 ∤ a), G(p^a,k) = P^{a/2}1_{p^a|k} − P^{a/2−1}1_{p^{a−1}|k} (6 | a) are pure Gauss-sum algebra of the modulus; the three error types i = 1, 6, 7 with squared central coefficients P^{−1}, (1 − P^{−1})², P^{−1} and (w, w_o) = (iℓ_p, e_iℓ_p) (18.28) (10033–10045) depend only on G.

**2.2 Single-prime case u = p^i u′, p ∤ u′h, worked with Section 4.2.** The column index is a = l_1 l_2 Π with l_1 squarefree carrying μ. Allocation of p^i: (i_1, i_2, i_slot) with i_1 ∈ {0,1} (else μ = 0), slots excluded ("p is outside every live slot window", 10008).
* i = 1: (1,0) or (0,1). Scalar μ(p) = −1 or 1; G(p,h) has modulus one (p ∤ h), G(p, hp^6) = 0; squared extraction coefficient P^{−1}. Residual l_1′ = l_1/p carries μ(l_1′) and the puncture (l_1′, p) = 1 — "The residual product is punctured at p" (10009) already imposes it.
* i = 6: (0,6) or (1,5). G(p^6,h) = P^3(1 − P^{−1}) by (13.5), central factor P^{−3}, coefficient (1 − P^{−1})², extra sign μ(p) in the second allocation; the fixed puncture χ_p(u)^{12} = 1_{(u,p)=1} is as at 10047.
* i = 7: (0,7) or (1,6). |G(p^7,h)| = P^3, central factor P^{−7/2}, coefficient P^{−1}, row phase χ_p(h) (10036).
In the Section 4.2 Gauss-sum language the three cases are the prime-level versions of γ_1(p) (squarefree part), the six-divisible power (cube variable b³ with b ∋ p²) and their product — precisely the support of the completed theta index nb³ of (5.2) and (14.2) ("Both primal ideals avoid S; they may share primes", 5520). The zero row G(u,0) = 0 unless u is a sixth power (10061) is unchanged (a sixth power has i_1 = 0 at every prime).

**2.3 Verdict of the check: PRESERVED, not enlarged, and not where the construction fails.** The same holds for every later ledger: (18.24), (18.32), (18.33), (18.38), (18.39), the aggregate list (18.51) and the exceptional count (18.41) see only total lengths; the t-allocation identity 1_{p|Πn_i} = Σ_{J≠∅}(−1)^{|J|+1}Π_{i∈J}1_{p|n_i} (10300) is for any number and kind of factors. The only coefficient-sensitive steps of Section 18 are (18.9)–(18.10) (reflection) and Lemma 18.3/(18.47)–(18.50) (centering), treated in §3.

## 3. What each reset does, and why the induction cannot close

**3.1 Hecke reset (primal), plain factor only.** (18.8)–(18.10), 9535–9580: a plain factor of length n > M/2 is replaced by the conjugate sum at scale C_k q_{d_0}/(Xq_{h_0}) of length ≤ M − n + ξ; the root number |ε_k| = 1 and the conjugation are harmless inside |·|² (9558–9561). Effect on the hybrid node: (r, m) ↦ (r, M − m + ξ). No analogue for M_r: 1/L(s,ψ_k) has no approximate functional equation (its Dirichlet series does not converge at the dual point), and μ = θ³ is not in the reflectable classes {θ, θ̄} (note I §0).

**3.2 Theta reset (dual side) — what it does to (r, m).** It can only be applied to the Möbius-dual column of §1.2, i.e. after (18.21). Lemma 14.3/(14.11) with H = K (the Gauss rows), the f⁴-twist primes l_2Π among the active j = 4 primes (so 2A_0 ⊇ 2(m+z), partially offset by the Ramanujan divisibility terms N_0 + 3B_0 ≤ m + z assigned to the dual ideals, 5838–5850) and N_* = r gives a reflected dual length
    T_d = 2K + 2A_0 + 2z_a − r − N_0 − 3B_0 ∈ [2K + (m+z) − r, 2K + 2(m+z) − r],   K = 2A − ρ (clean stage),
i.e. T_d ≥ 3r + 5m − 2ρ. It is ≤ r only if 2r + 5m ≤ 2ρ (and ≤ r with the full 2A_0 only if r + 3m ≤ ρ). Numbers at ρ = 1: (r,m) = (0.72, 0.41) → 2.62; (0.59, 0.41) → 2.23; (0.5, 0.5) → 2.50; it shortens only in the useless corner r + 3m ≤ 1 (e.g. (0.2, 0.1) → ≤ 0, i.e. trivial). So the "second reset" r ↦ M − r imagined in note G does not exist: the metaplectic functional equation has conductor (row)², and the Gauss rows h (length 2A − ρ ≥ A) are longer than the column, so reflection doubles the row length into the column instead of subtracting it. Independently, the reflected energy (14.14) satisfies E_ref ≥ max(H, v + ℓ_b) ≥ K, so even as a terminal bound it cannot beat the row count of §1.3: it reproduces A ≤ ρ (≤ M), exactly Section 17's capacity (the first invariant (17.9) F − M − Q − z_0 ≥ c is, after (17.69)/(9146), ρ − r − 2z ≥ c).

**3.3 The theta reflection is terminal: no second transform after it.** The second transform is the exact finite Fourier identity of Lemma 13.3 applied to Σ_u B(u)τ_1(u)q_u^{it}G(u,h) (10080–10100, (18.30)); it needs the row dependence to be through G(u,h). After Prop. 5.1/(14.3) the dependence of the reflected l_1-sum on the row h is through c = c_F(h_0)·r_h in the kernel scale V^♯(q_μX/q_c²) (5.1), through the local factors B_p(λ⁴μ) = χ_p(λ⁴μ)^{−j_p−2} = χ_p(μ)^{−3} at the residual row primes (item 2, 1496–1503), and through the sector scalar ζ (5.3), (14.4); there is no G(·,h). Section 17 continues from such an object only with its own masked Poisson (Lemma 17.5, 7305) on the rows, inside the canonical family of Lemma 17.2 — whose coefficient hypothesis (7003–7006: "no additional residual coefficient b(n) … only as the stated product of individual prime-slot coefficients") excludes the plain variable l_2 (note I §4). So in the hybrid induction the theta reflection can only end a branch, and it ends it at the same wall.

**3.4 The descent: the Section 18 induction cannot host any Möbius length above the floor.** This is the decisive point, stronger than notes G/I anticipated, and it is independent of the coefficient check of §2.
* Every branch descends to the floor. Induction order 9617–9640: bands of width σ/4, "Prove all zero-slot bands first, in increasing order of width"; terminal case 9635–9640: "At M ≤ ρ, the padded zero-slot core follows from absolute counting … Its exponent above M is at most A − q ≤ ρ + δ" — this uses A ≤ M + δ at the floor. 18.8 (10850–10870): "Every nonterminal call is to a child with nominal width M′ … at least σ below its parent … at most D − 2 nonterminal calls occur on a branch." There is no other terminal: the diagonal (18.31) and the exceptional rows (18.42) are per-stage costs.
* A clean stage preserves lengths. (18.32) with c = d = R = E = 0, K = K_0 = 2A − ρ, J = 0, g = J_+ + σ = σ, g_2 = t_2 = V = 0: m′ = 2a_0 − K − g = ρ − σ, q′ = q, M′ = M − σ, and the child lengths α_j = a_0 − c_2 = A (10208, 10348). So (A, M) ↦ (A, M − σ): the width is lowered artificially (the h-ball is enlarged by Z^σ to force strict descent) and the child carries the same lengths with excess A − M′ = (A − M) + σ. This is note G §1 item 3a; the children are then estimated by "the completed unrestricted zero-slot assertion at the declared width M′_act" (10490–10491), and that assertion exists at every width only because "the unrestricted zero-slot assertion follows once from the padded core by reflecting at most its two plains" (10859–10860).
* Consequence for the hybrid. Along a clean branch from (r, m; M) the width runs through every value down to ρ while r is unchanged (extractions c, w, c_2 shorten it only when common support happens to occur; they are not available on demand). At the first width W < r the branch needs
    Σ_{q_k ≪ Z^W} |M_r(k) S_{m′}(k)|² ≪ Z^{W+ε}   with r > W.
At the floor W ≤ ρ this is false by the paper's own counting (the squared product is Z^{A+ε} ≫ Z^{W}). Above the floor it is not merely unknown: for a fixed character, |M_r(k)|² ≪ Z^ε for all r is RH for ψ_k (Landau: a zero at σ_0 gives Σ_{l≤X}μ(l)ψ(l) = Ω(X^{σ_0−ε})), and on average over Z^W rows with r > W it implies #{k : L(s,ψ_k) has a zero with Re s ≥ σ} ≪ Z^{W − r(2σ−1)+ε}, which is sharper than the density hypothesis Z^{2W(1−σ)} exactly when r > W. The induction therefore needs, at every width below r, an input stronger than what it is trying to prove. Hence **the two-transform induction on (M_r, S_m) does not close for any r > ρ + δ**, and the region "r + min(m, M − m) ≤ M" claimed in note G §4(d) item 2(i) is not attained: that reading assumed the children live at one smaller width, whereas they descend to the floor.
* Slots do not help: greedy removal (18.39) shortens z, never r; the fallback 10543 ("If the slots disappear before the affine boundary is reached, the remaining plain lengths may be arbitrary") again needs the unrestricted assertion. Treating the Möbius factor itself as a removable slot (pointwise |M_r(k)|² ≪ Z^{(2β_old−1)r+ε} from the previous bootstrap half-plane, by the same Lemma 4.9 mechanism as (18.11)–(18.12)) charges 6κ_M = 6(2β−1) = 4.5 per unit of r in (18.3): m + 4.5 r ≤ M.
* Using Section 17 as an early terminal does not help either. At width W a hybrid node can be closed by Lemma 17.1 (plain factor as an η-rough product-form mark, note I §6a) iff r + 2m* ≤ W − c with m* ∈ {m, W − m} the current plain length. Unreflected this needs r + 2m ≤ 1 at the top; reflected it needs W ≤ 2m − r, with all intermediate nodes at r + m ≤ W′ (so 2r ≤ m) and, where A > 5W′/6, the exceptional condition of §3.5 at W′ = 2m − r, which gives r ≤ 8m/19. Both are r ≤ 0.18 at m = 0.41.

**3.5 Exceptional rows: a third gap, present even if §3.4 were waived.** On child rows whose inducing character lies in Θ (count Z^{(m′−f)/6}, (18.41), 10611–10615 — including the principal character) the paper bounds plain products by volume and removes the resulting deficit A − 5M/6 ((18.42)–(18.44)) either by the comparison with lengths L = M/4, A − z − L ((18.14)–(18.17), 9652–9680) and Lemma 18.3's cancellation of the main terms c²_{ϑ,R*}T^{1+it}I_1(t)I_2(t), (18.45)–(18.50), or by reflecting "the other factor" when one plain length is < L (9654). For the hybrid: the main term is M_{r′}(ϑ,R*,t)·c_{ϑ,R*}X_2^{1+it}I_2(t), linear in the plain scale with a row-dependent Möbius prefactor, so no comparison product with the same row data cancels it, and "reflect the other factor" is unavailable when the short factor is the plain one. The only saving is the Möbius one: Σ_l μ(l)ϑ(l)1_{(l,R*)=1}W(q_l/X) ≪ X^{β_Θ+ε}Z^{ε} from the paper's own theorem (zero-free Re s > 7/8 for the finitely many ϑ ∈ Θ), i.e. Z^{−2(1−β_Θ)r′} = Z^{−r′/4} on the squared product with r′ ≥ r − v (v = c + w + min(c_2,d_2), 10815). Redoing (18.50): excess ≤ A − 5M/6 − 2v/3 − 2(1−β_Θ)(r − v)_+ + 3σ, maximal at v = 0; so the padded range is admissible only when
    m* + (2β_Θ − 1) r ≤ 5M/6,   i.e.   m* + 3r/4 ≤ 5M/6   (β_Θ = 7/8),
with m* the chosen (possibly reflected) plain length. On r + m = M this is m* ≤ M/3: the AFE point (M/2, M/2) and the critical split (0.59, 0.41) are excluded (max r = 0.564 at m* = 0.41, 0.444 at m* = 1/2). With β_Θ → 1/2 the condition disappears, so this gap — unlike §3.4 — is of bootstrap type (note G §4(d) item 2(ii) was right about the arithmetic, wrong that it ever becomes active).

**(d) The minimal new input, as a lemma.** Two honest formulations, one per architecture.

## Summary

The proposed hybrid induction — Section 18's two-transform descent on (M_r, S_m) with Hecke reflection of S_m as one reset and the Section 14 cubic-theta reflection of the Möbius factor as a second — was taken apart step by step.

Finite check: every affine step of Section 18 is coefficient-agnostic. With the Möbius weight the first transform (18.21) is unchanged; the Gauss-row norms of 18.5, the amplifier identity G(p^i u,h) = R(p,u)^iχ_p(u)^{2i}G(p^i,h)G(u,h) with (13.5), the three error types i = 1, 6, 7 (worked at u = p^i u′: allocations restricted to valuation ≤ 1 on the Möbius variable, extra sign μ(p), central coefficients P^{−1}, (1−P^{−1})², P^{−1} unchanged), and the ledgers (18.24), (18.33), (18.38), (18.39), (18.41)–(18.44) all survive. After Poisson the Möbius column is exactly the canonical theta sum of Section 14 with the plain variable as the f⁴-twist (line 5507). Preserved, not enlarged.

Failure: (1) the induction descends in width to the floor with lengths preserved ((18.32): clean stage (A,M) ↦ (A,M−σ)); only the primal Hecke reflection shortens anything; the Möbius length r is never shortened, so every branch reaches widths W < r where the needed child bound is false at the floor and beyond the density hypothesis above it. The construction closes for no r above the floor; note G's "r + min(m, M−m) ≤ M" is not attained. (2) The theta reset lives on the dual side, lengthens the Möbius column (2(K+m) − r ≈ 3r + 6m − 2ρ, (14.11)), cannot beat the Gauss-row count that gives the (18.31) wall (A ≤ M), and destroys the G(u,h)-structure needed by the second transform — it is terminal. (3) Exceptional rows cannot be centered with a Möbius factor; the Re s > 7/8 Möbius saving covers the deficit only for m* + 3r/4 ≤ 5M/6.

Covered region at m = 0.41: r ≤ 0.17 (union of four weak sub-lemmas), versus r ≤ 0.84 for θ = 1/4. Predicted boundary: 0.874957, unchanged. Minimal input: a column-type plain factor in the one-Poisson canonical estimate (Lemma C), which would give only θ = 0; θ > 0 requires off-diagonal cancellation before Cauchy–Schwarz, beyond both architectures.
