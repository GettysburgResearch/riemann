### 0.2 Standard consequences (classical arguments; each is a one-paragraph proof from I1)

- **(C1) Chebyshev.** $\psi(x)=x+O(x^{\Theta}\log^2x)$ and $\theta(x)=x+O(x^{\Theta}\log^2x)$. *Proof.* Truncated von Mangoldt explicit formula with $T=x$: $\psi(x)=x-\sum_{|\gamma|\le T}x^{\rho}/\rho+O(x\log^2x/T+\log x)$ and $\sum_{|\gamma|\le T}|x^\rho/\rho|\le x^{\Theta}\sum_{|\gamma|\le T}|\gamma|^{-1}\ll x^{\Theta}\log^2T$ (Riemann–von Mangoldt). Conventions: $I(n)=\sigma(n)/n$; $\Delta(n):=I(n)-e^\gamma\log\log n$; $P^+(N)$ = largest prime factor; CA = colossally abundant: $N$ is CA iff there is $\epsilon>0$ with $\sigma(N)/N^{1+\epsilon}\ge\sigma(n)/n^{1+\epsilon}$ for all $n\ge1$. $\mathcal MF(s)=\int_1^\infty F(x)x^{-s-1}dx$ (the repository's convention). $F_-=\max\{-F,0\}$. All $O$-constants are absolute unless subscripted; "effective in principle" means computable from the explicit-formula constants once I1 is granted.

### 2(a) Robin programme

**Lemma A0 (CA asymptotics; classical argument, imports I1 only through C1/C3).**
Let $N$ be CA with $x:=P^+(N)$, $N=\prod_{p\le x}p^{a_p}$. Then, with $R(t)=\theta(t)-t$ and $U(x):=-\displaystyle\int_x^\infty\frac{R(t)(1+\log t)}{t^2\log^2t}\,dt$:
1. $\log I(N)=\gamma+\log\log x+E(x)+O(x^{-1/2}/\log x)$, where $E(x)=\sum_{p\le x}1/p-\log\log x-M$;
2. $\log N=\theta(x)+O(\sqrt x\log x)=x+O(x^{\Theta}\log^2x)$;
3. $\Delta(N)=e^\gamma\log x\cdot U(x)+O(x^{-1/2}\log x)+O(x^{2\Theta-2}\log^5x)$;
4. consequently $|\Delta(N)|\ll x^{\Theta-1}\log^2x\ll(\log N)^{\Theta-1}(\log\log N)^2$.

*Proof.* (Structure of CA numbers.) $a_p\ge k$ iff $\sigma(p^k)p^{-k(1+\epsilon)}\ge\sigma(p^{k-1})p^{-(k-1)(1+\epsilon)}$ iff $p^{\epsilon}\le1+1/(p+p^2+\dots+p^k)$; the right side is decreasing in $p$ and in $k$, so $a_p$ is nonincreasing in $p$ and every prime $\le x$ divides $N$. From $a_x\ge1$: $\epsilon\log x\le\log(1+1/x)<1/x$. From $a_{x'}=0$ for the next prime $x'\le2x$: $\epsilon>1/(3x\log x)$ for large $x$. Hence (i) $a_p\ge k$ whenever $p^k\le x/4$ (since then $\epsilon\log p<\log p/(x\log x)\le1/(4p^k)\le\log(1+1/(2p^k))$), so $p^{a_p+1}>x/4$ for all $p\le x$; (ii) $a_p\ge2$ forces $p^2<3x\log x$, and $a_p\le\log(3x\log x)/\log p+1$.
(1) $\log I(N)=\sum_{p\le x}-\log(1-1/p)+\sum_{p\le x}\log(1-p^{-a_p-1})$. The first sum is $\sum_{p\le x}1/p+\sum_{p\le x}\sum_{k\ge2}(kp^k)^{-1}=\log\log x+M+E(x)+(\gamma-M)-\sum_{p>x}\sum_{k\ge2}(kp^k)^{-1}$, using $M=\gamma-\sum_p\sum_{k\ge2}(kp^k)^{-1}$; the tail is $O(1/(x\log x))$. For the second sum, by (i) each term is $O(1/x)$ for $p\le\sqrt x$ (there are $\pi(\sqrt x)$ of them) and $O(p^{-2})$ for $p>\sqrt x$; total $O(x^{-1/2}/\log x)$.
(2) $\log N=\theta(x)+\sum_{p\le x}(a_p-1)\log p$ and by (ii) the second sum is $\le\pi(3\sqrt{x\log x})\log(3x\log x)\ll\sqrt x\log x$. Then (C1).
(3) From (1), $I(N)=e^\gamma\log x\,(1+E(x)+O(x^{-1/2}/\log x)+O(E^2))$ and from (2), $e^\gamma\log\log N=e^\gamma\log x+e^\gamma R(x)/x+O(x^{-1/2}\log x)+O(x^{2\Theta-2}\log^4x)$. Subtract: $\Delta(N)=e^\gamma[\log x\,E(x)-R(x)/x]+$errors, and by the exact Mertens identity (C3), $\log x\,E(x)-R(x)/x=\log x\cdot U(x)$.
(4) $|U(x)|\ll\int_x^\infty t^{\Theta-2}\log t\,dt\ll x^{\Theta-1}\log x$ by (C1), and $x\asymp\log N$ by (2). $\square$

**Proposition A1 (Robin's Proposition 1, additive form; classical).** Let $N'<N''$ be consecutive CA numbers and $N'\le n\le N''$. Then $\Delta(n)\le\max\{\Delta(N'),\Delta(N'')\}$.
*Proof.* There is a common $\epsilon$ for which both $N'$ and $N''$ maximize $\sigma(m)/m^{1+\epsilon}$ (the maximizer is a nonincreasing step function of $\epsilon$; at a jump both values are maximizers). Hence $\log I(n)\le\ell(\log n)$, where $\ell(u):=\log I(N')+\epsilon(u-\log N')$ is affine with $\ell(\log N')=\log I(N')$ and $\ell(\log N'')=\log I(N'')$. On $[\log N',\log N'']$ the function $h(u)=e^{\ell(u)}-e^\gamma\log u$ is convex (sum of a convex exponential and the convex $-e^\gamma\log u$), so $h(u)\le\max\{h(\log N'),h(\log N'')\}$, and $\Delta(n)\le h(\log n)$. $\square$

**Theorem A2 (upper bound for the Robin excess; IMPORTED I1).** There is an absolute, effectively computable $C$ such that for all $n\ge3$,
$$\frac{\sigma(n)}{n}\le e^\gamma\log\log n+C\,\frac{(\log\log n)^2}{(\log n)^{1/8}}\qquad\text{and for every }\epsilon>0:\quad \frac{\sigma(n)}{n}\le e^\gamma\log\log n+C_\epsilon(\log n)^{-1/8+\epsilon}.$$
With I1' the exponent $1/8$ may be replaced by $1-0.874957=0.125043$. For general $\Theta$: $\Delta(n)\ll(\log n)^{\Theta-1}(\log\log n)^2$.
*Proof.* By Lemma A0(4) there are $C_0,N_0$ with $\Delta(N)\le C_0(\log N)^{\Theta-1}(\log\log N)^2$ for all CA $N\ge N_0$. For $n\ge N_0$ choose consecutive CA $N'\le n\le N''$ with $N'\ge N_0$ (CA numbers are unbounded). Since only one new prime can enter at a time ($p\mapsto\log(1+1/p)/\log p$ is injective), $P^+(N'')\le2P^+(N')$, so $\log N''\le3\log N'$ for large $N'$ by Lemma A0(2). Proposition A1 gives $\Delta(n)\le C_0\max_{N\in\{N',N''\}}(\log N)^{\Theta-1}(\log\log N)^2\le3C_0(\log n)^{\Theta-1}(\log\log n)^2$. Finitely many $n<N_0$ adjust $C$. $\square$

*Comparison.* Robin (1984) unconditional: $I(n)\le e^\gamma\log\log n+0.6483/\log\log n$ ($n\ge3$, equality at $n=12$). Vinogradov–Korobov input (the best previously available route through Lemma A0) gives $\Delta(n)\ll\exp(-c(\log\log n)^{3/5}(\log\log\log n)^{-1/5})$, still not a power of $\log n$. Under RH (Ramanujan/Robin): $\limsup\Delta(n)\sqrt{\log n}\le-1.393$. Theorem A2 is thus the first power-of-$\log n$ upper bound and sits strictly between the classical unconditional and the RH-conditional statements.

**Theorem A3 (lower bound if RH is false; reconstruction of Robin 1984 §4 — I could not verify the theorem number or the exact printed range of $b$ in Robin's paper; the statement form "$C\log\log n/(\log n)^b$, $0<b<1/2$, infinitely many $n$" is quoted from memory and is consistent with the mechanism below).** Suppose $\Theta>1/2$. Then for every $b>1-\Theta$ there is $c_b>0$ and infinitely many CA numbers $N$ with
$$\frac{\sigma(N)}{N}\ge e^\gamma\log\log N+c_b\,\frac{\log\log N}{(\log N)^{b}}.$$
*Proof.* Step 1 ($U(x)=\Omega_\pm(x^{-b})$ for $1-\Theta<b<1/2$). Define $\Phi(u)=\int_2^\infty R(t)t^{-u-1}dt=-\frac{P'(u)}{u}-\frac{2^{1-u}}{u-1}$ ($P$ the prime zeta function), meromorphic on $\Re u>1/2$ with simple poles exactly at the zeros $\rho$ of $\zeta$ (residue $-m_\rho/\rho$; the poles of $-\zeta'/\zeta(u)/u$ and $-2^{1-u}/(u-1)$ at $u=1$ cancel), hence holomorphic on $\{\Re u>1/2,\ |\Im u|<14\}$. For $\Re s>\Theta-1$, Fubini and $t^{-s-2}/\log t=\int_{s+1}^\infty t^{-u-1}du$, $t^{-s-2}/\log^2t=\int_{s+1}^\infty(u-s-1)t^{-u-1}du$ give
$$\mathcal MU(s)=-\frac1s\Big[K_0-\int_{s+1}^\infty\big(1+(u-s-1)\big)\Phi(u)\,du\Big],\qquad K_0=\int_2^\infty\frac{R(t)(1+\log t)}{t^2\log^2t}dt .$$
The bracket vanishes at $s=0$, so $\mathcal MU$ is holomorphic on a neighbourhood of the real half-line $(-1/2,\infty)$, and it has a non-removable singularity at every $s=\rho-1$ (its derivative there is $-\Phi(s+1)+(\text{log-type term})$, a simple pole plus a logarithm). Now fix $b\in(1-\Theta,1/2)$ and $C>0$ and suppose $U(x)\ge-Cx^{-b}$ for all large $x$. The eventually nonnegative, locally integrable function $U+Cx^{-b}$ has Mellin transform $\mathcal MU(s)+C/(s+b)$, converging for $\Re s>\Theta-1$; by Landau's theorem (the repository's tail form is `reviews/C/pass4-math-completion/proofs/MELLIN_ANALYTIC_GAPS.md` M3) its real abscissa of convergence $\sigma_c$ is a singularity. The same with $-U$. Step 2 (transfer to CA numbers). $U$ is continuous and for consecutive primes $p\le x<p'$, $|U(x)-U(p)|\le(p'-p)\max_{[p,p']}|R(t)|(1+\log t)/(t^2\log^2t)\ll x^{\Theta-1.475}\log x=o(x^{-b})$ using $p'-p\ll p^{0.525}$ (Baker–Harman–Pintz) and $\Theta<0.975$; so there are infinitely many primes $p$ with $U(p)\ge(C/2)p^{-b}$. ## 4. Errata and clarifications after the referee pass (note R)

- (C1): the label "Chebyshev" means the prime number theorem with power error; $|\gamma|\ge14$ for zeta, so no small-$|\rho|$ issue.
- Theorem A2, step $P^+(N'')\le2P^+(N')$: the injectivity of $p\mapsto\log(1+1/p)/\log p$ only excludes two new primes entering at the same $\epsilon$; the conclusion follows instead from $a_p$ nonincreasing in $p$, which forces the new prime to be the next prime $\le2x$. In the final inequality, $N'\le n\le N''\le N'^3$ gives $(\log N')^{\Theta-1}\le3^{1-\Theta}(\log n)^{\Theta-1}$ and $(\log\log N'')^2\le3(\log\log n)^2$. "First power-of-$\log n$ upper bound" means: as a consequence of any zero-free half-plane; the route is Robin's own and the only new ingredient is the imported theorem.
- Theorem D1: "in particular $\xi(s)\ne0$ there" restates the hypothesis. Theorem D2 holds at every finite order by the Riesz–Herglotz representation, with no growth condition.
- The symbol $\Theta$ here is the supremum over zeta's zeros; note L uses it for the supremum over all Dirichlet $L$-functions.
