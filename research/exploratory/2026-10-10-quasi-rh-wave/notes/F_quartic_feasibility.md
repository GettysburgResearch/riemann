# F. Feasibility of a quartic (Q(i), 4-fold cover) version of the QRH argument

Working notes, 10 Oct 2026. Sources: main paper `qrh_main.txt` (Prop. 5.1, §5.3, §7.2), Kintali `kintali_qrh.txt` (§2–3, App. B), C note (§§1.3–1.4, 4), and the papers fetched today (text copies in `scratchpad/dl/quartic/`):
DDHL = David–Dunn–Hamieh–Lin, *Quartic Gauss sums over primes and metaplectic theta functions*, arXiv:2306.11875 v5 (Jan 2026, to appear ANT); DFDH = de Faveri–Dunn–Hoffstein, *Non-orthogonality of the cubic and quartic large sieves via Rankin–Selberg*, arXiv:2607.07911 v2 (Jul 2026); Bröker–Hoffstein, *Fourier coefficients of sextic theta series*, arXiv:1312.0568; Chinta, *Double Dirichlet series and theta functions* (survey, chinta.ccny.cuny.edu/publ/patterson.pdf); Friedberg–Ginzburg, *Descent and theta functions for metaplectic groups* (JEMS); Gao–Zhao arXiv:1101.3646; Goldmakher–Louvel arXiv:1112.1642.

**Bottom line (details in §4).** The C note's Target Lemma Q1 is mis-stated, and once corrected the quartic route is *structurally* blocked on the direct side, independently of what is or is not known about θ₄'s coefficients. The known/unknown split is the reverse of what the C note assumed: the quartic Gauss sum ḡ₄(a) is the θ₄ coefficient at the **square** index a² (Suzuki), while the coefficients at **squarefree** indices are the undetermined ones (Patterson's conjecture τ₄(π)² = 2·Ḡ(π)). The Möbius pairing g(χ_a)² = J·g(χ_a²) therefore has to pair the index a² of the theta with a character of modulus a, i.e. with the non-periodic mask D ↦ (m/D)₄^{∓1} on the theta index μ = D²; no cusp-exchange reflection of OpenAI's type (Route A) can realise it. The only reflection that exists for the resulting direct series (it is Kubota's quartic Dirichlet series at frequency m³) is the Eisenstein functional equation (Route B), which keeps the row m in a *quartic* coupling and has conductor N(m)³. Consequently the balanced row energy is governed by the quartic large sieve at (Z, Z): ≪ Z^{4/3} (BGL) and ≫ Z^{5/4} *provably* (DFDH Thm 1.2, whose extremal sequence is exactly our row sequence), instead of the Z^{1+ε} that the cubic argument gets from the quadratic sieve. The Part-I boundary is then σ₀ = 1/2 − 1/(2N) + E/2 with E ≥ 5/4, i.e. σ₀ ≥ 1: **no zero-free region at all**, even under DFDH's conjecturally sharp quartic sieve; the numerical scan of §3.4 confirms σ₀ = 1 is the infimum over all (X, Y, Z) choices and both routes. The predicted 7/8 (Part I) and ≈13/16 (Part II) of the C note were artefacts of assuming E = 1. The transfer L(s, χ∘N) = L(s,χ)L(s,χχ₋₄) would have covered all Dirichlet L-functions but no non-norm Hecke character of Q(√−3); this is now moot.

## 2. What is known about θ₄ (survey)

**2.1 Hecke structure (Kazhdan–Patterson; Bröker–Hoffstein §1, Chinta survey §2, DDHL §1.2, §4.5).** With the Hecke operator T_{p⁴} and the normalised Gauss sums G_j(m,p) = N(p)^{−1/2}Σ_r (r/p)^j_n e(rm/p), for (m,p) = 1:
 λ_{p⁴}τ_n(mp^j) = τ_n(mp^{j+n}) + τ_n(mp^{j−n}) + N(p)^{−1/2}G_{j+1}(m,p)τ_n(mp^{n−2−j}), periodicity τ_n(mp^n) = N(p)^{1/2}τ_n(m).
For n = 4 this gives (Bröker–Hoffstein (1.2)): τ₄(mp³) = 0; **τ₄(mp²) = G₃(m,p)τ₄(m)** (determined); **τ₄(mp) = G₂(m,p)τ₄(mp)** (self-referential, G₂ = (m/p)₂ since the quadratic Gauss sum is trivial here) — so τ₄(mp) = 0 unless (m/p)₂ = 1, and τ₄(p) itself is **not** determined. "In general there are n/2 − 1 undetermined coefficients among 1, π, …, π^{n−1}" (DDHL p. 5); for n = 4 that one coefficient is τ₄(π). Deligne / Kazhdan–Patterson: this is the non-uniqueness of the local Whittaker functional for n ≥ 4 on GL₂ (uniqueness only for n ∈ {r, r+1} on GL_r, so for GL₂ only n = 2, 3).

**2.2 Suzuki 1983** (J. reine angew. Math. 340, 70–117), as recorded in DDHL (4.37)–(4.41) and DFDH (6.14)–(6.16), in the residue normalisation τ₄(δ;u) = Res_{s=5/4}ψ(δ,s;u), ψ(δ,s;u) = Σ_{c≡u (4)} g₄(δ,c)N(c)^{−s}: for m ≡ 1 (λ³) squarefree, (m,δ) = 1,
 (i) τ₄(m⁴δ;u) = τ₄(δ;u) [Thm 3]; (ii) τ₄(m³δ;u) = 0, m ≠ 1 [Thm 4];
 (iii) **τ₄(m²δ;u) = g₄(δ,m)·N(m)^{−3/4}·τ₄(δ;mu)·(±1)** [Thm 5; DDHL (4.39)] — so with δ = 1 the coefficients at squares are quartic Gauss sums: ψ^{(4)}(a²) = g̃₄(a)N(a)^{−1/4} (DDHL (1.10)); in the K-Bessel normalisation of (4.35)/(6.10) the coefficient at a² has modulus 1;
 (iv) τ₄(mδ;u) = 0 unless g₂(mδ/m₁, m₁) = N(m₁)^{1/2} for every m₁ \| m [Thm 6].
Nothing is known about τ₄(π) for π prime (DDHL p. 17: "say nothing about ψ_β(π)").

**2.3 Patterson's conjecture (Patterson 1984; Eckhardt–Patterson, Proc. LMS 64 (1992) 225–264, Conj. 2.11; Eckhardt's 1989 Göttingen thesis).** D₂(w) := ζ_K(4w−1)Στ₄(m)²N(m)^{−w} (Rankin–Selberg of θ₄ with itself, not its conjugate) equals D₁(w)² where D₁(w) = ζ_K(4w−1)ΣG₁(1,m)N(m)^{−w} is the first Fourier coefficient of E^{(4)}; both sides have identical gamma factors and double poles at w = 3/4. Consequences: τ₄(m)² = G₃(1,m)Σ_{d₁d₂=m}(d₁/d₂)₂ for m squarefree and **τ₄(π)² = 2·G₃(1,π)** ("the square of the coefficient at a good prime is twice a normalised Gauss sum", Friedberg–Ginzburg p. 1914). Status: open over number fields; proved over F_q(T) by Hoffstein (1992); "shown to hold on average by Bump–Hoffstein [BH89, pp. 6–8]"; Suzuki (1997, function fields) proved the weaker argument statement τ₁(p)G₁(1,p) = τ₃(p); Friedberg–Ginzburg construct L² descent functions on the 4-fold cover whose coefficients satisfy the conjectured relation at a positive-density set of primes but cannot show they equal θ₄. Note that even if proved, the coefficient at a squarefree index would be a *square root* of a Gauss sum, determined only up to sign — useless for an Euler-product identity.

## 3. Quartic bookkeeping

**3.1 The pairing and the completed index (corrected).** Over Q(i), λ = 1+i, primary c ≡ 1 (λ³), χ_c = (·/c)₄, g̃₄(c) = N(c)^{−1/2}Σ_d(d/c)₄ě(d/c), \|g̃₄(c)\| = 1 for c squarefree. Hasse–Davenport/Jacobi: g(χ_π)² = J(χ_π,χ_π)g(χ_π²), J(χ_π,χ_π) = −χ_π(−1)·π̄ (Ireland–Rosen Ch. 9), so g̃₄(c)² = μ(c)·(explicit ray phase)·α(c)^{±1}·g̃₂(c): the Möbius sign survives, as in the C note. **But** the Gauss sum is the θ₄ coefficient at index c² (Suzuki (iii)), so the pairing must be: theta index μ = D², D = a b² (a squarefree), coefficient τ₄(D²) ∝ \|b\|·ḡ₄(a)·(reciprocity phases), paired with the character (m/D)₄^{∓1} of modulus D, orientation chosen so that Poisson in m returns the *same* Gauss sum (product g² = J·g₂); the opposite orientation gives g·ḡ = 1 (the degenerate, Möbius-free, N = 2-type pairing). The b²-part carries (m/b)₄^{∓2} = (m/b)₂: quadratic, the analogue of χ_n³ in the cubic L₀. Hence the quartic direct series is
 T₄(t,Ψ) = L(4t − 1/2, Ψ²)·Σ_{a sf} ḡ₄(a)Ψ(a)q_a^{−2t} (Ψ(D) = (m/D)₄^{−1}ν(D), zeros retained),
and the squarefree part is **Kubota's quartic series at frequency m³** (or m⁻¹): (m/c)₄^{−1}ḡ₄(c) = conj((m³/c)₄^{−1}g₄(c)) = conj(g₄(m³,c)) for (m,c) = 1, both sides 0 otherwise; the degenerate orientation is the frequency-m series g₄(m,c) = (m/c)₄^{−1}g₄(1,c).
Completion: the fourth-power completion c b⁴ with weight N(b) (ζ_K(4s−1)-type, the Eisenstein normalisation) or the square-index completion a b⁴ with weight \|b\| (ζ_K(2w−1/2)); either way the completed index on which the probe character acts is D = ab² (not "c n²" as the C note guessed; the index of θ₄ is D², and non-square indices cannot carry the pairing).

**3.2 Why no cusp reflection (Route A) exists.** OpenAI's reflection realises the mask on the theta index by finite Fourier inversion, i.e. the mask must be a *periodic* function of the index μ. The required mask μ = D² ↦ (m/D)₄^{−1} is not: for m = pq with N(q) ≡ 5 (8) and D' ≡ D (p), D' ≡ −D (q) one has D'² ≡ D² (mod m) but (m/D')₄ = (m/D)₄·(−1/q)₄ = −(m/D)₄. More generally every periodic multiplicative mask of μ restricted to p² ∥ μ is χ_p^{2j}, quadratic or trivial, whose Poisson Gauss sum g₂(p) multiplies τ₄(p²) ∝ ḡ₄(p) to give μ(p)·g₄(p)·(unit) (from g(χ²)ḡ(χ) = −χ(−1)α(π)N(π)^{1/2}g(χ)), a Möbius-twisted *quartic Gauss-sum* series, not 1/L; and at p ∥ μ the unknown τ₄(p) enters the principal-row Euler product (even under Patterson's conjecture it is ±√2·Ḡ^{1/2}, not an Euler factor). So the j = 1 twist of the full θ₄, which *does* have a reflection with quadratic column coupling χ_p^{−(j+1)} = χ_p^{−2}, has the wrong Poisson pairing, and the Möbius-pairing series is not a twist of θ₄ at all. This is a structural obstruction; the Rankin–Selberg L² bounds of DFDH do not touch it. (Conceptually: the twisted quartic Kubota series has twist-conductor N(m)¹ with a degree-2 gamma factor, so it is not the Mellin transform of any GL(2,Q(i)) form; it is the "standard L-series of the theta function on the 4-fold cover of GL(3)" (Chinta survey p. 6), the only Kazhdan–Patterson object with g₄ at squarefree index — but its reflection would be a GL(3) Voronoi formula with metaplectic Kloosterman sums and dual length ≍ q_c³/X, with no quadratic sieve in sight.)

**3.3 Route B (Kubota/Eisenstein functional equation) and the row energy.** The frequency-m³ series does have the functional equation of Prop. 4.3 (s ↔ 1−s, 24-cusp scattering matrix, poles at zeros of ζ_{Q(i),λ}(4s−3)-type denominators unless the completion is kept as a factor). Two consequences:
 (i) the frequency is unchanged, so the row m enters the dual through (m³/c)₄^{−1} = (m/c)₄ = (c/m)₄·(sector sign): a **quartic** coupling; the dual coefficients are the twisted-cusp Gauss sums g_{4,j}(m³,c), i.e. again DFDH's extremal sequence;
 (ii) the conductor is N(m³) = M³ (rows of norm M), so for completed length Z the dual length is M³/Z: at the balanced point M = Z the "reflection" lengthens the sum (Z → Z²) and is useless; one must instead bound the row energy directly by the quartic sieve.
Row energy E(M,Z) := log_Z Σ_{m≍M}\|C(Z;m)\|² with coefficients ḡ₄(c)q_c^{−1/2} (Σ_c q_c^{−1} ≍ 1):
 direct sieve: E = max(ρ, 1, 2(ρ+1)/3) (BGL), conj. max(ρ, 1, 3ρ/4+1/2, ρ/2+3/4) (DFDH 1.3), with M = Z^ρ;
 Route B: E_B = max(ρ, 3ρ−1, (2/3)(4ρ−1)) (BGL), conj. max(ρ, 3ρ−1, (9ρ−2)/4, (11ρ−3)/4);
 and unconditionally E ≥ max(ρ, 1, 3ρ/4+1/2, ρ/2+3/4) at the balanced regime by DFDH Thm 1.2, since our row sequence is their extremal sequence up to the sector signs and the smooth weight. At ρ = 1: **5/4 ≤ E ≤ 4/3**, versus E = 1 for the cubic argument.

**3.4 Part I.** Kintali (2),(4),(5): rows m of norm Q₀ = XY, completed length Z, Poisson frequencies H = u a^N with Σ_a q_a^{−Nz} = ζ_F(Nz), pole at z = 1/N, Mellin weight X^{1/2−z}Z^{x+z−1}Y^{w−1}. Principal residues (w = 1, z = 1/N): X^{1/2−1/N}Z^{x−1+1/N}, so with X = Z^a
 C(s) = s − 1 + 1/N + (1/2 − 1/N)a; N = 6, a = 1/2: s − 2/3 ✓; **N = 4, a = 1/2: C(s) = s − 5/8** (shift 3/4, X^{1/4}).
Direct bound (Cauchy–Schwarz over rows, additive norm Σ\|A_m\|² ≪ (Q₀+Y²)/Y which is N-independent since \|a_{s,d}\| ≪ Y^{−3/2} only uses \|g_{χ_s}\| = q_s^{1/2}): \|I\| ≪ Q₀^{−1/2}((Q₀+Y²)/Y)^{1/2}Z^{E/2}, at balance Z^{−1/2}Z^{1/4}Z^{E/2}. Hence
 **σ₀(N,E) = 1/2 − 1/(2N) + E/2** (balanced): N = 6, E = 1: 11/12 ✓; N = 4, E = 1: 7/8 (the C note's number); **N = 4, E = 4/3: 25/24; N = 4, E = 5/4: 1.**
Scan over all (a,b) with ρ = a+b (script `scratchpad/work/bookkeeping.py`, with a Poisson-side constraint (Z/X)^{1−1/N}Y^{−1/2}(Z/X)^{−κ} ≤ 1 for the ≍Z/X nonprincipal rows, κ ∈ {1/2, 1/4, 0}): for N = 6 with the reflection energy max(ρ, 2ρ−1) the minimum is 11/12 at balance for κ = 1/4 (consistency check); for N = 4 with E = min(direct, Route B), BGL or conjectural, the infimum is **exactly 1** for every κ (attained at ρ = 1 conj., ρ = 2 BGL); only the *hypothetical* quadratic-coupling energy max(ρ,2ρ−1) would give 7/8. Note the general identity: with the conjecturally sharp n-th order sieve used directly (E = 1 + 1/n at balance, n = N) one gets σ₀ = 1 for every N; the entire gain 1/(2N) of the cubic argument comes from converting the sextic coupling into a quadratic one by the cusp reflection.

**3.5 Part II (formal).** With amplification slope α = 1 − 1/N = 3/4, prime length ℓ, the C-note bookkeeping gives C_b(s) = s − 1 + 1/N + l_x(1/2 − 1/N) + ℓ/N, \|J\| ≪ Z^{l_x/2 + b/(2N)}, l_x = (1−b−ℓ)/2, hence B_N(ℓ) = 1 − 1/(2N) − 3ℓ/(2N): **B₄(ℓ) = 7/8 − 3ℓ/8** (13/16 at ℓ = 1/6, 25/32 if ℓ_max scaled like 1/N = 1/4). These assume the direct-side exponent of a quadratic-coupling marked reflection; replacing it by the quartic-sieve energy adds (E−1)/2 ≥ 1/8, giving B₄(ℓ) ≥ 1 − 3ℓ/8 (conj.) or 25/24 − 3ℓ/8 (BGL), i.e. ≥ 15/16 at ℓ = 1/6 even conjecturally, and this is before checking the marked-reflection step, which for N = 4 does not exist (§3.2). The floor constraint: for N = 6 it is C₀(b,ℓ) = −1/4 + 5ℓ/4 + b/6 (b/N term and an ℓ-slope whose N-dependence — 3(1−1/N)ℓ/2 would give 9ℓ/8 for N = 4, a literal 5ℓ/4 reading leaves it unchanged — cannot be fixed from the available text); either way the constraint C_b(β_*) ≥ C₀ only bounds β_* from below (≈ 5/8–2/3) and is not what limits the quartic route.

## 4. Verdict

**Missing lemmas (precise), difficulty, and what they would give.**

*Q1′ (square-index cusp reflection — what the C note's Q1 should have said).* For Ψ in a finite family of ray characters times Π_p χ_p^{j_p} (quartic Kummer symbols at moving primes), the series T₄(t,Ψ) = L(4t−1/2, Ψ²)Σ_{a sf}ḡ₄(a)Ψ(a)q_a^{−2t} continues to an entire function of polynomial growth and satisfies an identity of the form (5.1): a finite combination of dual sums Σ_μ d(μ)α(μ)ϑ(μ)q_μ^{−1/2}Π_pB_p(μ)V^♯(q_μX/q_c²) with explicit d supported on a "squarefree × square" set, with *quadratic* column factors χ_R(μ)² at row primes, no L-function denominators, and dual scale q_c²/X. **Status: false as stated, not merely unknown** — the mask D ↦ (m/D)₄^{−1} is not a periodic function of the θ₄ index D² (§3.2), the only available functional equation (Kubota, Prop. 4.3 of DDHL) keeps the frequency and so the quartic coupling, and its conductor is N(m)³ rather than N(m)². A substitute would be an automorphic object on GL(2,Q(i)) whose coefficient at squarefree c is g̃₄(c); the twist-conductor N(m)¹ of the twisted Kubota series shows no such GL(2) object exists (converse-theorem obstruction); the GL(3) 4-fold-cover theta has the right coefficients but a GL(3) Voronoi reflection with metaplectic Kloosterman sums (dual length q_c³/X) and no quadratic-sieve step.

*Q2 (local identity).* The quartic Lemma 7.1: with the completed index D = ab², Poisson frequencies u a⁴, g(χ_π)² = J g(χ_π²), four valuation cases, giving ζ_{Q(i)}(4z)L(w,ψ_u)/L(x,ηψ_u)·H. **Routine** (all inputs are explicit: Suzuki (iii) and the Jacobi-sum identity), and it is the one place where the quartic structure is *better* than the cubic one (fewer cases, slope 3/4). Pointless without Q1′.

*Q3 (row energy).* Σ_{m≍Z}\|Σ_{c≍Z}(m/c)₄^{−1}ḡ₄(c)q_c^{−1/2}V\|² ≪ Z^{1+ε}. **Provably false**: DFDH Thm 1.2 gives ≫ Z^{5/4−ε} (this sum is their extremal sequence), and BGL gives ≪ Z^{4/3+ε}; closing the gap 5/4 ↔ 4/3 is DFDH's Conjecture 1.3 ("any power saving on (AB)^{2/3} for n ≥ 4 would be a substantial advance"), and even its truth gives σ₀ = 1.

*Q4 (coefficient control).* Mean-square bounds for θ₄ at all indices and cusps: **done** (DFDH Lemma 7.3, Prop. 8.2, Lemma 8.4), and exact values at squares (Suzuki). The undetermined values τ₄(π) are irrelevant to the obstruction.

**Predicted boundary.** Part-I-type: σ₀ = 1/2 − 1/8 + E/2 with 5/4 ≤ E ≤ 4/3, i.e. σ₀ ∈ [1, 25/24] — **nothing**, for every choice of X, Y, Z and whether or not Route B is used; the C note's 7/8 presupposed E = 1. Part-II-type: ≥ 15/16 formally and in fact undefined since the marked reflection does not exist. The only scenario in which the quartic family would deliver 7/8 (Part I) / ≈ 25/32–13/16 (Part II) is a new GL(2)-type cusp reflection with quadratic coupling for the twisted quartic Kubota series, which the conductor count rules out.

**Transfer.** Had it worked: for a Dirichlet character χ, L(s, χ∘N_{Q(i)/Q}) = L(s,χ)L(s,χχ₋₄), so a zero-free region for all finite-order Hecke L-functions over Q(i) would give one for all Dirichlet L-functions (and for ζ via χ = 1: ζ(s)L(s,χ₋₄)). Hecke characters of Q(√−3) are covered only when they are of the form χ∘N (then L(s,χ)L(s,χχ₋₃), two Dirichlet L-functions); genuinely non-norm finite-order Hecke characters of Q(√−3) (the family the cubic proof actually handles) would **not** be covered. The two routes are complementary in scope, not nested; but since the quartic one yields no region, the point is moot.

**What survives from the quartic literature for the cubic programme.** (1) DFDH's Zagier-regularised Rankin–Selberg machinery (Props. 5.1, 5.4, 8.1) is exactly the "Lindelöf-on-average for Kubota's series" input needed to make the bias-corrected sextic row count (C note, direction 2) unconditional. (2) DDHL's observation that the Kubota series at frequency a² has conductor N(a)² and needs an average bound (1.11) rather than convexity is the quartic shadow of the same phenomenon. (3) The identity "direct n-th order sieve ⇒ σ₀ = 1 exactly" makes precise why the cubic reflection-to-quadratic is the engine of the whole method and why the (KD)^{2/3} term can only be attacked through its explicit bias main term, not through a better inequality.
