# I. Attack on the mixed second moment  Σ_u |M_r(u) S_m(u)|²  (Target Lemma M) through the Section 17 machinery

Sources: main paper `dl/qrh_main.txt` (line refs), notes `B_bootstrap_barrier.md`, `A_exponent_model.md`;
numerics `scripts/mixed_moment.py` (log `scripts/log_mixed.txt`); literature datum DIPP `dl/dipp_2607.27131.txt`.
Verdict up front: **the factor 2 in r + 2z is structural, not bookkeeping; Target Lemma M is not provable by the
Section 17 architecture, and neither is any inverse capacity r + z/c_I ≤ 1 with c_I > 1/2.** Details, exact
line numbers, the numerics for the hypothetical dials, and the only idea I think has a chance, below.

## 0. The objects, in the paper's own coefficient algebra

Everything hinges on the Gauss-sum identities (4.7), line 814: for squarefree c,
    γ₂(c)³ = μ(c)α(c),   γ₁(c)γ₂(c) = μ(c)α(c)G(c),   |G| = 1,  G a finite-ray phase,  α a (Grössencharacter-type) unit phase.
So on squarefree ideals **Möbius is the cube of the normalized cubic Gauss sum**: μ = γ₂³ ᾱ. Mod finite-ray phases
and powers of α, the coefficient classes generated by θ := ᾱγ₂ ("theta", the canonical coefficient of Prop. 5.1,
line 1416: D(s,Ψ)=Σ γ₂(n)ᾱ(n)Ψ(n)q_n^{-s}) are the six classes θ^k, k mod 6, with θ³ = μ:
    θ⁰ = 1 (plain),  θ¹ = θ,  θ² = μ·θ̄,  θ³ = μ (Möbius),  θ⁴ = μ·θ,  θ⁵ = θ̄.
Two operations act on these classes:
* **Poisson in the row u** (Lemma 17.5, line 7305) multiplies the column coefficient by the Poisson Gauss sum
  γ₋₁(z), and the paper records (line 8858) the identity μ(z)γ₋₁(z) = ᾱ(z)γ₂(z)χ_z(−1)G(z), i.e. γ₋₁ ≡ μθ = θ⁴.
  Hence Poisson maps  μ → θ  (Möbius polynomial ↦ canonical theta sum: this IS Section 17)  and  1 → μθ
  (plain polynomial ↦ Möbius-twisted theta: NOT canonical).
* **Reflection** (metaplectic functional equation, Prop. 5.1, line 1389; marked version Cor. 14.1, line 5563;
  energy version Lemma 14.3, line 5861) exists for θ and θ̄ only. On the primal side the plain class 1 has instead
  the *Hecke* reflection in the column (Section 18.1, lines 9478–9560, length m ↦ (conductor) − m).

Table (R = reflectable):            primal: 1 (Hecke R), θ (R), θ̄ (R), μ (–), μθ (–), μθ̄ (–)
                                    dual  : μθ (–),      θ̄ (R), μ (–),  θ (R), μθ̄ (–), 1 (Hecke R)
The inverse witness M_r is reflectable only after Poisson; the plain witness S_m only before Poisson. The two
witnesses live on opposite sides of the duality. This is the whole story of (c) below.

## 1. Where "r + 2z ≤ m" and "2r + 8z ≤ 3m" come from (task 1a)

Initialization 17.6 (lines 8666–9232). The product M_u(Z^r)Q_u is rewritten (8670–8700) as ONE Möbius polynomial of
length D′ = r + z − 2G with a product-form mark d₀ of cap z₀ = z − G (G = overlap with the slot primes; take G = 0 for the
accounting). After ONE masked Poisson in u (Lemma 17.5) the canonical parameters are (17.69), (17.74):
    M^c_init = 2D′ − m − 2P₁   (dual rows k = d′h′),     F^c_init = N + V = D′ − P₁   (dual columns n of length D′−B−v, labels f = d′s),
and the puncture Q_init ≤ P₁ + G (17.84). The two canonical hypotheses (17.9), line 7382,
    F − M − Q − z₀ ≥ c_node,       4F − 3M − 6z₀ ≥ c_node,
become (lines 9146–9147)
    F − M − (P₁+G) − z₀ = m − r − 2z + 2G,        4F − 3M − 6z₀ = 3m − 2r − 8z + 10G + 2P₁.
So the ledger of z is exactly:
* **first z**: the prime product lengthens the Möbius column from r to r + z; Poisson turns column length into dual-row
  length with factor 2 (M = 2D′ − m) and into dual-column length with factor 1 (F = D′), so F − M = m − (r + z) drops by z.
  This is the honest length of the combined polynomial and is unavoidable (large-sieve quality would charge it too).
* **second z**: the explicit "− z₀" in the first canonical invariant: the dual rows must be shorter than the FREE part of
  the dual column (M ≤ N − z₀ instead of M ≤ N). This is a genuine loss relative to large-sieve quality.
* **8z = 2z + 6z₀**: 2z from the row doubling, 6z₀ from the second invariant, which is the Heath-Brown branch (see §2).
Neither hypothesis involves κ or a zero-free region (confirmed, grep 6923–9329: none).

## 2. What produces the "− z₀" and the "− 6z₀": the terminal reflected energy (task 2, the induction ledger)

The recursion of 17.5 (lines 7361–8665) carries z₀ through unchanged: child margins (17.51), lines 8343–8346,
    F_ch − M_ch − Q_new − z₀ ≥ c_node − 5η,    4F_ch − 3M_ch − 6z₀ ≥ c_node − 7η,
"the nominal cap z₀ is unchanged because the surviving mark is a subcollection" (8348). The recursion is z-neutral.
z₀ is charged ONLY at the terminal node, Lemma 14.3 (lines 5861–5890), through three formulas:
    (14.11)  T_d = 2H + 2A₀ + 2z_a − N_* − N₀ − 3B₀         (reflected dual length; the ACTIVE marked primes enter the
                                                            conductor of the metaplectic functional equation and are
                                                            doubled, like the row length 2H),
    (14.13)  u = min{v, z_a, (v + z_a)/3},
    (14.14)  E_ref = O/2 + max(H, v + ℓ_b) − S₀ − B₀ + z_a − u − ℓ_b − 2e_λ/3 − ½(T_d − v − 3ℓ_b − e_λ)₊ .
The term z_a − u = max{z_a − v, 0, (2z_a − v)/3} is EXACTLY the excess of Heath-Brown's cubic large sieve over large-sieve
quality in the bilinear form (prime tuples P, |P| ≍ Z^{z_a}) × (reflected dual index n, ≍ Z^v): Lemma 14.2, line 5682,
    (14.7)  Σ_k |Σ_P a(P)q_P^{-1/2} Σ_{n,b} β(n,b) χ_k(nb)³ χ_P(n)^{-2}|² ≪ (K + NB)·B·{N + L + (NL)^{2/3}},
and {N + L + (NL)^{2/3}}/N = Z^{z_a − u}. The (K + NB) factor is the quadratic large sieve (large-sieve quality, the rows
couple to the dual only through the quadratic part χ_k(·)³ after reflection); the cubic defect (NL)^{2/3} sits in the
tuple variable only. Then, with (14.17) line 6075,  T_d − S₀ − B₀ ≤ 2M − O + Q + V − N_* + 2z_a, the two branches of
the proof of Lemma 17.2 (lines 7448–7476) read:
* row branch (max = H):            E_ref ≤ M + z_a − u ≤ M + z₀     ⇒ needs  F − M − z₀ ≥ c   [short dual v ≪ z_a: Heath-Brown loss]
* column branch, u = z_a (v ≥ 2z_a): E_ref ≤ O/2 + (T_d − S₀ − B₀) ≤ M − O/2 + 2z_a − z₀ − c + 5d  (17.12)
                                    ⇒ needs F − M − z₀ ≥ c AGAIN        [long dual: conductor doubling 2z_a in T_d]
* column branch, u ≠ z_a (u ≥ v/2): E_ref ≤ O/2 + (T_d − S₀ − B₀)/2 + z_a ≤ M/2 + 3z₀/2 ⇒ needs 4F − 3M − 6z₀ ≥ c  (17.14)
                                    [this is the (NL)^{2/3} term; it is the source of the 6z₀, hence of 2r + 8z ≤ 3m]
So the second z has TWO independent sources, each sufficient on its own:
  (α) in the short-dual regime, Heath-Brown's (AB)^{2/3} in the tuple × dual bilinear form (Lemma 14.2);
  (β) in the long-dual regime, the marked primes join the conductor of the theta functional equation (2z_a in (14.11)),
      and the energy of the reflected sum is its length (large sieve with columns longer than rows is sharp).
Equivalently: the canonical lemma is "Cauchy–Schwarz over the prime tuples + large-sieve-quality theta bound per tuple",
which is exactly M ≤ N − z₀. To reach r + z ≤ 1 one would need large-sieve quality for the coefficient (theta × mark),
i.e. both (α) a cubic large sieve without the (AB)^{2/3} term for product-form tuple coefficients against theta-type
duals, and (β) a way to keep the mark out of the conductor. [DFDH26] (cited by DIPP, line 117) proves the (AB)^{2/3}
cannot be removed in general; and (β) is a property of the functional equation itself.

## 3. Which properties of Q_z are used (task 1b)

1. Disjoint prime lists ⇒ every tuple product is squarefree and the mark is of product form (14.1), line 5509:
   d(A) = Σ_{tuples} Π a_i(p_i) 1_{p_i | A}. The product form (not just the divisor bound) is used at every extraction:
   priority identity (17.15)–(17.16), lines 7500–7508, "Assignments only delete slots" (7430), overlap extraction with
   μ(n₀) = μ(c)μ(P₀) (8676), and Möbius inversion of the cube (17.10) needs d(h³A) = d_h(A).
2. Bounded coefficients |a_i(p)| ≤ 1 independent of the row and of the label (Lemma 14.2 needs a independent of k,n,b).
3. Bounded number of slots K_slot: the divisor losses are d_O(f)^{1+2K_slot} (17.80), so slot number ~ log Z is fatal.
4. NOT used: centering / absence of main terms. The principal (diagonal) contribution is the direct bound O(Z^{m+ε})
   (8757–8760) for arbitrary bounded coefficients. The "no main term" property of primes matters only in Section 18
   (rows inducing characters in Θ, 9360–9375). Also NOT used: any pointwise bound on the prime sums, any κ.
5. The marks must be marks on the dual theta sum: Lemma 17.2 says explicitly (lines 7004–7006) "There is no additional
   residual coefficient b(n), even one independent of k, f: arbitrary bounded weights are permitted only as the stated
   product of individual prime-slot coefficients."

## 4. Can S_m be substituted? Exactly which step breaks (task 1c, 2(i), 2(ii))

(i) Representation. Buchstab/fundamental-lemma decomposition: ideals a ≍ Z^m with all prime factors > Z^η and at most
1/η of them are a sum of O_η(1) "pattern" sums, each a product of ≤ 1/η prime polynomials over disjoint dyadic windows
(the windows can be made disjoint by refining the dyadic mesh; the paper allows arbitrary slot lengths, 6975–6979).
Each pattern IS a Q_z with z = m and coefficients a_i(p) = ν(p). So S_m^{(η)} := (Z^η-rough part of S_m) is in the scope of
Lemma 17.1 and one gets, rigorously within the paper,
    Σ_{u≍U} |M_r S_m^{(η)}|² ≪ U^{1+ε}      whenever  r + 2m ≤ 1 − c  and  2r + 8m ≤ 3 − c.
The Z^η-smooth part (density ρ(s/η) of the mass, s = smooth length) cannot be absorbed: the Cauchy-over-smooth-part loss
is Z^{s}ρ(s/η)² and ρ(s/η)² ≤ Z^{−s} requires η ≲ log log Z / log Z, i.e. ~log Z slots, which breaks item 3 of §3.
(This is a technicality; even if fixed, the capacity is r + 2m, useless.)
(ii) Amplifier factorization: |M_{1−m}S_m| large ⇒ |M_{1−m}Q_z| large with z ≈ m is NOT provable in general: the
pattern decomposition bounds S_m by the largest pattern product up to Z^{o(1)} only for the rough part; the plain
witness is large for every class (Prop. 8.3, 3549–3685, uses only the one zero via the Γ-integral), while the prime
sums are large only in the x = 1/2 classes (Lemma 19.1, 11016). In the binding class x = 1/2 the prime sums ARE large and
Lemma 17.1 already uses them with z_M = (1−r)/2; the factorization buys nothing new and reduces to the factor 2.
(iii) The exact step that breaks for a plain factor. After Poisson in u, the dual coefficient on c = l·a (l from M_r,
a from S_m) is θ(l)·γ₋₁(a)·(cross phases) = θ(c)·μ(a)·(phases) by (4.7)/(8858): a theta coefficient on the whole index
carrying a Möbius mark Σ_{a|c, q_a≍Z^m} μ(a)ϑ(a). A Möbius mark over windowed a IS a product-form mark with a_i(p) = −ϑ(p),
so the Section 17 machinery accepts it — as a mark of length m, charged 2m: the mark enters the conductor of the theta
functional equation ((14.11)) and the tuple sum is bounded by Heath-Brown ((14.13)). There is no step at which the
plain factor is treated as a *column* rather than as a *mark*; the only column coefficient with a reflection is θ, θ̄.
The alternative order (reflect S_m first by Hecke, 9478–9560, then Poisson) fails because the Hecke root number is a
Gauss sum in the ROW variable u, so after Hecke reflection the row sum itself is a theta sum of length 1 and its Poisson
dual has length 2·2(r+1−m) − 1 > 1. There is no side of the duality on which both M_r and S_m are reflectable.

## 6. Deliverables (task 4)

(a) **Strongest mixed-moment lemma the Section 17 machinery proves.** Let S_m^{(η)} be the plain witness restricted to
ideals with no prime factor of norm < U^η (hence ≤ 1/η prime factors), decomposed into O_η(1) disjoint-window prime
products. Then for fixed η, c > 0 and every ε > 0,
    Σ_{u∈O, 0<q_u≪U, 6th-power-free} |M_r(u) S_m^{(η)}(u)|² ≪_{η,c,ε} U^{1+ε}   provided  r + 2m ≤ 1 − c,  2r + 8m ≤ 3 − c,
uniformly in the window coefficients; the full S_m cannot be included (smooth part forces ~log U slots). Capacity region:
{(r, m): r + 2m ≤ 1}. On the detector's splits (r ≥ t − ½, m = t − r, t ≥ 1) this forces m = 0: the lemma is vacuous
for counting. Equivalently: Lemma 17.1′ with r + z/c_I ≤ 1 holds for c_I = 1/2 only.
(b) **The step that stops r + z ≤ 1.** The terminal reflected energy, Lemma 14.3: (14.11) line 5871 "T_d = 2H + 2A₀ +
2z_a − N_* − N₀ − 3B₀" (marked primes doubled in the reflected conductor) and (14.13)–(14.14) lines 5882–5886
"u = min{v, z_a, (v+z_a)/3}", "E_ref = … + z_a − u …", fed by Lemma 14.2 (14.7) line 5690 "(K + NB)B{N + L + (NL)^{2/3}}".

## 7. How Section 17 evades the DIPP 1/11 limit, and the one idea with a chance

DIPP (arXiv:2607.27131, Thm 10.5.2 line 3267, eq. (38) line 3337): twisted second moment Σ_a L(½,χ_a)²χ_a(b) with error
X^{5/6+ε}|rad b|^{5/12+ε} (r = 3, δ̃₁ = 5/6), hence a mollifier of length M = X^{1/11}: in our language an asymptotic mixed
moment at (r, m) = (1/11, 1/2), r + m = 13/22 — the barrier is Heath-Brown's (AB)^{2/3} (their line 113–117). The mixed
upper bound via Heath-Brown alone needs r + m ≤ ½. Section 17 reaches r ≤ 1 − c because (1) there is NO plain factor:
the Möbius polynomial, via μ = γ₂³ᾱ (4.7), is a theta-type object whose Poisson dual (×γ₋₁ ≡ μθ) is the canonical theta
sum, (2) the theta sum is bounded by its metaplectic functional equation + the QUADRATIC large sieve (the (K + NB) factor
of (14.7), which has large-sieve quality), and (3) the cubic Heath-Brown defect is confined to the mark variable, whose
length z ≤ (1 − r)/2 is small. DIPP's twist cost |b|^{5/12} and the paper's mark cost (z once in the column, once in the
conductor) are the same phenomenon: a twist enters the conductor. The mechanism absorbs a plain factor only as a mark
(cost 2m), never as a column, because the plain factor's dual class is μθ, not θ. It absorbs the Möbius factor for free
precisely because μ is the class that Poisson sends to θ.

Idea with a chance ("theta-cubed" route). Since μ = γ₂³ᾱ, the inverse witness is Σ_n ᾱ(n)γ₂(n)³ χ_n(u)W and the mixed
product M_r S_m is the sum over c = l·a of γ₂(l)³ᾱ(l)·1(a)·χ_c(u) — a convolution of the classes θ³ and θ⁰. After Poisson
every term is θ(c)·μ(a); the Möbius mark is the problem. But μ(a) = γ₂(a)³ᾱ(a) itself, so the mark is a *cube of Gauss
sums*: the dual coefficient is θ(l)·θ(a)⁴·ᾱ(a)² (classes 1 ⊗ 4). Classes 1 ⊗ 1 (theta × theta, i.e. the coefficient of
a theta-squared / Rankin–Selberg-type series) have a known structure (the metaplectic Eisenstein series on the 3-fold
cover, Rankin–Selberg of θ with itself, Patterson–Brubaker–Bump–Friedberg); classes 1 ⊗ 4 = θ ⊗ μθ do not. The only
witness pairs that stay inside the reflectable classes under Poisson are (μ, θ̄) and (θ, μθ̄)-type: so the *plain*
witness could be replaced by a "theta-bar witness" T_m(u) = Σ_a γ̄₂(a)α(a)χ_a(u)W(q_a/N), whose Poisson dual is μ (!) —
no. The pair that works is (M_r, Θ̄_m) with Θ̄_m having primal class θ̄ (dual class μ, non-reflectable) — also no. Scan
of all 6 × 6 pairs: the dual coefficient on c = l·a is reflectable for both factors iff both duals lie in {θ, θ̄}, i.e.
primal classes in {μ, θ}; the product M_r·Θ_r′ (Möbius × theta polynomial) has dual θ ⊗ θ̄ — a theta-type column with a
theta-type mark, for which Cor. 14.1's marked reflection does NOT apply as written but a Rankin–Selberg-type reflection
might. This is the only pair on which the "+2z" mechanism could conceivably be replaced by a "+z" mechanism, and the
theta polynomial Θ_r′(u) = Σ_a γ₂(a)ᾱ(a)χ_a(u)W is NOT a detector witness. So the useful statement of the idea is:
**build a detector whose second witness is a theta-type polynomial** (e.g. detect the zero of L(s,ψ_u) through the
metaplectic Eisenstein series / Rankin–Selberg of the cubic theta function twisted by ψ_u, in which L(s,ψ_u) appears as
a factor of the constant term or of the Dirichlet series of θ·θ̄-coefficients), so that the mixed moment is
Σ_u |M_r Θ_m|² with dual θ ⊗ θ̄, and then prove a θ×θ̄-reflection analogue of Cor. 14.1. I could not find such a detector
in the paper; everything else I tried (Hecke-reflect S_m first; positivity Σ|M S|² = Σ|1 + tail|²; row-weight |S_m|²;
twisted inverse moment Σ|M_r|²χ_u(a₁/a₂)) returns to the dual-row length 2(r + m) − 1 with a Möbius mark of length m.

Secondary, cheaper: the only *proof-internal* slack is Lemma 14.2's cubic defect in the SHORT-dual regime v < 2z_a with the
(K + NB) quadratic factor sharp; in the long-dual regime the conductor doubling is exact. Replacing the tuple-vs-dual
cubic large sieve by a bound exploiting that the tuple coefficients are PRODUCTS over slots (a multilinear, not bilinear,
form) might lower the second invariant 4F − 3M − 6z₀ (i.e. 2r + 8z ≤ 3m) but not the first; the first (r + 2z ≤ m) is
what binds in every application (A §3), so this does not move B.
