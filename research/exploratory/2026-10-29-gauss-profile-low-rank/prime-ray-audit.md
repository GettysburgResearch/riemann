# Fixed-ray prime cancellation: the remaining source interface

The desired balanced prime-overlap bound is

    Σ_(π prime in fixed good ray class) V(Nπ/Y) ξ(π) overline(α(π)) γ₂(π)
       ≪ Y^(11/12+ε),                                              (1)

with fixed smooth V and fixed finite-ray ξ. It would make the normalized prime/Gauss overlap `≪Y^(−1/12+ε)`. With compatible conventions, `μ(π)overline(γ₁(π))=overline(α(π))overline(G_finite(π))γ₂(π)` by quasi-RH (4.7). Its (5.10) changes γ₂ to the trace-additive cubic Gauss sum by the fixed finite factor `χ_π(λ)^2`. Thus (1) is a fixed finite-ray twist of a nonzero angular-mode cubic prime sum. The source's ray class can be refined to make each of the local factors constant, but this still requires cancellation in that class.

The September-25 first-moment paper's Theorem A.1 states `o(Y^(5/6)/log Y)` for the **unweighted union of primary prime ideals**, with each fixed nonzero angular mode; at ℓ=−1 this would be stronger than (1). It does not state a version for an arbitrary fixed finite-ray factor ξ or an individual fixed ray class. A cancellation estimate for a union does not logically imply cancellation on every component. Finite Fourier expansion of the class indicator simply exposes the missing twisted estimates; it does not prove them. Fixed S exclusions remove finitely many primes. For a norm twist of height |h|, a sharp-cutoff estimate with fixed constants can be transferred to a smooth profile at a cost O(1+|h|) by partial summation, but a growing-height statement still needs the corresponding loss budget.

The proof in its Appendix A has several separate places to replay before the fixed-ray extension is justified:

1. **A.2 / A.5–A.6:** the angular Voronoi formula and its explicit Type-I bound are stated for θ_ℓ and a cubic moving conductor. The fixed finite ξ changes the relevant cusp/local data. Quasi-RH Proposition 5.1 does give an **entire** completed series for its precise `overline α·γ₂·Ψ` coefficients with every fixed S-supported Ψ, so there is no theta pole in that particular completed series. Matching all A.5 terms and proving their level-uniform R and vertical bounds from the transformed local factors is still required; source constants may depend on the moving prime set.
2. **A.3 / Proposition A.5:** fixed-type Hecke functional equations remain available after a fixed finite twist, and common finite ξ factors may be absorbed in common coefficient vectors. The end cases, conductor-class partition and all uniform constants must nevertheless be replayed for the actual twisted decomposition; the printed proposition does not assert them for this enlarged class.
3. **A.4 / Lemma A.6 and Proposition A.7:** the (SW) condition must be re-established for the ξ-twisted sequence, and the prime-ideal theorem used for its largest-prime factor must be uniform after multiplication by ξ. The nonzero infinity type prevents a principal Hecke character, but this observation alone is not the whole (SW) proof. The dispersion diagonal and exceptional covariance require their exact twisted main terms and fixed local masks; they cannot be inferred merely from an absolute-value estimate for untwisted β.
4. **A.5 / final sharp-cutoff passage:** after the preceding interfaces are obtained, check that the twisted model, all cusp residues and their finite-ray phases are carried through the Vaughan decomposition and that the constants are fixed before the truncation and height ranges. An unweighted all-primary-prime asymptotic is insufficient evidence for this step.

The completed-series argument in [mobius-overlap.md](mobius-overlap.md) already handles the **full squarefree Möbius vector** for every fixed finite ray and fixed finite set of prime masks, without proving (1). It does not handle the prime restriction. Even a proof of (1), or of the stronger 5/6 exponent, would remove only the known fixed-profile Gauss component. Xu–Sang's `Y^(13/12)` residual is a bound on a fixed profile subspace, and even a hypothetical global residual of that size would still exceed the desired Y operator scale by `Y^(1/12)`. No global spectral decomposition is asserted.
