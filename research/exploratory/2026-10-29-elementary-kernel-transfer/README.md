# Low-height transfer from an elementary abelian kernel

**Status:** Proposed deduction from the cited Hecke and Dedekind zero-free regions, Stark's low-order theorem and standard Artin–Hecke identities. Independent proof review and a broader priority search are required. The analytic inputs are not reproved. There is no claimed new zero-free region for the Riemann zeta function and no claim of superiority to newer fixed-half-plane results.

Let `K/k` be a finite Galois extension with group `G`. Let `H<=G` have a normal elementary abelian subgroup `N` of order `P=p^f`, where `p` is an odd prime, with cyclic quotient `H/N` of order `r>=1`. No splitting, faithful action or fixed-point-free action is assumed. Write

```text
M=K^H,  E=K^N,  F=K^(H²),  D=|disc(K/Q)|,
g=gcd(2,r),    s=phi(r).
```

Here `H²` is the subgroup generated by squares. Assume `[F:Q]>=7`. The group `H/N` acts on the projective lines of the character group `N^`; let `t` be the smallest orbit size. One may instead use the minimum orbit size under `N_G(N)/N`. Put `w=(p-1)max{t,p^(f-1)}` and

```text
a_p = 1/15.10 if p=3, and 0.1149 if p>=5,
C = min{ a_p w, P s/15.10, P r/(g*11.51910) }  if r>=3,
C = min{ a_p w, P/11.51910 }                      if r=1 or 2.
```

Then, in the **explicit all-discriminant region**

```text
Re(s)>1-C/log D,       |Im(s)|<=0.13,
```

every irreducible Artin L-function of degree at least two, and every nonreal one-dimensional factor, is holomorphic and nonzero. Among real one-dimensional factors there is at most one simple real zero, and no other zero. The trivial factor has its usual pole at `s=1`, where every nontrivial irreducible factor is nonzero. If `k=Q` and `G` has no quotient of order two, `zeta_K` is zero-free in the same region apart from its pole at 1.

The height and coefficient inputs are Ahn–Kwon's [order-at-least-three theorem](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jnt.2014.06.008), its order-at-least-five coefficient `.1149` as quoted in [Zaman, equation (1.1)](https://arxiv.org/html/1502.05679), and [Das et al., Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.19319). The use of `.1149` has been checked against Zaman's quotation, not against the full original Ahn–Kwon proof.

## Subgroup and conductor identities

Since `N` has odd order, every element of `N` is a square in `N`, so `N<=H²`. Under the projection `H->H/N≅C_r`, the image of `H²` is the subgroup of squares of `C_r`. Thus

```text
|H²|=P r/g,      H/H²≅C_g,      d_F^(P r/g) <= D.
```

Both extensions `K/E` and `E/M` are abelian, even when `H` itself does not split. Their ordinary Artin–Hecke factorizations give the identity of meromorphic functions

```text
zeta_K/zeta_F = (product over omega in (H/N)^ with ord(omega)>2 of L_M(s,omega))
                 * (product over nontrivial lambda in N^ of L_E(s,lambda)).  (1)
```

All factors are the primitive Hecke L-functions with the complete Artin local factors: the first product omits exactly the characters of `H/N` trivial on its subgroup of squares; the second product comes from `K/E`. Each `lambda` has order `p`; each displayed `omega` has order at least three. Equation (1) follows from tower factorizations and does not need induced irreducibility or any power `r`.

Let `A_lambda=d_E N f_lambda`, with finite Artin/Hecke conductor `f_lambda`. Multiplication by `F_p^×` preserves the conductor of a nontrivial character; normalizer conjugation transports the conductor by a field automorphism of `E` and so preserves its absolute norm. If a projective orbit has size `t_lambda`, the combined scalar/conjugation orbit contains `(p-1)t_lambda` distinct characters all with the same `A_lambda`. The conductor–discriminant formula, including the trivial character's conductor, gives

```text
D = d_E * product_(lambda != 1) A_lambda,
log A_lambda <= log D / [(p-1)t_lambda].                      (2)
```

There is a second, independent conductor bound. Let `L_lambda=K^(ker lambda)`. The cyclic extension `L_lambda/E` has degree `p`, and its `p-1` nontrivial characters all have the same conductor as `lambda`. Hence `d_Llambda=d_E A_lambda^(p-1)`, while the tower `K/L_lambda/E` gives `D>=d_Llambda^(P/p)`. Therefore

```text
log A_lambda <= log D/[p^(f-1)(p-1)].
```

Combining this with (2) gives `log A_lambda<=log D/w`. The group-orbit count and the tower count must not be multiplied together.

For `r>=3`, the quotient product in (1) may be nonempty. For the cyclic extension `E/M`, choose a generator `omega0` of its character group, and let `Q=N f_omega0`. Its `phi(r)` faithful powers have the same conductor, and every other power has conductor dividing that of `omega0`. Thus

```text
d_E >= d_M^r Q^s >= (d_M Q)^s,
D >= d_E^P,
log(d_M N f_omega) <= log D/(P s).                            (3)
```

The inequality `s<=r` is used in the first line. All these conductors are finite conductors, and the relevant ray class groups are narrow if needed to account for real places.

## Analytic transfer

From the combined conductor bound, Ahn–Kwon's order-`p` estimate excludes zeros of every `lambda` factor throughout the claimed region. For `r>=3`, (3) and the order-at-least-three estimate exclude zeros of every `omega` factor. For `r=1` or `2`, the `omega` product is empty and no complement bound is needed. The `0.13` height is inside both input rectangles. Thus (1) is holomorphic and zero-free there, and away from 1

```text
ord_s zeta_K = ord_s zeta_F.                                  (4)
```

Because `[F:Q]>=7`, the Das rectangle contains the claimed region by `d_F^(Pr/g)<=D` (for r=1 or 2, Pr/g=P); there is at most one real zero there. The separate Ahn–Kwon Dedekind zero-free result at width `1/(2 log d_F)` and height `1/(2 log d_F)` ensures that any such real zero is simple. By (4), `ord_s zeta_K` is zero or one away from the pole at 1. Stark's low-order theorem supplies holomorphy of all irreducible Artin factors there. Their degree-weighted zero orders are nonnegative and sum to at most one, so all factors of degree at least two are nonzero; at a real exceptional point nonreal one-dimensional factors occur in conjugate pairs and cannot vanish either. The standard Brauer–Hecke theorem handles nontrivial factors at 1.

For the final assertion, a real one-dimensional character has order at most two; no quotient of order two leaves only the trivial factor. The actual region lies in `Re(s)>1/2`, since its lambda width is at most `.1149/log 2` and `[F:Q]>=7` gives `d_E>=2`. The cited [Platt–Trudgian verification](https://doi.org/10.1112/blms.12460) and the real-axis sign show `zeta_Q` has no zero there at `|t|<=.13`. Hence no factor of `zeta_K` vanishes away from its pole.

## Example

For `G=PSL_2(F_27)`, its Borel subgroup has `N=C_3^3`, `r=13`, and `[F:Q]=28` over `Q`. The action on nonzero vectors is multiplication by the subgroup of squares in `F_27^×`; its projective orbit has size `t=13`, so `w=26`. The two other entries of the minimum exceed `26/15.10`. Therefore every nontrivial irreducible Artin factor, and `zeta_K` apart from its pole, is zero-free in `Re(s)>1-(26/15.10)/log D` for `|t|<=.13`. [Zywina](https://doi.org/10.4153/S0008439522000534) realizes this group over `Q`, so the group family is nonempty. This example and numerical corollary are not claimed to be globally novel or the strongest known.

For `G=PSL_2(F_25)`, the Borel subgroup has `N=C_5²`, `r=12`, `t=3` and `[F:Q]=52`. Here the tower count improves `w` from 12 to 20 and gives coefficient `.1149*20=2.298`; the two other entries of the minimum are larger. This assertion is conditional on the same cited classical inputs, with no claim that this group has a particular realization over `Q`.

For comparison, the much thinner-height result of [Louboutin, Theorem 3](https://doi.org/10.7169/facm/1598), gives at most one real simple zero in `Re(s)>=1-.59110/log D`, `|Im(s)|<=1/(4 log D)`. Stark eliminates it for Galois extensions of `Q` without quadratic subfields. On the real axis, Louboutin gives the still larger coefficient `2(sqrt(2)-1)^2/(1-1/sqrt(5))=0.62075649...` with the same exceptional-zero conclusion. These statements apply beyond the elementary-kernel hypothesis and should be included when comparing any particular small coefficient.

## Automatic low-order bound at thinner heights

The degree hypothesis `[F:Q]>=7` and the Das entry of `C` can be removed if the height is restricted. Retain all other assumptions and set

```text
C_thin = min{a_p w, P phi(r)/15.10}.
```

If `F!=Q`, the same Artin conclusions hold for `Re(s)>1-C_thin/log D`, `|Im(s)|<=.13` and `|Im(s)|<1/(2 log d_F)`; if `F=Q`, they hold for `Re(s)>1-C_thin/log D`, `|Im(s)|<=.13`. In the no-quadratic-subfield case over `Q`, the corresponding Dedekind zeta region is zero-free away from the pole.

Indeed, for any nontrivial `lambda` in `N^`, the conductor bound gives `C_thin/log D<=a_p/log A_lambda`, while `F⊆E`, `[E:F]=r/g>=2` and `A_lambda>=d_E` imply

```text
d_F^(r/g)<=d_E<=A_lambda,
C_thin/log D <= a_p g/(r log d_F) <= .1149/(2 log d_F)
                 < 1/(2 log d_F).
```

For `F!=Q`, Ahn–Kwon's separate Dedekind theorem then gives at most one real simple zero throughout this thinner-height region, and the same tower and Stark argument applies. The strict height inequality is required by the quoted theorem. If `F=Q`, then `H=G=H²` and `k=Q`, so `G` has no quotient of order two; additionally `[E:Q]=r>=3`, whence `A_lambda>=d_E>=2` and the region lies in `Re(s)>1/2`. The partial-RH verification and real-axis sign exclude the `zeta_Q` factor at `|t|<=.13`, giving the nonexceptional conclusion. This is an additional proposed corollary, subject to the same source and independent-review qualifications as the theorem above.

## Symmetric and alternating regular-affine examples

Let P=p^f≥7 with p odd, and let N≅F_p^f act regularly by translations on P letters. In S_P, the full normalizer contains the affine group N⋊GL(f,p). Its GL(f,p) part acts transitively on the nonzero dual vectors of N, so the projective orbit size in the theorem is `t=(P−1)/(p−1)` and `w=P−1`. Choose H=N⋊F_P^×, whose quotient is cyclic of order P−1. The other two entries in the theorem's minimum exceed the lambda entry, yielding

    C = 0.1149(P−1)  if p≥5,
    C = (P−1)/15.10  if p=3.

For A_P and f≥2, the normalizer contains N⋊SL(f,p). Every elementary transvection is an even permutation of F_p^f, since p is odd, so SL(f,p) lies in A_P; it is transitive on nonzero dual vectors. Thus its projective orbit size is again `(P−1)/(p−1)`. Take H=N⋊(F_P^×)², with quotient order `(P−1)/2`; it lies in A_P because translation is even and the sign of multiplication by a field element is its quadratic character in F_P. Its square subgroup has sufficiently large index, and the same coefficient C follows. The finite-field scalar normalizer gives the H needed in the theorem; the orbit count may use the larger normalizer in G.

These applications are not optimized A_P/S_P zero-free regions. For example, in A25 the disjoint cycles of lengths 5, 7, 11 give a cyclic subgroup of order 385 and totient 240; the cyclic transfer with the order-at-least-five input gives a much larger uniform coefficient than the 24 produced by this affine orbit. A direct comparison with all existing results or recent claimed fixed half-planes has not been performed.

A convenient corollary needs no cyclic normalizer quotient at all. Let N≤G be elementary abelian of order P=p^f for p odd, assume [K^N:Q]≥7, and let t be the minimum projective orbit size under N_G(N)/N. Taking H=N (so r=1) gives the same conclusion with `C=min{a_p(p−1)max(t,p^{f−1}), P/11.51910}`. In this case the only Hecke factors needed are those of the abelian extension K/K^N; the normalizer is used solely for conductor-orbit counting.
