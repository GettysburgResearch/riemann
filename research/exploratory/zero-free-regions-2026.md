# Zero-free-region literature benchmarks (28 October 2026)

Status: exploratory literature reconnaissance; no new zero-free theorem is claimed.

Scope: zeta, Dirichlet, and selected Hecke and Dedekind zeta families. The list is not an exhaustive priority search. A fixed half-plane and a height-dependent region answer different quantitative questions.

Exact sources or dependencies: primary preprints, their stated formalization scopes, and published articles linked below. Recent claims and any formalization-to-classical-statement correspondence have not been independently verified here.

What was actually run: source and abstract searches and inspection of the linked statements and formalization descriptions; no Lean build, proof replay, or independent review of the papers was performed.

Smallest remaining gap: to use any claimed fixed region as an imported theorem, check the exact proof and formal statement, definitions and pole conventions, and whether the needed analytic estimates are included. To claim novelty, compare the precise family and region against the latest versions of all relevant work.

## Recent fixed-half-plane claims

**Update, 28 October 2026, approximately 23:45 UTC:** The tracker and preprints below supersede smaller refinements listed later. These are reported source statements, not mathematical or formal audits performed here. The tracker target is all Dirichlet characters and does not by itself assert the stronger bound for arbitrary Hecke families.

* The [QuasiRiemannTracker PR #90](https://github.com/GTimG/QuasiRiemannTracker/pull/90) is merged and states `Re(s) > 8739461/10000000 = 0.8739461` for all Dirichlet L-functions, with the principal pole excluded. The PR describes its analytic chain as depending on the OpenAI `7/8` theorem and inherited source/count/return machinery. At head `3c225e927dae1d0f1283ed10ac9222607a6395c5`, GitHub records successful verification and admission jobs. The tracker describes Comparator and three Lean kernels as its admission process; the present investigation did not replay those jobs or audit the correspondence to classical L-functions. The pinned [Lean target](https://github.com/GTimG/QuasiRiemannTracker/blob/3c225e927dae1d0f1283ed10ac9222607a6395c5/submissions/2026-10-28-density-refined/src/Candidate/Main.lean) quantifies over positive moduli and mathlib Dirichlet characters and excludes `χ = 1` at `s = 1`; it has no explicit analytic premise but imports a proof with those transitive dependencies. We did not audit the mathlib-to-classical correspondence. The method refines an earlier density chain with 59 source transitions and exact endpoint certificates. This is a reported merged tracker result, not a claim of independent mathematical peer review.
* At the same observation time, [PR #93](https://github.com/GTimG/QuasiRiemannTracker/pull/93) states `Re(s) > 87393379/100000000 = 0.87393379` for the same family. It was open at head `70c4d642c4c6263a09bf0f3930a74e2aa7c99f00`, with tracker verification in progress. The author reports a local Lean build, no extra axioms, and 4,526 source transitions. Local author checks are not tracker admission; its mathematical and statement-to-paper scope have not been independently audited here. The evolving open-PR status must be rechecked before relying on it.
* Baiying Liu, [*Zero-free half-planes for Hecke L-functions over number fields*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26835), v1, 19 October 2026, asserts `Re(s) > B_flat = 0.874886647039964...` for finite-order Hecke L-functions over every number field, with principal poles permitted; `B_flat` is the root near 0.8749 of `133488 B^4 - 376596 B^3 + 384534 B^2 - 167541 B + 26231 = 0`. The preprint builds on earlier cubic-theta estimates and adapts Xu–Sang's average numerator estimate; it asserts its value is the infimum for a specified comparison system, not an impossibility bound on other methods. We have not checked its analytic proof. It subsumes an elementary transfer of the Xu–Sang bound to norm-pullbacks from finite abelian extensions of `Q(sqrt(-3))`.


* OpenAI, [*The Quasi-Riemann Hypothesis: A Zero-Free Half-Plane*](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-Quasi-Riemann-Hypothesis-September-30-2026/paper.pdf), claims nonvanishing for `Re(s) > 7/8` for Dirichlet L-functions (including zeta) and finite-order Hecke L-functions over `Q(sqrt(-3))`, with pole conventions. Its [Lean scope note](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/lean/docs/003.md) describes formalizations; a separate [build report](https://github.com/tomoto0/quasi-riemann-hypothesis-7-8-verification) reports rebuilding the zeta and Dirichlet results with standard axioms, and explicitly limits its review of the paper and Hecke case. These are reported checks, not checks performed here.
* Baiying Liu, [*Improved zero-free half-planes for the quasi-Riemann hypothesis*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12234), reports refinements using the OpenAI argument and its analytic inputs, with Lean formalization. Version 2 states the boundary `0.874957019420098946...`, defined exactly there through a cubic equation. The earlier `(1507 - 2 sqrt(921))/1653 = 0.874957069799...` is weaker. Check the precise assumptions and formalized statements before use.
* Yuanda Xu and Hejian Sang, [*Amplified moments and a refinement of the quasi-Riemann hypothesis*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15291), claim `Re(s) > 0.8748867` for the same Hecke and Dirichlet families **assuming the analytic inputs of the OpenAI work**. This is a conditional comparison unless those inputs and the transfer are established in the form used. No independent proof assessment is made here.
* Dean Cureton and Jesse Thorner, [*A quasi-Riemann hypothesis for Dedekind zeta functions*](https://hexagonmath.org/2610.00200v2), claim `Re(s) > 31/32` for Dedekind zeta functions of arbitrary number fields and, wherever holomorphic, finite-order Hecke L-functions. Their [formalization repository](https://github.com/AxiomMath/DedekindZetaZeroFree) reports a Lean proof using standard axioms. The manuscript and definitions have not been audited here.
* Boyang Hu, [*A seven-eighths zero-free half-plane for unitary Weil L-functions*](https://hexagonmath.org/2610.00216v1), claims `Re(s) > 7/8` for unitary Hecke characters over arbitrary number fields and, more generally, finite-dimensional continuous unitary Weil representations, with the stated pole exceptions. This is a preprint claim; no independent verification is asserted here.

## Height-dependent zeta benchmarks

For `s = sigma + it`, these results exclude zeros in the stated domains. They remain useful quantitative comparisons, but a fixed half-plane, if established, eventually dominates every region whose width tends to zero.

* Chiara Bellotti, Tim Trudgian and Andrew Yang, [*Zero-free regions inspired by work of Heath-Brown*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21490), state the classical-shape region `sigma > 1 - 1/(4.896 log t)` for `t >= 3`. Their paper also discusses stronger restricted-range and conditional-on-input estimates. It is a 2026 preprint, not a proof checked here.
* Andrew Yang, [*Explicit bounds on ζ(s) in the critical strip and a zero-free region*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmaa.2024.128124), gives `sigma > 1 - log(log |t|)/(21.233 log |t|)` for `|t| >= 3`. This can improve a classical-shape bound at intermediate heights; compare the formulas at the actual height rather than comparing constants alone.
* Chiara Bellotti, [*Explicit bounds for the Riemann zeta function and a new zero-free region*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10680), states a Vinogradov–Korobov region with denominator `54.004 (log |t|)^(2/3) (log log |t|)^(1/3)` for `|t| >= 3`, and constant `48.0718` for sufficiently large `|t|`. The latter statement has an unspecified large-height threshold in the abstract; it must not be used as an all-height bound. Other versions and sources give different numerical constants, so match the bound to its exact source and version.
* Tanmay Khale, [*An explicit Vinogradov–Korobov zero-free region for Dirichlet L-functions*](https://doi.org/10.1093/qmath/haae010), gives a family-specific comparison. The conductor dependence, ordinate range, and possible exceptional-zero hypotheses must be read from the exact theorem before comparing another Dirichlet result.

In particular, an improvement over the older zeta constant `5.558691` alone is not evidence of a new record. Conversely, an explicit bound can be valuable in a finite height range even when a claimed fixed half-plane is asymptotically wider; numerical crossover and hypotheses must be checked.
