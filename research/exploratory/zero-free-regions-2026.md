Status: exploratory literature reconnaissance and local research. The formal candidate and source-dependent family estimates below have distinct verification and scope limitations.

## Height-dependent zeta benchmarks

For `s = sigma + it`, these results exclude zeros in the stated domains. They remain useful quantitative comparisons, but a fixed half-plane, if established, eventually dominates every region whose width tends to zero.

* Chiara Bellotti, Tim Trudgian and Andrew Yang, [*Zero-free regions inspired by work of Heath-Brown*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21490), state the classical-shape region `sigma > 1 - 1/(4.896 log t)` for `t >= 3`. Their paper also discusses stronger restricted-range and conditional-on-input estimates. It is a 2026 preprint, not a proof checked here.
* Andrew Yang, [*Explicit bounds on ζ(s) in the critical strip and a zero-free region*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmaa.2024.128124), gives `sigma > 1 - log(log |t|)/(21.233 log |t|)` for `|t| >= 3`. This can improve a classical-shape bound at intermediate heights; compare the formulas at the actual height rather than comparing constants alone.
* Chiara Bellotti, [*Explicit bounds for the Riemann zeta function and a new zero-free region*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10680), states a Vinogradov–Korobov region with denominator `54.004 (log |t|)^(2/3) (log log |t|)^(1/3)` for `|t| >= 3`, and constant `48.0718` for sufficiently large `|t|`. The latter statement has an unspecified large-height threshold in the abstract; it must not be used as an all-height bound. Other versions and sources give different numerical constants, so match the bound to its exact source and version.
* Tanmay Khale, [*An explicit Vinogradov–Korobov zero-free region for Dirichlet L-functions*](https://doi.org/10.1093/qmath/haae010), gives a family-specific comparison. The conductor dependence, ordinate range, and possible exceptional-zero hypotheses must be read from the exact theorem before comparing another Dirichlet result.

In particular, an improvement over the older zeta constant `5.558691` alone is not evidence of a new record. Conversely, an explicit bound can be valuable in a finite height range even when a claimed fixed half-plane is asymptotically wider; numerical crossover and hypotheses must be checked.

## A concrete analytic target, not a proved improvement

The OpenAI manuscript's [Lemma 17.1](https://wcook04.github.io/plectis-atlas/papers/The-Quasi-Riemann-Hypothesis-September-30-2026.html) states a marked inverse second-moment estimate. In its notation, `M_u` is the normalized smooth Möbius inverse polynomial of length `Z^r`, and `Q_u` is a product of a fixed number of normalized, independently weighted prime-slot sums with total length exponent `z`. For rows `0 < q_u << Z^m`, the asserted bound is `sum_u |M_u Q_u|^2 << Z^(m+epsilon)` provided, with fixed positive margins, `r + 2z < m` and `2r + 8z < 3m`. The statement includes uniformity over the disjoint prime supports and their bounded row-independent coefficients, plus restrictions on twists, punctures, and test functions. The source's exact hypotheses control the allowed limit processes; these strict schematic inequalities do not replace them.

A useful target is an independently proved extension to a specified open parameter region across one of these two faces, with all of the required uniformity and a demonstrated transfer into the current marked-density chain. A numerical exponent optimization which assumes the extension would be only conditional. A bound for one selected weight, a family average with different masks, or a fixed row does not automatically give the uniform moment needed by the detector. The inverse moment is only one step: the local Euler correction, simultaneous witnesses, nonprincipal row count, and target-uniform positive power margin also require proof at the new parameters. Extending the moment range alone does not establish an improved zero-free boundary.

There are two tests of such an extension. First, on the model in §17 with trivial fixed character, `r=0`, a test supported at the unit ideal, and one positive prime slot of exponent `z>0`, rows `u=v^6` contribute coherently. Among `N(v) <= c Z^(m/6)`, all but `O(Z^(m/6)/log Z)` avoid every prime in a fixed proportional annulus of norms `~Z^z`, since the sum of their reciprocal norms is `O(1/log Z)`. For these rows, the normalized prime sum is `>> Z^(z/2)/log Z` by the prime ideal theorem, while the inverse factor equals one. The sixth-power map has bounded multiplicity. Thus the moment is `>> Z^(m/6+z)/(log Z)^2`, forcing `z <= 5m/6` if the proposed `Z^(m+epsilon)` bound is to hold for every epsilon. This necessary condition does not decide whether the present `z < 3m/8` range can be extended, and it does not apply unchanged to positive inverse length `r`, where Möbius cancellation matters.

Second, Xu and Sang, [*A matching lower bound for the sextic large sieve and structured prime moments*, v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17675v2), claim that the squarefree sextic character operator's squared norm matches, up to subpowers, `A+B+A^(5/6)B^(1/3)+A^(1/3)B^(5/6)` over the full cutoff range; at balance this is `Q^(7/6+o(1))`. Their v2 cites the OpenAI unmarked moment inputs; its v1 gives a more restricted lower bound without those additional moment assumptions. This generic operator obstruction does not itself rule out a better bound for the specified Möbius/prime product: its extremizing coefficient need not have that structure. Conversely, a proof cannot silently substitute an ideal square-root generic large sieve for the actual sextic operator. Their Theorem 1.2 additionally gives a `Q^((37-10 sqrt(6))/6+epsilon)` upper bound for an **unnormalized fixed smooth prime sum** over squarefree rows of norm about `Q`, assuming the cited unmarked inverse moment and buffered detector. That exponent is approximately `2.08418`, smaller than the generic balanced `13/6`, after accounting for the prime coefficient norm. It is not a uniform arbitrary-weight marked inverse estimate and does not directly transfer to Lemma 17.1. The sixth-power-row obstruction above uses nonsquarefree rows and likewise does not transfer to the squarefree-row result. These source claims and the sixth-power argument have not received an independent review here.

The [structural-ceiling analysis](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/40/v1) asserts a `5/6` bookkeeping floor for a specific class of rescaled compensated probes, and a density threshold within Liu's stated exponent system; it does not rule out different probes or later marked-density constructions. The [zeta-lab hunt](https://github.com/teal-sea/zeta-lab/blob/main/hunts/below_seven_eighths/MISSION.md) reports a separate `13/15` floor in a specified Part II assembly model and explicitly labels its scope. Both are exploratory comparisons, not universal impossibility theorems or results verified here. The present tracker chain is already beyond the initial published parameter optimizations; its current constraints must be extracted before asserting that an improved moment moves the latest bound.
