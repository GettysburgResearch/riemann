**Short source replay.** In the proof of OpenAI Proposition 8.3, the sole displayed estimate using the numerical t range is the shifted-Gamma bound `−3+t(5/4−Re ρ)`, which improves as t decreases from 1. **Plain squarefree-kernel moment.** Every good integral ideal q has the unique decomposition `q=q₁v²` with q₁ squarefree, without requiring `(q₁,v)=1`. Because original character extensions are multiplicative with their zero masks, `χ_g(q)=χ_g(q₁)χ_g(v²)` also when q shares factors with g. For a fixed v, the row multiplier χ_g(v²) has modulus at most one. The fixed ν(v²) and any row-profile norm-twist factor are also modulus at most one; applying the squarefree source sieve after removing these multipliers is therefore valid. The inner q₁ support has length `Q_v≈D/(Nv)²`, up to absolute endpoint factors. If `Q_v` is below one, it is still bounded below by a positive absolute constant whenever the support is nonempty, and the unit q₁ case is covered by an O(1) endpoint box. The common coefficient vector has squared mass `≪1/(Nv)²`, by ideal counting and the original D^(−1/2) normalization. At `D≤X^(1/2+O(η))`, its row ℓ² norm is `≪X^(1/2+O(η)+ε)/(Nv)`. Minkowski's inequality and `Σ_{Nv≤C√D}1/Nv≪log(2D)` yield (5).

For common profile parameters, the q₁ profile can be written as a fixed annular function of `Nq₁/Q_v`, with uniformly bounded fixed-order derivatives, even as v varies. When the actual zero ordinate and real part vary by row, the finite-dimensional Sobolev reduction must be applied to the whole rowwise family and its differentiated vectors. Differentiation of the v-dependent norm twist and global normalization contributes at worst powers of log(Nv) and log D; χ_g(v²) itself is independent of these real parameters. Thus the same Minkowski sum applies after a fixed number of derivatives, with a fixed polynomial height cost and logarithmic losses. Applying the source bound directly to a separately chosen rowwise coefficient vector without this Sobolev step would be invalid; the README states the necessary interface explicitly. Finite ray-class partitioning absorbs the reciprocity phase into a common q₁ coefficient vector in each class, with original noncoprime masks retained.
