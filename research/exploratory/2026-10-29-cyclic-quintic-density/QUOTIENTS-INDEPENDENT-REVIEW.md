# Independent review of cyclic quartic and quintic quotient corollaries

This reviews [the cyclic quintic note](README.md) and [the controlled-core quartic note](../2026-10-29-cyclic-quartic-density/README.md), conditional on the explicitly named family-density, quadratic large-sieve, detector and classical class-field inputs. It is not a new individual zero-free theorem or a verification of those analytic inputs.

For quintic labels g, every nonzero good valuation modulo 5 gives a tame local character of exact order 5. For k=1,…,4, the n-free ideal g_k obtained by multiplying each valuation by k modulo 5 has the same support. De Faveri §2.5.1, equation (2.4), gives exact ideal-index multiplicativity with source zero extensions, and χ_(h⁵)(q)=1 whenever `(h,q)=1`; hence χ_g(q)^k=χ_(g_k)(q) on all good q, including their common zero mask. Equality outside finite S determines the primitive Hecke characters. Since k is invertible modulo 5, the local characters and their kth powers have the same kernels at every place, so the conductors are exactly equal. The quotient `ζ_L/ζ_K` is the product of the four entire nontrivial characters. A union bound over their labels is valid; a field has at most four generating primitive characters. Distinct prime supports give ≫X/log X distinct fields. The conductor–discriminant formula is `D_L=D_K⁵(Nfχ)^4`; the resulting exponent and a slightly reduced positive height power transform as stated.

For the quartic construction, local order four at a and order two at b make each distinct prime pair a distinct character and give a cyclic degree-four extension; each field has only two order-four generators. Equation (2.4) yields `χ_(ab²)^2=χ_a²·1_((·,b)=1)` on good ideals: the extra deleted Euler factor does not change the primitive inducing character. The core has good conductor exactly a and uniformly bounded fixed bad conductor. The fixed-field quadratic sieve and the stated detector give the conditional σ=4/5 core exponent 13/25. For `Na≈X^κ`, `Nb≈X^(1−κ)`, the exceptional core labels cost `X^{1−κ+13κ/25+ε}`; it is below the quartic 13/15 exponent exactly when κ>5/18. At κ=3/10 it is 107/125. The height must be chosen strictly below κ times the core theorem's available power, as the note does.

The quartic conductor–discriminant identity is `D_L=D_K⁴(Nfχ)^2 Nf(χ²)`, so the chosen box has `D_L≈X^(23/10)` and transforms the exception exponent to 26/69, against ≫`Y^(10/23)/(log Y)^2` fields. The quintic and quartic quotients exclude the fixed ζ_K factor; neither result gives a zero-free rectangle for the full ζ_L. No defect was found in these deductions under their source qualifications.
