# Cyclic prime relative zeta families from natural-label density

**Status.** Source-qualified conditional consequences of the [all-order natural-label detector calculation](../2026-10-29-full-order-natural-fourth/README.md) and the [powered FHL-family bridge](../2026-10-29-full-order-density/POWERED-FAMILY-INDEPENDENT-REVIEW.md), together with class field theory. The source large sieve, fixed-field short detector, common-profile/height transfer and their cited preprints are not independently established here. These are relative-quotient family-density statements at σ=4/5, not individual zero-free regions or exhaustive priority claims.

Fix an odd prime p≥5, K=Q(ζ_p), and fixed FHL data and good set S. For a good p-th-power-free ideal u, let χ_u denote the primitive character induced by the source presentation. Write R_p for the applicable exponent from the cited natural-label note: for p∈{5,7} it is 13/20 and 137/224 respectively; for 11≤p≤20 take 29/50, and for p≥21 take 14/25. These are fixed-p statements; implicit constants and the permitted height power can depend on p.

For each nontrivial χ_u, the good local exponent at every prime dividing u is nonzero modulo p, so χ_u has exact order p and determines a cyclic degree-p field L_u/K. Subject to the cited inputs, for every ε>0 there is τ>0 such that

    #{L_u : Nu≤X, ζ_(L_u)/ζ_K has a zero in
       Re s≥4/5, |Im s|≤X^τ} ≪_{p,ε} X^(R_p+ε).          (1)

The family in (1) contains ≫X distinct fields. Indeed, distinct p-free u determine distinct primitive χ_u by their good local inertia exponents; each cyclic degree-p field has p−1 nontrivial character generators, so the field fiber has size at most p−1. Squarefree u≤X alone already supply ≫X labels. This yields a density-one zero-free rectangle for the selected relative quotients, with the common global-X height shown in (1).

For the proof, each j∈{1,…,p−1} changes the chosen root embedding in the FHL construction, giving the powered family χ_u^j with the same label u, good zero masks and conductor bounds. The natural-label estimate applies to each of these finitely many fixed source families; take the minimum of their permitted τ. Artin factorization uses the primitive all-place inducing characters; their zeros in Re s>0 agree with those of the original S-deleted presentations. A finite union then bounds the relative-quotient zero count. One must use the powered-family source matching, not merely assert that the labels of χ_u^j remain p-free of the same norm under the original embedding.

## Discriminant ordering: squarefree-label subfamily

For a squarefree good u, every nontrivial power of χ_u has identical local kernel and primitive conductor, including at primes in S. Its good conductor is u and its bad conductor is bounded uniformly. Therefore

    |D_(L_u)|=|D_K|^p [Nf(χ_u)]^(p−1) ≍ (Nu)^(p−1).     (2)

Let C_p be the set of fields admitting such a squarefree label. For Y≥2, (2) puts every L_u∈C_p with |D_(L_u)|≤Y among squarefree labels `Nu≤C Y^(1/(p−1))`, with a fixed C. Apply the cumulative global-X estimate (1) at `X=C Y^(1/(p−1))` and choose τ′<τ/(p−1). This gives

    #{L∈C_p : |D_L|≤Y, ζ_L/ζ_K has a zero in
       Re s≥4/5, |Im s|≤Y^τ′}
          ≪_{p,ε} Y^(R_p/(p−1)+ε),                         (3)

while `#{L∈C_p: |D_L|≤Y}≫Y^(1/(p−1))`. The estimates already use one global X and its X-power height for all smaller labels; no additional small-label height split is needed in this particular deduction. Fixed conductor factors and the strict inequality in the height power absorb the constant C.

For arbitrary p-free u, only the upper bound `|D_(L_u)|≪(Nu)^(p−1)` follows from u's natural norm; the good conductor is rad(u), which can be much smaller than u. Thus (1) gives a family with label cutoff X contained in a discriminant range of size ≪X^(p−1), but it does **not** by itself count every field generated by arbitrary p-free labels with |D_L|≤Y. The squarefree restriction is what justifies the reverse conductor-to-label bound in (3).

For example, p=5 yields relative-quotient exception exponent 13/80 against field-count exponent 1/4; p=7 yields 137/1344 against 1/6; p=11 yields 29/500 against 1/10; and p≥21 yields 14/[25(p−1)] against 1/(p−1). These are consequences for the stated squarefree-label family; the original p-free-label theorem may hold for many additional fields.

## Base zeta and the candidate boundary

The proposed θ=0.8738694 Dirichlet candidate, if admitted with its conventional interpretation and source-chain qualifications, makes the cyclotomic base ζ_K zero-free in Re s>θ through its factorization into Dirichlet L-functions, away from the pole at 1. The family estimates above are only at σ=4/5<θ. They therefore cannot be combined with that base input to assert full ζ_L nonvanishing in the σ=4/5 rectangles. A full-zeta consequence would require an independently established relative-family estimate at a fixed σ>θ or a stronger base-field zero-free input. The existing candidate does not supply either through this note.
