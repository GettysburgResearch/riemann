# Mathematical digest: Hilbert pair correlation and simple critical-line zeros

**Status:** proof-oriented digest. This document is not an independent proof.
The Riemann Hypothesis remains unproved.

## 1. The theorem

Let

- `N(T)` be the number of nontrivial zeros `rho = beta + i gamma` with
  `0 < gamma <= T`, counted with multiplicity;
- `N_0^s(T)` be the number of such zeros that are simple and satisfy
  `beta = 1/2`;
- `N_d(T)` be the number of distinct zeros in the same range.

Lamzouri proves

\[
\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{N_0^s(T)}{N(T)}
\ge C_0,
\qquad
C_0=
\frac32-\frac1{\sqrt2}\cot\!\left(\frac1{\sqrt2}\right)
=0.6725007037\ldots,
\]

and

\[
\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{N_d(T)}{N(T)}
\ge
\frac{1+C_0}{2}
=
\frac54-\frac1{2\sqrt2}\cot\!\left(\frac1{\sqrt2}\right)
=0.8362503518\ldots .
\]

The second constant is not a separate optimization. Both bounds come from the
same pair-energy estimate.

## 2. Rescaling the zeta zeros

The functional equation sends a zero

\[
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
\]

to

\[
1-\overline{\rho}.
\]

After multiplying `rho - 1/2` by an imaginary logarithmic scale, horizontal
reflection about the critical line becomes ordinary complex conjugation. In
the Lean source the basic ordered-pair coordinate is

\[
\frac{i(\rho-\rho')\log T}{2\pi}.
\]

Thus:

- a zero on `Re(s)=1/2` becomes a real point in the rescaled model;
- an off-line functional-equation pair becomes a nonreal conjugate pair;
- zero multiplicity becomes point multiplicity.

The zeta problem is therefore converted into a problem about a finite
conjugation-invariant multiset.

## 3. The abstract finite-multiset theorem

Let `Z` be the support of a nonempty finite conjugation-invariant multiset and
let `m(z) >= 1` be its multiplicity. Choose a real even function `eta` that is
square-integrable, compactly supported in `(-lambda,lambda)`, and normalized by

\[
\widehat{\eta^2}(0)=1.
\]

Define the entire kernel

\[
K(z)=\widehat{\eta^2}(z).
\]

Write

\[
M=\sum_{z\in Z}m(z)
\]

for total mass and

\[
\mathcal E_K(Z,m)
=
\operatorname{Re}
\sum_{z,s\in Z}m(z)m(s)K(z-s)^2
\]

for the ordered pair energy.

The proposition gives

\[
\#\{z\in Z:z\in\mathbb R,\ m(z)=1\}
\ge
2M-\mathcal E_K(Z,m),
\]

and

\[
\# Z
\ge
\frac32M-\frac12\mathcal E_K(Z,m).
\]

These statements simultaneously penalize:

- nonreal conjugate pairs;
- repeated real points;
- repeated nonreal points.

The use of the real part is essential because individual off-line terms need
not be real or nonnegative.

## 4. Why the Hilbert proof works

The kernel is a Gram kernel generated by modulated copies of `eta`. Squaring
the kernel and summing over ordered pairs produces the norm square of a
two-variable Hilbert-space vector. The exact source formalizes this through
real inner-product spaces, Bochner integration, tensor-like expansions,
symmetry decompositions, and Bessel bounds.

The proof organizes the generating vectors into nested subspaces recording:

```text
multiple real points
    subset
all real points
    subset
all points, including nonreal conjugate pairs.
```

An orthonormal basis adapted to this flag is obtained by Gram-Schmidt. The
coordinates of the aggregate tensor vector in the successive basis ranges
encode the combinatorial defects:

- failure of a real point to be simple;
- failure of a point to be real;
- repeated support points.

Bessel's inequality bounds the sum of the squared coordinates by the total
Hilbert norm. Elementary scalar inequalities in the three ranges then yield
the two displayed counting bounds.

### Conceptual translation

The proof is a continuous/operator version of an effective-rank inequality:

\[
\text{large trace and controlled Hilbert-Schmidt norm}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{large rank}.
\]

The Alpoge-Furman proof realized this with a finite matrix compression,
Hermitian inertia, and a rank-trace inequality. Lamzouri realizes the same
geometry directly in the ambient Hilbert space.

## 5. Why termwise positivity is not available

On RH, rescaled zeros are real and classical positive kernels can be used
termwise. Off RH, the kernel is evaluated at complex differences. A naive
replacement would need a nonconstant entire function with globally
nonnegative real behavior strong enough to control every off-line term. The
paper explains why this cannot be the mechanism.

The Hilbert norm is positive only after all terms are assembled. This
aggregate positivity is powerful, but it is also the source of a later RH
firewall: it controls average rank loss, not every individual zero direction.

## 6. The unconditional pair-correlation input

The BGST theorem controls a weighted ordered-pair sum. In the formal source the
weight is

\[
W(z)=\frac4{4-z^2}.
\]

For an even test function `f` supported in `[-1,1]`, the normalized weighted
sum tends to

\[
f(0)+2\int_0^1\alpha f(\alpha)\,d\alpha
\]

with a quantitative error of order `1/sqrt(log T)` in the encoded interface.

The Hilbert proposition instead needs an unweighted sum of `K^2`. Lamzouri
chooses a compactly supported convolution profile and adds a second-derivative
correction. Fourier differentiation supplies precisely the polynomial factor
that cancels the rational weight. The Lean source records an exact identity
before taking asymptotics.

This correction is structurally important for Riemann: it is a model example
of converting a theorem available in one metric into the metric required by
an operator inequality without hiding normalization factors.

## 7. Montgomery-Taylor optimization

The remaining scalar problem minimizes the pair-correlation main term subject
to compact support, evenness, and normalization. Smooth admissible functions
approximate the Montgomery-Taylor extremizer and give

\[
C_{\mathrm{MT}}
=
\frac12+\frac1{\sqrt2}\cot\!\left(\frac1{\sqrt2}\right)
=
1.327499296\ldots .
\]

Since the simple-real inequality has the form

\[
N_0^s(T)\gtrsim 2N(T)-C_{\mathrm{MT}}N(T),
\]

the final constant is

\[
C_0=2-C_{\mathrm{MT}}.
\]

The distinct count gives

\[
\frac32-\frac12C_{\mathrm{MT}}
=
\frac{1+C_0}{2}.
\]

The extremal theorem cited in the paper shows that this scalar
fixed-support quadratic optimization is already sharp. Smoothing the same
profile more cleverly cannot improve `0.6725007...`.

## 8. What the theorem says about bad zeros

If

\[
B(T)=N(T)-N_0^s(T),
\]

then the theorem gives only

\[
\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{B(T)}{N(T)}
\le 1-C_0.
\]

It does not distinguish among:

- finitely many off-line zeros;
- infinitely many off-line zeros of density zero;
- multiple zeros on the critical line of density zero;
- mixtures of these exceptional sets.

Likewise, a density-one strengthening would still permit finitely many
off-line zeros and therefore would not by itself imply RH.

## 9. Precise method barrier

The current proof consumes only second-order information:

```text
total zero mass       ~ trace
ordered pair energy   ~ Hilbert-Schmidt norm squared.
```

Trace plus Hilbert-Schmidt norm controls effective rank, hence a proportion.
It cannot force the smallest singular value to be positive and cannot exclude
a finite-dimensional defect.

To go further, at least one of the following is required:

1. local/effective control in every height window;
2. support growing with `T`;
3. vector-valued or multi-kernel inequalities;
4. higher zero correlations giving higher Schatten moments;
5. a separate individual-zero detector;
6. a propagation theorem turning one off-line zero into a non-dilutable family
   of prime-side witnesses.

Those are the improvement lanes developed in the companion roadmap.

## 10. Mathematical verdict

This is a major simplification and conceptual unification of the new
two-thirds theorem. Its abstract proposition is highly reusable. It does not
close RH, but it exposes the exact operator-theoretic reason the present
second-moment method stops at a proportion: effective rank is not complete
capture.
