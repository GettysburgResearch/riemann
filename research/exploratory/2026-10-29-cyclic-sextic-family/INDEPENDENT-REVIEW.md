# Independent review of the sextic relative-zeta family

This audit checks the arithmetic reduction and source matching in [README](README.md). It does not independently establish the cited density preprints, fixed-field quadratic detector, or formal-to-conventional Hecke bridge. The resulting estimate is for relative quotients in a selected family, not an individual zero-free strip.

The order-six FHL presentation must be chosen compatibly with the principal cubic family in paper 52 v5. Because Z[ω] is a PID, choose each fixed FHL representative E_i=(m_i), with m_i congruent to 1 modulo 3, and choose the auxiliary modulus c divisible by 3. If q=(β), with β its unique primary generator β≡1 modulo 3, the FHL decomposition q=(x)E G⁶, with x≡1 modulo c and G=(γ), yields β=x m_E γ⁶: both sides generate q and are primary, since γ is a 3-adic unit and every unit of (O/3)× has sixth power 1. Consequently, for any good z coprime to q, χ_q^(6)(z)=(β/z)₆; the original character is zero when (q,z)≠1. Its square is (β/z)₃=(z/β)₃ by primary cubic reciprocity, exactly the principal cubic residue family defined in paper 52. Its cube is the corresponding quadratic residue symbol. This is an explicit compatible choice, not a consequence of merely matching conductors or allowing an unspecified moving S-local twist.

Let ρ=χ_B^(6) and χ=χ_(B⁵)^(6)=ρ⁵. Since ρ² is the principal cubic character κ_B, the exact orientations are χ²=κ_B²=overline κ_B and χ⁴=κ_B; χ³=ρ³ is quadratic. All identities are on good ideals with the original zero masks, and equality away from fixed bad primes determines the primitive inducing characters. For the cubic pair, symmetric-height zero counts agree under conjugation, so paper 52's principal natural-row theorem applies at row B and index norm ≍X without introducing a nonprincipal finite twist. Its stated exponent at σ=4/5 is (8−7σ)/(3(2−σ))=2/3. The preprint's theorem itself remains an input.

At the good prime B, ρ has exact order six, so each χ^j, 1≤j≤5, is nontrivial and ramified at B; their primitive conductors have good radical B and uniformly bounded bad part. The conductor–discriminant formula gives |D_L|=|D_E|⁶∏_{j=1}⁵Nf(χ^j)≍X⁵. Distinct B give distinct fields because their good conductor supports differ, and the prime ideal theorem gives ≫X/log X fields in a fixed B box. A finite union of symmetric zero counts yields exponent 2/3, with the quadratic 13/25 input smaller. One top box gives discriminant exception exponent 2/15 and family exponent 1/5 at a suitably shortened height; no cumulative common-height assertion is implied without the separate small-scale split.

The earlier sextic fourth-witness exponent 59/90 is algebraically valid under its stated sieve and detector replays, but de Faveri Corollary 1.5 already gives the better direct squarefree-index exponent 209/326 at σ=4/5 for ρ_B. Since χ=overline ρ_B, it gives the same count for χ at symmetric height. This comparison is for that named source, subject to its stated height factor; the cubic 2/3 remains the largest of these three family-factor exponents. No novelty or exhaustive prior-art claim follows.

The asserted all-height θ=0.8738694 consequence is separate and conditional on admission of that candidate and its identification with the conventional finite-order Hecke L-functions over E. Under that hypothesis, each nontrivial factor in ζ_L/ζ_E is nonzero in Re s>θ; the base ζ_E=ζ L(s,χ₋₃) is also covered by the conventional Dirichlet bridge, and ζ_L has its ordinary pole at 1. This transfer does not establish the σ=4/5 density estimate or discharge the semantic bridge.
