# Cyclic sextic relative-zeta density over the Eisenstein field

**Status.** A source-qualified family consequence of [de Faveri Corollary 1.5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04045v1), [rStation paper 52 v5, Theorem 5.7](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/52/v5), and the fixed-field quadratic-sieve/detector calculation in the [quartic core note](../2026-10-29-cyclic-quartic-density/README.md). These recent analytic claims and source-to-detector replays are not independently established here. The result is a density-one statement for a relative quotient, not a new individual zero-free region or a priority claim.

Let `E=Q(ω)` and fix the source's good set S containing the primes over 2 and 3. Choose a compatible FHL presentation: its fixed modulus c is divisible by 3 and each ideal representative `E_i` is generated by a primary element `m_i≡1 mod 3`. This choice is available because E is a PID. For a good ideal `q=(β)` with primary β and an evaluation ideal z coprime to q, the FHL construction writes `q=(x)E G⁶`, where `x≡1 mod c`, `E=(m_E)` is a product of those representatives and `G=(γ)` is **chosen coprime to z**, as in de Faveri §2.3.4. The element `x m_E γ⁶` is primary and generates q, so uniqueness gives `β=x m_E γ⁶`. Since γ is a unit modulo z, its sixth-power residue symbol is one; hence the FHL character equals `(β/z)_6`. Both are zero when z meets q or the fixed bad modulus. Primary cubic reciprocity identifies its square with the principal cubic symbol `(·/β)_3` used by paper 52. This construction fixes the normalization; a different FHL presentation needs its own finite-ray comparison.

For a good prime ideal B with `NB≈X`, let χ be the primitive finite-order character induced by the source sextic character `χ_(B⁵)`. At B its local character has exact order six, so χ has order six. Its good conductor is B and its bad conductor is supported on S with uniformly bounded norm. Class field theory attaches to χ a cyclic degree-six extension L/E.

Assuming the source inputs, for every ε>0 there is τ>0 for which the number of these L with a zero of `ζ_L/ζ_E` in

    Re s≥4/5,       |Im s|≤X^τ

is `O_ε(X^(2/3+ε))`. There are `≫X/log X` distinct such fields in a fixed positive-width B box, so the exceptional proportion tends to zero.

## The five factors

The Artin factorization is

    ζ_L(s)/ζ_E(s)=∏_(j=1)^5 L_E(s,χ^j).                      (1)

All five factors are nontrivial and entire. Their local exponents at B are nonzero modulo six, so each has good conductor B, while its bad conductor is uniformly bounded. In particular

    |D_L|=|D_E|⁶ ∏_(j=1)^5 Nf(χ^j) ≍ X⁵.                     (2)

The five factors are controlled as follows, with a common height power chosen smaller than each finite collection of admissible powers.

* χ and χ⁵ are complex conjugates. The latter is the standard squarefree sextic label B. De Faveri Corollary 1.5 gives exponent `209/326≈0.6411` at σ=4/5 for the natural-index family, with an explicit polynomial height factor. After choosing the height power sufficiently small, restriction to prime B bounds their exceptional labels by `O(X^(209/326+ε))`. Fixed finite-ray and primitive-character matching are retained. An alternative source-qualified derivation via the general-order fourth witness gives the weaker exponent `59/90`; it is not needed for this field consequence.
* χ² and χ⁴ are the two conjugate cubic residue characters of B. With the compatible presentation above, `(β/·)_6²=(·/β)_3` by primary cubic reciprocity; the original zero at a shared prime is the same. Because χ is induced by the **fifth** power of the sextic character, its square is the conjugate principal cubic symbol and its fourth power is the principal cubic symbol. At good local primes, this also agrees with the source identity `sexticChar=cubicChar²·quadraticChar`. Paper 52 v5 Theorem 5.7, with principal fixed twist, gives natural-index exponent 2/3 at σ=4/5, uniformly over all good primary labels of norm ≤X at some small power height. Restricting its nonnegative sum to prime labels B gives `O(X^(2/3+ε))` exceptional labels. This step does not introduce a moving finite twist.
* χ³ is the quadratic residue character of B at all good primes, with the same zero mask. The fixed-field quadratic Hecke sieve and the cited saturated-detector replay give exceptional count `O(X^(13/25+ε))` at σ=4/5 and a sufficiently small power height. Its exact local conductor at B prevents distinct prime B from yielding the same quadratic core; fixed bad factors do not affect the count.

The nonnegative zero count with multiplicity bounds the number of exceptional labels and therefore the number of exceptional fields. Conversely, the good conductor support of a cyclic sextic field is exactly B: all its nontrivial characters above have that same support. Thus different good prime B give different fields, and the prime ideal theorem supplies `≫X/log X` fields.

For an absolute-discriminant parameter Y, take one fixed top-scale box `X≈Y^(1/5)` with constants small enough that (2) places every field below Y. Choosing `τ′<τ/5` gives

    O(Y^(2/15+ε)) exceptional fields, versus
    ≫Y^(1/5)/log Y total fields,                              (3)

in `Re s≥4/5`, `|Im s|≤Y^τ′`. This is a one-box statement. Counting a cumulative union of small boxes at a common Y-height requires a separate small-scale split.

## Relationship to the inherited all-height candidate

Separately, the isolated Lean candidate discussed in the [retune packet](../2026-10-29-return-retune-8738694/README.md) has θ=`4369347/5000000=0.8738694`. **If** it is admitted and its formal Dirichlet and E-Hecke statements are identified with the conventional L-functions, then every finite-order Hecke factor of ζ_L is nonzero for `Re s>θ` at all heights, by the standard abelian Artin factorization. The fixed base ζ_E is also zero-free there by its Dirichlet factorization; ζ_L has its usual simple pole at s=1. Thus that inherited candidate would yield an individual all-height strip for these ζ_L and their relative factors. The conventional E-Hecke semantic bridge and tracker status remain separate conditions, and this conditional consequence is inherited from the candidate; it does not prove the σ=4/5 family-density statement above or claim an independent stronger strip.

The source cubic exponent `2/3` dominates the combined quotient estimate (3). Thus this is a joint family consequence, not a new record for sextic character density. No exhaustive comparison with all prior cyclic-sixth field results is made; the natural-index sources supply both the sextic and cubic factor bounds, while the quadratic detector replay is an additional dependency.
