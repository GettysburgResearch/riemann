# Independent review of the full-inverse growth identity

**Scope:** This checks the proposed limsup identity under the fixed Eisenstein field, fixed principal ray sector and the standard finite-ring character-sum, reciprocity and fixed-field Hecke analytic inputs stated in the adjacent note. It is not a proof of the Riemann hypothesis or of a new zero-free region. The fixed annular W must have a Mellin transform with no zeros on Re s>0 and V must be nonnegative with positive integral.

Let `Θ=sup{Re ρ : ζ_K(ρ)=0, 0<Re ρ<1}` and fix `0<r<1/4`. Subject to the inputs above, the argument supports

    limsup_{X→∞} log[X^(−1) Σ_g V(Ng/X)|M_g(X^r)|²]/log X
      = r(2Θ−1).                                                (1)

Here the g-sum is over good squarefree ideals in the specified principal ray class modulo 9, and log 0 is interpreted as −∞. The constant finite Euler factors at S are retained. The result includes Θ=1/2 and Θ=1; neither endpoint should be inferred by inserting θ=1 into a formula stated only for θ<1.

**Sector and local masks.** The condition that g has a generator congruent to 1 modulo 9 fixes the needed primary convention and ensures the cubic residue symbol is the correct finite-order Hecke character on good ideals. Ray-class projection has only finitely many components, with a positive density for the principal sector. The original good-prime zero masks remain essential: after writing a=h a₀, b=h b₀ for squarefree a,b and h=(a,b), one has `χ_g(a)χ̄_g(b)=1_((g,h)=1)χ_g(a₀b₀²)`. If two indices have unequal good cubic exponent vectors, at some good prime their quotient character is ramified; fixed modulo-9 characters cannot cancel it. Conversely equal vectors are exactly `a₀=a₀'` and `b₀=b₀'`. The masks from h,h' are still present and may be dropped only for an absolute upper bound. The corresponding nonprincipal g-character has conductor radical `≪D²` on the full annular convolution range.

**Average and arithmetic errors.** Fixed-ray orthogonality and the squarefree Dirichlet series give the diagonal mean with local coprimality density `c_p=Np/(Np+1)`. The nonprincipal smoothed sector sum is `≪X^(1/2+ε)Q^(1/2+ε)` for good conductor Q, with a subpower cost for h masks, using the stated finite-ring Poisson/Gauss estimate and squarefree expansion. The individual-index radical is ≪D, and the number of divisor triples of norm ≪D is ≪D^(1+ε). Thus after dividing by the ≍X family mass and √D, the mean error is `≪X^(−1/2+ε)D^(1+ε)`. At D=X^r with r<1/4 this is a negative X-power, and it is smaller than D^(−η) after choosing η and ε sufficiently small. The class-sector projection must be retained in this estimate; a 3-primary generator alone would not justify the Hecke-family identification.

**Off-critical lower bound.** The diagonal mean has Mellin transform `Ŵ(s)H_S(s)/ζ_{K,S}(s)`. In Re s>1/2, H_S converges absolutely and is nonzero; fixed S Euler factors are nonzero there, and Ŵ is nonzero by construction. A zero of ζ_K at β+iγ with β>1/2 therefore gives a pole. If the unnormalized mean were O(D^(β−η)), its Mellin integral would be analytic past that pole; hence along an unbounded sequence its normalized magnitude is ≥D^(β−1/2−η). Choosing X=D^(1/r), the average error is smaller, and nonnegative weighted Cauchy–Schwarz gives the second-moment lower exponent `r(2β−1−2η)`. Taking β arbitrarily close to Θ gives the lower side of (1) when Θ>1/2, including Θ=1 as a supremum.

**Critical endpoint.** Independently of the existence or position of a zero at height zero, write

    H_S(s)=ζ_{K,S}(2s)G_S(s),
    G_S(s)=∏_{p∉S}(1+c_p (Np)^(−2s))(1−(Np)^(−2s)).

Each local factor is nonzero for Re s>0, and differs from 1 by `O((Np)^(−1−2Re s)+(Np)^(−4Re s))`; therefore G_S is holomorphic and nonzero for Re s>1/4. At s=1/2, ζ_{K,S}(2s) has a pole, Ŵ(1/2)>0, and ζ_{K,S}(1/2) is finite, possibly zero. The Mellin transform of the diagonal mean thus has a pole there, not a cancellation. The same Mellin Ω argument gives normalized mean `Ω(D^(−η))` along an unbounded sequence. The mean error remains smaller, so the second moment has limsup exponent at least zero. This supplies the lower side when Θ=1/2 without relying on the continuation of the original H Euler product to the whole line Re s=1/2.

**Upper bound.** For any θ>Θ with θ<1, fixed-field zero-free logarithmic control and the rapidly decaying Mellin transform of W give `Σ_d μ(d) W(Nd/Y)≪Y^(θ+η)` uniformly for Y in the annular support; Y below a fixed positive constant gives an empty sum. After the d-sum the coefficient indexed by (h,a₀,b₀) obeys (12) in the adjacent note. Equal-character pairs contribute at most `X D^(2θ−1+2η)` because the h and a₀b₀ powers have exponent greater than one. For unequal pairs, the row-character conductor is ≪D², giving `X^(1/2+ε)D^(1+ε)` per pair. The total coefficient ℓ¹ norm is `≪D^(1/2+ε)` directly from the pre-d-sum triple divisor count; all unequal pairs therefore contribute `≪X^(1/2+ε)D^(2+ε)`. Its X-exponent is strictly less than `1+r(2θ−1)` provided `r<1/[2(3−2θ)]`, which follows uniformly from r<1/4 and θ≥1/2. Letting θ↓Θ yields the upper side of (1) for Θ<1. If Θ=1, the pointwise divisor bound `|M_g(D)|≪D^(1/2+ε)` and the O(X) family count give limsup ≤r, equal to r(2Θ−1).

The open source-facing obligations are exactly the fixed-sector cubic reciprocity and the uniform smoothed finite-ring character sum with masks, plus the standard analytic facts used in the zero-free-region Mellin shift. They are not supplied by the family density theorem. An `O(X^(1+ε))` full-inverse moment at one such fixed r and W is therefore an exact conditional reformulation of RH for ζ_K, not a consequence of the relative-family sieve.
