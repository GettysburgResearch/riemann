# Independent adversarial review of the fixed-field detector packet

The six parts of the packet form a consistent proof replay for the indicated cubic family, conditional on the listed standard fixed-type Hecke facts and de Faveri's family/reciprocity/sieve inputs. I found no remaining need to posit an additional abstract rowwise-profile moment: the explicit finite-dimensional Sobolev lemma in [detector-profile-sobolev](../2026-10-29-detector-profile-sobolev/README.md) supplies that reduction from moments for common coefficient vectors. This review does not independently establish the imported preprint results or standard Hecke analytic theorems.

1. **Conductor, masks, and poles.** For a nonunit cube-free good label, each good p dividing g is tamely ramified in χ_g and ν is unramified there. It follows that the finite primitive conductor contains rad(g), is O(X), and cannot belong to a pure norm character. The exact source-original L-function equals the primitive L-function times its finite deleted Euler product, including fixed S; the deleted radical is O(X). Each deleted factor is nonzero in Re s>0. Only g=1 can give a pole-bearing pure norm presentation, and its fixed-character zero count is negligible at the permitted height. Conjugation preserves the finite presentation dictionary and the conductor bounds.

2. **Buffer and logarithm.** With fixed `1/2<σ_f<σ₀` and e<σ₀−σ_f, the nested maxima over a row and its conjugate are finite and lie in `[σ_f,1]`. The selected plateau and rounding have `a≥σ₀−e>σ_f`, so their maximum is attained by a genuine zero. For center `2+iv`, `|v|≤(3i+2)T₁`, the disk radius `2−a−2e` has real part at least `a+2e` and lies within the enlarged height window, hence is zero-free. The inner 49/100 disk lies strictly in Re s>1; its Euler logarithm is uniformly bounded. On the intermediate disks the fixed-field strip bound, Borel–Carathéodory and three-circles give `log L≪(log C_X)^κ` with κ<1 uniformly over fixed a,e; exponentiation supplies an arbitrary small X-power. The disk-radius lemma itself works down to a=1/2. The point a+6e must be understood inside this smaller disk or on the absolute Euler line; no all-height reciprocal estimate follows.

3. **Reflection, masks, and partial contours.** On `Re s=1−a−6e≥−6e`, the fixed-type functional equation contributes `Q^(a−1/2+6e)` and fixed-polynomial height growth. The deleted-product upper bound contributes `X^(6e+η)`, giving (7). On the inverse contour, both the shifted segment and its horizontal joins stay in the one buffered rectangle; the unshifted absolute-line tails use the rapidly decaying Mellin transform, with the norm twist in the L-argument. The plain reflected shift uses only upper bounds and is allowed outside the reciprocal rectangle. Assigning the selected zero, the product-Fourier variable and each Mellin variable shares of one cumulative height allowance avoids an unlicensed renewal of the buffer. The external test decay order can be chosen after τ. Since de Faveri's sieve accepts arbitrary coefficient vectors, the arithmetic moment itself does not require that growing external derivative order.

4. **Gamma integral and cancellation.** Because all retained primitive presentations are non-pure-norm, the original L-function is entire. Moving the Γ integral to `Re z=−1/4` crosses only z=0, whose residue is zero at the selected zero. There `Re(ρ+z)>1/4`, so the row-deletion factors cost a small power, and the truncated inverse has a polynomial X-bound. A sufficiently large fixed B makes the reflected integral o(1) after τ is chosen small. On the original line, the exact convolution coefficient is `ψ(n) Σ_{l|n}μ(l)V≤(Nl/D*)`; it vanishes for `1<Nn≤D*`, and the unit is `1+o(1)`. The source cutoff `1−V≤(2Nn/D*)` removes the unit while preserving the nonzero tail. Dyadic partitioning and Fourier separation yield support `r≤t+O(1/log X)`, `r+m≥t−O(1/log X)` and a product lower bound with exponent `(2β−1)(r+m)≥δ(r+m)`; this last comparison uses r+m≥0. The same selected presentation and Fourier variable occur in both factors. Source estimates remain valid for every fixed `t∈(1/2,3/2]`; the t<1 extension is a proof replay rather than the source's printed statement.

5. **Saturation.** Combining the product lower bound with the two pointwise upper bounds gives `2δ(m−1/2)_+≪η`. The fixed floor makes δ uniformly positive, so reducing η gives the requested m and r ranges. Dividing by the plain bound yields the inverse lower bound, and dividing by the inverse bound yields the plain lower bound, even when m exceeds 1/2 by the preliminary loss. Endpoint scale 1 can be included in an O(1) dyadic block.

6. **Common profiles and norm dependence.** After grouping finite ray/presentation labels and dyadic scales, detector profiles depend on the selected row only through β and the common h=γ−v. The cutoff ratio D/D* is then common. The fixed-parameter Sobolev inequality on `[σ_f,1]×[−H,H]` bounds the sum of rowwise suprema by integrals of the four common-coefficient moments with first/mixed derivatives; its constant is independent of H, with O(H) cost from the integration length. Derivatives insert powers of log x on fixed annuli. For the plain block, the unique q=q₁v² decomposition keeps the original masks, ψ_g(v²) is a parameter-independent row multiplier with modulus ≤1, and the normalized annular argument is exactly `Nq₁/(D/(Nv)²)`. The squarefree coefficient mass is `≪(Nv)^−2`, and Minkowski plus the ideal harmonic sum gives only logarithmic loss. The fixed ray class and frequency are chosen before every common-coefficient sieve application; no row-specific coefficient vector is passed to the sieve. The dependence of τ on ε and all fixed profile/height orders is essential.
