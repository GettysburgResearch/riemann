# Lowering the detector bin floor

**Status:** Source-proof replay for an arbitrary fixed floor above 1/2, conditional on the fixed-field, fixed-infinity-type analytic inputs and the stated profile/height uniformity. The printed source uses 51/100; the reformulation below is not its verbatim theorem. It is not a new individual zero-free region.

Fix `1/2<σ_f<σ<1` and a fixed totally complex number field K. For each X≥2 let a finite family of primitive nonprincipal unitary Hecke characters be given, with finitely many fixed infinity types, conductor norms at most `C X`, and original presentations obtained by deleting Euler factors of squarefree radical norm at most `C X`. Assume the standard fixed-field Hecke functional equation, polynomial strip growth, Euler-factor estimates, and the buffered Mellin/profile uniformity used in the [fixed-field detector replay](../2026-10-29-fixed-field-saturated-detector/README.md); include conjugates among the finite presentations. Then, for each fixed `1/2<t≤1`, ε>0 and sufficiently small fixed τ>0, any row with a primitive zero of real part at least σ and height at most X^τ has a bin endpoint a and a selected zero ρ for which

    a ≥ σ−e > σ_f,    δ=2a−1 ≥ 2σ_f−1>0,
    |Im ρ| ≪_{σ_f,e} X^τ,
    r ≤ t+O(1/log X),  r+m ≥ t−O(1/log X),
    0≤m≤1/2+O_{σ_f}(ε),  r≥t−1/2−O_{σ_f}(ε),
    |M_r|² ≫ X^{δr−ε},       |S_m|² ≫ X^{δm−ε},

with centrally normalized inverse and plain annular blocks using the same selected presentation and a common norm-twist frequency. Here e>0 is a sufficiently small fixed bin step chosen before τ, and the usual fixed-profile, bounded-frequency and polynomial-height costs are included in the assumptions. Integer-ideal endpoint effects may be absorbed into the displayed O(1/log X) losses. The result gives witnesses, not a zero-density conclusion without an additional common-coefficient moment and Sobolev reduction.

The changes to the source proof are as follows.

1. In Lemma 8.1 replace every occurrence of 51/100 in the nested maxima and floor grid by σ_f. Choose `0<e<min(10^(−3),σ−σ_f)`. The maxima remain in `[σ_f,1]`; the same I=`ceil(1/e)+2` pigeonhole argument and rounding give `a≥σ−e>σ_f` for a row originally having a zero at σ. Therefore the selected maximum is an actual zero, rather than the floor. The number of bins is fixed in X. No global extremal zero-free hypothesis is needed for this finite-family construction.
2. The source's Lemma 4.9 itself is stated uniformly for `1/2≤a≤1`. Its inner disk radius 49/100 stays fixed, the zero-free disk radius `2−a−2e` stays below 3/2, and the strict bounds on the nested maximum make the full disk zero-free. Three-circles and Borel–Carathéodory therefore require no alteration beyond the fixed strip/field generalization already assumed. Deleted Euler factors have no zeros in positive real part, and on the reflected line `1−a−6e≥−6e`; their negative-strip loss is unchanged. The functional equation's conductor factor is controlled by `(CX)^(a−1/2+6e)` and no lower conductor bound is used.
3. Lemma 8.2's Mellin shifts only use the buffered rectangle, reflected bound, compact length ranges, and a single cumulative height allowance. They yield the same exponents `δr` and `δ min(m,1−m)`, after choosing e, the fixed height orders and τ with the usual hierarchy. Finite field degree and fixed archimedean shifts change the polynomial height order but not the conclusion. The external Fourier tail order is chosen after τ.
4. In the Gamma detector shift to `Re z=−1/4`, the new L-argument has real part at least `σ_f−1/4>1/4`, so the deleted-factor estimate still holds with an arbitrarily small X-power. The source's original numerical calculation `−3+t(5/4−Re ρ)<0` remains valid, now with `Re ρ≥σ_f>1/2` and any t≤1; for a general fixed field choose the smoothing exponent B sufficiently large against its polynomial strip bound. The nonprincipal hypothesis removes possible L-poles; the residue at z=0 is zero because ρ is a zero. The Möbius convolution, smooth cutoff insertion, dyadic support, terminal truncation and product-profile Fourier separation are independent of the specific floor.
5. The saturation comparison gives `2δ(m−1/2)_+≤O(ε_prelim)`. As `δ≥2σ_f−1`, the constant in the m-bound is finite but deteriorates as σ_f decreases to 1/2. Choose the preliminary detector loss smaller accordingly, then choose τ after all fixed profile and height orders. This is why no uniformity as σ approaches 1/2 is asserted.

Thus the floor 51/100 is not essential to the detector argument for any single fixed σ>1/2. The statement remains conditional on the already separate fixed-field analytic/profile transfer; it does not claim source uniformity as σ_f approaches 1/2 or remove the source's other hypotheses.
