# Prior art for mixed-exponent fixed-order second moments

**Status:** Targeted source search, not a priority determination. The candidate is the source-conditional bound in [the conjugate-conductor-sieve note](2026-10-29-conjugate-conductor-sieve/README.md), and depends on its general-order rectangular sieve replay and approximate-functional-equation uniformity. None of the sources or the proposed replay is independently proved here.

For fixed `n>=4`, the proposed family consists of good labels `g=a b^(n−1)` with `a,b` squarefree and coprime, in two norm boxes. Its conjectured/conditional estimate is

```text
sum_(Na<=A, Nb<=B) mu²(ab) |L(1/2,psi chi_(a b^(n−1)))|²
   <<_(K,S,n,psi,epsilon) (AB)^(1+epsilon),
```

for a fixed unitary background psi. The index norm can be `Na (Nb)^(n−1)`, whereas the good primitive conductor is `N(ab)`. These orderings coincide only in special subfamilies.

* [de Faveri, Theorem 1.2 and Proposition 8.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04045v1) establish this type of conductor-box estimate for **n=3**, with a fixed unitary twist; the proof's rectangular sieve is stated only for n=3. The full cubic cube-free index family is exactly of the form `a b²`. His Remark 8.2 describes a possible column-support extension for the cubic result, not a general-n mixed-exponent theorem.
* [Diaconu–Ion–Paşol–Popa, Theorems A and D](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27131v1), state smoothed second-moment asymptotics for squarefree and n-th-power-free **index-norm-ordered** families, respectively. The printed general setup assumes the fixed field contains `mu_(2n)` and a suitable S; Theorem D weights the n-th-power-free label by `Q_g=product_p ord_p(g)>=1`. It explicitly remarks that this family is ordered by `Ng`, not conductor. The sources use partial L-functions and the theorem stated in the introduction is untwisted; a coefficient twist `chi_g(c)` in Theorem 10.5.2 is not the same as an arbitrary unitary twist **inside** the L-function. A fixed finite set of omitted Euler factors is bounded above and below at the central point, so the distinction between partial and full L-functions is harmless for an upper bound in the fixed-S family.

  For psi=1, positivity, the weight `Q_g>=1`, and conjugation allow one to select the smaller of `a b^(n−1)` and `b a^(n−1)`. Their norms are comparable to `AB B^(n−2)` and `AB A^(n−2)`, respectively. Thus, up to the source's fixed smooth-cutoff and `epsilon` losses, that index-ordered theorem gives a bound at scale at most `AB min(A,B)^(n−2)`, not the proposed `AB`. At balanced `A=B=T`, the source cutoff is `T^n`, whereas the proposed conductor-box cutoff is `T²`. The gap disappears when either factor has bounded norm; Theorem A already controls the squarefree-index subfamily. The source's printed `mu_(2n)` condition in particular should not be silently treated as an unconditional theorem over `Q(i)` for n=4, which does not contain `mu_8`.

* [Castillo–de Faveri–Dunn, *Non-vanishing for quartic Hecke L-functions and ranks of elliptic curves*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01316v2), studies quartic characters over `Q(i)`, obtains a mollified second-moment asymptotic with power-saving error in a **squarefree index** family, and allows an angular frequency in the displayed setup. Section 4.4.2 separately describes the general primitive quartic family `q_1 q_2² q_3³`, but that classification is not itself a conductor-ordered mixed-box second-moment theorem. In particular, the described analytic second moment does not directly yield the full `a b³` rectangle.
* [Gao–Zhao, *Moments of central values of quartic Dirichlet L-functions*](https://web.maths.unsw.edu.au/~leezhao/Presentations/MomQuartDiriLFun.pdf), reports an unconditional `Q^(7/6+epsilon)` second-moment bound for primitive quartic **Dirichlet characters over Q** of conductor up to Q. Their [later moment paper](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0019357722000635) obtains conjectural-order bounds under GRH for that rational family. These are meaningful conductor-ordered precedents, but they do not state the same full Hecke family over `Q(i)` or the two independent ideal boxes. Standard GRH-based uniform Lindelöf would also imply the proposed box upper bound, but that is a stronger global hypothesis than the source-conditional sieve replay.

The n>=4 mixed-exponent conductor rectangle would therefore be a potentially useful family moment **if** the rectangular sieve transfer and uniform AFE are proved. I found no exact previous theorem with the same boxes, fields, and fixed unitary twist in this targeted search. The existing squarefree-index and general index-norm results, and the order-three conductor result, mean the claim should remain carefully scoped; no novelty or priority is asserted.
