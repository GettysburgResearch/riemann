# Prior density results and the short-detector candidate

**Status:** Targeted source comparison, not an exhaustive priority search. The candidate below is source-conditional: its general-order rectangular sieve, short detector and fixed-field profile transfer are not established by the cited density papers.

The proposed exponent is

```text
E(sigma) = (7−6sigma)/(6−4sigma),       1/2<sigma<1,
```

for the conductor-ordered cubic family, or the restricted `a b^(n−1)` fixed-order family, with height `T<=X^tau` for some unspecified sufficiently small `tau>0` depending on the fixed data and requested loss. See [the short-detector note](2026-10-29-short-detector-density/README.md). It is a family statement and does not establish an individual zero-free half-plane.

* [Khale–O'Kuhn–Panidapu–Sun–Zhang, Lemma 3.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.09677v3) bounds the zeros of all primitive finite-order Hecke characters in a conductor-norm cutoff, also averaging angular modes, in the fixed Galois-field framework. At fixed or polylogarithmic height its conductor exponent is `H(sigma)=2 min(3/(2−sigma),2/sigma)(1−sigma)`. For `sigma>=4/5`, `H=4(1−sigma)/sigma`. At `sigma=4/5` this is `1` versus the candidate's `11/14`; at `5/6` it is `4/5` versus `3/4`; at `17/20` it is `12/17` versus `19/26`. The two curves meet at `(47−sqrt(97))/44=0.844344140868...`; the candidate is smaller below and the 2021 broad-family bound is smaller above. The 2021 result also has an explicit height factor; the comparison for `T=X^tau` depends on tau. This direct comparison is secure for the fixed finite-order twist and norm shifts; an arbitrary fixed ramified infinite type needs an additional source-basis check. Distinct good-prime local exponents make the cubic/conjugate-power labels distinct, so the broader family bound also bounds their label sum.
* [rStation Paper 52, version 5](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/52) states, over the Eisenstein field and sufficiently small power heights, a natural-index density bound `P^D`, `D=(8−7sigma)/(3(2−sigma))`, throughout `1/2<sigma<1` for principal finite twist, and a stronger cube-free-index theorem from `sigma=5/6` with fixed finite twists. Theorem 5.7 has `D(4/5)=2/3`, but the index is not the conductor: conjugate-label reordering gives conductor exponent at most `(3/2)D=1`, not `2/3`. Theorem 1 gives `3/5` at `5/6`, whose reordered conductor exponent is `9/10`; the candidate gives `3/4`. The source explicitly does not claim conductor ordering, and its analytic imports have not been independently checked here. Small index dyads require a separate trivial zero-count split to match a single global height; see [the conductor-density review](2026-10-29-cubic-conductor-density-review.md).
* [Blomer–Goldmakher–Louvel, Corollary 1.6](https://arxiv.org/abs/1112.1650) gives `8/11` at `4/5` for the squarefree-index subfamily, with an explicit height power. Its index and conductor are comparable on that subfamily, so its exponent is stronger than `11/14` there. It does not directly cover the complete cubic `a b²` family. [de Faveri, Corollary 1.5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04045v1) enlarges to n-th-power-free **index** ordering; conjugate reordering loses the factor described in the prior audit. De Faveri Theorem 1.2 concerns cubic central second moments ordered by conductor, rather than zero density.
* [Gao–Zhao, Theorem 1.3](https://dr.ntu.edu.sg/server/api/core/bitstreams/03a8ae6e-241a-4d6c-b7fd-816e15e2bf42/content), proves a one-level statistic for low-lying zeros of a squarefree, congruence-restricted cubic Hecke family, with test-function Fourier support in `(-3/5,3/5)`. It is not the same statistic as the off-critical zero count in a conductor-ordered cube-free family and supplies no immediate competitor to `E(sigma)`.
* [Xia's 2006 thesis, Theorem 1.1](https://etd.ohiolink.edu/acprod/odb_etd/ws/send_file/send?accession=osu1148497121&disposition=inline), treats squarefree Eisenstein labels `c≡1 mod 9` and states a norm exponent `10(1−sigma)` with `T^(1+epsilon)`; it is a narrower family. BGL footnote 3 explicitly questions a step in the 2007 paper's proof, so this is not used as a validated density input. [Corrigan](https://web.maths.unsw.edu.au/~ccorrigan/nn002.pdf) studies conductor-ordered fixed-order **Dirichlet** families over Q; that is a different family from all Hecke characters over a root-of-unity field.
* [Corrigan–Zhao, *Zero-density theorems for families of Dirichlet L-functions*, Theorem 2.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.00260), is direct earlier methodological precedent for combining an inverse polynomial with a plain L-function. It treats an arbitrary finite family of **primitive Dirichlet** characters, assumes a family large-sieve function `Delta(Q,N)` and an integrated central-line fourth moment bounded by `L`, and obtains a two-branch zero-density estimate involving `L^(1/3) Delta_T(Q,X)^(2/3) Y^(2(1−2sigma)/3)` and `Delta_T(Q,U) U^(1−2sigma) exp(−2U/Y)` for some `X<<U<<Y²`. Its proof uses a convolution tail and a mixed inverse/L witness. This does not state the cubic Hecke family, the source's finite-height detector saturation, or an applicable conductor-ordered cubic fourth moment; it is not an established bound with the candidate exponent here. It shows that the broad strategy of combining reciprocal and plain witnesses predates this note.
  Its arbitrary-coefficient hypothesis is a substantive limitation: one cannot insert the squarefree-column conductor bound `X+sqrt(X)N` as an unrestricted large-sieve norm. Set `N=sqrt(X)`, take columns `j=t³` with `Nt<=X^(1/6)` and equal l²-normalized coefficients, and take `>>X/log X` good prime-index rows `g` with `Ng~X`. The columns are coprime to each such g and all cubic symbols `chi_g(t³)` equal 1, giving energy `>>X^(7/6)/log X` for a coefficient vector of unit norm, whereas `X+sqrt(X)N=2X`. This disproves the unrestricted substitute even with an arbitrarily small power loss. The candidate's separate inverse and plain moments instead use their special coefficients and exact zero masks.
* Dihedral weight-one zero-density work predates this candidate. For example, [Chen An, Theorem 5.2](https://numdam.org/item/10.5802/jtnb.1109.pdf), applies Kowalski–Michel to automorphic representations attached to a family of dihedral quartic fields with fixed biquadratic closure data, obtaining a bound `T^B X^{c0(1−sigma)/(2sigma−1)}` for `sigma>=3/4` and `c0>21`, in that paper's discriminant parameter. [Cho–Kim](https://numdam.org/articles/10.5802/jtnb.812/) study density-one zero-free rectangles in dihedral and cyclic field families. These results demonstrate substantial precedent for dihedral-family density, but their families, ordering and parameters differ; their displayed results do not directly assert the candidate's `11/14` bound for the fixed quadratic-base conductor family. No claim of general novelty for dihedral density is warranted.
* [Hu's claimed unitary Weil zero-free half-plane](https://hexagonmath.org/2610.00216v1), if accepted, gives zero exceptional characters for `sigma>7/8`, at all heights; it is stronger there than any positive-power density estimate. Its manuscript explicitly records incomplete independent verification. It does not settle the comparison below `7/8`.

## Endpoint and lower-bound caution

There is a genuine obstruction to density-one zero-free rectangles at the **closed endpoint** `sigma=1/2` when `T=X^tau`, `tau>0`. For fixed degree and archimedean type, the uniform Hecke zero-counting formula gives order `T log(Q T^d)` nontrivial zeros for each primitive character of conductor `Q<<X`, for sufficiently large X. Its functional equation and conjugation give the symmetry `rho -> 1−conjugate(rho)` within the same L-function and the same height window. Thus at least half the counted zeros have real part at least `1/2`; every family member is exceptional at this endpoint. The candidate's exponent tends to 1 there, consistent with this obstruction.

This endpoint argument yields no positive lower bound for any fixed `sigma>1/2`: GRH predicts the exceptional count is zero. Likewise, a lower bound for a character large-sieve norm is not a lower bound for off-critical zeros. No unconditional lower barrier in the open half-strip was located in this search.
