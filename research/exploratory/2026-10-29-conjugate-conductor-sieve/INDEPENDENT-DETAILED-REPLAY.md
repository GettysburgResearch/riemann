# Independent detailed source replay of the general-order rectangular sieve

This audit concerns (1) of the adjacent README, with squarefree column q and unrestricted good a,b. It imports de Faveri v1's character construction, finite-ray reciprocity, conductor identities, Lemmas 3.1 and 3.3, Lemma 6.1, Theorem 1.1, and the standard ideal-counting/divisor and smooth Mellin estimates appearing in the source proof. It checks their hypotheses and how they enter the proposed extension; it is not a proof of the imported preprint. The source prints Proposition 8.1 only for n=3. No unaddressed cubic-specific identity or all-n counterexample was found in this replay.

1. **Kernel, symmetry, and original masks.** For each good q, χ_q is a zero-extended order-dividing-n character. Therefore χ_q(a b^(n−1))=χ_q(a) conjugate(χ_q(b)), also if a or b is not coprime to q. Interchanging the a,b variables and conjugating the coefficient vector swaps the two operator norms. Thus A≤B is allowed. This use of order n replaces, exactly, the source's opening cubic identity; it does not invoke cubic reciprocity.

2. **Source §8 opening and (8.2).** Smooth the unrestricted b-sum by a nonnegative majorant. Expand its square, partition original squarefree q-columns into finitely many source ray classes, and set `d=(q₁,q₂)`, `q_i=d q_i'`. Then q_i' are squarefree, mutually coprime and coprime to d. The χ_d mask survives as the condition `(b,d)=1`; it is not discarded. Source (2.4)–(2.5), conductor factorization (3.5), and Möbius inversion of the `(b,cd)=1` mask produce the source's (8.2), with r|cd and column coefficient `λ'_{q'}=λ_{dq'} χ^(q')(r) 1_((q',d)=1)`. All phases, masks and r divisors are independent of the a,b variables in the appropriate place. The terms with `Nd≈Q` have residual q_i' of bounded norm and give `AB(ABQ)^ε` by the source's trivial diagonal-range estimate; no order-three assumption appears.

3. **Nontrivial primitive quotient.** In the remaining `Nd<Q/2` ranges, the residual ideals have norm >1 and the source's finite ray-class partition puts q₁',q₂' in the same ray class. Source Lemma 2.4 and (3.5) give the primitive quotient character of conductor `q₁'q₂'` (the common fixed bad components cancel). It is nonprincipal because that conductor is nonunit. This remains true for composite n: since q_i' are squarefree, each good local exponent is 1, not an exponent divisible by n, and the source conductor formula applies. Lemma 3.1 is available because K contains μ_n for n≥3 and is totally complex. The zero dual ideal therefore contributes zero in the source Poisson formula.

4. **Root numbers and orientation.** In source Lemma 3.3 write each n-free q_i'=q_i'^* q_i'^flat. Since q_i' is squarefree, `q_i'^*=q_i'` and `q_i'^flat=1`; both cross-character factors are identically 1. The remaining η depends only on the already fixed ray class, while the β_q are unit-modulus factors attached to single columns. These can be absorbed into λ'. The b-side quotient is the conjugate of the a-side quotient; Poisson reflects it to the same orientation as the a-side, giving `χ_{q₁'}(a e) conjugate(χ_{q₂'}(a e))`. This works for any fixed n, not just n=3. The dual ideal's S-supported part s contributes fixed-column factors of modulus ≤1 and is summed over a subpower number of possibilities. None of these factors may be replaced by 1 prematurely.

5. **Mellin separation, second gcd, and row multiplicity.** Source (3.1)–(3.2) and its compactly supported W give `E≪Z Q² Nr/[B (Nd)² Ns]` for the good dual ideal e; the remaining E and Mellin-frequency tails cost an arbitrary fixed negative power after choosing decay orders. Norm weights are separated into one-column factors. The Möbius removal of `(q₁',q₂')=1` introduces g, `q_i'=g f_i`. For each fixed g the two q columns have the same g, and `χ_g(ae) conjugate(χ_g(ae))=1_((g,ae)=1)`. The inner expression is the square of one complex sum; hence it is legitimate to remove this row restriction by positivity after taking the absolute value. It would not be legitimate earlier. Combining a,e into u=ae introduces at most the ideal divisor count `τ_K(u)≪_ε (Nu)^ε`; the resulting row is unrestricted good and has norm in a fixed union of dyads around AE. The λ-vector depends on d,r,s,g and the common Mellin variable, but not on the individual row u.

6. **Source L₂ orientation and the general replacement for (8.3).** The remaining operator is the source L₂ with unrestricted good row u of length AE and n-th-power-free columns of length `Q/N(dg)`; the actual f-columns are squarefree and can be extended by zero. Lemma 6.1 extracts nth powers from unrestricted rows and bounds L₂(X,Y) by the maximum of `R L₁(X/R^n,Y)`, up to ε powers, for `R≲X^(1/n)`. Source finite-class reciprocity bounds L₁ by Θ_n of Theorem 1.1. Applied to the source terms `X+Y+X^(1−1/n)Y^(2/n)+X^(2/n)Y^(1−1/n)`, the four powers of R after extraction are `1−n, 1, 2−n, −1`. Thus

       L₂(X,Y) ≪ (XY)^ε [X+X^(1/n)Y+X^(1−1/n)Y^(2/n)+X^(2/n)Y^(1−1/n)].

The n=3 source (8.3) combines the last two terms; both are needed for n>3. At the unit-end of a dyad, replace a scale below 1 by an O(1) scale. The polynomial displayed above is increasing in both nonnegative arguments; the dyadic operator norm itself is not assumed monotone.

7. **Source p. 21–22 summations and ε bookkeeping.** Localize `Nd≈D`, `Nr≈R`, `1≪R≪D≪Q`. The source pre-(8.3) contribution is

       B Σ_{d≈D} Σ_{r|cd, r≈R} Σ_{s|c∞} Σ_g
         L₂(AE, Q/N(dg)) Σ_{f≈Q/N(dg)} |λ'_{gf}|²/[N(rgf)],

up to subpower losses and O(1) row dyads. Put h=gf. Because `Nh≈Q/Nd` and `Nr≈R`, the coefficient weight is `≪D/(RQ)`. Inserting the positive polynomial from step 6 allows `Q/N(dg)` to be enlarged to `O(Q/D)` and `AE` to be enlarged to `O(A Q²R/(BD²))`, including the subpower E cutoff. For each h, the number of g divisors is `≪(Nh)^ε`; for each original column q=dh the number of d divisors and r|cd choices is `≪(ABQ)^ε`. The fixed-prime s sectors and dyadic ranges also cost subpowers. This gives the adjacent README's prefactor `BD/(QR)` and coefficient mass, without assuming a monotonicity statement about L₂. If the E bound is below a positive constant there is no such range.

8. **Final monomials.** With `X=AQ²R/(BD²)`, `Y=Q/D`, multiplying by `BD/(QR)` yields the four localized terms recorded in the README, including the second term's `D^(−2/n)` and fourth term's `D^(−3/n)R^(2/n−1)`. All D,R exponents are nonpositive for n≥3. The first is bounded by the third since A≤B. On dropping D,R factors, the fourth is bounded by the second when AQ≤B and by the third when AQ≥B, since its ratios are `(AQ/B)^(1/n)` and `(B/AQ)^((n−3)/n)`. Adding the diagonal AB term proves the asserted two-term bracket, conditional on the source inputs.

The key indispensable restrictions are fixed n and K, q squarefree, the source's original ray and noncoprime masks, a common coefficient vector per sector, and arbitrary small ε losses selected after all fixed field/order/smoothness parameters. The argument does not extend by the same phase separation to nonsquarefree q, whose q^flat factors in Lemma 3.3 create cross-column terms. It also does not prove the separate detector or the original preprint's underlying analytic results.
