# Source-by-source audit of the conjugate-exponent sieve

**Scope.** This checks the proposed general-`n` transfer in [README](README.md) against [de Faveri v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04045v1), especially Sections 3, 6 and 8. It treats the source's Poisson formula, conductor/reciprocity identities, root-number lemma, large sieve, Mellin decay and divisor estimates as imported inputs; they are not independently proved here. The check concerns the displayed squarefree-column operator, not a nonsquarefree extension, detector, or a zero-free result. No source step has been identified that requires `n=3` once the input kernel is explicitly conjugate-paired and the general four-term large sieve is used. The calculation below records how the source hypotheses enter rather than asserting an independent verification of the full source paper.

1. **Kernel and symmetry.** On good ideals the source's zero extension gives `chi_q(a b^(n−1))=chi_q(a) overline(chi_q(b))`, including primes dividing `q`. Swap the two row scales and conjugate the coefficient vector to obtain equality of the two operator norms. The reduced case `A<=B` is therefore legitimate for all `n>=3`.

2. **Gcd and ray sectors.** Expand the square in the smooth nonnegative b-weight and write original squarefree column ideals `q_i=d q_i'`, where `d=(q_1,q_2)`. Then `q_1',q_2'` are squarefree, mutually coprime and coprime to `d`. Partition their finitely many source ray classes; the `a`- and `b`-dependence is a product of quotient characters with opposite orientation. Source equation (8.2) removes the common-factor/bad-prime b-mask by a divisor sum `r|c d`; the modified column coefficient has the form `lambda_(qd) chi^(q)(r) 1_((q,d)=1)`, with its source ray condition. Here `chi^(q)` is the good-conductor component from source (3.4), and this coefficient depends on `q,d,r`, not on `a` or the later dual variable. The dyads with `Nd` comparable to `Q` have only boundedly many possible residual column ideals and contribute `AB` up to the usual divisor and epsilon factors, as in the source.

3. **Primitive conductor and root number.** For the remaining dyads, put the two residual columns in the same fixed ray class. Source (3.5) identifies the primitive quotient with conductor `q_1' q_2'`: the local good characters are primitive at their respective coprime squarefree supports and the fixed bad conductor components cancel. The quotient is nonprincipal, so the Poisson zero frequency is absent. Lemma 3.3 is explicitly stated for general n. Since `q_i'` is squarefree, its source extra-power factor `q_i'^flat` is 1 and its sharp factor is `q_i'`; hence the cross terms in Lemma 3.3 are 1. The remaining `eta`, which depends only on the fixed classes, is constant in the sector, and the root number is a product of one-column unit phases. This is the precise point where squarefree columns are used. No use of `n=3` is needed to obtain these facts.

4. **Poisson orientation and fixed-prime factors.** Source Lemma 3.1 reflects the b-side primitive quotient. The b-side quotient is the complex conjugate of the a-side quotient, so the reflected quotient has the same orientation as the a-side factor, yielding `chi_(q_1')(a e) overline(chi_(q_2')(a e))` after the source's decomposition of the nonzero dual ideal into its fixed-bad-prime part `s` and good part `e`. The root number and `s`-dependence contribute only one-column factors of modulus at most 1; after including them in `lambda'_q`, the latter depends on `q,d,r,s` and the common Mellin frequency, but not on `a` or `e`. The fixed `s` lies in a set supported on finitely many primes, so the source's norm cutoff contributes only a subpower number of sectors.

5. **Dual gcd and coefficient mass.** Source Mellin separation moves all variable norm weights into common coefficients. It removes the residual coprimality condition by Möbius inversion, introducing `g` and writing `q_i'=g f_i` inside the enlarged sums, while retaining a common class for `f_1,f_2`. The common factor contributes `chi_g(ae) overline(chi_g(ae))=1_((g,ae)=1)`; because the resulting diagonal quadratic form is nonnegative, this row restriction can be dropped, with the relevant divisor factors retained. Combine `a e` into an unrestricted good ideal row `u`; its multiplicity is divisor-bounded. The resulting source `L_2` operator has row scale comparable to `AE`, n-th-power-free (indeed squarefree) columns with scale `Q/N(dg)`, and a coefficient vector determined by `lambda'_(gf)`. Its original q-mask was kept until this positivity step. Sum over g using divisor bounds: each original squarefree coefficient `lambda_h` occurs a divisor-bounded number of times. This is exactly why one cannot replace the row-dependent masks by 1 before expanding the square.

6. **General `L_2` and orientation.** The source definition of `L_2(X,Y)` has unrestricted good row ideals and n-th-power-free columns; it is the orientation appearing in the preceding step. Source Lemma 6.1 bounds it by the maximum of `R L_1(X/R^n,Y)`. Source `L_1` is controlled, through its finite ray-class reciprocity partition, by Theorem 1.1. Termwise maximization in `R` gives

   `X + X^(1/n)Y + X^(1−1/n)Y^(2/n) + X^(2/n)Y^(1−1/n)`

   up to arbitrary small powers. The n=3 source collapses the last two terms; for general n both must be retained. This estimate holds for squarefree column support by extension by zero, including the inherited coefficient weights.

7. **Localized scales and tails.** In source p. 21, rapid decay restricts the nonzero good dual ideal to `E << Z Q² Nr/(B (Nd)²)`, with `Z=(ABQ)^small`. Localizing `Nd~D`, `Nr~R` gives `1<<R<<D<<Q`; if the E upper bound is below a positive constant, no E-dyad occurs. Source p. 22 then contributes, up to a fresh small power, the prefactor `BD/(Q R)`, the operator bound at `X~A E`, `Y~Q/D`, and the divisor-bounded coefficient mass `sum_(d~D,h~Q/Nd)|lambda_(hd)|²`. The operator itself need not be monotone in a dyadic scale: monotonicity is used only for the positive polynomial **after** inserting the `L_2` bound, to replace `Q/N(dg)` by `O(Q/D)` and `AE` by `O(A Q²R/(B D²))`. Source's `s`-sum, Mellin t-integral and e-tail are absorbed by an arbitrarily small power after the field, n and smooth weights are fixed. Dyadic counts and the repeated d,h representations are divisor-bounded and likewise absorbed. These are source imports; no uniformity in growing n or varying field is claimed.

8. **Final algebra.** Multiplying the four terms by `BD/(QR)` gives exactly the four displayed localized expressions in the README. All D and R powers are nonpositive. With `A<=B`, the global first is at most the global third. If `AQ<=B`, the fourth is at most the second; if `AQ>=B`, it is at most the third, since `n>=3`. Thus, within the explicitly imported source identities and uniform estimates, the asserted general-`n` rectangular bound follows with the claimed two off-diagonal terms. The source itself does not state this general-`n` proposition.

This audit does not extend the claim to n-th-power-free but nonsquarefree q: then the extra-power factors in Lemma 3.3 need not be 1 and couple the two columns. It also does not certify the independent correctness of the source's analytic lemmas or the separate approximate-functional-equation consequence.
