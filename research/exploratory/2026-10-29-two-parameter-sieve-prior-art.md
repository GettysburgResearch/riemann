# Two-parameter sextic rows: source comparison

**Status.** This is a nonexhaustive source search, not a proof of a new large sieve or a zero-free region. The row decomposition, residue identities and source-to-physical sector matching are the inputs in [the square-part theta note](2026-10-29-squarepart-cubic-theta-moments.md).

Write a good sixth-free row as `b=w v²`, with w squarefree and v cube-free, retaining common-prime zeros. On a dyad `Nw≲W=B/V²`, `Nv≈V`, multiplicativity gives

    χ_n(wv²)=χ_n(w) χ_n(v)².

Fixing w makes `χ_n(w)` a common column coefficient and gives the cubic Θ₃ cap `W Θ₃(V,D)` after summing w. Fixing v makes `χ_n(v)²` common and gives the sextic cap `V Θ₆(W,D)`. Original row masks and the completed theta cube-index contractions must remain in both arguments. Neither step lets a coefficient that depends simultaneously on the free w and v variables be treated as common for both.

## Relevant source statements

* [De Faveri, arXiv:2610.04045v1, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.04045v1#S1.Thmtheorem1) states the unrestricted-complex-coefficient n-free FHL sieve for fixed K containing μ_n, n≥3, including n=3,6. At n=3 the norm factor is `V+D+(VD)^(2/3)`; at n=6 it is `W+D+W^(5/6)D^(1/3)+W^(1/3)D^(5/6)`. These are **one-row-variable** estimates and justify the two fixed-variable caps, not a new joint mixed-order operator inequality. Both row and column are n-free good ideals with fixed FHL data; finite reciprocity sectors and literal zeros remain necessary.
* [Goldmakher–Louvel, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1112.1642), gives the optimal `M+D` quadratic Hecke sieve for squarefree good ideals over fixed K. It controls a quadratic variable when the column vector is common after other parameters are fixed. The factor `χ_n(v)²` is cubic and varies with n,v; treating it as a single coefficient independent of v is invalid. Applying the quadratic sieve separately after discarding that shared dependency does not prove a joint bound.
* [Baier–Young, arXiv:0804.2233v4, Theorems 1.4 and 1.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0804.2233v4), establishes a sextic **Dirichlet** large sieve with arbitrary squarefree rational-column coefficients, primitive rational-conductor character sums and factor `(QM)^ε min{Q^(5/3)+M, Q^(4/3)+Q^(1/2)M, Q^(11/9)+Q^(2/3)M, Q+Q^(1/3)M^(5/3)+M^(12/5)}`. Theorem 1.5 replaces the cubic order condition by order dividing six; its proof is omitted there as analogous. This does not directly cover both arbitrary Eisenstein ideal variables or the two-parameter `w v²` row kernel. Comparing the displayed exponents numerically after identifying rational sizes with ideal norms would be unjustified.
* [Dunn, *Metaplectic cusp forms and the large sieve*, Algebra & Number Theory 19 (2025), Theorem 1.2](https://msp.org/ant/2025/19-9/ant-v19-n9-p04-s.pdf), treats bilinear Fourier-coefficient sums for a **fixed cuspidal** cubic metaplectic form. The source completed cubic theta is residual/noncuspidal, and the additional `χ_n(w)` factor is moving as w varies. No source uniformity over those growing twists or resulting mixed row operator is stated. This does not establish a saving over Θ₆/Θ₃ in the critical dyads.
* [Gao–Zhao, *Mean values of some Hecke characters*, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.08164), evaluates particular smooth untwisted first moments of quadratic, cubic or quartic symbols; its cubic example is over E. It is not an arbitrary-coefficient squared norm and does not contain the joint sextic-plus-quadratic row dependence.

At the previously identified obstruction with `V=1`, the fixed-w cubic cap for a fourth-theta moment is of size `B Θ₃(1,Z²)≍B Z²` up to losses, while the unstratified Θ₆ cap is `Θ₆(B,Z²)`. At `B=Y^(10283/11000)` and `Z=Y^(4093/5500)`, their exponents are respectively `2.42318...` and `17071/11000≈1.55191`; hence the fixed-w cubic cap supplies no gain there. This is an upper-bound comparison, not a lower bound or a barrier to a new joint estimate. The current stratified endpoint has its own optimization, so this specific numerical example is not claimed to identify every subsequent active dyad.

A directly useful new source would bound the common kernel over both w and v, with the actual Möbius/Gauss or completed-theta coefficients and masks, uniformly in the dyadic parameters and the fixed source-sector/profile families, with a power saving over the minimum of the existing caps in at least the active region. No cited theorem above gives that conclusion; no exhaustive nonexistence claim is made.
