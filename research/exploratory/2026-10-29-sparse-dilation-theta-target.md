# A sparse theta estimate sufficient for the large-gcd divisor terms

**Status.** This is a conditional reduction, not a proved theta estimate or zero-free region. It uses the phase calibration and fixed-sector Poisson formula in [the low-dual note](2026-10-29-low-dual-theta-bound.md). The cited paper 5 theorem does not prove the new uniform sparse estimate below. The small original-gcd range remains open even if the estimate holds.

Keep the notation of that note: `P=Y^(6/5)`, original squarefree common gcd `d`, its good divisor `k|d`, auxiliary squarefree projector variable `g` with `(g,d)=1`, and dual frequency `v`. Fix norm dyads `Nd≈D₀`, `Nk≈K`, `Ng≈G`, `Nv≈H`, with `1≤K≲D₀` and residual column length `D≈P/(D₀G)`. Fixed bad primes in the source's divisor k are handled by finitely many fixed sectors and do not change power exponents. Let `S_{h,f}(D;d)` be paper 5 (9.1) for the fixed puncture `Q=d`, angular order −1 and fixed ray-sector twists. For the factorized norm profiles generated by the Poisson kernel, the following is a precise proposed new input:

    Σ_(Ng≈G, (g,d)=1) Σ_(Nv≤H)
       |S_{vk,gk}(D;d)|²
       ≪_ε (YPD₀KGH)^ε G(H+DG).                              (A)

The label `gk` is squarefree, and the source's zero extensions make (A) meaningful even when v meets d or g. Each smooth column profile and norm twist has the same fixed-seminorm and polynomial-height costs as paper 5; the bound must be uniform in all good k|d and the indicated fixed-sector data. The outer sum is over g, not over every label of size GK, and the inner sum is over v, not all rows of size HK. The known paper 5 first alternative proves (A) for k=1; applying it naively at general k gives an extra K², since it enlarges the row and label sets. There is no justification here for removing that loss.

Assuming (A), the factorized physical summand for each fixed `(d,k)` has exterior weight `(Y/P)/(GK)`: `1/G` is the common auxiliary divisor from the two `1/sqrt(Nr)` weights and `1/K` is the original Poisson mask-divisor factor. Cauchy across the two profiles gives the same bound as their sum of squares. The number of pairs `(d,k)` in the two original norm dyads with `k|d` is `≪D₀^(1+ε)`, by the ideal divisor bound. Consequently the whole `(D₀,K,G,H)` dyad is bounded by

    ≪ Y^ε (Y D₀/(P K))(H+P/D₀)
     = Y^ε [Y/K + Y D₀H/(P K)].                             (B)

The source transform confines nonnegligible frequencies to `H≲Y^ε P² K/(Y D₀²)`, whence (B) is `≪Y^ε(Y+P/D₀)`. Summing the logarithmically many dyads would therefore control **all** good divisor terms with `D₀≥P/Y=Y^(1/5)` at `O(Y^(1+ε))`. Principal restoration and fixed bad-prime divisors have the same separate treatments as in the low-dual note. The region `D₀<Y^(1/5)` is not controlled by this calculation.

The assumed estimate is genuinely stronger than paper 5 (9.3) in the moving-k situation: the character relation `χ_n(v) overline(χ_n(k))=χ_n(vk)χ_n(k)^4` produces a growing-conductor twist and a coupled sparse pair of row/label dilations. Allowing arbitrary fixed finite-ray twists does not supply uniformity in k. No claim that (A) holds, or that (A) is necessary, is made.

**Subsequent unconditional-to-the-stated-sources progress.** The [growing-k aggregation](2026-10-29-low-dual-theta-bound.md#a-proposed-growing-k-aggregation-under-independent-review) obtains a different partial estimate without assuming (A), by fixing `d′=d/k` and averaging the coupled labels `f=gk` and rows `h=vk` together. Combined with the generic source bound it controls all divisor scales for logarithmic `d≥12/35`, subject to the cited analytic inputs. It does not prove (A), and (A), if available, would push the all-divisor threshold further to `d≥1/5`.

## A generic-sieve range of (A)

There is also a portion of the coupled problem where no new sparse estimate is needed. For each fixed g and k, absorb `χ_n(k)^5 χ_n(g)^4` and the `d` puncture into the column coefficients, which retain subpower ℓ² mass; then apply the unrestricted-row sextic large sieve to the v sum. The resulting bound is

    G (YD)^ε [H+D H^(1/6)+H^(5/6)D^(1/3)+H^(1/3)D^(5/6)].   (C)

The bracket is `≪H` whenever `H≥D²`. At the top natural dual scale `H₀=P²K/(Y D₀²)` and `D=P/(D₀G)`, this condition is exactly

    K G² ≥ Y,                                                   (D)

up to fixed-power margins and the harmless smooth cutoffs. Even when a particular dual dyad has `H<H₀`, positivity allows enlargement to `H₀`; the same estimate then bounds the entire dyadic portion. Inserting `G H₀` for (A)'s right side into the pair-counting argument gives `≪Y^ε P/D₀`. Therefore, conditional only on the source Poisson formula and unrestricted sextic sieve, every large-auxiliary-g dyad satisfying (D) is controlled at `O(Y^(1+ε))` whenever `D₀≥Y^(1/5)`, irrespective of K. This is a genuine partial result, not the full proposed estimate (A); the complementary range `KG²<Y` still requires other input. Taking a fixed margin in (D) absorbs the localizations' small powers, or equivalently include them in the final ε.

More generally retain all four terms in (C), again enlarged to the top natural scale, rather than imposing (D). Write `D₀=Y^d`, `K=Y^κ` and `G=Y^g`. The entire `(d,κ,g)` dyad is bounded, up to `Y^ε`, by

    Y^(6/5−d)
    +Y^(37/30−d/3−g−5κ/6)
    +Y^(41/30−d−g/3−κ/6)
    +Y^(19/15−d/2−5g/6−2κ/3).                            (E)

The four powers follow by multiplying the bracket in (C) by `(Y/P)D₀/K` (equivalently, multiplying the whole expression (C) by `(Y/P)D₀/(KG)`) and substituting `D=P/(D₀G)` and `H₀=P²K/(YD₀²)`. In particular the g-localized portion is `O(Y^(1+ε))` whenever

    d≥1/5,
    g≥7/30−d/3−5κ/6,
    g≥11/10−3d−κ/2,
    g≥8/25−3d/5−4κ/5.                                     (F)

Negative lower bounds on g may be replaced by zero. Criterion (F) includes (D), and can be stronger; for instance `(d,κ)=(1/4,1/10)` only requires `g≥3/10` in (F), whereas (D) requires `g≥9/20`. This retains the original g localization; one cannot drop it and claim the entire `(d,κ)` block is controlled. Summing the finitely many logarithmic g and h dyads is harmless, while a fixed margin or an adjustment of ε handles the smooth localization constants.

**Independent review.** High_match checked the generic-sieve exponents, the `1/G` exterior normalization, the localization criterion and source qualifications. The proposed general estimate (A) remains unproved.
