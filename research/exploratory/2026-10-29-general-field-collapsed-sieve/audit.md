# Independent audit: all-ideal sextic row reduction

**Scope and status:** Source review of [the proposed general-field note](README.md), with primary text checked in A. de Faveri, [arXiv:2610.04045v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04045v1), especially Theorem 1.1 and §§2.3–2.5, and Xu–Sang, [arXiv:2610.17675v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17675v2), Remark 2.1 and Lemma 5.2. Neither preprint's analytic proof was replayed. This audit supplies no new zero-free region or priority claim.

The asserted all-ideal-row estimate follows from de Faveri's stated theorem by a shorter direct identity. His §2.5.1, equation (2.4), states that for good integral ideals, the zero-extended family is multiplicative in its row index, including at collisions. Since its values are in `mu_6` or zero, for good integral `w,v,c`

```text
chi_(w v^6)(c) = chi_w(c) * 1_((v,c)=1).                    (A1)
```

No coprimality between `w` and `v` is needed. In particular this identity applies to nonprincipal rows without choosing generators, finite Kummer phases or any additional reciprocity convention. For `a=w v^6`, `w` uniquely sixth-power-free, fix `v`, apply Theorem 1.1 to `w` with the masked column coefficients, and sum `v`. The four terms in (6) of the main note result from the corresponding sums of powers `1,(Nv)^(-6),(Nv)^(-5),(Nv)^(-2)` and standard fixed-field ideal counting. The column masks never increase the coefficient squared norm. This establishes the corollary **conditional on de Faveri's theorem and its construction**, with the source's fixed field, fixed S, representatives and good ideals.

Theorem 1.1 bounds `Theta_6`, whose row and column ideals are both sixth-power-free; the general reduction respects those hypotheses. The source denotes the family by `chi_a(b)` and the row index by `a`. It is not legitimate to transpose to a literal `(u/c)_6` family over arbitrary number fields without proving the phase comparison. The main note's frozen-presentation/Kummer argument is aimed at that distinct element-row comparison: outside finitely many places its character is the Kummer character of the frozen numerator, and comparison by the finite `K(S,6)` class and the explicit coprimality mask is plausible. I found no new fatal issue there, but did not independently reconstruct all class-field identifications from first principles.

The factorization in the element-row argument uses `v` as an **arbitrary integral ideal**, not necessarily a good ideal: its S-supported part absorbs sixth powers in the S-valuations. Only the good part of `v` belongs in the phase lemma and column mask; the norm of the full `v` controls summation. Its residual `s` has exponents 0 through 5 and hence finitely many possibilities. A norm restriction on elements alone does not control unit multiplicity over a general field; the main statement appropriately assumes bounded multiplicity per principal ideal. The coefficient-mass bound relies on a fixed number of slots and row-independent bounded coefficients; it does not cover row-dependent profiles or arbitrary independent orientation choices without further work.

## Prior art and scope of any obstruction

The sixth-power stripping is not a new technique. De Faveri §1.3.1, (1.10), already removes n-th powers inside his proof sketch. More directly, Xu–Sang Lemma 5.2 explicitly uses `J_6(t^6 u,b)=J_6(u,b)1_((t,b)=1)`, makes no coprimality assumption on `t,u`, and displays the same four summation terms, with a tail parameter. Their lemma's formal statement has additional scale restrictions and squarefree columns; its unsimplified proof is direct precedent for the Eisenstein calculation. This search did not locate the exact general-field all-row corollary or the marked-polynomial formulation verbatim; that is not evidence of novelty.

For Eisenstein squarefree rows and columns, Xu–Sang Theorem 1.1 claims the lower bound containing `A^(5/6)B^(1/3)`. Assuming that theorem and its stated OpenAI zero-slot moment input, an arbitrary-coefficient `O(A^(1+epsilon))` estimate cannot hold when `B=A^(1/2+delta)` for fixed `delta>0`, since the squared operator norm is at least `A^(1+delta/3-o(1))`. This does not exclude a stronger estimate for the special Möbius/prime product, and it is not a lower bound proved here for every number field. Their Theorem 1.2's structured prime estimate is a distinct, unnormalized fixed-weight result and does not directly supply the marked inverse estimate required by the detector.
