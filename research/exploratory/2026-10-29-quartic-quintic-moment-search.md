# Targeted quartic and quintic moment search

This note records source scope, not an exhaustive prior-art search or a new bound. The sought input is a coefficient-specific inverse or ordinary fourth moment over the **full** natural fourth-free or fifth-free FHL family, with bounded common smooth profiles, original zero masks and polynomial norm-height control. A moment for squarefree labels or central L-values alone does not automatically provide that input.

* [Castillo–de Faveri–Dunn, arXiv:2604.01316v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01316v2), Theorem 1.2 and Propositions 1.4–1.5, prove central-value nonvanishing using twisted and mollified central first and second moments of quartic Hecke characters over Q(i). Their row label q is squarefree and lies in a fixed congruence class modulo `(1+i)^7`. The mollifier has the coefficient and length constraints in §1.2. This is meaningful quartic precedent, but the source does not state a uniform annular ordinary or inverse fourth moment over every fourth-free label or at the detector's polynomial-height profile parameters.
* [Diaconu–Ion–Paşol–Popa, arXiv:2607.27131v1, Proposition 7.2.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27131v1#S7.SS2.SSS4), bounds a weighted second moment of central values `|L(1/2,ψχ_{a f})|²` over r-free a, coprime to a fixed r-free f, with a cost `N(rad f)^(1/2+ε)`. Its field hypotheses include μ_{2r}⊂K, so the r=4 theorem is not literally a result over Q(i). It is a central-value L² estimate, not the rowwise fourth moment for a freely truncated inverse or ordinary Dirichlet polynomial needed at the detector witness.
* [Gao–Zhao, arXiv:2306.10726](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10726), treat first central moments for several quadratic, cubic, quartic and sextic families. Their first moments do not by themselves bound a zero-detecting fourth moment. Their separately studied Dirichlet families and one-level densities are different objects from the full Hecke n-free family ordered by natural ideal norm.

[Liu's sextic product-moment input](2026-10-29-liu-mixed-witness/README.md) is substantially stronger in its explicitly stated sextic presentations: its unmarked ordinary fourth moment and inverse second moment yield stronger sixth-free density consequences than the generic sieve. Its cubic theta/exceptional-representation construction is specific to that order and no analogous full fourth- or fifth-order family theorem is asserted there. A finite-order base change or the inclusion of μ₆ in a larger field does not turn an arbitrary quartic or quintic character family into the same sixth-order row family at the original norm scale.

For comparison with the published arbitrary-coefficient input, [de Faveri, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04045v1), covers n-free rows and columns and is the source of the four Θ_n faces used in the [direct density calculation](2026-10-29-direct-nfree-density/README.md). The presently located specialized results above do not state a smaller general full-family ordinary/inverse moment at the detector's σ=4/5 or θ=0.8738694 scales. Every such deduction also needs uniformity for the chosen presentations, conductor variation and a common height; these cannot be inferred from the central-point labels alone.
