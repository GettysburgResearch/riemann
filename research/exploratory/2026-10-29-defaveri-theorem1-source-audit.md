# Source audit: de Faveri's n-free large sieve

**Scope.** This checks the formulation and the dependency chain in [de Faveri, arXiv:2610.04045v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.04045v1), especially the uses in the direct-density note. It is not an independent proof of the Poisson/root-number argument or all referenced Hecke theory. No fatal contradiction was identified in this limited audit.

## Exact statement and hypotheses

The source's §1.1 fixes an integer n≥3, a **fixed** number field K containing μ_n, the FHL presentation and a finite set S of finite places containing all primes dividing n, large enough that O_S has class number one. Both sums in (1.2) are over *integral ideals prime to S*, and the ♭ marker means n-th-power-free, not merely squarefree or prime elements. Both cutoffs are absolute ideal norm. For arbitrary complex column λ, Theorem 1.1 asserts the operator bound

    Θ_n(A,B) ≪_(K,S,n,ε) (AB)^ε
       [A+B+A^(1−1/n)B^(2/n)+A^(2/n)B^(1−1/n)].               (1)

The implied constants depend on all fixed family data; the paper states this in the introduction's footnote. This is not a uniform theorem for a growing base field, n or S, nor is its row ordering by primitive conductor. It counts every prescribed n-free label, not a new family of all n-torsion characters. A fixed unitary coefficient multiplier on the column is harmless in (1), but a moving row-dependent twist is not automatically a common column multiplier.

Section 2.5.1 explicitly gives the source character modulus c·a, its primitive good conductor equal to the radical of n-free a, fixed bad conductor dividing c, trivial infinity type, and multiplicativity including the original zero extension for integral indices. At a good prime dividing an n-free label the local tame character has order n/gcd(n,e) where e is the label exponent; hence exponents 1,…,n−1 determine the local character provided the source's root-of-unity embedding is fixed. The imprimitive and primitive L-functions differ by fixed-bad and label-supported deleted Euler factors; the original zeros at shared primes are needed in applications.

Theorem 1.1 itself does not furnish the detector, the separate primitive/zero-extended L-function contour transfer, Sobolev uniformity, or a growing-row twist. These are separate dependencies in the direct-density proof. Finite ray-class reciprocity phases are fixed by splitting finitely many classes; one cannot silently erase masks or regard an arbitrary FHL presentation as an exact chosen symbol.

## Printed proof chain

1. §4 defines dyadic ℒ₁, ℒ₂, ℒ₃. Lemma 4.1 establishes ℒ₁(A,B)≍ℒ₁(B,A) using finite ray reciprocity followed by duality; it is not literal entrywise symmetry. Lemma 4.3 passes to all good row ideals by positivity after the finite class partition. Lemma 4.4 expands the square, sorts `(b₁,b₂)=d`, retains the full `(a,d)=1` mask, and replaces the difference by a primitive product after matching ray classes; inclusion–exclusion divisors r|cd then lead to the ℒ₃ inequality with `Δ₁≪Δ₂`. The coefficient dependence on d,r is allowed because the norm is a supremum. The n-free conditions on bᵢd remain in the coefficient restriction.
2. Lemma 2.4 gives primitive product conductor `b₁* b₂*` for coprime labels in one fixed ray class. Lemma 3.3 factors its root number into unit phases attached separately to the two labels, finite class phases, and the explicit cross-characters; (3.4) deliberately permits auxiliary Hecke characters with opposite infinite types. Lemma 3.1 is applied only to nontrivial primitive characters. In Lemma 5.1, the B=1 case is treated trivially; for B>1, coprime dyadic labels cannot both be units, so the product character has nonunit conductor and the stated nontriviality is consistent. The resulting estimate is the ℒ₃-to-ℒ₂ Poisson inequality of Lemma 5.1, with separate K_i,L_i ranges and finite-ray/prime masks. Its delicate character splitting and Fourier-decay estimates are imported here, not independently re-proved.
3. Lemma 6.1 writes every ideal uniquely as `a=d r^n` with n-free d and retains `χ_b(r)^n=1_(r,b)=1` as a column mask. Dyadic r and an L² supremum yield `ℒ₂(A,B)≪(AB)^ε max_{R≤(2A)^(1/n)} R ℒ₁(A/R^n,B)`, up to the usual bounded unit-scale convention. The ideal is not assumed principal.
4. Lemma 7.2 starts with the elementary ℒ₂≪AB and invokes Lemmas 5.1, 4.4 and 4.3 to establish E₂, without using the conclusion. Proposition 7.1 turns E_α into E_{2−(n−1)/(nα−1)} for α>1+1/n. Starting α=2, these iterates decrease to 1+1/n. For each requested ε choose a **finite** iterate with excess <ε, absorbing `B^excess` into `(AB)^ε`; no assertion of uniform constants in an infinite limit is needed. Finite-class symmetry lets one eliminate the remaining `B^(1+1/n)` term by using A≥B or swapping A,B. Finally dyadic summation gives (1).

The source's proof contains no visible logical circle in this chain: the initial bound and Poisson/gcd estimates precede the iteration and do not invoke Theorem 1.1. The separate conductor-ordered cubic moment in §8 does use Theorem 1.1 but is not needed for its proof. The arbitrary-coefficient n-free norm in (1) and fixed-K uniformity are exactly the form required for the inverse witness and the extracted squarefree columns; the further row-varying detector profiles still require the separately stated common Sobolev/separation proof.

**Residual audit limitation.** The strongest source-dependent steps remain Lemma 2.4, Lemma 3.3's auxiliary infinite-type root-number splitting, and the coupled double sum and truncation in Lemma 5.1. The discussion above checks their precise hypotheses and how the proof uses them, not their full proofs. A full source verification would need to replay those analytic/local identities and the smoothing/finite-sector constants independently. In particular, the theorem should be described as a recent imported preprint claim, not an independently established input.
