# Conditional density-one rectangles for dihedral weight-one forms

**Status:** A class-field and Artin-induction consequence of the [restricted-family density estimate](../2026-10-29-short-cubic-conductor-detector/README.md). It inherits that estimate's explicit fixed-field detector, short-cutoff, lowered-floor, general-order rectangular-sieve and profile/height assumptions. Those analytic inputs are not proved independently here. This is a density-one statement, not an individual zero-free region, and no priority claim is made.

Fix n in {3,4,6}. Take K=Q(√−3) for n=3 or 6 and K=Q(i) for n=4. In the cited fixed-order Hecke family, let g range over good ideals of the form `a b^(n−1)`, where a,b are squarefree and coprime, and retain those whose finite-order primitive character χ_g satisfies `χ_g^c≠χ_g`, where c is the nontrivial element of Gal(K/Q). Let π_g be the cuspidal dihedral weight-one automorphic representation of GL₂/Q obtained by automorphic induction, equivalently the representation attached to `Ind_{W_K}^{W_Q} χ_g`. Treat isomorphic representations as one element of the family and use their conductor as the ordering parameter.

Assuming the cited density result, for every fixed `1/2<σ<1` and ε>0 there is τ>0 such that the number of these distinct representations of conductor at most X having a zero in `Re s≥σ`, `|Im s|≤X^τ` is

    ≪_{K,n,σ,ε} X^{1−(2σ−1)/(2(3−2σ))+ε}.                       (1)

Each zero is understood in the standard degree-two L-function of π_g. The family contains ≫X/log X distinct representations of conductor at most X, so the exceptional proportion tends to zero. For example, at σ=4/5 the exceptional exponent is 11/14. The constants and the permitted positive height power may depend on all fixed parameters, and no uniformity as σ approaches 1/2 is claimed.

To verify the transfer, finite-order Hecke characters correspond to finite-image one-dimensional Weil/Artin characters by global class field theory. Since `χ_g^c≠χ_g`, induction is irreducible and the standard Artin formalism and conductor formula give

    L_Q(s, Ind_{W_K}^{W_Q} χ_g)=L_K(s,χ_g),
    N(Ind_{W_K}^{W_Q} χ_g)=|D_K| N f(χ_g).                       (2)

The induction is odd: complex conjugation lies outside W_K and acts in the induced two-dimensional representation with eigenvalues +1 and −1. Classical theta-series/dihedral automorphic induction attaches a cuspidal holomorphic weight-one newform to each such irreducible induction, with standard L-function and level as in (2); its nebentypus varies with the character. These are standard class-field, induction, and theta-correspondence inputs, not consequences of the large sieve.

The upper bound (1) follows by applying the cited estimate to labels with conductor at most X/|D_K|, using a slightly smaller admissible height power to absorb the fixed factor |D_K| and then counting each exceptional isomorphism class at most once. If the zero count is first summed over labels, an exceptional class admits at least one exceptional label, so no injectivity is needed for the upper bound.

For the lower bound, use good prime labels g=𝔭 over rational primes p splitting as p=𝔭 𝔭̄ in K and avoiding the finite bad set. At 𝔭 the character χ_𝔭 is tamely ramified, and away from S and 𝔭 it is unramified. Its conjugate χ_𝔭^c is instead ramified at 𝔭̄, so it is not χ_𝔭. Its conductor norm is O_K(p), because the S-supported part is bounded in the fixed family. The splitting prime theorem supplies ≫X/log X such prime labels with conductor O(X); adjusting the fixed cutoff gives the stated lower bound. If two induced representations are isomorphic, restriction to W_K shows that their characters differ by at most conjugation by c. Consequently each isomorphism class occurs for at most two distinct primitive characters. The prime labels have distinct good conductor supports, hence distinct primitive characters, so induction loses at most a factor two. This also shows that the conclusion is about distinct weight-one representations, not merely multiple labels of one form. □

The same conditional argument works over any fixed totally real k and CM quadratic extension K/k with μ_n⊂K, for any fixed n≥3. For finite-order χ_g with `χ_g^c≠χ_g`, quadratic automorphic induction gives a cuspidal dihedral representation of GL₂(A_k), and the conductor ideal satisfies `f(Ind χ_g)=D_(K/k) N_(K/k)(f(χ_g))`. Its standard L-function is L_K(s,χ_g). At each real place the induced representation is odd; the associated theta series gives parallel weight-one Hilbert forms. Order representations by the absolute norm of their conductor. Chebotarev supplies ≫X/log X prime ideals of k splitting in K with norm at most a fixed multiple of X; choosing one prime of K above each gives the same ramification asymmetry and a bounded-to-one representation count. The density exponent and sufficiently small power-height rectangle are unchanged. For example one may take K=Q(ζ_n) and k=Q(ζ_n+ζ_n^−1). This extension also uses the fixed-field n-general sieve and detector assumptions in the cited analytic result, and standard CM theta-series theory.
