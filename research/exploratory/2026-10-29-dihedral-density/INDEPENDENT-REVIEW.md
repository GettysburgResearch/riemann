# Independent audit of the dihedral transfer

The stated transfer is correct under the explicitly assumed restricted-family Hecke zero-density theorem and the standard global class-field, Artin-induction and theta-series facts. It is a conditional density-one assertion for standard L-functions, not an individual zero-free region; no verification of the analytic density input or priority is implied.

A finite-order Hecke character of K gives a finite-image one-dimensional Artin character. Its infinity type is trivial. For a quadratic extension K/k with involution c, the restriction of its induction back to the subgroup is `χ⊕χ^c`; therefore the induction is irreducible precisely when `χ≠χ^c`. Artin formalism gives `L_k(s,Ind χ)=L_K(s,χ)`, and the conductor-discriminant formula for induced representations gives

    f(Ind χ)=D_(K/k) N_(K/k)(f(χ)).

For k=Q this is the integer level `|D_K| N_(K/Q)f(χ)`. For general fixed k, taking absolute norms gives `N_k f(Ind χ)=N_k D_(K/k) · N_K f(χ)`. Thus an induced-conductor cutoff X corresponds to a Hecke-conductor cutoff X divided by a fixed positive constant. The equality concerns the standard finite conductor, with the imprimitive original row masks replaced by the primitive character conductor as in the analytic input.

If K/k is CM and k is totally real, every real complex conjugation acts outside the index-two subgroup. In the induced two-dimensional representation its trace is zero and its square is the identity, so its eigenvalues are +1 and −1 and the representation is totally odd. Standard theta-series/dihedral automorphic induction therefore attaches a cuspidal parallel-weight-one Hilbert representation to an irreducible finite-order induction, with the displayed conductor and standard L-function. For k=Q this gives a cuspidal holomorphic weight-one newform, with varying nebentypus. These standard modularity statements are additional inputs to, not consequences of, the analytic sieve.

For the lower family size, take distinct primes p of k splitting as `p O_K=P P^c`, outside a fixed finite set. The label g=P lies in the restricted family. Its primitive Hecke character is tamely and nontrivially ramified at P and unramified at P^c, whereas χ_P^c is ramified at P^c and unramified at P. Hence χ_P≠χ_P^c. The remaining conductor factors are supported on the fixed bad set with uniformly bounded exponents, so `N_K f(χ_P)≪N_k p`. Chebotarev supplies ≫X/log X such p with `N_k p≤X/C` for a fixed large C. Distinct P give distinct primitive characters by their good ramification supports. If `Ind χ≅Ind χ'`, restriction to W_K yields equality of the unordered pairs `{χ,χ^c}` and `{χ',χ'^c}`, so at most two distinct inducing characters yield the same representation. This proves the stated lower bound for distinct representations. The argument works for the stated cyclotomic examples and any fixed CM K/k satisfying μ_n⊂K and the family hypotheses.

For the upper bound, the L-function identity transfers each exceptional representation to at least one exceptional character label; injectivity is unnecessary. Adjusting the conductor cutoff by `N_k D_(K/k)` affects only a fixed multiplicative constant. If the density input allows height `Y^τ`, choose any smaller fixed τ′>0 and take X large enough that `X^τ′≤(X/N_k D_(K/k))^τ`; this justifies the power-height rectangle for the induced family. The resulting exponent, including the 11/14 example at σ=4/5, is unchanged. Counting representations by conductor norm means the statement is cumulative; it does not assert that every individual representation is zero-free.
