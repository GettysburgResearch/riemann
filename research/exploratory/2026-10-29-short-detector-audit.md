# Adversarial audit of the short saturated-detector replay

**Scope:** A source comparison with OpenAI's [30 September 2026 manuscript](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/The-Quasi-Riemann-Hypothesis-September-30-2026/paper.pdf), especially Lemmas 8.1–8.2 on pp. 56–58 and Proposition 8.3 on pp. 59–60. This does not independently establish the paper's global strip estimates, Hecke analytic continuation, Mellin bounds, or the uniform arithmetic moments used after detection. The proposed lower-floor and fixed-field transfers remain explicit hypotheses.

## A definite scope defect and its correction

The original [fixed-field detector note](2026-10-29-fixed-field-saturated-detector/README.md) assumed that the primitive inducing character was nonprincipal and unitary, then used the assertion that its L-function was entire. This is false for general unitary Hecke characters: a nontrivial pure norm character `psi(a)=(Na)^(it0)` has `L(s,psi)=zeta_K(s−it0)`, with a pole at `1+it0`.

This is a concrete analytic gap in the originally stated broad lemma. The pole can lie in the disk centered at `2+it` used to define a holomorphic logarithm in source Lemma 8.1. In the Gamma-integral contour shift in Proposition 8.3, if it lies between the lines, it gives an additional residue at `z=1+it0−rho`; there is no general reason for the finite truncated inverse to cancel that residue. The source's finite-order nonprincipal assumption does imply entire L-functions and therefore does not have this issue.

The fixed-field note has been corrected to assume that every primitive inducing L-function in the detector is entire. In the actual fixed-background family `nu chi_g`, with `cond(nu)` supported on S and g a good cube-free label, every good prime dividing g is ramified in `chi_g` and cannot be canceled by nu. A pure norm character has no finite ramification, so only the unit-ideal label can induce one. A fixed finite presentation dictionary retains only boundedly many such exceptions. Their fixed norm-shifted Dedekind zeta zero counts are controlled separately by `O((T+1) log(2+T))`. The [consolidated packet](2026-10-29-density-proof-packet/README.md) already expressly separates principal **and** pure norm presentations, so the defect does not by itself invalidate that packet's restricted conditional claim.

## Where the printed proof uses t

The source states `1<=t<=3/2` on p. 59, then defines `D*=U^t` and `Y*=U^20`. Its moved-contour estimate is `U^(−3+t(5/4−beta)+o(1))` after the field-specific strip estimate. For any fixed `0<t<1` and `beta>=sigma0>1/2`, this is smaller than the worst value in the printed range. In a fixed-field replay with `Y*=U^B`, the corresponding input is a uniform polynomial strip bound times `U^(−B/4) D*^(5/4−beta)`, with a fixed polynomial height factor. Choosing B sufficiently large first and the positive height exponent afterward makes the integral `o(1)`. This requires the corrected entire-L assumption and fixed-type uniformity; it does not require `t>=1`.

On the original absolute-convergence line, both ideal variables range over integral ideals; the product norm is multiplicative. Since the inverse cutoff is 1 through `D*`, the coefficient of any nonunit ideal of norm at most `D*` cancels by `sum_(l|n) mu(l)=0`. The unit term survives. Multiplication by `1−V_le(2 Nl Nk/D*)` removes that unit and leaves the canceled terms unchanged. It forces `D N >> D*` on every surviving dyadic block; the inverse cutoff forces `D << D*`. These are the printed source support claims, with no use of `D*>=U`. The terminal cutoff `U^(B+1)` dominates the fixed exponential scale `U^B`, and ideal counting controls the far tail.

The source's product profile is uniformly smooth in logarithmic coordinates on a fixed annulus. Its Fourier transform has uniformly rapidly decaying tails, and separation introduces **one** common frequency into both ideal variables. Source p. 60 uses uniform L1 control of that transform and only logarithmically many block pairs to select one product witness. The zero ordinate and that same frequency appear in both blocks. The real exponent in the profiles varies in a compact interval. Moving `t` below 1 does not alter this support or Fourier mechanism. A rowwise choice of profiles and height still needs the separate Sobolev/common-coefficient step when an arithmetic moment is applied; this is not supplied merely by pointwise bounds.

Source Lemma 8.2, p. 57, explicitly permits bounded nonnegative inverse/plain length ranges. It does not require `t>=1`. Provided its fixed-field analogue holds uniformly for the chosen profiles, the product witness and the pointwise upper bounds force `m<=1/2+O(eta/delta)` and give both individual saturated lower bounds, where `delta=2a−1` has a fixed positive lower bound. Then `r+m>=t−o(1)` gives `r>=t−1/2−O(eta/delta)`. The constants cannot be uniform as the chosen floor approaches 1/2; fixing `sigma0>1/2`, then the floor and losses, suffices for the stated use.

## Remaining assumptions

No further source step depending on `t>=1` was identified in this replay. This finding is not a proof that the printed proposition already covers `t<1`: it does not. The residual work is to verify the fixed-field, fixed-infinity-type strip and reflected bounds, the exact original Euler masks, the one cumulative buffered frequency allowance, and the finite-seminorm/Sobolev reduction for the actual arithmetic moment. In particular, the common physical height is polynomially large and the external Fourier-tail order must be selected after the permitted positive height exponent, without increasing the moment's required internal seminorm order. These are analytic assumptions, not consequences of the scalar exponent calculation.

## An unbalanced-box comparison at sigma=4/5

Consider the standard principal-twist cubic Eisenstein family on coprime squarefree ideal boxes `Na~X^(1/4)`, `Nb~X^(3/4)`. Both parts are nonunit for large X; a squarefree-index theorem cannot simply be applied to these mixed labels. The conductor norm is comparable to X and the smaller conjugate label is `b a²`, of norm comparable to `X^(5/4)`.

[rStation paper 52 v5, Theorem 5.7](https://rstation.ai/problems/riemann_hypothesis/papers/52), claims the cumulative natural-index exponent `D(4/5)=2/3` at a sufficiently small power height for the principal twist. It therefore gives the conductor-box exponent `(5/4)(2/3)=5/6`, by applying its cumulative theorem at `P=C X^(5/4)` and using conjugation symmetry. The cutoff covers small indices directly; choose the conductor height power so `X^tau<=P^h`. This is a conditional comparison with the source theorem, not an independent verification. Reordering [de Faveri Corollary 1.5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04045v1), whose index exponent at 4/5 is `8/11`, gives `10/11`; the broad all-character bound of Khale et al. gives exponent 1 at fixed height. The unbalanced short-detector candidate is `3/4`, contingent on its detector and source moment inputs.

For an arithmetic check of that candidate, set `delta=2sigma−1=3/5` and `t=5/6`. The cubic rectangular inverse moment has exponent `max{1, r+5/12, 2r/3+7/12}`. For `r<=1/2` it is 1; for `r>=1/2` the third term is at most `r+5/12`. With the stated diagonal-size plain moment and `r+m>=t`, the inverse and plain witness counts have exponents bounded by the minimum of `max{1,r+5/12}−delta r` and `1−delta(t−r)`. Their maximum over `0<=r<=t` is `3/4`, reached at `r=t/2` and `r=t`. This is algebra only; the fixed-profile and small-height moment hypotheses remain essential.
