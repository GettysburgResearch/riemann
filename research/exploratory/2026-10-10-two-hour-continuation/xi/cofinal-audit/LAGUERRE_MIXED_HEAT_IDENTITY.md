## LH1. Exact finite Laguerre identity

Represent each +/- pair by its member with positive real part, retain
distinct conjugates separately, and write its integer multiplicity as m.
All sums below are over the complete remaining source. Put

\[
 K(u)=\sum m_\rho e^{-\rho^2u},\qquad
 C_k(t)=\sum m_\rho(\rho^2+t^2)^{-k},\qquad u>0,\ t>A.
\tag{LH1}
\]

Fix t>A, set `c=t^2-A^2>0`, and let

\[
 b_n=c^n C_{n+1}(t),\qquad
 D_{n,j}=\sum_{h=0}^j(-1)^h{j\choose h}b_{n+h}.
\tag{LH2}
\]

These are exactly the normalized Hausdorff moments and mixed differences
in HC3 and HC5. For every finite n,j>=0,

\[
 D_{n,j}=\frac{c^n j!}{(n+j)!}
       \int_0^\infty u^n e^{-t^2u}K(u)L_j^{(n)}(cu)\,du,
\tag{LH3}
\]

where the generalized Laguerre polynomial is defined by the finite sum

\[
 L_j^{(n)}(x)=\sum_{h=0}^j
       \frac{(-1)^h}{h!}{n+j\choose j-h}x^h.
\tag{LH4}
\]

Indeed the complete Gamma/Laplace identity gives
`C_(n+h+1)(t)=integral u^(n+h)e^(-t^2u)K(u)du/(n+h)!`.
The polynomial multiplying the common integrand is

\[
 \sum_{h=0}^j(-1)^h{j\choose h}\frac{n!}{(n+h)!}x^h
                =\frac{L_j^{(n)}(x)}{{n+j\choose j}}.
\tag{LH5}
\]

Thus every constant and normalization in LH3 is exact. The finite sum is
not an infinite asymptotic expansion. For each h, absolute Fubini is paid
by `sum m/(Re rho^2+t^2)^(n+h+1)<infinity`: large denominators are comparable
to |rho|^2, and inverse-square summability suffices. In particular LH3
preserves every complex occurrence and the complete heat tail.

## LH2. Exact derivative form, including both endpoints

Define the shifted trace

\[
                  H_A(u)=e^{-A^2u}K(u)
                    =\sum m_\rho e^{-(\rho^2+A^2)u}.
\tag{LH6}
\]

Since `Re(rho^2+A^2)>= (Re rho)^2>0`, its derivative series converges
normally on every compact positive-u interval. Rodrigues' finite polynomial
identity, after substituting x=cu, is

\[
 L_j^{(n)}(cu)=\frac{e^{cu}u^{-n}}{j!}
                 \frac{d^j}{du^j}\left(e^{-cu}u^{n+j}\right).
\tag{LH7}
\]

Repeated integration by parts therefore gives the COMPLETE identity

\[
 D_{n,j}=\frac{c^n}{(n+j)!}
       \int_0^\infty e^{-cu}u^{n+j}(-1)^j H_A^{(j)}(u)\,du.
\tag{LH8}
\]

Here is an explicit endpoint justification using only the complete
inverse-square sum. Write `E_rho=rho^2+A^2`. Outside a finite set,
`|E_rho|` is comparable to |rho|^2, and
`Re E_rho >= |rho|^2/2`. For each fixed integer a>=0, dominated convergence
applied with weights `m/|rho|^2` gives

\[
                   u^{a+1}H_A^{(a)}(u)\longrightarrow0
                         \quad(u\downarrow0).
\tag{LH9}
\]

To see the domination, the absolute contribution after division by its
summable weight is at most a constant times
`(u|rho|^2)^(a+1) exp(-u|rho|^2/2)`, uniformly bounded on [0,infinity).
For each fixed occurrence it tends to zero. The finitely many exceptional
occurrences cause no problem. For 0<=a<=j-1,

`d^(j-1-a)[e^(-cu)u^(n+j)]/du^(j-1-a) = O(u^(n+1+a))` at zero.

Its product with LH9 is `o(u^n)`, hence vanishes also when n=0. These are
all the boundary terms generated by integration by parts. At infinity,
every fixed derivative of H_A is bounded on [1,infinity), by the same
summability and `Re E_rho>0`; the remaining exponential e^(-cu) times a
fixed polynomial tends to zero.

The final integral in LH8 is absolutely convergent, rather than merely a
formal boundary limit. Termwise absolute integration bounds it by

\[
 (n+j)!\sum m_\rho
       \frac{|E_\rho|^j}{(c+\Re E_\rho)^{n+j+1}}<\infty.
\tag{LH10}
\]

The summands outside a finite set are bounded by a constant times
`m/|rho|^(2n+2)`. The intermediate integrations have the same bound with
lower fixed derivative orders. Hence all exchanges, both endpoints and
every repeated integration are accounted for.
