# Finite twists of the 125/144 inverse-moment target

**Status.** A conditional zero-boundary implication, not a proof of the needed moment estimates or a new zero-free theorem. The fixed-row Mellin argument uses standard Hecke functional equations and fixed-strip reciprocal-L bounds, and the final classification uses Kummer theory and the cited sextic-symbol identification. The source's physical masks and the family of required fixed twists must be retained.

Let `E=ℚ(ω)`, and choose a fixed finite set S of prime ideals including the primes above 2 and 3. Fix a smooth nonnegative annular W₀ with zero-free Mellin transform on `Re s>0` as in the [sector note](../2026-10-29-mobius-marked-boundary/README.md), and set `W(x)=sqrt(x)W₀(x)`. Fix a nonnegative `V∈C_c^∞((0,∞))` positive on a nonempty open interval. For a fixed finite-order Hecke character ν whose conductor is supported on S, define the all-row smooth-shell moment with `P=Y^(6/5)` by

    M_ν(Y)= Σ_(u good primary) V(Nu/Y)
       | Σ_(a good) μ_E(a) ν(a) χ_a(u) W(Na/P)/sqrt(Na) |².    (1)

Here good means coprime to S, rows contain one primary representative per ideal, and all sextic residue symbols retain their zero extensions. The source normalization is exactly `P^(−1/2)W₀(Na/P)`. A bound below always means that for **every** ε>0 the implicit constant may depend on ε and the fixed ν, W₀ and S, but not Y.

Let w be any fixed good sixth-free primary element, and let ψ=ν·ψ_w, where `ψ_w(a)=χ_a(w)` on good ideals a. Write Θ_ψ for the supremum of the real parts of nontrivial zeros of its primitive Hecke L-function. The row subfamily `u=w v⁶` occurs in (1), for all good prime ideals `(v)` outside Sw. It satisfies

    χ_a(wv⁶)=χ_a(w)·1_((a,v)=1).                            (2)

Choose a fixed prime annulus `Nv≈(Y/Nw)^(1/6)` so that `V(N(wv⁶)/Y)` is bounded below by a positive constant. There are `≫_w Y^(1/6)/log Y` such distinct rows; the primary element v⁶ is independent of the chosen generator. Let

    S_ψ(X)=Σ_(a good) μ_E(a)ψ(a)W₀(Na/X).

Its Mellin transform is `Ŵ₀(s)/L_S(s,ψ)`, with the original good-prime and w-zero masks. Any additional fixed Euler deletions are nonzero on `Re s>0`. Every nontrivial zero ρ=β+iγ of L(s,ψ) therefore yields, for any η>0, an unbounded sequence with `|S_ψ(X)|≥X^(β−η)`. For a good prime v with ψ(v)≠0 the exact deletion identity is

    S_(ψ,v)(X)=Σ_(j≥0) ψ(v)^j S_ψ(X/(Nv)^j).               (3)

On any fixed line right of Θ_ψ, standard reciprocal-Hecke bounds give `S_ψ(T)≪T^(Θ_ψ+η)`, and the j≥1 terms in (3) are uniformly of lower order on a near-Θ_ψ Ω sequence. Consequently the w-fixed subfamily has lower growth exponent

    1/6+(12/5)(Θ_ψ−1/2).                                    (4)

Thus `M_ν(Y)≪Y^(21/20+ε)` forces `Θ_ψ≤125/144`. The same conclusion holds for every fixed good sixth-free primary w, so even the original ν=1 target covers the represented Kummer characters ψ_w, not just the trivial character. The constants in (4) may depend on w; no conductor-uniform estimate follows from this argument.

## All sextic-torsion characters from finitely many fixed twists

Let Ξ_S be the finite set of Kummer characters `ν_{α₀}(a)=χ_a(α₀)` for sixth-power classes α₀ generated by the six units and by fixed generators of the prime ideals in S. The symbol is evaluated on good a, and the primitive Hecke character is the corresponding Kummer character. Its conductor is supported on S. Finite multiplicity or coincident characters in Ξ_S is harmless.

Assume (1) satisfies

    M_ν(Y) ≪_ε Y^(21/20+ε) for every fixed ν∈Ξ_S.            (5)

Then every finite-order Hecke character of E whose order divides 6 has no nontrivial zero to the right of `125/144`. Indeed `E` contains μ₆, so Kummer theory identifies such characters with classes `α∈E×/E×⁶`. Since E has class number one, choose b∈E× to reduce all good-prime valuations of α modulo 6 and select a primary generator w of the resulting good integral sixth-free ideal. Then

    α=α₀ w b⁶

for some S-supported unit/prime class α₀; its unit correction absorbs the choice of primary generator. For every good prime away from w, the standard sixth-power residue symbol identifies the Kummer Frobenius character as

    ψ_α(a)=ν_{α₀}(a)χ_a(w),                                (6)

up to a single convention-wide inversion. The latter only replaces α by α⁵ and is already covered by the same finite family. The good-prime symbol orientation is source-checked in [the independent arithmetic review](../2026-10-29-complete-source-four-cycle/INDEPENDENT-ARITHMETIC-REVIEW.md); the usual global Kummer/Hecke and class-field identification is an external classical input. Finitely many deleted Euler factors do not vanish for `Re s>0`, so the zero-boundary inference transfers to the primitive L-function. Apply (4) for ν=ν_{α₀} and this fixed w.

In particular, conditional on (5), if L/E is any finite abelian extension whose Galois group has exponent dividing 6, the Artin factorization of ζ_L into these degree-one Hecke L-functions gives the same `125/144` boundary for ζ_L and each one-dimensional Artin factor. The extension L/ℚ need not be abelian. This finite-twist hypothesis is stronger than the single untwisted target in the [125/144 note](../2026-10-29-target-125-144/README.md); one cannot silently infer (5) from that single moment.

**Independent review.** Other_families and Families checked the fixed-w deletion and Ω argument, primary sixth-power rows, PID decomposition of Kummer classes, finite S-supported twists, good-prime symbol zeros and Artin factorization. This review retains the finite set of moment hypotheses and the cited classical/source inputs; it does not prove the moment or a new region.

## One finite family of signed core estimates

The extra fixed twists in (5) are compatible with the existing source-qualified dyadic reduction. For a fixed ν∈Ξ_S, insert ν(a) in the original column coefficient. On good ideals its values have modulus one and are multiplicative. Thus, in the first common-gcd split, `ν(dr₁) overline(ν(dr₂))=ν(r₁)overline(ν(r₂))`, and in the auxiliary projector `r_i=g n_i` the common `ν(g)` cancels. The residual columns have the same one fixed twist ν(n). The source's inverse input (10.2) and theta means explicitly allow a fixed finite-ray character; multiplying it by the finitely many fixed sector characters remains within the same input class. The original ν cancels on the theta diagonal, so the exact signed `Σ_(g|r)μ(g)=0` cancellation, the row zero masks, and the later ℓ/k divisor algebra are unchanged. Constants may depend on ν; Ξ_S is finite, so their maximum is finite.

A concrete sufficient new estimate is therefore: **for each** fixed original column ray sector C and each `ν∈Ξ_S`, bound by `O_ε(Y^(21/20+ε))` the signed sum of all nonprincipal physical dyadic contributions satisfying

    d+g+(6/5)κ < 7/50,

in the notation of the [125/144 note](../2026-10-29-target-125-144/README.md). The physical common kernel, all original masks and the fixed profiles V,W are retained. First recombine and remove the exact theta diagonal across **all** auxiliary g-ray classes and norm dyads; the above core is selected only from the remaining nonprincipal expression. The hypothesis can bound the signed sum over the whole core in each original sector, or the sum of absolute dyadic contributions; it need not assert cancellation between distinct original sectors. The reviewed source estimates control the complementary dyads and separately bound the principal restoration. The ε-losses absorb the logarithmic number of dyads. Under the stated source/profile matching, this finite family of core estimates yields (5), and hence the sextic-torsion boundary. These core estimates are not proved here.

## Why fixed sixth-power row families do not automatically amplify

For one fixed w, the rows `u=w v⁶` already give `Y^(1/6−o(1))` copies of the same Hecke character ψ=νψ_w. Taking finitely many other fixed w changes constants but supplies no new power of Y. For the trivial-character ζ_E obstruction in the ν=1 moment, a good sixth-free primary w can have trivial ψ_w only when w=1: any good prime occurring in a nonunit w has valuation 1–5 and causes tame ramification in its Kummer character. Equivalently, in the unique sixth-free-part decomposition of any good primary row u, the trivial good character forces `u=v⁶`, giving only `O(Y^(1/6))` such ideals in a norm-Y shell. Thus there is no polynomial family of distinct sixth-free good w representing that same trivial character. A better exponent from jointly varying w would require an additional result, for example sufficiently many distinct ψ_w sharing an appropriate zero with compatible Ω sequences and uniformly controlled constants. No such common-zero or multiplicity result follows from (2)–(4).

Families independently checked the smooth-shell prime-v selection, fixed-ν propagation through both common-divisor splits, the core/diagonal ordering, and the tame-ramification obstruction to additional trivial-character row families.
