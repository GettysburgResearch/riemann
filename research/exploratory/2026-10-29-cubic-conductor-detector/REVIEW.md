# Independent review of the conductor-ordered cubic detector

**Verdict:** The rectangular-sieve exponent calculation and conductor ordering are consistent under the stated fixed-field saturated-detector and profile hypotheses. This review does not independently prove that detector for arbitrary fixed unitary infinity type, replay De Faveri Proposition 8.1, establish novelty, or prove an individual zero-free region.

## Coefficients, classes and conductors

De Faveri §2.5.1 gives `cond(chi_g)=c_g rad(g)` with `c_g` dividing a fixed S-supported modulus. For `g=a b²` with coprime squarefree `a,b`, the good part of the conductor is exactly `ab`: at each good prime its cubic local character is nontrivial even when its exponent in g is 2. A fixed unitary `nu` with finite conductor supported on S is unramified at these primes, so twisting cannot cancel that good ramification. Its primitive conductor is therefore between `N(ab)` and a constant multiple of `N(ab)`, the constant depending on the fixed S-local data of `nu`. Infinity type affects the fixed analytic conductor constants, not this finite-norm comparison.

De Faveri (2.5) gives a finite reciprocity phase depending on the ray classes of g and q. Fix the class of `g=a b²` before applying Proposition 8.1; the resulting phase is a function of q alone and can be absorbed into the column coefficient together with the fixed `nu(q)`, Möbius factor and profile. `mu(q)` supplies the squarefree q-support required by the proposition. For q meeting g both source and target characters retain the zero value. A conjugate witness is handled by conjugating the whole polynomial and its coefficients, leaving its squared norm unchanged. Removing the coprimality condition from the nonnegative outer sum only enlarges it. These checks use the source's reciprocity and conductor statements; they are not a new proof of those statements.

For a fixed annular normalized inverse block the column squared norm is `O(1)`: the coefficient size is `D^(-1/2)` times a bounded smooth weight and the number of ideals of norm `O(D)` is `O_K(D)`. With `U=AB`, `gamma=log(min(A,B))/log U in [0,1/2]`, De Faveri (8.1) gives the displayed exponents `1`, `r+(1+gamma)/3`, and `2r/3+(2-gamma)/3`. Swapping A and B is only in this symmetric numerical majorant; it does not change the actual character orientation.

## Detector, profile and endpoints

The full detector transfer is an explicit hypothesis of the proposed theorem. Subject to it, taking its total-length parameter `t=1` gives `r<=1+O(1/log U)` and, from the plain witness `m<=1/2+O(epsilon_det)` and `r+m>=1-O(1/log U)`, also `r>=1/2-O(epsilon_det)`. The inverse spike is `|M|²>=U^(delta r-epsilon_det)`. The bin rounding yields `delta>=2sigma-1-O(e)`, not a literal `o(1)` unless the loss parameter is actually varied; e and the preliminary loss may instead be fixed arbitrarily small relative to the requested final epsilon.

For fixed `delta in (0,1)`, the count exponent is the maximum of three affine functions of r. A convex function on `[1/2,1]` is bounded by its endpoint maximum. At `r=1/2` the maximum is `1-delta/2`; at `r=1` it is `(4+gamma)/3-delta<=3/2-delta`. Since `1-delta/2<=3/2-delta` for `delta<=1`, the uniform exponent is `3/2-delta=5/2-2sigma`, up to the fixed detector losses. In particular this endpoint argument does not require `delta>=2/3`; the positive saving compared with X begins at `sigma>3/4`.

The large sieve requires coefficients fixed across rows. To make a row-dependent witness height/profile compatible, one needs the source's finite-dimensional Sobolev reduction, not pointwise substitution of rowwise coefficients. A standard sufficient setup is a bounded compact parameter family of smooth annular profiles and a height interval of length O(T): derivatives in the profile parameters and height insert at most fixed profile seminorms and powers of `log D`; integrate the resulting common-coefficient large-sieve bounds over that parameter box, and use Sobolev to take the rowwise supremum. The cost is a fixed polynomial in `T` and logarithms. The detector's actual finite parameter list, conjugations and deleted masks must be shown to fit this setup; this is not contained in Proposition 8.1 itself.

## Summation and interpretation

The conductor restriction and fixed S give `AB <<_nu X` in every dyadic box. For `U>=X^lambda`, choose the global height `X^tau` below the local detector allowance `U^eta` by fixing `tau<=lambda eta` after eta and the finite height orders. Count every bad row, then use the standard individual Hecke zero-count bound to account for multiplicity and the `X^tau` height factor; choose tau small enough to absorb it into the desired epsilon. For `U<X^lambda`, the `O(X^(lambda+epsilon))` label count and the same individual zero-count bound cost `X^(lambda+tau+epsilon)`; choose these exponents below `5/2-2sigma`. Finitely many principal presentations are isolated and their ordinary zero count is harmless at sufficiently small height. Dyadic losses are logarithmic.

Distinct prime-ideal labels give at least `X/log X` distinct primitive characters: the good conductor supports contain different primes, even after fixed S-supported twisting. Thus, if all imported detector and moment hypotheses are established, the bound gives density-one zero-free *rectangles* at a sufficiently small power height for fixed `sigma>3/4`; it gives no uniform individual half-plane.

An independent targeted source search found earlier cubic Hecke zero-density work, including [Xia (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jnt.2006.10.006); no broad priority or novelty assessment was completed. The rStation comparison in the companion note is restricted to a specified conjugate-reordering use of its norm-ordered estimate, and does not rule out a better direct conductor argument.
