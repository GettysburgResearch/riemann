# Artin zero-free transfer: targeted prior-art scan

Status: exploratory literature comparison; not an independent proof or comprehensive priority search.

Scope: the cyclic transfer in `research/exploratory/2026-10-29-cyclic-transfer/README.md`, its Dedekind-zeta corollary, and its PSL(2,7) application. Sources consulted below are primary papers or their published abstracts. Search performed 29 October 2026; source text and endpoint conventions should be rechecked before relying on any comparison.

What was run: web searches and reading of the quoted statements, followed by algebraic comparison of the stated denominators. No original paper proof, Lean proof, or complete bibliography was independently checked.

Smallest remaining gap: obtain and audit the original Ahn–Kwon theorem with its improved order-at-least-five constant, and check whether its quantitative transfer already occurs in the literature.

## Established transfer precedent

The cyclic reduction and its discriminant accounting are classical. [Zaman, *Bounding the least prime ideal in the Chebotarev density theorem*, Section 4.2, equations (4.9)–(4.10)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1508.00287), explicitly attributes this reduction to Deuring (1935), citing Lagarias–Montgomery–Odlyzko (1979, Section 3). Given `g` in a Galois group, it takes `H=<g>` and `E=L^H`, writes `zeta_L=product_chi L(s,chi,L/E)` with primitive Hecke characters, and uses `log d_L=sum_chi log(d_E N f_chi)`. This is the same subgroup factorization and conductor-discriminant formula used in the present argument. No claim of novelty for this mechanism is justified; this search did not locate the exact `phi(m)/15.10` numerical corollary, but cannot establish priority for it.

The [Lagarias–Montgomery–Odlyzko 1979 introduction](https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/bitstream/handle/2027.42/46605/222_2005_Article_BF01390234.pdf?sequence=1) goes further: it explicitly observes that a large abelian subgroup permits a Hecke factorization over its fixed field that gives a better Dedekind-zeta zero-free region. This is direct historical precedent for the broad large-subgroup improvement idea as well as the factorization. The exact elementary-kernel projective-orbit coefficient in the present notes was not located in this targeted search; its priority remains unresolved.

[Lemke Oliver, Thorner and Zaman, *An approximate form of Artin's holomorphy conjecture and non-vanishing of Artin L-functions*](https://lemkeoliver.github.io/papers/27-ApproximateArtin.pdf), Theorems 3.3–3.4 and Lemma 5.2, explicitly develop zero-free transfer to Artin factors via induction and positivity of cyclic factors, including specified normal-subgroup quotients. They prove large regions for almost all fields in suitable families; that quantified statement should not be conflated with an explicit region holding for every individual field. Their general transfer concerns a normal subgroup and a quotient to its fixed field; the proposed odd-prime subgroup need not itself be normal. The basic transfer mechanism is therefore well precedented even if an exact numerical corollary is not located.

More precisely, their Theorem 3.3 says that if a normal subgroup `N` satisfies their hypothesis `T(G,N)`, then nonvanishing of `zeta_K/zeta_(K^N)` on a region implies holomorphy and nonvanishing of the Artin factors with `ker(rho)` not containing `N`. Theorem 3.4 proves `T(G,N)` when `[G:N]` is a prime power, when `N` is solvable, for `|G|<=2000`, or for a transitive permutation group of degree at most 31. Thus all of `A5`, `A6`, `PSL(2,7)`, `PSL(2,11)`, and `S5` from the proposed examples are covered by their group criterion. Their Lemma 5.2 is a positive cyclic-induction transfer in the reverse direction, from the normal-subgroup quotient to cyclic Hecke factors. The proposed argument instead starts with the nonnormal subgroup `H` and controls `zeta_K/zeta_(K^H)`; this alone does **not** imply the normal-subgroup quotient is zero-free, since `zeta_(K^H)/zeta_(K^N)` can vanish. An additional low-order argument is required. The proposed positive coverage plus Stark supplies such an argument directly, without assuming `T(G,N)`. This distinguishes hypotheses and proof route; it does not establish publication priority.

Their Theorem 3.1 proves a much wider region for all but a quantified exceptional family of fields, with constants depending on group and base field. That almost-all statement and the current all-fields, explicit, restricted-height statement have different quantifiers. Their introduction attributes the `N=G` zero-free transfer to classical Aramata–Artin–Brauer arguments; it would be incorrect to credit this project with originating that principle.

## Dedekind zeta: classical nonexceptional benchmarks

The absence of an exceptional zero in normal extensions of `Q` with no quadratic intermediate field is also classical. [Pierce–Turnage-Butterbaugh–Wood, Theorem 5.9 and Theorem N](https://dukespace.lib.duke.edu/bitstreams/6ca77058-c44c-4932-8fa5-cb7c8c9570ec/download), explicitly discuss this consequence of Stark's theorem: an appropriate simple exceptional zero must descend to the base or a quadratic intermediate field. The base zeta function has no real zeros in `(0,1)`, so a Galois extension of `Q` with no quotient of order two has no such exceptional zero.

Combining this fact with [Das–Gaba–Lee–Savalia–Wong, Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.19319), for degree at least seven one obtains the all-fields zero-free rectangle of width `1/(11.51910 log D)` and height `|t|<=1`. When `[M0:Q]>=7`, the proposed cyclic corollary instead has width `phi(m)/(15.10 log D)` for `|t|<(1/3)tan(pi/8)`; the ratio of widths is `11.51910 phi(m)/15.10`. Thus for `phi(m)=2` it improves that coefficient by about 52.6%, while covering a smaller height range. Field-specific widths can be larger, but require the intermediate discriminants.

The separate Ahn–Kwon Dedekind theorem already yields width `1/(2 log D)` for `|t|<1/(2 log D)`, with at most one simple real exceptional zero; the same Stark argument removes the latter under the no-quadratic assumption. This is stronger in its thinner height range when `phi(m)<=6`, and the cyclic coefficient is larger when `phi(m)>=8`. The two results have different height domains.

Das et al. Theorem 1.4 gives the stronger real-axis constant `1/1.61094` in degree at least seven, but its stated conclusion is merely “at most one real zero.” In Section 5.1 the proof supposes `beta0<=beta1` and uses two zero contributions to the logarithmic derivative, suggesting that multiplicity is included; the statement does not explicitly say the remaining zero is simple. Until that interpretation and the required hypotheses are independently checked, one should not use Theorem 1.4 alone with Stark to claim a zero-free real interval of that width.

An independent classical source explicitly gives the simplicity needed for a sharper real-axis comparison. [Louboutin, *Simple zeros of Dedekind zeta functions*, Theorem 3](https://doi.org/10.7169/facm/1598), defines `S(c,d)` by `Re(s)>=1-c/log D`, `|Im(s)|<=d/log D`, and states that there is at most one zero in `S(c,d)`, necessarily real and simple, for `S(c2,0)`, where `c2=2(sqrt(2)-1)^2/(1-1/sqrt(5))=0.62075649...`, and also for `S(.59110,1/4)`. Thus Stark's no-quadratic-subfield argument makes both regions zero-free for a Galois extension of `Q` with no quotient of order two. The real-axis coefficient beats `phi(m)/15.10` for `phi(m)<=8`; the cyclic result covers much larger fixed heights. This is a reported theorem of the cited paper, not an independent verification of its proof. A secondary online table reverses the height inequalities; the original theorem has `<=`.

[Louboutin, *Real zeros of Dedekind zeta functions*, Corollary 7](https://doi.org/10.1142/S1793042115500463), explicitly states for odd-degree Galois extensions of `Q` an even wider real-axis interval with coefficient `(2-sqrt(3))*(5+sqrt(5)) = 1.938898...`. For that family, this stated width exceeds `phi(m)/15.10` when `phi(m)<=28`; the cyclic theorem additionally covers nonreal heights and groups of even order. However, the brief proof accompanying the related Corollary 4 shows nonpositivity by excluding odd-order zeros; that alone does not exclude even-order zeros. The exact support for Corollary 7's stronger nonvanishing wording merits independent scrutiny. Louboutin's Theorem 6 provides an at-most-`m` real-zero bound counting multiplicities, under `log D>2 c_m`, with `c_m=(5+sqrt(5))*(sqrt(m+1)-1)^2/2`. Its `m=2` case and the absence of simple real zeros do not by themselves rule out double real zeros, so the odd-degree corollary should not be extended to arbitrary groups without quadratic quotients on that basis.

## Sharper Hecke inputs and an essential intersection

[Ahn and Kwon](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jnt.2014.06.008) give the all-field `15.10` constant and the fixed height `(1/3) tan(pi/8)` in their abstract. [Zaman, equation (1.1)](https://arxiv.org/html/1502.05679), reports their sharper constant `c=0.1149` for Hecke characters of order at least five, for `|t| <= 0.13`. This is a quotation of the original theorem; its original hypotheses have not been independently checked here.

For the notation in the prime-cyclic note, write `L=log(D/d)` and assume the quoted result applies. For `p>=5`, the narrower-height transfer region must intersect the Hecke region with the Dedekind low-order region. A sufficient width is

    min(0.1149*(p-1)/L, 1/(11.51910*log d)).

It is incorrect to use only the first term in the general low-order proof: `0.1149 > 1/11.51910`. Since `D>=d^p`, a simpler sufficient width is

    min(0.1149*(p-1), p/11.51910) / log D.

For `p=5` this gives coefficient `5/11.51910`, hence denominator `2.30382 log D` (not the unsupported `1/(4*0.1149) log D`); for `p=7` the coefficient is `7/11.51910`. The `15.10` estimate remains valid in its larger stated height interval.

Zaman's Theorem 1.1 also gives order-dependent bounds for `|t|<=1`, with `c=0.1764` for characters of order at least six, `c=0.1489` for order five, and `c=0.1227` for orders at least three. Its denominator includes `log d_M + (3/4)log Nq + n_M nu(n_M)` and its hypotheses require a sufficiently large quantity. These terms and the non-explicit onset prevent replacing the all-field bound by a single explicit constant without further work.

The theorem bounds the product over all ray class characters of order at least `r` by at most one zero, counting multiplicity, and says that any such exceptional character and zero are real. An odd-prime-order character is nonreal, so the statement excludes its zeros in that rectangle. To use this input for the cyclic transfer, choose the common ray modulus and intersect with the low-order region for `zeta_M`; the height and onset must remain attached. This is a potentially useful large-conductor result, not an explicit all-field replacement for the fixed `0.13` comparison.

## Recent competing claims

Boyang Hu, [*A seven-eighths zero-free half-plane for unitary Weil L-functions*](https://hexagonmath.org/2610.00216v1), claims the strict half-plane `Re(s)>7/8` for all finite-dimensional unitary Weil representations, explicitly including Artin L-functions, with stated pole exceptions. This directly covers the PSL(2,7) family, if its proof holds. For the uniform `(15.10/6) log D` bound, `D > exp(8/(15.10/6))` suffices for Hu's claimed half-plane to be wider; the degree of a PSL(2,7) Galois extension over Q is 168, so Minkowski's discriminant bound is far stronger than that threshold. The newer claim is not used in the classical-input argument and has not been audited here. The independently derived classical corollary may still be of interest as a transparent deduction from older inputs, but should not be described as an unqualified frontier result.

Cureton and Thorner, [*A quasi-Riemann hypothesis for Dedekind zeta functions*, v2](https://hexagonmath.org/2610.00200v2), claim the half-plane `Re(s)>31/32` for all finite-order Hecke characters over arbitrary number fields and report a Lean formalization. Applying ordinary Brauer induction transfers this claimed nonvanishing to Artin L-functions away from principal poles; the value at `s=1` for nontrivial irreducibles is treated separately by the classical theorem. A comparison with a region of width `1/(R log D)` requires `R log D>32`, which holds comfortably for the degree-168 PSL(2,7) case by Minkowski. This deduction is conditional on the cited claim and its applicable definitions; neither its proof nor the statement-to-formalization correspondence was independently audited here.
