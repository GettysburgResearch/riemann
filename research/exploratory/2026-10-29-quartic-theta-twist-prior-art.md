# Quartic θ and moving cubic twists: targeted prior comparison

**Scope.** This is a nonexhaustive comparison of printed source statements, not a novelty claim. The proposed estimate is conditional on the moving-twist detector and moment inputs recorded in [bounded-order twist ball](2026-10-29-bounded-order-twist-ball/README.md). Write `θ=4369347/5000000=0.8738694`, and order good **squarefree** quartic FHL labels over `K=Q(i)` by their natural ideal norm `Nu≈X`. The claimed per-common-twist exponent is `R=842755/1674204≈0.5033765`; a union over finite-order cubic twists of conductor norm at most `X^(1/10)` gives `R+1/10≈0.6033765` at a sufficiently small common power-height. All statements remain source-conditional and do not prove an individual zero-free region.

* [de Faveri, *Optimal large sieve for fixed order characters*, arXiv:2610.04045v1, Corollary 1.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.04045v1): untwisted natural n-free FHL labels over a fixed field containing μ_n, including the squarefree subfamily, satisfy a zero-density bound `X^(D_n(σ)+ε) T^(1+d(1−σ)/(3−2σ)+ε)`. For `n=4`, `d=2` and `σ=θ`, the **middle** branch applies because `θ<7/8`; its exponent is `D_4=2361255842955/4687372174364≈0.50374831678`. It is slightly larger than R. The T exponent is approximately `1.20144455`; choose the polynomial height sufficiently small before comparing. The corollary itself is for its unshifted source family, so it does not supply the displayed moving cubic-twist uniformity.
* [Blomer–Goldmakher–Louvel, *L-functions with n-th order twists*, Corollary 1.6](https://arxiv.org/html/1112.1650): its squarefree natural-label source family has fixed-height exponent `g(σ)=2(10σ−7)(1−σ)/(24σ−12σ²−11)` for `5/6<σ≤1`. At θ, `g≈0.54209480278`; the printed T exponent is the same as above. Its finite-ray conventions must be matched to the chosen FHL family, and it does not state the claimed uniform moving-common-twist bound. Gao–Zhao's quartic value-distribution work cites this density theorem for its own congruence-restricted Gaussian family; it does not turn the fixed-family statement into the growing-twist estimate.
* [Khale–O’Kuhn–Panidapu–Sun–Zhang, Lemma 3.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.09677v3), applies over the fixed Galois base to the much larger family of primitive finite-order Hecke characters ordered by conductor. At θ the fixed-height exponent is `B=4(1−θ)/θ≈0.57734302`. Every product νχ_u has conductor norm `≪X^(11/10)`. Since the twist order divides 3 and the quartic order divides 4, taking fourth powers recovers ν, then χ_u; good inertia recovers the squarefree label. Thus the all-character theorem yields a simultaneous comparison exponent `(11/10)B≈0.63507732`, plus the source's explicit height factor and arbitrarily small losses. This recovery is an elementary corollary, not the printed statement; it uses finite-order twists, coprime orders and the fixed-good-sector conductor identification. A possible principal product and the source's zero conventions need their usual separate treatment. For sufficiently small height power this remains above the conditional `0.6033765` exponent.
* [Castillo–de Faveri–Dunn, arXiv:2604.01316v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01316v2), prove central nonvanishing for a squarefree congruence-restricted quartic Gaussian family, with mollified central moments. [Gao–Zhao](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.01701) study one-level density and central nonvanishing. Neither cited statement is a zero count in `Re s≥θ`, uniformly in cubic twists of growing conductor.
* [Liu, arXiv:2610.26835v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26835v1), claims individual zero-freeness for finite-order Hecke L-functions over arbitrary number fields in `Re s>0.874886647…`; [Hu, hexagon:2610.00216v1](https://hexagonmath.org/2610.00216v1), claims `Re s>7/8` in unitary Weil L-function scope. These claims have not been independently verified here and both boundaries lie **to the right** of θ. They would qualitatively supersede corresponding density bounds strictly to the right of their stated boundaries, but do not themselves cover θ. OpenAI's 7/8 finite-order theorem is stated for Eisenstein-field Hecke L-functions and Dirichlet L-functions; that field scope alone does not cover the Gaussian quartic family.

The comparisons above do not rule out a stronger specialized quartic off-critical density result or a moving-twist theorem elsewhere.
