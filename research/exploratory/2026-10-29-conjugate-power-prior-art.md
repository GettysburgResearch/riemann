# Conjugate-power sieve and conductor-density: source comparison

**Status:** Targeted literature search and partial source audit, not an exhaustive novelty search or proof of the proposed inequality. See [the proposed sieve](2026-10-29-general-order-conductor-sieve/README.md) for its hypotheses and all remaining steps.

The proposed rectangular matrix has rows `a,b` and squarefree columns `q`, with entries `chi_q(a b^(n−1))`, and uses the identity `chi_q(a) conjugate(chi_q(b))`, including zero extensions. The claimed family is restricted to indices `a b^(n−1)`; it is the entire cube-free family only at `n=3`.

* [de Faveri, arXiv:2610.04045v1, Proposition 8.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04045v1) states the conjugate-power rectangular conductor sieve **only for n=3**. Theorem 1.1 is general n but orders both sides by index norms. Corollary 1.5 gives general-order density for n-th-power-free **index ordering**. Theorem 1.2 is conductor-ordered but is a cubic *central second moment*, not a general-order zero-density theorem. Remark 8.2 discusses relaxing the **column** squarefreeness in the cubic result; it does not state the proposed n>3 rectangular estimate.
* De Faveri Lemmas 3.1 and 3.3 are formulated for general n, and Lemma 6.1 handles unrestricted row n-th powers. In the source's Prop. 8.1 proof, after the gcd split, the residual columns are squarefree; in Lemma 3.3 their extra-power parts are units, making the cross root-number factors trivial, while the remaining `eta` depends only on the finitely many fixed ray classes. Because the b-character enters conjugated, the transformed character has the orientation needed to recombine the remaining variable as `a e` for every n. I found no failure in this root-number step. This does **not** independently verify the entire proof: the general-order replay must still keep the source's coprimality masks, exceptional diagonal, fixed-prime and ray partitions, separated coefficient vectors, Poisson tails and divisor summation through to its displayed dual norm.
* The unrestricted-row bound obtained from Theorem 1.1 and Lemma 6.1 contains four terms for general n, including `X^(2/n)Y^(1−1/n)`, which merges with an existing term at n=3. The proposed proof's case split for this additional term is algebraically valid. Calling the n>3 result a verbatim application of Proposition 8.1 would nevertheless be incorrect.
* [Gao and Zhao, *Large sieve inequalities for quartic characters*](https://webdoc.sub.gwdg.de/ebook/serien/e/mpi_mathematik/2011/2011_016.pdf) treats quartic characters and squarefree-index character matrices, as well as rational quartic Dirichlet characters ordered by rational conductor. That is an important quartic precedent but does not state the two-variable unrestricted `a,b` rectangular estimate for general fixed n. [Blomer–Goldmakher–Louvel](https://arxiv.org/abs/1112.1650) establish a general fixed-order squarefree family sieve and norm-ordered zero-density estimate, with conductor comparable to the index on that squarefree subfamily; it does not supply the entire conjugate-power family for n>3.
* [Corrigan, *A note on the zeros of L-functions associated to fixed-order Dirichlet characters*](https://web.maths.unsw.edu.au/~ccorrigan/nn002.pdf), gives conductor-ordered density results for fixed-order **Dirichlet characters over Q**. Their rational family is not the entire family of Hecke characters over K containing μ_n. [Khale–O'Kuhn–Panidapu–Sun–Zhang, Lemma 3.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.09677v3), supplies a broader conductor-ordered Hecke density bound in its fixed-Galois-field framework, covering our distinct labels with a fixed finite-order twist. The application to arbitrary fixed angular type requires a further check of the source's modulus-dependent angular basis. At fixed or polylogarithmic height, its conductor exponent `4(1−σ)/σ` for `σ≥4/5` is stronger than the proposed `5/2−2σ` when `σ>(13−sqrt(41))/8`. The source also provides explicit height dependence, unlike the candidate's unspecified sufficiently small power height.

Targeted searches located no exact published theorem with the proposed two-factor general-n kernel and all displayed A/B/Q exponents, and no exact matching conductor-ordered density theorem restricted to this particular family. This is not evidence of priority. The general-field implication, the fixed-type detector, and the complete general-n rectangular replay remain under review; no individual zero-free region follows from the density statement.
