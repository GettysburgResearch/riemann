# Independent review of the full-order and quartic density calculations

I checked the two notes' conductor bookkeeping, boxwise sieve combination, ordinary-block reduction, scalar detector minimax and prime-label lower counts. I found no concrete algebraic or quantifier error under their stated source assumptions. This review does not independently prove de Faveri's general-n rectangular-sieve replay, the compatible order-two normalization of the quadratic Hecke large sieve, or the fixed-type detector's imported analytic inputs.

For a full n-free label `g=∏a_j^j`, fixed local ν is unramified at good p|g while the j-th residue power is nontrivial, even when n is composite. Thus rad(g) divides the primitive conductor and the fixed-S conductor and deletion factors are bounded. Dyadic localization, followed by choosing the group with largest `sqrt(A_j A_(n−j))` or singleton `A_(n/2)`, is legitimate because the group choice is constant in a box. Freezing all other variables gives one common q-coefficient vector of modulus at most the original vector; enlarging the nonnegative outer row sum preserves the bound. From `P≤h^(n−1)` one gets `P/h≤X^(1−1/(n−1))`. For a maximizing pair, the remaining `P U^(−1/m)Q^(2/m)` term is bounded by the displayed weighted AM–GM between X and `X^(1−1/(n−1))Q`. For the quadratic singleton, the separate stated source-matching hypothesis is essential.

The q=q₁v² decomposition keeps all zero masks, places the row-dependent value ψ_g(v²) outside the inner squarefree sum, and has coefficient squared mass `≪(Nv)^−2` uniformly in the normalized annular profile and its bounded derivatives. Applying the squarefree moment and Minkowski over v yields the stated E(m), including the off-diagonal Q term because `Σ_v(Nv)^−2` converges. The common-parameter Sobolev step incurs a fixed polynomial height factor; choosing the small height exponent after its fixed derivative orders and preliminary losses handles the rowwise selected witnesses.

For n≥5, `c=1−1/(n−1)≥3/4`. At t=1/2, the piecewise function f has f(x)≤`c+1/2−δ/2` for x∈[1−c,1/2]; a larger value below 1−c forces x<1/4, so both witnesses cannot exceed this bound when r+m≥1/2. The source detector is used at a fixed t>1/2 with fixed losses chosen arbitrarily small, so the endpoint is approached rather than assumed. The feasible symmetric pair r=m=1/2 for t≤1 and r=t,m=0 for t≥1 give the scalar lower bound. Thus the full-order exponent is `c+1−σ`, below one exactly for σ>c. Prime ideals produce ≫X/log X distinct primitive labels, including exact-order-n characters when ν=1.

For n=4 the alternatives from the quartic pair and quadratic singleton combine to `X+X^(2/3)Q`. The two detector cases give `7/6−δ/2` when `1/3<δ≤2/3`, and `1−δ/[3(2−δ)]` when `δ≥2/3`. The feasible witness lower bounds and the comparison at δ=2/3 check; at σ=4/5 the exponent is 13/15. Its proper-order exponent-two component remains covered only under the explicit quadratic-source compatibility hypothesis. All conclusions count zeros with multiplicity after absorbing the standard individual zero-count factor into a sufficiently small fixed power-height cost.

The powered-family choice argument is consistent with de Faveri §2.3: R_(c,n) is a free (Z/n)-module of the fixed rank stated in (2.3), its selected basis reduces to a basis for m|n, and the same ideal representatives may be used. For a given q,b, the weak-approximation correction makes the m-power ideal coprime to b. More explicitly, the original n-symbol numerator and the adjusted m-symbol numerator differ by `(Mz)^m`, where `M=∏m_(E_i)^(ℓ_i)` and z cancels the b-valuations of the m-power ideal. Since the original n-decomposition has its power ideal coprime to b, `Mz` is a b-unit; both residue-symbol numerators are b-units. This verifies the powered-symbol identity for compatible source choices, modulo the separately stated effective-order-two matching. Equality of conductor supports alone would not be sufficient.
