## 4. An equivariant refinement of the zero Boolean coefficient

There is one additional exact distinction in this specific source.
Let H_j^+ and H_j^- be the positive and negative ordered histories above.
The symmetric group S_j permutes the j label slots and preserves each set.
These are finite permutation representations over Q or C; physical primes
and source weights are not asserted to possess this symmetry.

For a permutation sigma, let a be its number of odd-length cycles and b
its number of even-length cycles. The difference character is
\[
 \boxed{\chi_j(\sigma)
 =3^b-2\,{\bf1}_{a=0}\,2^b+(-1)^a.}
\]

Proof. A fixed history assigns one of the three colours R,S,M to each
cycle. A cycle of length l contributes the signed factor 2+(-1)^l.
Exclude R-empty and S-empty colourings by subtracting twice the product
of 1+(-1)^l, then restore the all-M colouring with sign (-1)^j.
Taking odd and even cycles separately gives the display.

At the identity this is 1+(-1)^j for j>=1: the familiar rough Boolean
coefficient. But for every odd j>=3, a single transposition has a=j-2,
b=1 and character 2. Thus the dimension-zero virtual history
representation is nonzero for every such odd depth. In particular the
positive and negative history sets are not S_j-equivariantly bijective,
and their permutation modules are not equivariantly isomorphic.
This does NOT prohibit the existing equivariant signed-sum map or its
zero value on the constant input.

For j=3 the character values on identity, transposition and three-cycle
are 0,2,0. They are exactly the character of 1-sgn. Equivalently the
positive set is two three-point permutation orbits, whereas the negative
set is the regular S_3-set:
\[
 2(1+\mathrm{Std})-(1+\mathrm{sgn}+2\mathrm{Std})
 =1-\mathrm{sgn}.
\]
This explains why forgetting to a scalar coefficient can cancel data that
still differ equivariantly. It is not a native Frobenius realization, not
a common Walsh/Kummer projector, and not proof that the unspecified native
