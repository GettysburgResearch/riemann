# Monomial Artin zero-density comparison and a counting-source correction

This is a targeted source comparison, not a claim of priority. The proposed full-power-free Hecke-family estimates are **conditional on the detector and sieve inputs** in the related research notes; none is established here.

For a finite-order character χ of a fixed Galois extension K/Q, Artin induction gives the exact identity L_Q(s, Ind χ)=L_K(s,χ) and conductor |D_K| Nfχ. Thus a conductor-ordered Hecke zero-count estimate also bounds the sum over the distinct induced representations in its image. A density-one claim needs an independent lower bound for the number of distinct representations in that image. If K=Q(i), Ind χ is irreducible precisely when χ differs from its complex-conjugate character; each irreducible induction is projectively dihedral, since the projective restriction to G_K is cyclic and the nontrivial coset acts by inversion. It is **not** necessary that χᶜ=χ⁻¹. If K=Q(ζ₅), irreducibility of the degree-four induction requires that the Gal(K/Q)-orbit of χ have size four; otherwise it decomposes. Images can be more general than a fixed Frobenius group. Under irreducibility, Mackey restriction shows that at most [K:Q] inducing characters in a Galois orbit yield one representation.

The candidate at σ=4/5 is X^(13/15+ε) for K=Q(i), order 4, and X^(19/20+ε) for K=Q(ζ₅), order 5, at sufficiently small power height, if the required source transfer is proved. The family is precisely the image of the chosen FHL presentations, not all dihedral GL₂ forms or all monomial GL₄ forms of bounded conductor. The two bounds require separate verification of the sieve and detector.

## Direct source comparisons

* Chen An, [*ℓ-torsion in class groups of certain families of D₄-quartic fields*](https://www.numdam.org/item/JTNB_2020__32_1_1_0.pdf), Theorem 5.2, gives a sum over associated irreducible two-dimensional dihedral Artin L-functions, with a fixed biquadratic resolvent and quartic-field discriminant X. For σ≥3/4, T≥2, its bound is T^B X^{c₀(1−σ)/(2σ−1)} with c₀>21. Its X exponent at σ=4/5 exceeds 7. This is a restricted D₄ field family with a different ordering parameter, and therefore is not a direct sharp comparator for all representations in the FHL image.
* Cho and Kim, [*Dihedral and cyclic extensions with large class numbers*](https://jtnb.centre-mersenne.org/article/JTNB_2012__24_3_583_0.pdf), use automorphic zero density to find near-1 zero-free subfamilies for various fixed groups, including D₃, D₄, D₅ and cyclic C₄, C₅, C₆. This does not state an exponent 13/15 or 19/20 at σ=4/5 for the full inducing-character image.
* Chen An, [*Log-free zero density estimates for automorphic L-functions*](https://arxiv.org/html/2004.14410v2), Theorem 1.1, applies to cuspidal GL_n/Q families with a conductor bound X^A, family size X^d, Ramanujan at finite places and uniformly bounded archimedean parameters. At σ≥3/4 and T≥2, its conductor exponent is (2d+4nA+A/2+1+ε)(1−σ), and the height exponent is (n/2+3+ε)(1−σ). On the irreducible induced images, fixed finite order gives Ramanujan and bounded infinity types; assuming the standard X^{1+η} inducing-character count gives A=1, d=1+η. At σ=4/5 the resulting exponents are 23/10+O(η) for GL₂ and 39/10+O(η) for GL₄; corresponding T exponents are 4/5 and 1, respectively. This is a valid broad direct automorphic comparator; reducible inductions must be excluded or treated separately. Theorem 1.1 is distinct from the field-count asymptotic issue below.
* Humphries and Thorner, [*New variants of arithmetic quantum ergodicity*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00220-024-05203-3), Lemma 3.1, cover the full cuspidal automorphic families GL_n/Q for n≤4, thus including these irreducible induced images. With π′ trivial and 0<ε≤1 they give at most ≪_ε X^ε zeros in the region σ≥1−ε/150, T≤X^{1/11}+6. This is a strong near-1 family rectangle but does not reach σ=4/5: ε would need to be 30, outside the stated range. It has no restriction to the FHL image.
* For the separate comparison with all conductor-ordered Hecke characters, including the stronger Khale bound and the limitations of rational quartic/quintic Dirichlet-family density results, see [the detailed Hecke comparison](2026-10-29-quartic-quintic-density-prior-art.md). A rational Dirichlet character theorem does not by itself cover all Hecke characters of Q(i) or Q(ζ₅).

## Field-count caveat in An Theorem 1.2

Theorem 1.2 of An's same [paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.14410v2) defines Z⁎_n(k;Y) by norm of the *relative discriminant*, includes a total-ramification restriction, and prints the asymptotic c_n Y^{1/(n−1)}. Immediately afterward the paper explicitly says all cyclic prime-degree extensions satisfy its restriction. For k=Q(ζ₅), n=5, this count is therefore all cyclic quintic extensions of k.

Wright's original [*Distribution of discriminants of abelian extensions*](https://doi.org/10.1112/plms/s3-58.1.17), Theorem 1.2, gives the abelian-extension count with logarithmic factor. The precise parameters are reproduced in the introduction of [Mammo's dissertation](https://cst.temple.edu/sites/cst/files/theses5/mammo.pdf): α=|G|(1−1/p), ν=(number of elements of order p in G)/[k(ζ_p):k], where p is the smallest prime dividing |G|; the count is asymptotic to a positive constant times Y^{1/α}(log Y)^{ν−1}. For G=C₅ and k=Q(ζ₅), α=4 and ν=4, so Wright gives cY^{1/4}(log Y)^3, contradicting the printed pure-power auxiliary asymptotic. This does not by itself disprove the separate exceptional-count or Chebotarev assertion in An's theorem.

The proof's apparent source of the discrepancy is explicit. In arXiv version 2, §6.4, printed p. 41, An defines B(s) in an unnumbered display as the product over Dirichlet characters ψ modulo n of the Hecke series with coefficients ψ(Nm(ideal)), and says it has only a simple pole at s=1. For n=5 and k=Q(ζ₅), every ideal coprime to 5 has norm congruent to 1 modulo 5: each ψ∘Norm is the principal Hecke character. Hence, for the source's allowed finite bad set S, B(s)=ζ_{k,S}(s)^4 and has a pole of order 4; with S consisting only of primes above 5, B(s)=ζ_{k,{5}}(s)^4. The version of record has not been checked. We have not audited the rest of §6.4 or independently verified all hypotheses behind the separate Chebotarev estimate. An independent label-count argument is in [the companion audit](2026-10-29-an-cyclic-count-audit/README.md).
