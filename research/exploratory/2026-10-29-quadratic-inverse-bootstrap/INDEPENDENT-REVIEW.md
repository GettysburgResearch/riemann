# Independent review: quadratic density-assisted bootstrap

The exponent calculation and fixed-point conclusion are correct under the stated all-Dirichlet strip, Corrigan–Zhao quadratic density bound, quadratic large sieve, and uniform fixed-field local reciprocal estimates. I found no arithmetic contradiction. The review does not independently establish the cited zero-density theorem or the formal candidate strip.

For u=d k², d squarefree and a odd, the Jacobi/Kronecker symbol is `χ_d(a)1_(a,k)=1`. The primitive character inducing χ_d has conductor at most 4d; d=1 is the principal case. For fixed k its mask is a common source coefficient, so the squarefree quadratic large sieve gives `≪(Y/k²+D)(YD)^ε`. Summing k≤Y/D proves the small-k term O(Y) for D≤Y.

For the high-k term, the local reciprocal estimate can be derived in the standard finite-height way, rather than requiring an all-height zero-free strip. For each primitive nonprincipal χ of conductor q≪Y, let b be a floor `1/2+e` or the largest real part of a zero in a buffered height window `|Im s|≤C T`, where C>1 is fixed. For `|t|≤T`, disks centered at 2+it with their left edge a fixed margin to the right of b are zero-free. The Euler logarithm at the center, fixed-strip polynomial Dirichlet-L growth, Borel–Carathéodory and three-circles bound both L and its reciprocal on the smaller disks by `(qT)^η` for any fixed η>0, with constants depending on the margin. Mellin-shift only the portion within the height window; keep the outside tails on the absolute line and choose their smoothness order after T=Y^τ is fixed. The deletion product over p|k has radical ≤k and is bounded with its reciprocal by `Y^η` on every line Re s≥1/2+e. This gives `|M_(dk²)(D)|²≪Y^η D^(2b−1+η)` uniformly in d,k after choosing the margins and height costs in the standard order. The principal d=1 reciprocal is holomorphic at s=1 and may be bounded separately from the assumed global ζ strip; it is not an omitted quadratic character in Corrigan–Zhao.

Bin b using finitely many fixed width intervals. For a bin whose lower edge is σ>1/2+e, Corrigan–Zhao bounds the number of primitive quadratic χ_d of conductor ≤C Y/k² having a zero at height ≤C T by `(Y/k²)^{H(σ)+η}Y^{O(τ)+η}`, where `H(σ)=3(1−σ)/(2−σ)`. The factor 4 in the conductor and the bounded number of congruence classes are harmless; multiplicities are unnecessary for a count of characters with a zero. When Q=Y/k² is near 1, use Q+2 and add the one principal character: both are O(Q^{H(σ)}) for Q≥1. A floor bin uses the trivial number of rows and has arbitrarily small D-power. The uniform mask bound remains valid for the prime support of k, including overlap with d.

On a dyadic k-block K₀, the nonprincipal contribution is bounded by

    Y^{H(σ)+o(1)} K₀^{1−2H(σ)} D^{2σ−1+o(1)}.

For H≥1/2, the worst K₀ is Y/D, giving exponent at most 1 provided `r≤H/[2(σ−1+H)]=3/[2(1+σ)]`. This is ≥5/6 for σ≤4/5. At H=1/2, the k-sum has only a logarithm. For H<1/2 the worst K₀ is √Y and contributes at most `√Y D^{2θ₀−1+o(1)}` using the global strip. The principal d=1 square rows have the same latter bound and are covered separately. Thus the displayed two-term estimate holds throughout the note's r≤4/5 range, subject to the standard analytic and density inputs.

A useful quantifier clarification: choose the requested final ε, then the finite bin width and reciprocal-strip margin small enough for the D-exponent losses, then choose τ small enough for all fixed polynomial height orders, and finally choose the exterior Mellin decay order. The note's wording may allow a different order, but this explicit order avoids assuming a height allowance uniform as the margin shrinks.

At `r=1/[2(2θ₀−1)]`, the second term is Y and the square-row obstruction gives `Θ_ζ≤1/2+1/(4r)=θ₀`. The endpoint is used with an arbitrarily small margin when absorbing losses.
