# Native sources: positivity, approximation and the arithmetic obstruction

**RH remains unproved.** The results here are source-qualified component selections in an integration candidate. Their underlying reviews are complete at the selected scopes; approval of this combined presentation and its exact repository tree is still pending. The [published baseline](../../../canonical/CURRENT.json) remains separate. This guide adds to, rather than replaces, the earlier Robin, SHARP, fixed-prime, wavelet, xi, heat and family results in [the cumulative catalogue](../../../RESULTS.md).

## What the project has gained

There is now a whole-function positivity certificate on a unit window, rather than just positive samples; quantitative inverses for the complete arithmetic divisor graph; finite arithmetic minima with their infinite tails paid before optimization; and ordinary causal inputs whose complete fixed-horizon errors are rigorously small. Exact Green, harmonic and prime-discrepancy representations identify the remaining signed quantities. Source-preserving counterexamples also rule out several proposed ways of combining these advances.

These gains do not establish the needed unbounded arithmetic estimate. The distinction is no longer simply "finite computation versus an infinite theorem": several results already hold on growing families or pay an entire infinite tail. What remains is a sign or upper bound for the **same actual source in the required norm**.

## How the approaches relate

The physical floor-residual norm, the harmonic divisor-graph energy, the parity-detail norm, the causal output error, and the inverse-input energy are **different quantities**. Their common use of Möbius coefficients does not identify them. A transfer is usable only when its source, metric, boundary terms and quantifiers agree with the consumer.

The arithmetic graph controls fluctuations around its harmonic mean. The literal stopped-Möbius norm can sit exactly in that mean channel. The graph's constant centered gaps on product reservoirs coexist with logarithmically growing anchoring costs. In another coefficient model, a uniform graph-to-physical transfer necessarily loses order $Y/\log Y$. Thus the available graph estimates cannot simply be multiplied by an approximation rate to conclude RH.

The approximation theorems retain the actual inner factor of the factorial source. They converge to its intrinsic floor; they do not identify the floor as zero. Similarly, a small parity-detail error leaves a coarse coupling, and summable square-cell prime detail leaves accumulated cell levels. Those are precise remaining arithmetic tasks, not omitted bookkeeping.
