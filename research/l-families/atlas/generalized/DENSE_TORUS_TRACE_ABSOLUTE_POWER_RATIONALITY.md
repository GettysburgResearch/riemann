# Dense unitary tori: trace absolute powers and exact local degree

Status: **proposed exact local theorem; external novelty unreviewed**

Issue: [#764](https://github.com/gfreund123/riemann/issues/764)

Claim labels: GLO764.DENSE_TORUS_FINITE_CHARACTER,
GLO764.DENSE_UNITARY_TRACE_ABSOLUTE_GATE, and
GLO764.TRACE_EVEN_POWER_ROOT_LATTICE_SUPPORT

## Scientific firewall

This packet generalizes the one-circle dense-orbit recurrence gate to a
compact torus and applies it to traces of powers of one diagonal unitary
matrix.  For rank \(n\geq2\), and only under a dense-orbit hypothesis, it
classifies rationality of

\[
\sum_{r\geq0}\left|\sum_{j=1}^{n}e^{2\pi i r\theta_j}\right|^\lambda T^r,
\qquad \operatorname{Re}\lambda>0,
\tag{0.1}
\]

where the value at a zero trace is fixed to zero.  The same theorem holds for
a dense orbit in the determinant-one torus.

This is one exact unramified local state-space calculation.  It does not
construct a prime-indexed family, Euler product, ramified factors, conductor,
completion, functional equation, automorphic representation, motive,
infinite-dimensional parent, explicit formula, or zero theorem.  It has no
RH or GRH consequence.

The restrictions are substantive.  Rank one gives a constant absolute trace
for every exponent.  A nondense diagonal orbit can also make the absolute
trace constant for every exponent.  The chambers
\(\operatorname{Re}\lambda\leq0\), including the separate \(0^0\) convention
problem, are excluded.

## 1. Full and determinant-one torus orbits

Write

\[
\mathbb T^n=\{(z_1,\ldots,z_n):|z_j|=1\}
\tag{1.1}
\]

and

\[
\mathbb T^{n-1}_{\det=1}
=\{z\in\mathbb T^n:z_1\cdots z_n=1\}.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Fix \(n\geq2\), put

\[
\xi=(e^{2\pi i\theta_1},\ldots,e^{2\pi i\theta_n}),
\qquad z(r)=\xi^r,
\tag{1.3}
\]

and assume one of the following two hypotheses.

1. **Full torus:** the cyclic subgroup generated by \(\xi\) is dense in
   \(\mathbb T^n\).
2. **Determinant one:** \(\sum_j\theta_j\in\mathbb Z\), and the cyclic subgroup
   generated by \(\xi\) is dense in \(\mathbb T^{n-1}_{\det=1}\).

The characters of the full torus are

\[
\chi_v(z)=z^v=\prod_{j=1}^n z_j^{v_j},
\qquad v\in\mathbb Z^n.
\tag{1.4}
\]

On the determinant-one torus, \(v\) and \(w\) define the same character
exactly when

\[
v-w\in\mathbb Z(1,\ldots,1).
\tag{1.5}
\]

This follows by substituting \(z_n=(z_1\cdots z_{n-1})^{-1}\): the restricted
character has exponent vector \((v_1-v_n,\ldots,v_{n-1}-v_n)\).

Density has an elementary dual consequence used repeatedly below:

\[
\chi(\xi)=\psi(\xi)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\chi=\psi.
\tag{1.6}
\]

Indeed, \(\chi\psi^{-1}\) is one on the dense cyclic subgroup and hence,
by continuity, on the whole torus.

Let

\[
p_r=\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{diag}(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n)^r)
=\sum_{j=1}^n\xi_j^r.
\tag{1.7}
\]

For \(\operatorname{Re}\lambda>0\), define

\[
A_\lambda(w)=
\begin{cases}
\exp(\lambda\log|w|),&w\neq0,\\
0,&w=0,
\end{cases}
\tag{1.8}
\]

and

\[
G_{\lambda,\xi}(T)=\sum_{r\geq0}A_\lambda(p_r)T^r.
\tag{1.9}
\]

The strict inequality on the real part makes (1.8) continuous at zero.

## 2. Dense torus samples: recurrence is finite character support

The key statement does not depend on the trace observable.

**Theorem (GLO764.DENSE_TORUS_FINITE_CHARACTER).**  Let \(X\) be a compact
torus, let \(\tau\in X\) generate a dense cyclic subgroup, and let
\(f:X\to\mathbb C\) be continuous.  Fix \(x_0\in X\).  If

\[
s_r=f(x_0\tau^r),\qquad r\geq0,
\tag{2.1}
\]

satisfies a nonzero constant-coefficient recurrence for all sufficiently
large \(r\), then \(f\) has finite character support.  More precisely, if

\[
\sum_{\ell=0}^{d}c_\ell s_{r+\ell}=0
\qquad(r\geq N),
\tag{2.2}
\]

and \(Q(z)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{d}c_\ell z^\ell\neq0\), then

\[
\operatorname{supp}\widehat f
\subseteq\{\chi\in\widehat X:Q(\chi(\tau))=0\},
\qquad
\#\operatorname{supp}\widehat f\leq\deg Q.
\tag{2.3}
\]

Conversely, a continuous function with finite character support produces a
constant-coefficient recurrence, and its ordinary generating series along
the orbit is rational.

### Proof

First, every tail of the positive orbit of \(\tau\) is dense.  One direct
argument is to take a convergent subsequence of positive powers in the
compact group.  Quotients of two far-out members give positive powers
approaching the identity, and multiplication by \(\tau^{-1}\) shows that
\(\tau^{-1}\) lies in the closure of the positive powers.  That closure
therefore contains the dense bilateral cyclic subgroup.  Translating it by
any fixed power proves the tail assertion.

Define the continuous function

\[
R(x)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{d}c_\ell f(x\tau^\ell).
\tag{2.4}
\]

Equation (2.2) says that \(R\) vanishes on a tail of the dense orbit, so
\(R=0\) everywhere.  With normalized Haar measure and convention

\[
\widehat f(\chi)=\int_X f(x)\overline{\chi(x)}\,dx,
\tag{2.5}
\]

the \(\chi\)-coefficient of (2.4) is

\[
Q(\chi(\tau))\widehat f(\chi)=0.
\tag{2.6}
\]

The evaluation map \(\chi\mapsto\chi(\tau)\) is injective by density, as in
(1.6).  A nonzero polynomial has at most \(\deg Q\) distinct roots, which
proves (2.3).  Fourier uniqueness on a compact torus identifies \(f\) with
the resulting finite character sum.

Conversely, if

\[
f(x)=\sum_{\chi\in E}a_\chi\chi(x)
\tag{2.7}
\]

for a finite set \(E\), then

\[
s_r=\sum_{\chi\in E}a_\chi\chi(x_0)\chi(\tau)^r.
\tag{2.8}
\]

This is a finite exponential sum, so it has denominator dividing
\(\prod_{\chi\in E}(1-\chi(\tau)T)\). \(\square\)

A formal power series over \(\mathbb C\) is rational exactly when its
coefficients satisfy an eventual constant-coefficient recurrence.  Thus the
theorem is an exact rationality gate, not merely an asymptotic statement.

## 3. The trace zero is a genuine cusp on both tori

Put

\[
S(z)=z_1+\cdots+z_n,
\qquad F_\lambda(z)=A_\lambda(S(z)).
\tag{3.1}
\]

The following local fact is what makes the classification sharp.

**Lemma.**  For \(n\geq2\), the trace \(S\) has a simple zero along a
real-analytic curve in \(\mathbb T^{n-1}_{\det=1}\).  Consequently, for
\(\operatorname{Re}\lambda>0\), the restriction of \(F_\lambda\) to either
the determinant-one torus or the full torus is a finite character sum exactly
when

\[
\lambda\in2\mathbb Z_{>0}.
\tag{3.2}
\]

### Proof

Let \(\omega=e^{2\pi i/n}\), and choose a unit complex number \(c\) with

\[
c^n=(-1)^{n-1}.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Set \(q_j=c\omega^{j-1}\).  Then

\[
\sum_{j=1}^{n}q_j=0,
\qquad
\prod_{j=1}^{n}q_j
=c^n\omega^{n(n-1)/2}=1.
\tag{3.4}
\]

Thus \(q=(q_1,\ldots,q_n)\) is a trace-zero point of the determinant-one
torus.  The curve

\[
\gamma(t)=(q_1e^{it},q_2e^{-it},q_3,\ldots,q_n)
\tag{3.5}
\]

stays in that torus, and

\[
\frac{d}{dt}S(\gamma(t))\bigg|_{t=0}
=i(q_1-q_2)\neq0.
\tag{3.6}
\]

Hence \(S(\gamma(t))=t h(t)\), where \(h\) is real analytic and
\(h(0)\neq0\).  Near zero, \(|h(t)|\) and
\(\exp(\lambda\log|h(t)|)\) are nonzero real-analytic functions.

Suppose \(F_\lambda\) were a finite character sum.  It would be real
analytic, as would its restriction to \(\gamma\).  If its zero at \(t=0\)
has integer order \(a\geq1\), then on \(t>0\)

\[
\frac{F_\lambda(\gamma(t))}{t^a}
=t^{\lambda-a}\exp(\lambda\log|h(t)|)
\tag{3.7}
\]

must tend to a finite nonzero limit.  Magnitude forces
\(\operatorname{Re}\lambda=a\), and phase forces
\(\operatorname{Im}\lambda=0\).  Thus \(\lambda=a\in\mathbb Z_{>0}\).
Comparing the two sides of zero in

\[
F_a(\gamma(t))=|t|^a|h(t)|^a
\tag{3.8}
\]

then forces \(a\) to be even.

Conversely, if \(\lambda=2m\), then on either torus

\[
F_{2m}(z)=|S(z)|^{2m}
=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}z_j\right)^m
 \left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}z_j^{-1}\right)^m,
\tag{3.9}
\]

a finite Laurent polynomial. \(\square\)

This proof treats complex exponents, zeros, and determinant one directly.  It
does not infer a higher-rank cusp from a generic smoothness slogan.

## 4. Rationality classification and sharp degree

**Theorem (GLO764.DENSE_UNITARY_TRACE_ABSOLUTE_GATE).**  Assume \(n\geq2\),
\(\operatorname{Re}\lambda>0\), and either dense-orbit hypothesis of
Section 1.  Then

\[
G_{\lambda,\xi}(T)\in\mathbb C(T)
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\lambda\in2\mathbb Z_{>0}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

If \(\lambda=2m\), the reduced denominator degree and minimal recurrence
order are

\[
d_n(m)=\#\left\{v\in\mathbb Z^n:
\sum_{j=1}^{n}v_j=0,
\ \frac12\sum_{j=1}^{n}|v_j|\leq m\right\}.
\tag{4.2}
\]

The reduced denominator itself is

\[
\prod_{\substack{v\in\mathbb Z^n\\
                  \sum v_j=0\\
                  \frac12\sum|v_j|\leq m}}
\left(1-e^{2\pi i(v\cdot\theta)}T\right).
\tag{4.3}
\]

The statements and the exact degree are the same for the full and
determinant-one tori.

### Proof

The coefficient is \(A_\lambda(p_r)=F_\lambda(\xi^r)\).  If the series is
rational, Section 2 makes \(F_\lambda\) a finite character sum.  Section 3
then forces \(\lambda\) to be a positive even integer.  Conversely, (3.9) is
a finite character sum and therefore gives a rational series.

Section 5 identifies the character support of (3.9) with the set in (4.2)
and proves that every coefficient is nonzero.  Density makes their orbit
eigenvalues pairwise distinct by (1.6), so none of the linear denominator
factors can merge or cancel.  This proves (4.2)--(4.3). \(\square\)

## 5. Exact \(A_{n-1}\) root-lattice support count

The ball here uses half the \(\ell^1\)-norm, equivalently distance in the
graph whose steps are the roots \(e_i-e_j\); it is not a Euclidean ball.

Expand

\[
\left(\sum_jz_j\right)^m
=\sum_{|\alpha|=m}\binom{m}{\alpha}z^\alpha,
\qquad
\left(\sum_jz_j^{-1}\right)^m
=\sum_{|\beta|=m}\binom{m}{\beta}z^{-\beta}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

Every resulting exponent is \(v=\alpha-\beta\), so \(\sum v_j=0\) and

\[
s(v):=\sum_{v_j>0}v_j
=\sum_{v_j<0}(-v_j)
=\frac12\sum_j|v_j|\leq m.
\tag{5.2}
\]

Conversely, if \(v\) satisfies these conditions, put \(v^+_j=\max(v_j,0)\)
and \(v^-_j=\max(-v_j,0)\).  Choose any weak composition \(\gamma\) of
\(m-s(v)\) into \(n\) parts and take

\[
\alpha=v^++\gamma,
\qquad
\beta=v^-+\gamma.
\tag{5.3}
\]

Thus every point of the \(A_{n-1}\) root-lattice ball in (4.2) occurs.
Moreover, its Laurent coefficient is

\[
C_v=\sum_{\substack{|\alpha|=|\beta|=m\\
                     \alpha-\beta=v}}
\binom{m}{\alpha}\binom{m}{\beta}>0,
\tag{5.4}
\]

so there is no scalar cancellation.

There is also no determinant-one collision inside this support.  If
\(v,w\) both have coordinate sum zero and define the same character on the
determinant-one torus, then \(v-w=k(1,\ldots,1)\).  Taking coordinate sums
gives \(nk=0\), hence \(v=w\).

**Theorem (GLO764.TRACE_EVEN_POWER_ROOT_LATTICE_SUPPORT).**  For \(m\geq0\),
the exact support count in (4.2) is

\[
\begin{aligned}
d_n(m)=1+\sum_{s=1}^{m}
&\sum_{a=1}^{\min(n-1,s)}
\sum_{b=1}^{\min(n-a,s)}
\binom{n}{a}\binom{n-a}{b}\\
&\times\binom{s-1}{a-1}\binom{s-1}{b-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5.5}
\]

Equivalently, with \(\binom ab=0\) when \(a<b\) or \(a<0\),

\[
d_n(m)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}
\binom{n-1}{k}^{\!2}
\binom{m-k+n-1}{n-1}.
\tag{5.6}
\]

### Proof

For a nonzero exponent on the shell \(s(v)=s\), let \(a\) be the number of
positive coordinates and \(b\) the number of negative coordinates.  Choose
their disjoint positions in
\(\binom n a\binom{n-a}b\) ways.  The positive coordinates are a composition
of \(s\) into \(a\) positive parts and the negative magnitudes are a
composition into \(b\) positive parts, giving the last two binomial factors
in (5.5).  Adding the zero vector proves (5.5).

For completeness, let \(c_n(s)\) denote the shell count.  Mark positive
coordinate mass by \(x\), and coordinate sum by a Laurent variable \(y\).
The one-coordinate series is

\[
1+\frac{xy}{1-xy}+\frac{y^{-1}}{1-y^{-1}}
=\frac{1-x}{(1-xy)(1-y^{-1})}.
\]

Taking the constant term in \(y\) after raising this expression to the
\(n\)-th power gives

\[
\sum_{s\geq0}c_n(s)x^s
=(1-x)^n\sum_{r\geq0}\binom{r+n-1}{n-1}^{\!2}x^r.
\]

The elementary binomial-square identity

\[
\sum_{r\geq0}\binom{r+d}{d}^{\!2}x^r
=\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{d}\binom dk^2x^k}{(1-x)^{2d+1}}
\]

follows by applying \(\frac1{d!}\frac{d^d}{dx^d}\) to
\(x^d(1-x)^{-d-1}\).  Leibniz's rule gives the numerator
\(\sum_{k=0}^{d}\binom dk\binom{d+k}k x^k(1-x)^{d-k}\);
the coefficient of \(x^j\) in this numerator is
\(\binom dj\sum_{k=0}^{j}(-1)^{j-k}\binom jk\binom{d+k}d
=\binom dj^2\), by the iterated Pascal difference identity.
Putting \(d=n-1\) yields the standard \(A_{n-1}\) coordination series

\[
\sum_{s\geq0}c_n(s)x^s
=\frac{\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\binom{n-1}{k}^2x^k}
       {(1-x)^{n-1}}.
\tag{5.7}
\]

Since \(d_n(m)=\sum_{s=0}^{m}c_n(s)\), multiplying (5.7) by
\((1-x)^{-1}\) and extracting the coefficient of \(x^m\) gives (5.6).
\(\square\)

The algebraic formula extends to \(m=0\), where it counts the constant
Laurent polynomial.  The analytic classification does **not** include
\(\lambda=0\), because (1.8) then has a separate discontinuous zero-value
convention.

## 6. Low-rank recoveries and hostile controls

For \(n=2\), the root lattice consists of \((q,-q)\), \(|q|\leq m\), so

\[
d_2(m)=2m+1.
\tag{6.1}
\]

This exactly recovers the rank-two absolute-power degree from the earlier
irrational-rotation theorem.  On the determinant-one torus, the trace is
\(z+z^{-1}\), and the explicit trace-zero curve in Section 3 reduces to the
ordinary cosine cusp.

For \(n=3\), the \(A_2\) shells have size \(6s\), hence

\[
d_3(m)=1+3m(m+1).
\tag{6.2}
\]

Some further exact counts are

\[
\begin{array}{c|rrrrr}
 &m=0&m=1&m=2&m=3&m=4\\ \hline
n=2&1&3&5&7&9\\
n=3&1&7&19&37&61\\
n=4&1&13&55&147&309\\
n=5&1&21&131&471&1251\\
n=6&1&31&271&1281&4251
\end{array}.
\tag{6.3}
\]

These rows are checks, not the proof of (5.5).

The hypotheses cannot be weakened silently:

- for \(n=1\), \(|p_r|=1\) and every exponent gives the constant sequence;
- if \(\xi=(e^{2\pi i\alpha},\ldots,e^{2\pi i\alpha})\), its orbit is
  nondense and \(|p_r|=n\) for every \(r\);
- at \(\operatorname{Re}\lambda\leq0\), the continuous extension across the
  trace-zero set used in Section 2 is unavailable; and
- determinant one changes the ambient character lattice, but it does not
  identify two root-lattice exponents in (4.2).

## 8. L0--L9 interpretation

| Object | L0 | L1 | L2 | L3 | First stop |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| non-even absolute trace power on a dense torus | continuous only for \(\operatorname{Re}\lambda>0\) with zero fixed | scalar absolute-value multiplicativity is formal when supplied by inputs | formal only under L1 | fails by (4.1) | L3 |
| even power \(2m\) | polynomial and branch-free | scalar multiplicativity is formal when supplied by inputs | formal only under L1 | passes with exact degree \(d_n(m)\) | L4 |

The finite state space at an even exponent is the span of the surviving
characters in the \(A_{n-1}\) ball.  There is also an honest balanced tensor
parent \(V^{\otimes m}\otimes(V^*)^{\otimes m}\), of rank \(n^{2m}\):
the trace of its \(r\)-th power is \(|p_r|^{2m}\).  The positive integers
\(C_v\) are its weight multiplicities.  Its representation dimension must
not be confused with the smaller scalar recurrence degree \(d_n(m)\),
which counts distinct weights.  Neither fact by itself produces a standard
determinant inverse or an automorphic lift.

## 9. Literature and novelty boundary

Katznelson's
[*An Introduction to Harmonic Analysis*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/an-introduction-to-harmonic-analysis/67C4CE356E7420BA17F3F1337291EF82)
is a standard reference
for characters, Haar Fourier analysis, uniqueness, and locally compact
abelian groups.  The implication from an eventual recurrence on one dense
orbit to finite character support is proved directly in Section 2.

Michael Baake and Uwe Grimm,
[*Coordination sequences for root lattices and related graphs*](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9706122),
give the published root-lattice coordination-sequence context surrounding
(5.7).  The identification of the trace-power Laurent support, its positive
coefficients, and the determinant-one no-collision statement are proved
directly here.

All ingredients are standard compact-torus Fourier analysis, a local cusp
test, multinomial expansion, and \(A\)-type lattice counting.  The assembled
local theorem is not a priority claim.  External novelty is unreviewed.

## 10. What remains open

This packet does not treat nondense subtori beyond the two stated ambient
tori, several independent monomial relations, scalar cancellation strata for
other observables, fixed-branch signed complex powers, nonpositive real-part
exponents, nonunitary eigenvalues, ramified data, or any global \(L\)-function
structure.  Quantitative stability under nearly nondense angles and coherent
representation-theoretic L4 laws are also open.
