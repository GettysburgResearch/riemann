Work over Q. Let V=Q^3, m=2 or 3, R=direct_sum_j (Sym^j V)^{tensor m}, W=Sym^m V with its coefficient-one orbit-sum embedding, E=R_1 and S=Sym(W). Let e be the average of the factor-permutation group and Q=ker(e:E->E). Then E=W direct_sum Q. ## 1. The action is a source operation

The Koszul complex K_i(W;R)=Lambda^i W tensor R has its usual differential d. For q in the complementary subspace, multiplication by q on the R factor commutes with d. Thus it induces a degree-one operator on B. These operators commute, because multiplication in R commutes, and define a graded Sym(Q)-module structure on B. They are equivariant for diagonal GL(V) and factor permutations, because the source multiplication and the symmetrizer are equivariant.

For w in W, let h_w(c)=w wedge c, with the wedge placed first before reordering. The Koszul identity is

    d h_w(c) + h_w d(c) = w c.

Consequently W acts by zero on homology. Replacing a representative of E/W by a W-correction does not change its action. The chosen complementary subspace makes this action source-defined without identifying a marked homology section with an equivariant one.

For i=0, B_0=R/(W)R is an actual finite graded algebra. Its multiplication is not encoded by its Hilbert series. For i>0, B_i is a module over B_0, since the full R action descends and (W)R acts by homotopies.

## 2. What the certificate establishes

In each weight, exact elimination constructs a basis of boundaries and selects cycle representatives whose classes complement them inside the kernel. Every selected cycle is substituted into d. Every reported action satisfies an identity in the original chain coordinates

    q c_a = sum_b M(q)_(b,a) c_b + d eta_(q,a).

The retained eta is an actual next-chain vector, not a character calculation. Independent boundary rank, kernel dimension and quotient dimension checks prove these classes form a basis. These identities then determine the complete induced multiplication matrix. Replaying all columns establishes a finite action theorem, including all vanishing actions.

For m=3 the known graded supports are B_0=(1,17,11) in grades (0,1,2), and B_1=(20,65) in grades (2,3). Thus the only new positive-degree B_1 multiplication is the map from a 340-dimensional domain to a 65-dimensional target. Its image, kernel and annihilators must be computed; neither the dimensions nor the Euler numerator decide them. The comparison case m=2 has Q dimension3 and B_1 dimensions3,1.

The source-action identities also imply commutativity. The replay checks it explicitly on B_0, and checks that multiplication by Q kills the top B_0 grade. Associativity in the quotient follows from the original associative algebra and the ideal quotient, not from a finite table fitted to dimensions.

### Acquired finite action theorem

The complete m=3 action Q tensor B_(1,2) -> B_(1,3) has rank65: every degree-three first-Tor class is generated by complementary-variable multiplication from degree two. Its 340 columns all have original-chain boundary witnesses. The quotient multiplication Q tensor B_(0,1) -> B_(0,2) has rank11. No nonzero element of Q annihilates all of B_(0,1), and no nonzero element of Q annihilates all of B_(1,2). These last two assertions concern the linear Q action; they do not assert that the polynomial algebra Sym(Q) acts faithfully.

It follows that B_0 is a level Artinian algebra with Hilbert function (1,17,11) and socle B_(0,2), of dimension11. Indeed Q identifies with B_(0,1), generates B_0, and annihilates its top grade; the zero linear annihilator excludes a degree-one socle. The unit is not in the socle. This is a consequence of the acquired multiplication and the known graded support, not of the Hilbert function alone.
