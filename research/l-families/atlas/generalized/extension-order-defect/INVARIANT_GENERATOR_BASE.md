# When the extension-order defect can be finite over the after algebra

This is a source-algebra sequel to `EXTENSION_ORDER_DEFECT.md`, separate from
that frozen packet. It applies elementary finite-group invariant theory to
the exact comparison. It is not a novelty claim for integrality of invariants
or a scalar characterization of a unique arithmetic source.

Let k have characteristic zero, I be a finite group, and A be a connected
commutative nonnegatively graded k-algebra with A_0=k and grading-preserving
I-action. Its augmentation is I-equivariant.
Let S be a finite-dimensional graded I-representation in positive degrees.
All tensor products below are over k. Set

    B=A^I tensor Sym(S^I),
    C=(A tensor Sym S)^I,
    Q=C/B.

The natural map B->C is the injection from the finite-extension packet.
The cokernel is a graded B-module, not a quotient algebra.

## 1. Exact finiteness criterion

**Theorem.** The following conditions are equivalent:

1. C is a finitely generated B-module.
2. Q is a finitely generated B-module.
3. I acts trivially on S.
4. The comparison B->C is an isomorphism.

**Proof.** The exact sequence 0->B->C->Q->0 makes the first two conditions
equivalent: lift a finite set of generators of Q and adjoin 1. If S is
trivial, its monomials are trivial representations and taking invariants
gives C=A^I tensor Sym S=B.

Conversely the equivariant augmentation of A gives a surjection

    C -> (Sym S)^I

whose section is f->1 tensor f. The image of B is exactly Sym(S^I).
Thus finiteness in condition1 would imply that (Sym S)^I is finite over
Sym(S^I). For any v in S, the orbit polynomial

    product_(g in I) (X-gv)

is monic with coefficients in (Sym S)^I. A basis of S is therefore a finite
integral generating set, proving Sym S finite over (Sym S)^I. Transitivity
would make Sym S finite over Sym(S^I). If S has a vector outside S^I,
extend a basis of S^I to a basis of S. The extra polynomial variable is
transcendental over the fraction field of Sym(S^I), contradicting integrality.
Hence S=S^I. QED.

The integral/finite implications used here are classical; see the primary
[Stacks Project, finite and integral ring extensions](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00GH).
The orbit-polynomial method for finite-group invariants also appears in
[Stacks Project, group actions and integral closure](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0BRE).
The proof above supplies the required special case directly.

In particular a boundary cokernel can have finite-dimensional pieces in
every grade and still fail to have any finite B-module generating set.
Its support on finitely many points does not repair that failure. No
claim about an infinite ordinary L-determinant follows from its support.

## 2. The canonical full-generator invariant base repairs finiteness

Assume in addition that A is finitely generated as a k-algebra. There is a
larger, canonically specified subalgebra

    B'=A^I tensor (Sym S)^I  subset C.

Then C is finite as a B'-module. Indeed orbit polynomials make A finite
over A^I and Sym S finite over (Sym S)^I. Their tensor product is finite
over B'. Reynolds averaging is a B'-linear projection from that finite
module onto C. Applying the projection to a finite generating list gives
a finite list for C. No choice of a homogeneous system of parameters is
required for this particular finite base.

This is a canonical enlargement relative to the specified A, S and I.
It is not asserted to be the unique smallest finite base; different finite
parameter subalgebras can exist. It is also not the after algebra B: the
new invariant polynomials in nonfixed generator directions are retained.

## 3. Actual boundary rings in the S3 comparison

At an old C2 branch, choose S=Std=x_+ direct-sum y_- with both vectors in
grading degree2. Then

    Sym(S^I)=k[x],
    (Sym S)^I=k[x,y^2].

The latter supplies a genuinely new degree4 generator. Writing A=A_+ plus
A_-, the complete comparison is

    C=A_+[x,y^2] direct-sum A_-[x] y k[y^2].

It is finite over B'=A_+[x,y^2] when A is finitely generated, whereas the
old B=A_+[x] cannot make the nonzero cokernel finite. This is the algebraic
reason that the y^2 tower in the first packet cannot be replaced by a finite
list of defect vectors over B.

For the actual Segre algebra A, this finite repair is still not free. The
degree-one space Std tensor Perm has three positive and three negative
C2 eigenvectors. The algebra is generated by that space. Every odd-parity
monomial contains a negative degree-one coordinate; removing one leaves
an invariant monomial. Hence A_- is generated over A_+ by exactly those
three negative coordinates. They are minimal because (A_-)_1 has dimension
three and A_- has no degree-zero piece. Consequently C has minimal
B'-module generator polynomial

    1+3t^3.                                                   (3.1)

The Segre ring is an integral domain and its C2 action is nontrivial.
For any nonzero negative coordinate a, the ratio of any other negative
element to a is invariant in the fraction field. Thus A_- has rank one
over A_+. The displayed decomposition of C gives rank two over B', while
(3.1) has four minimal generators at the homogeneous vertex. C is therefore
not free there. This is a concrete source refinement, not a deduction
from an alternating scalar numerator.

The original cokernel Q=C/B does not become a B'-module through this
enlargement: multiplication of 1 in B by y^2 leaves B. Thus B is not a
B'-submodule of C. The repaired finiteness assertion concerns C over a new
base; it does not replace the original infinite B-module Q by a finite
module while preserving its category.

At infinity, C3 acts on S with eigenvectors p,q of weights +1,-1. Then

    (Sym S)^C3 = k[pq,p^3,q^3],
    (p^3)(q^3)=(pq)^3.

The generator degrees are4,6,6 and the relation degree is12. To prove this
presentation, every weight-zero monomial p^a q^b has a-b divisible by3.
Remove min(a,b) copies of pq; the remainder is a power of p^3 or q^3.
The two resulting normal forms show that the displayed single relation
is the entire kernel. Its Hilbert series is

    (1-t^12)/[(1-t^4)(1-t^6)^2].

Residual nonsplit Frobenius interchanges p and q. In the normal-form basis,
only powers of pq are fixed, so its trace series is 1/(1-t^4). The
difference between the full dimension series and this trace series is
therefore a source action, not a choice of a smaller vector space.

At the new quadratic point the untwisted S is inertia-trivial. The criterion
gives B=C in every grade, matching the frozen zero-defect calculation.

The same finite-base obstruction explains the separate binary Chow-module
control in `../segre-hadamard-source/MATHEMATICS.md`: retaining a full invariant
ring or invariant complex can preserve finiteness, whereas replacing it by
polynomials in the fixed degree-one inputs need not do so. That is a shared
algebraic mechanism, not an identification of the two underlying sources.
