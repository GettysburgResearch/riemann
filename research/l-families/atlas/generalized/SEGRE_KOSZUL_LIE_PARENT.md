## 1. Prior art and the precise internal gain

This is the standard construction of the graded odd Lie algebra dual to a
commutative Koszul algebra. Gorbounov--Schechtman,
[*Homological Algebra and Divergent Series*](https://sigma-journal.com/2009/034/sigma09-034.pdf),
SIGMA 5 (2009), 034, sections 3.4.1--3.4.4, explicitly give the quadratic
dual Lie presentation, its Chevalley resolution, alternating Euler factors,
and an equivariant version. Their section 3.4.5 relates complete intersections
to termination after Lie degree two. Their grade-sum regularization does not
supply a meromorphic continuation of our all-prime Euler product.

Cederwall--Jonsson--Palmkvist--Saberi,
[*Canonical Supermultiplets and Their Koszul Duals*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00220-024-04990-z),
CMP 405 (2024), 127, sections 3.2.1--3.2.3, equations (42)--(45), give the same
duality and partition-function mechanism. They discuss highest-weight-orbit
rings and positive Borcherds--Kac--Moody pieces. The Segre ring is a
Cartan-product ring for the product of the special linear groups. We do not
need, construct, or claim a new Borcherds presentation here.

The internal gain is to replace the formerly formal virtual classes by
**signed duals of actual, independently presented Lie modules in every grade**.
A no-gap lemma then gives strict alternating dimensions in every
nonexceptional equal-rank grade. Independent Tor calculations test more than
a repeated Hilbert-series identity. This closes a classical mechanism for
reuse; it does not open an unexplored L-function theory.

## 2. The independent ring and its sorting presentation

Let k>=1 and let V_i be complex vector spaces of positive dimensions n_i.
Set G=product_i GL(V_i), E=tensor_i V_i, and

\[
 R=\bigoplus_{r\geq0}R_r,\qquad
 R_r=\bigotimes_{i=1}^k\operatorname{Sym}^r V_i.
 \tag{2.1}
\]

Multiplication is coordinatewise symmetric multiplication, fixed before
computing any scalar Hilbert series. Our convention uses V_i rather than
V_i^* for the degree-one polynomial modules. The product-group action
preserves this multiplication.

With bases, write x_alpha for the generator indexed by
alpha in product_i {0,...,n_i-1}. Map x_alpha to product_i v_(i,alpha_i)
inside the tensor product of polynomial rings. The kernel in Sym(E) is
generated by the sorting quadrics

\[
 x_\alpha x_\beta-
 x_{\alpha\wedge\beta}x_{\alpha\vee\beta}
 \quad(\alpha,\beta\text{ incomparable coordinatewise}).
 \tag{2.2}
\]

Here is a native normal-form argument. Order the indices by a linear
extension of coordinatewise order, with larger indices later in the
variable list, and use graded reverse lexicographic order. Among the
changed variables the join is last; it occurs in the sorted term only.
Thus the unsorted term leads, and reduction terminates. An unreduced
monomial is a coordinatewise chain. Its image records the sorted multiset
in each coordinate, and those multisets uniquely reconstruct the chain.
Consequently different standard monomials have different images. Every
polynomial relation therefore reduces to zero: (2.2) is a quadratic
Groebner basis of the actual Segre ideal.

This is classical: Morales,
[*Segre embeddings, Hilbert series and Newcomb's problem*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1306.6910),
Theorem 4 and Lemma 3, section 3.1, give this sorting basis.
We import the standard quadratic-Groebner/PBW-implies-Koszul theorem
(Priddy, [*Koszul resolutions*](https://math.mit.edu/~hrm/palestine/priddy-koszul-resolutions.pdf),
Theorem 5.3, printed p.51). Thus R is Koszul; in particular

\[
 \operatorname{Tor}^R_i(\mathbb C,\mathbb C)_j=0
 \quad(i\ne j).
 \tag{2.3}
\]

The all-degree assertion uses that theorem, not the finite tests below.

## 3. Actual quadratic Lie presentation and the necessary dual

Let mu:E tensor E -> R_2 be multiplication and let J=ker(mu).
Since R is commutative, J contains Lambda^2 E. Define

\[
 Q=J^\perp=\operatorname{im}(\mu^*:R_2^*\longrightarrow E^*\otimes E^*)
       \subseteq\operatorname{Sym}^2(E^*).
 \tag{3.1}
\]

Put E^* in odd parity and internal degree one. Then

\[
 \mathfrak g=
 \operatorname{FreeLie}_{\rm super}(E^*)/
       \langle Q\rangle_{\rm Lie},
 \qquad
 U(\mathfrak g)=T(E^*)/(Q)=R^!.
 \tag{3.2}
\]

These are finite generators and finite quadratic relations, not generators
whose dimensions are fitted to a formal Euler transform. In characteristic
zero, the degree-two part of the free odd Lie algebra identifies with
Sym^2(E^*) by [x,y]=xy+yx (the factor two for x=y is invertible).
Thus Q really is a space of Lie relations. The equality of enveloping
algebras follows from their universal properties.

Equivalently, give T(E^*) its super-Hopf coproduct with generators primitive.
For odd x,y the cross terms cancel in Delta(xy+yx), so Q is primitive and
the quotient is a cocommutative super-Hopf algebra. Super PBW embeds
mathfrak g in U(mathfrak g); no arbitrary Hopf structure is postulated on
the original commutative ring. Each g_d is finite-dimensional, has parity
d mod 2, and carries the natural G action.

Write I_2=ker(Sym^2 E -> R_2). The first modules are

\[
 \mathfrak g_1^*=E,\qquad \mathfrak g_2^*=I_2.
 \tag{3.3}
\]

More generally g_d is contravariant in the generators, whereas g_d^* is an
actual polynomial G-module of multidegree (d,...,d). A scalar c on one V_i
acts by c^(-d) on g_d and by c^d on g_d^*. This is a useful duality check.

### Theorem GLO764.SEGRE_KOSZUL_LIE_REALIZATION_V1

For the unique virtual grades defined by

\[
 H_R(T)=\sum_{r\ge0}[R_r]T^r
       =\prod_{d\ge1}\sigma_{T^d}(W_d)
 \quad\text{in }K_0(\operatorname{Rep}G)[[T]],
\]

one has the exact identity

\[
 \boxed{W_d=(-1)^{d+1}[\mathfrak g_d^*]\quad(d\ge1).}
 \tag{3.4}
\]

Proof. The G-equivariant Koszul resolution has degree-d free term
R tensor (R!_d)^*, with homological degree and internal shift both d.
Its degreewise finite Euler characteristic gives

\[
 H_R(T)H_{(R^!)^*}(-T)=1.
 \tag{3.5}
\]

Super PBW, as a graded G-module, gives symmetric powers of even g_d and
exterior powers of odd g_d. After dualizing and substituting -T, the even
pieces contribute sigma_(T^d)(g_d^*) and the odd pieces contribute
lambda_(-T^d)(g_d^*)=sigma_(T^d)(g_d^*)^(-1). Invert (3.5), obtaining
(3.4). Uniqueness follows grade by grade because sigma_(T^d)(W) starts
1+WT^d. All operations in a fixed degree are finite. QED.

For equal V_i=V, restriction to the diagonal GL(V) recovers exactly the
parent's coefficient powers Tr(Sym^r A)^k. In determinant notation,

\[
 F_{n,k}(A,T)=
 \prod_{d\ge1}\det(1-T^d\,\mathfrak g_d^*(A))^{(-1)^d}.
 \tag{3.6}
\]

This is signed effectivity, not an all-positive representation parent.

## 4. No gaps, strict signs, and exact equal-rank exceptions

A graded Lie superalgebra generated in degree one satisfies
g_d=[g_1,g_(d-1)] for d>=2, where brackets denote linear span.
Indeed, apply super Jacobi
[[x,u],v]=[x,[u,v]]-(-1)^(|x||u|)[u,[x,v]]
inductively to shorten the left entry of any bracket until it is a
generator. Every bracket word is a sum of nested generator brackets.
Therefore g_d=0 at one degree forces every later degree to vanish.

### Corollary GLO764.SEGRE_STRICT_ALL_GRADE_SIGNS_V1

For equal ranks n>=2, k>=2, outside (n,k)=(2,2), every g_d is nonzero and

\[
 (-1)^{d+1}W_d=[\mathfrak g_d^*]\ne0,\qquad
 (-1)^{d+1}\dim W_d>0 \quad\text{for every }d\ge1.
 \tag{4.1}
\]

Otherwise the no-gap lemma and (3.4) would give a finite graded virtual
parent, contradicting the frozen finite-parent classification. Equivalently,
finite-dimensional g would give a finite cyclotomic super-PBW product at
the identity, contradicting the parent's noncyclotomic numerator theorem.
This all-grade result is not extrapolated from the global packet's
eventual asymptotic signs.

All equal-rank exceptions are explicit:

- k=0 uses the separately defined trivial ring C[t], with one odd abelian
  Lie generator and trivial action (the coefficient convention is x^0=1).
- k=1 gives the polynomial ring Sym(V); g=V^* is odd abelian.
- n=1 gives a polynomial ring on the line E=tensor_i V_i; g=E^* is odd
  abelian. On the diagonal its dual has character V^(tensor k).
- For two rank-two factors, g_1^*=V_1 tensor V_2 and
  g_2^*=det(V_1) tensor det(V_2), with no higher terms.
  With generators a,b,c,d from a 2x2 array the only nonzero brackets,
  up to a common normalization, are [a,d]=z and [b,c]=-z.
  All squares vanish. The identity
  [x,[x,y]]=[[x,x],y]/2 makes z central, using the first bracket for a,d
  and the second for b,c. Hence the Lie algebra has four odd dimensions
  in degree one and one even dimension in degree two.
  Diagonal restriction gives W_2=-(det V)^2.

For unequal ranks (3.4) and the no-gap lemma still hold. This packet does
not advertise a new full unequal-rank finite-parent classification.

## 5. Held-out predictions from multiplication, not scalar fitting

The producer constructs R_r from monomial exponent vectors. Multiplication
is vector addition with integer coefficient one. For internal degree j,
the reduced bar complex has

\[
 C_\ell(j)=
 \bigoplus_{j_1+\cdots+j_\ell=j,\ j_a>0}
 R_{j_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes R_{j_\ell},
\]

and differential the alternating sum of adjacent multiplications.
(The implementation uses an overall negative of one usual convention;
homology and d^2 are unchanged.) It checks d^2=0 column by column and
computes exact rational ranks separately in each product-torus weight.
It never derives those ranks from 1/H_R(-T).

The declared panels contain ranks (2), (2,2), (2,3), (2,2,2) and every
internal degree 1 through 4: sixteen complete finite complexes.
All off-diagonal homology vanishes. Their diagonal Tor dimensions are:

| ranks | Tor_1 degree 1 | Tor_2 degree 2 | Tor_3 degree 3 | Tor_4 degree 4 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| (2) | 2 | 1 | 0 | 0 |
| (2,2) | 4 | 7 | 8 | 8 |
| (2,3) | 6 | 18 | 40 | 81 |
| (2,2,2) | 8 | 37 | 144 | 540 |

For the genuinely held-out unequal (2,3) panel in internal degree four,
(C_1,C_2,C_3,C_4) have dimensions (75,804,1944,1296), and (d_2,d_3,d_4)
have ranks (75,729,1215). Thus Tor_4 has dimension 81, **not** R_4's 75.

A second route starts with Q=im(mu^*) itself. For each coordinatewise
unordered pair, its relation is the sum of all ordered E^* words mapping
to that pair. In tensor degree three, form the associative ideal
Q tensor E^* + E^* tensor Q and the actual bracket span

\[
 [x,[y,z]]=xyz+xzy-yzx-zyx.
\]

Taking the rank increase after adding that span computes the image g_3
inside U(g)_3, justified by PBW. The quotient controls are:

| ranks | dim Q | dim g_2 | dim U(g)_3 | dim g_3 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| (2) | 3 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| (2,2) | 9 | 1 | 8 | 0 |
| (2,3) | 18 | 3 | 40 | 2 |
| (3,3) | 36 | 9 | 181 | 16 |
| (2,2,2) | 27 | 9 | 144 | 16 |

For (2,3), the two positive dual weights are
((0,0,1),(0,1,2)) and ((0,1,1),(0,1,2)), each once. Consequently

\[
 \mathfrak g_3^*\cong
 (V_1\otimes\det V_1)\otimes\det V_2.
 \tag{5.1}
\]

This follows from the whole torus character and characteristic-zero
semisimplicity, not just dimension two. With the 2x3 array (a,b,c;d,e,f),
its three minors q=(bf-ce,cd-af,ae-bd) satisfy
a q_1+b q_2+c q_3=0 and d q_1+e q_2+f q_3=0.
The producer independently obtains rank 16 for the eighteen
variable-times-minor columns in ambient polynomial degree three, so there
are exactly these two independent linear syzygies.

These are Tor over R and, separately, linear syzygies over Sym(E); they
must not be conflated. The broader Lie-cohomology/syzygy relation is
classical; see Gorodentsev--Khoroshkin--Rudakov,
[*On syzygies of highest weight orbits*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0602316),
sections 3.1--3.6, especially Theorem 3.6.1. We do not machine-prove that
general theorem. A nonzero off-diagonal bar homology class would be a
direct finite falsifier of the claimed Koszul mechanism.

degrees 1..4 are accepted; Boolean and floating inputs are rejected.
Before allocating bases, exact binomial dimensions and composition counts
bound the total bar basis slots strictly below 12000. The cubic Lie replay
allows at most nine degree-one generators and 729 tensor words.
These are structural allocation caps, not CPU-time or bit-complexity
certificates. Sparse elimination uses exact Fraction arithmetic; there
are no floating ranks, numerical tolerances, or assertion-only gates.

Default source authentication checks the compiled manifest contract,
frozen Git commit/blob identities, LF-normalized SHA-256 and current
parent bytes before replay. Literature URLs are declarations, not a
machine proof or fresh retrieval of external theorems. The fixture binds
note, producer, tests and manifest by LF hashes, and its payload by
canonical SHA-256. Acceptance is exact typed canonical equality with
recomputation; matching self-reported hashes alone is insufficient.
Strict JSON rejects duplicate keys/nonfinite numbers and caps input bytes.
No giant matrix is serialized; only dimensions, ranks and nonzero cubic
weight multiplicities are retained.
