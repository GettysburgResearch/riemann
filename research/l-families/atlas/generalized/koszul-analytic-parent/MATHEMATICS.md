# A canonical analytic parent for the first non-finite Segre case

Status: proposed exact equivariant algebra and operator theorem.
Scope: the Segre source of ranks `(2,3)` over `C`, with unitary input
automorphisms for analytic statements. RH and GRH remain unproved.
External priority is not claimed. The algebraic inputs named in Section 2
are imported classical theorems; the source construction, equivariant
identification, analytic bounds, and sharpness arguments are given here.

Frozen repository precursor: `8834fdc7a0dfe15f6bb95eefe0729cb77c93c807`,
[multiplicative recurrence and mixed parents](../MULTIPLICATIVE_RECURRENCE_AND_MIXED_PARENTS.md).
The precursor supplies only a formal virtual product and proves that this
rank profile admits no universal finite graded determinant parent. This
note does not alter that statement.

## 1. The source is an algebra, before it is a scalar function

Let `V,W` be Hermitian complex vector spaces of dimensions two and three.
Set `E=V tensor W`, and define the graded algebra

    R = direct_sum_(r>=0) Sym^r(V) tensor Sym^r(W),

with the multiplication induced by the two symmetric algebras. This is
the homogeneous coordinate algebra of the Segre embedding
`P(V*) x P(W*) -> P(E*)`. Write
`G=U(V) x U(W)`. All maps below are `GL(V) x GL(W)` equivariant; the
compact group is used only for invariant Hilbert norms and analytic bounds.

Choose bases temporarily and write the six generators as

    Z = [[a,b,c],[d,e,f]].

The algebra map `S=Sym(E) -> R` sends `z_ij` to `v_i w_j`. Its kernel is
generated by the three minors

    ae-bd, af-cd, bf-ce.                                  (1.1)

Here is a direct presentation proof. A degree-r monomial maps to a monomial
with specified row totals and column totals. Two 2-by-3 nonnegative
exponent tables with the same totals are connected by 2-by-2 exchanges:
if the first-row count in column j is too large and that in column k is
too small, exchange one `z_1j z_2k` for `z_1k z_2j`. Both factors exist,
and the sum of the absolute discrepancies decreases by two. Induction
connects any two such tables. Their differences are therefore generated
by (1.1), while images with different totals are linearly independent.
In particular

    dim R_r = (r+1) binom(r+2,2),
    H_R(t) = (1+2t)/(1-t)^4.                              (1.2)

The second identity follows by expanding `(1-t)^(-4)` and comparing its
coefficient plus twice its preceding coefficient with the first formula.

There is a coordinate-free quadratic presentation `R=T(E)/(Q)`, where
`Q` is the kernel of `E tensor E -> Sym^2(V) tensor Sym^2(W)`. In particular
`Lambda^2 E` is contained in Q. Define, from this quadratic source alone,

    B = R! = T(E*)/(Q-perp),
    L = FreeLieSuper(E* odd)/(Q-perp).                    (1.3)

The subspace `Q-perp` lies in `Sym^2(E*)`; it is interpreted in the free
Lie superalgebra as the corresponding odd anticommutators. It follows
directly from the universal enveloping presentation that `B=U(L)`.
The internal degree-n space `L_n` has parity `n modulo 2`. The spaces

    M_n = (L_n)*                                         (1.4)

are the actual source modules used below. They are not chosen by solving
for eigenvalues or by factoring a desired generating function.

The dual in (1.4) is essential: `L_1=E*` and hence `M_1=E`.
Replacing M_n by L_n would reverse the input action and generally recover
the wrong character series. Isometries of V and W induce compatible
isometries of all these constructions. PBW embeds L in B; the quotient
norm on B_n from `(E*)^(tensor n)`, the subspace norm on L_n, and the dual
norm on M_n make every `rho_n(g)` unitary for `g in G`.

## 2. Exactly which classical results are imported

We use the following three standard facts, with their hypotheses retained.

1. Polynomial rings over C are Koszul, and their Segre product is Koszul.
   The affine-semigroup version and its proof are in Bjorner--Welker,
   [*Segre and Rees products of posets, with ring-theoretic applications*](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0312516),
   Corollary 3 and Section 4. Their discussion also identifies the earlier
   Backelin--Froberg result. Here both factors are ordinary polynomial
   rings, so no weighted or noncommutative variant is needed.
2. For a quadratic Koszul algebra, the canonical complex
   `R tensor (B_n)*`, with its contraction/multiplication differential,
   resolves C. See Galvez--Gorbounov--Shaikh--Tonks,
   [*The Berkovits Complex and Semi-free Extensions of Koszul Algebras*](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.5802/afst.1497.pdf),
   Definition 2.1 and Theorem 2.3. The differential is defined by the
   identity tensor in `E tensor E*`, so it is equivariant, not just an
   equality of dimensions.
3. Super PBW identifies the associated graded vector space of `U(L)`
   with `Sym(L_even) tensor Lambda(L_odd)`, equivariantly for graded
   automorphisms. See Lofwall,
   [*Hilbert series, Poincare series and homotopy Lie algebras of graded algebras*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.07735),
   Sections 2.2--2.4, and the preceding Berkovits paper, Sections 2.2--2.3.
   This is a graded-vector-space/character statement; it does not declare
   the enveloping algebra commutative.

Those cited sections were read directly. We do not import the Berkovits
paper's later homology results, any conjecture about syzygies, or an
analytic continuation theorem from those algebraic sources.

## 3. Equivariant recovery and the parity sign

For `g=(A,B0) in G`, write

    F_g(t) = sum_(r>=0) tr(g|R_r)t^r
           = sum_(r>=0) h_r(A)h_r(B0)t^r.

We use B0 for the second input matrix, distinct from the quadratic dual B.
In each fixed total internal degree the exact Koszul complex is finite.
Taking its equivariant Euler characteristic gives, in the completed
representation ring,

    F_g(t) sum_(n>=0) (-t)^n tr(g|(B_n)*) = 1.           (3.1)

Apply equivariant PBW and then dualize each finite internal-degree piece.
Even L_n gives symmetric powers of M_n; odd L_n gives exterior powers.
Consequently

    sum_(n>=0) u^n tr(g|(B_n)*)
      = product_(n even) det(1-u^n rho_n(g))^(-1)
        product_(n odd)  det(1+u^n rho_n(g)).             (3.2)

Putting `u=-t` and inverting (3.2) in (3.1) yields

    F_g(t) = product_(n even) det(1-t^n rho_n(g))
             / product_(n odd) det(1-t^n rho_n(g)).      (3.3)

Initially this is a coefficientwise finite, formal identity. Thus the
precursor's unique virtual grades are exactly

    W_n = (-1)^(n+1) [M_n].                             (3.4)

In particular `M_1=E`, and `M_2=det(V) tensor Lambda^2(W)` has dimension
three. An even grade belongs in the numerator. A positive-only determinant
interpretation would erase precisely the sign needed for the relation
module, and is excluded by the precursor.

## 4. Exact multiplicities and their exponential growth

Put `epsilon_n=dim M_n`, a nonnegative integer because M_n is an actual
finite-dimensional module. At the identity, (3.3) and (1.2) imply

    n (-1)^(n+1) epsilon_n
       = sum_(d|n) mu(d) [4+(-1)^(n/d+1) 2^(n/d)].       (4.1)

To see this without assuming the answer, expand the formal logarithm of
(1.2): its coefficient at t^k is `[4+(-1)^(k+1)2^k]/k`.
The logarithm of (3.3) has coefficient
`sum_(n|k) n(-1)^(n+1)epsilon_n/k`. Mobius inversion gives (4.1).

For n>1 the constant-four terms cancel. The divisor d=1 contributes
exactly `2^n` to `n epsilon_n`. Every other divisor has `n/d<=n/2`, so

    |n epsilon_n-2^n| <= (n-1) 2^(n/2),       n>1.      (4.2)

This intentionally coarse bound is sufficient and uniform. Dividing by
2^n proves

    epsilon_n ~ 2^n/n.                                 (4.3)

The first values are `6,3,2,3,6,...`; they are consequences of the source
modules and not a definition of them. In particular infinitely many even
and infinitely many odd grades occur. No finite tail check is used to
prove (4.3) or infinitude.

For the bounded exact controls below, (4.2) also gives the deliberately
coarse bound `epsilon_n <= 3*2^n` for every n>=1. Indeed epsilon_1=6;
for n>1, divide (4.2) by n and use `2^(n/2)<=2^n`.

## 5. The Hilbert-space parent and sharp operator-ideal domains

Define the separable graded Hilbert space and its operator by

    H = Hilbert_direct_sum_(n>=1) M_n,
    H_even = direct_sum_(n even) M_n,
    H_odd  = direct_sum_(n odd) M_n,
    K_g(t)|M_n = t^n rho_n(g).                           (5.1)

The source, its Hilbert metrics, and its G-action were defined before F_g.
The block grading is intrinsic. No basis diagonalization or fitted list
of roots is part of this definition.

### Theorem KOSZUL23.ANALYTIC_SUPER_PARENT

For every `g in U(2) x U(3)`:

* `K_g(t)` is compact exactly for `|t|<1`; it is bounded and noncompact
  at `|t|=1`, and the block rule is unbounded for `|t|>1`.
* For every real `p>=1`,

      K_g(t) in S_p  iff  |t| < 2^(-1/p).               (5.2)

  On that disk, `||K_g(t)||_p^p=sum_n epsilon_n |t|^(pn)`.
  Every boundary point is excluded, including the trace-class circle
  `|t|=1/2`.
* On `|t|<1/2`, the ordinary Fredholm determinants exist and

      sdet_F(1-K_g(t))
       := det_F(1-K_g(t)|H_even)/det_F(1-K_g(t)|H_odd)
        = F_g(t).                                      (5.3)

The assertions are uniform in unitary g, including scalar, repeated, and
nongeneric inputs.

#### Proof

Unitarity makes every singular value of the nth block equal to `|t|^n`,
with multiplicity epsilon_n. Truncation after grade N therefore converges
in operator norm for `|t|<1`, and the errors have norm at most
`|t|^(N+1)`. At `|t|=1` the infinitely many nonzero grades give infinitely
many singular values one. For `|t|>1`, their norms are unbounded.

The displayed Schatten sum follows directly from the block singular
values. By (4.3) its tail is comparable to
`sum_n (2|t|^p)^n/n`, which converges exactly when `2|t|^p<1`; at equality
it is a harmonic tail. This proves (5.2) with its boundary cases.

For `|t|<1/2`, the block determinants converge absolutely and locally
uniformly: on `|t|<=r<1/2`, the sum of absolute logarithms is bounded by

    sum_n epsilon_n r^n/(1-r^n)
      <= (1-r)^(-1) sum_n epsilon_n r^n < infinity.      (5.4)

The logarithms here are the power-series branches at zero; each block
has norm less than one. Hence both determinant factors are holomorphic
and nonzero, and their quotient is the analytic realization of (3.3).
The series defining F_g converges at least for `|t|<1`, since
`|tr(g|R_r)|<=dim R_r` and (1.2) has radius one. Equality of Taylor
coefficients therefore proves (5.3). This is ordinary trace-class
Fredholm recovery, not a declaration of a regularized determinant.

The map `t -> K_g(t)` is holomorphic in the S_p norm on its disk: the
series of single-block operators and each differentiated series converge
locally in that norm, since polynomial factors in n do not affect its
strict radius. Changing the bases of V,W conjugates every block by the
induced unitary map, preserving all statements.

For an explicit finite-grade error certificate, the omitted logarithm
after grade N, uniformly for `|t|<=r<1/2`, has absolute value at most

    delta_N(r) = 3 (2r)^(N+1) / ((1-r)(1-2r)).          (5.5)

This follows from (5.4), restricted to n>N, and the preceding coarse
bound. For real t at the identity, let D_N be the finite signed product;
all its factors are positive. If delta_N(|t|)<1, then

    (1-delta_N) D_N <= F_identity(t) <= D_N/(1-delta_N).

Here `exp(-delta)>=1-delta` and `exp(delta)<=1/(1-delta)` for
0<=delta<1. The replay checks these rational enclosures exactly; their
validity for an infinite omitted tail comes from (5.5), not a tail scan.

## 6. An independent finite resolution and sharp zero-free disk

Let

    A2 = det(V) tensor Lambda^2(W),
    A3 = V tensor det(V) tensor det(W).

There is a native equivariant complex over S=Sym(E):

    0 -> S(-3) tensor A3 -> S(-2) tensor A2 -> S -> R -> 0. (6.1)

The map into S sends A2 to the minors (1.1). The preceding map sends
`v tensor eta tensor (w1 wedge w2 wedge w3)` to

    sum_(j=1)^3 (-1)^(j+1) (v tensor wj)
       tensor eta tensor (w1 wedge ... omit wj ... wedge w3). (6.2)

Formula (6.2) is basis independent and equivariant. In coordinates its
two columns are the row syzygies `(c,-b,a)` and `(f,-e,d)` for the ordered
minors `(ae-bd,af-cd,bf-ce)`. Their composition with the minors is zero.
These columns are independent over the fraction field of S: a nonzero
2-by-2 minor of their 3-by-2 matrix proves rank two. S is a domain, so
the first map in (6.1) is injective. The presentation proof in Section 1
identifies the cokernel at S with R. Finally

    H_R(t) = (1-3t^2+2t^3)/(1-t)^6

shows, grade by grade, that the remaining middle homology has dimension
zero. Each grade is finite dimensional, so (6.1) is exact. This dimension
argument is used only after injectivity, the zero composition, and the
correct quotient have been established.

Taking equivariant Euler characteristics gives the all-matrix identity

    F_(A,B0)(t) = N_(A,B0)(t)/det(1-t A tensor B0),
    N_(A,B0)(t) = 1-det(A) tr(Lambda^2 B0)t^2
                     +tr(A)det(A)det(B0)t^3.            (6.3)

No diagonalizability or distinct-eigenvalue hypothesis is needed for this
formal identity; equivariance and exactness imply it for all input
endomorphisms. Alternatively polynomial coefficient identities extend
the invertible case to all endomorphisms.

### Theorem KOSZUL23.SHARP_UNITARY_ZERO_DISK

For every unitary pair A,B0, F_(A,B0)(t) is nonzero for `|t|<1/2`.
It has a zero on `|t|=1/2` if and only if

    A=a I2 and B0=b I3, with |a|=|b|=1;

in that case the unique boundary zero is `t=-1/(2ab)` and is simple.

#### Proof

The denominator of (6.3) has all its roots on `|t|=1`. Unitarity gives
`|tr(Lambda^2 B0)|<=3` and `|tr(A)|<=2`, so

    |N(t)-1| <= 3|t|^2+2|t|^3 < 1      if |t|<1/2.

If N vanishes at radius one half, equality holds throughout this bound.
Thus both trace bounds must be equalities. The two unit-modulus
eigenvalues of A then coincide. The three pairwise products of the
unit-modulus eigenvalues of B0 also coincide, forcing those three
eigenvalues to coincide. Unitary diagonalizability therefore makes both
matrices scalar. Conversely, setting `z=ab t` gives
`N=(1-z)^2(1+2z)` and `F=(1+2z)/(1-z)^4`, proving the claim.

This finite resolution independently checks the sharp zero-free radius
predicted by the analytic parent. A zero at the trace-class boundary does
not imply that a trace-class determinant vanished there: at that point
the parent is no longer trace class.

## 7. Scope and next analytic gate

The ordinary Fredholm superdeterminant is now a genuine analytic parent
on its stated disk. The scalar F_g also has a rational continuation, by
(6.3). Those are different assertions. No trace-class continuation of
the fixed graded operator beyond its sharp disk is possible.

Higher regularized determinants can exist on the larger S_p disks, but
their counterterms must be specified and their relation to F_g proved.
The phrase "the scalar continues" does not choose those counterterms or
prove an operator-level continuation. This is the next analytic gate.

The exact replay constructs the quadratic source relations and checks
bounded low-grade dual/Lie dimensions, equivariant torus characters,
the two independent Hilbert computations, and the rational numerator.
It does not replace Koszul exactness, PBW, the infinite asymptotic, or
the operator-ideal proof by finite numerical evidence.

No prime-indexed family, conductor, gamma factor, functional equation,
automorphic realization, or RH/GRH consequence is obtained here. The
prior finite-parent obstruction remains true, and the necessary even
grades are not converted into positive representation multiplicities.
