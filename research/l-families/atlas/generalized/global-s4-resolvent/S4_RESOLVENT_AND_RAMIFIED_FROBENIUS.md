# A Bring-quartic source with all five S4 arithmetic constituents

This packet extends the actual-curve construction from S3 to S4. It does
not assume that a convenient character table is already an arithmetic
source. The source is the displayed smooth quartic curve, its splitting
cover, and explicit intermediate curves. In particular ramified invariant
dimensions are not substituted for Frobenius traces.

## 1. Source and geometric group

Let k=F_Q have characteristic p>3, and take b,c in k with

    b != 0,             Delta = 256 c^3 - 27 b^4 != 0.

Put f(x)=x^4+bx+c and E:y^2=f(x), with smooth projective models throughout.
E has genus one and two rational points at infinity. The map u=y has
degree four. The three critical x-values satisfy 4a^3+b=0 and are distinct
and nonzero. Their f-values are c+3ba/4, hence distinct; none is zero by
Delta!=0. Therefore the six finite branch points u=+-sqrt(f(a)) are
distinct and have transposition inertia. At infinity the inertia is a
double transposition, with two ramified points of E of index two.

The degree-four cover is geometrically connected. Its geometric group is
a transitive subgroup of S4 and has order prime to p. The prime-to-p tame
fundamental-group presentation says that the branch generators have
product one; in particular infinity is generated by the six finite
transpositions. A transitive permutation group generated by transpositions
is S4: the edges corresponding to generators form a connected graph, whose
edge transpositions generate every permutation. Thus the splitting cover
Z -> P1_u is geometrically S4 and also arithmetically S4. Riemann--Hurwitz
gives 2g(Z)-2=24(-2+7/2)=36, so g(Z)=19.

The excluded b=0 stratum is meaningful even when f remains squarefree:
over an algebraic closure, x^4=u^2-c gives a cyclic C4 splitting group.
Smoothness alone does not preserve the S4 source. Delta=0 is a separate
singular-curve failure.

## 2. Actual sign and resolvent curves

The discriminant quotient is

    D: s^2 = 256(c-u^2)^3 - 27 b^4.                    (S4-1)

Its right side has six simple roots, so g(D)=2. Its infinity contribution
to #D(k) is 1+chi_k(-1), since its leading coefficient is -256.

For quartic roots r1,...,r4, the three pair partitions give the roots
ri*rj+rk*rl of the classical cubic resolvent

    z^3 - 4(c-u^2)z - b^2 = 0.

Multiplying by z and putting Y=2zu gives the explicit genus-one curve

    R: Y^2 = -z^4+4cz^2+b^2z.                         (S4-2)

The quartic is squarefree: z=0 is simple because b!=0, and a common
nonzero root of its cubic factor and derivative would force Delta=0.
The point (0,0) and the two geometric points at z=infinity all lie over
u=infinity. Thus the *base fibre* of R there has 2+chi_k(-1) rational
points. In a direct projective point count, (0,0) is already affine;
only 1+chi_k(-1) points are added to the affine z sum.

These two genus-one curves and D are explicit quotient sources for the
standard three-dimensional representation, the two-dimensional pair-
partition representation, and the sign representation respectively.

## 3. The sign-standard source and its degree-eight determinant

Write

    g(x) = -16x^6-40b x^3-27b^2.

The discriminant of the remaining cubic after selecting a quartic root x
is exactly g(x): that cubic is

    X^3+x X^2+x^2 X+x^3+b.

Equivalently, disc_X(f(X)-f(x))=(4x^3+b)^2 g(x). Thus

    C: y^2=f(x), v^2=g(x)                             (S4-3)

is Z/A3, where A3 fixes the selected root. The polynomial g is squarefree
and coprime to f under the source hypotheses: as a quadratic in x^3 its
discriminant is -128b^2, both roots are nonzero, and common f/g roots
would force Delta=0. C is a smooth connected biquadratic cover of P1_x.
Its two other quotient curves are G:v^2=g of genus two and H:w^2=fg of
genus four, giving g(C)=1+2+4=7 and

    P_C = P_E P_G P_H.                                (S4-4)

Here P_X(T)=det(1-TF|H^1(X_bar,Q_l)), with l!=p and geometric Frobenius.
The permutation representation on S4/A3 decomposes as

    Ind_A3^S4(1) = 1 + sign + std + (sign tensor std).

Consequently there is an actual eight-dimensional Frobenius space M_tw
and its degree-eight determinant P_tw, satisfying

    P_C = P_D P_E P_tw,       P_G P_H = P_D P_tw.       (S4-5)

Precisely, M_tw=Hom_S4(tw,H^1(Z)); the full central-idempotent isotypic
part is tw tensor M_tw and has dimension 24, not eight. Equivalently one
can take the A3-invariant part of that isotypic space, since dim tw^A3=1.
These identities construct P_tw before any finite computation. They are
not evidence from polynomial division that an unknown rational function
is a determinant. All irreducibles of S4 are rational and have invariant
symmetric forms. The alternating Poincare form on the isotypic part
induces an alternating perfect pairing on each multiplicity space. Hence
P_tw is a Q-reciprocal Weil polynomial of degree eight; in particular

    P_tw(T) = Q^4 T^8 P_tw(1/(QT)).                    (S4-6)

Integrality also follows from the monic rational factor of the curve
Frobenius polynomial and algebraic integrality of its eigenvalues.
The complete splitting-cover determinant is

    P_Z = P_D P_R^2 P_E^3 P_tw^3,                     (S4-7)

of degree 4+4+6+24=38, independently agreeing with g(Z)=19.

## 4. Ramified Frobenius, including the sign change

Order the representations as 1, sign, two, std, tw. Their character table
is

| class | size | 1 | sign | two | std | tw |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| identity | 1 | 1 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 3 |
| transposition | 6 | 1 | -1 | 0 | 1 | -1 |
| double transposition | 3 | 1 | 1 | 2 | -1 | -1 |
| three-cycle | 8 | 1 | 1 | -1 | 0 | 0 |
| four-cycle | 6 | 1 | -1 | 0 | -1 | 1 |

At an unramified rational u, the number of quartic roots and the quadratic
character of its discriminant determine the class: respectively
(4,+1),(2,-1),(0,+1),(1,+1),(0,-1). At a finite branch, the double root a
is rational over its residue field and the remaining quadratic is
X^2+2aX+3a^2, with discriminant -8a^2. Inertia I is the transposition of
the coalescing pair. Frobenius modulo I is identity if chi(-2)=+1 and
the disjoint transposition if chi(-2)=-1. Thus the invariant *traces* are

    finite branch split:      (1,0,1,2, 1),
    finite branch nonsplit:   (1,0,1,0,-1).             (S4-8)

In particular tw has a one-dimensional invariant stalk whose Frobenius
eigenvalue is +1 or -1. It is false that its local denominator is always
1-T^deg(v); it is 1-chi_residue(-2) T^deg(v).

At infinity write x approximately as +/-sqrt(u), +/-i sqrt(u). Inertia
changes both signs. Frobenius modulo inertia is identity when Q=1 mod4
and a single transposition when Q=3 mod4. Hence

    infinity, Q=1 mod4:       (1, 1,2,1, 1),
    infinity, Q=3 mod4:       (1,-1,0,1,-1).            (S4-9)

This gives the full R infinity fibre 3/1 and the C infinity fibre 4/0.
C's direct projective count adds 2(1+chi(-1)); the factor two comes from
the two rational E infinity points. The G and H infinity contributions
are each 1+chi(-1). For Z the finite branch fibre has 12 rational points
only in the split case, and zero in the nonsplit case; its infinity fibre
similarly has 12/0. These are normalized cover fibres, not multiplicities
of roots in a singular affine splitting scheme.

For any nontrivial irreducible rho of dimension d, its Artin conductor is

    a(rho) = 6(d-chi_rho(trans))/2
              +(d-chi_rho(double))/2,
    dim H^1(P1,j_*rho) = a(rho)-2d.

The dimensions are 4,2,2,8 for sign,two,std,tw. This independently checks
the quotient-curve construction. With the actual invariant stalks above,
the Artin L-functions are P_D,P_R,P_E,P_tw. They obey weight-one purity
by their geometric cohomology, not by fitted numerical root locations.

## 5. Classical inputs, new packet scope, and replay

The discriminant/resolvent conventions agree with Keith Conrad,
[Galois groups of cubics and quartics](https://kconrad.math.uconn.edu/blurbs/galoistheory/cubicquartic.pdf),
equation (1.1) and the cubic-resolvent section. The finite-cover invariant
cohomology, trace formula and curve duality are the standard results in
Milne, [Lectures on Etale Cohomology](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/LEC.pdf),
sections 24--26 and 29. These are imported theorems, not re-proved by the
finite replay. No external priority is claimed for resolvents, Artin
factorizations, biquadratic decompositions or the Weil theorem.

The contribution of this packet is the explicit connected source,
its complete ramification adapter and bounded primitive replay of every
displayed factor. `REPLAY_CONTRACT.md` separates independently counted
extensions from coefficients supplied by the proved reciprocity. No
claim about number-field RH, or a new proof of the finite-field RH, is
made.
