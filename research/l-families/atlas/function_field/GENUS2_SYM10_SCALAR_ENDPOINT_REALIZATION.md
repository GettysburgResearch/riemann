# Exact scalar endpoints in the genus-two \(\operatorname{Sym}^{10}\) tower

Status: **exact realization and orbit lower bound for every**
\(q=3^{4k}\), \(k\geq1\).

Scope: the member-uniform family \(\mathcal H_5(q)\) of monic squarefree
quintics.  The replay performs no finite-field, field-element, curve, or root
enumeration.  It uses 97 small exact operations and reads two committed
compatibility certificates after closing the arithmetic proof.

## Result

Let

\[
 C:\quad y^2=x^5+1
\]

over \(\mathbf F_3\).  For every \(k\geq1\), put \(q=3^{4k}\) and view the
same equation over \(\mathbf F_q\).  Its normalized Frobenius class is

\[
 U_C=(-1)^kI_4.
\]

Consequently

\[
 P_{C,q}(T)=(1-(-9)^kT)^4,qquad
 (a_C,b_C)=(-4(-9)^k,6q),                                  \tag{1}
\]

and the reciprocal \(\operatorname{Sym}^{10}\) channel attains the compact
ceiling:

\[
 r_C(10)=286q^5,qquad \chi_{(10,0)}(U_C)=286.              \tag{2}
\]

The full affine orbit contains two equally sized square-affine orbits, one at
each scalar sign.  Thus each sign occurs in at least a proportion
\(1/(10q^3)\) of all monic squarefree quintics, and their union has proportion
\(1/(5q^3)\).

## 1. The seed factor without a field sweep

In characteristic three,

\[
 D(x)=x^5+1,qquad D'(x)=2x^4.
\]

Because \(D\bmod x^4=1\), the gcd of \(D\) and \(D'\) is one.  The quintic is
therefore squarefree.

For \(n=1,2\), the multiplicative groups of \(\mathbf F_{3^n}\) have orders
\(2\) and \(8\).  Both are coprime to five, so \(x\mapsto x^5\) is a bijection
of the whole field.  If \(\chi\) is the quadratic character extended by
\(\chi(0)=0\), then

\[
 \sum_x\chi(x^5+1)=\sum_u\chi(u+1)=\sum_v\chi(v)=0.
\]

There are therefore \(3^n\) affine points and one point at infinity:

\[
 N_1=4,qquad N_2=10.                                      \tag{3}
\]

If \(s_j=\sum_i\alpha_i^j\) denotes the Frobenius power sum, (3) gives

\[
 s_1=3+1-N_1=0,qquad s_2=3^2+1-N_2=0.
\]

Newton's identity then gives \(e_2=(s_1^2-s_2)/2=0\).  Hence

\[
 P_{C,3}(T)=1+9T^4,qquad
 \operatorname{charpoly}(\operatorname{Frob}_3;X)=X^4+9.   \tag{4}
\]

This establishes (4) from two character-sum bijections, not an enumeration of
the three- or nine-element fields.

## 2. Every fourth-degree base change is scalar

Equation (4) says that every Frobenius root satisfies \(\alpha_i^4=-9\).
After base change from \(\mathbf F_3\) to \(\mathbf F_{3^{4k}}\), all four
roots become

\[
 \alpha_i^{4k}=(-9)^k.
\]

Since \(q=3^{4k}=81^k\) and \(\sqrt q=9^k\), division by \(\sqrt q\) gives
the scalar class \((-1)^kI_4\).  Expanding its fourth power proves (1).
Moreover,

\[
 [T^{10}](1-(-9)^kT)^{-4}
 =\binom{13}{3}(-9)^{10k}
 =286q^5,
\]

which proves (2) directly.  No compact-group sampling or root approximation
enters the argument.

The point-count sign is also fixed.  Since the local-factor convention is
\(\#C(\mathbf F_q)=q+1+a_C\), the first tower field \(q=81\) has
\(U=-I\), \(a_C=36\), and \(\#C(\mathbf F_{81})=118\).  Its quadratic twist
has \(U=I\), \(a=-36\), and 46 points.  This independently checks both signs
used below.

## 3. Exact affine stabilizer and both twist signs

Use the full monic affine action

\[
 D(T)\mid(\alpha,\beta)
 =\alpha^{-5}D(\alpha T+\beta).                             \tag{5}
\]

The \(T^4\)-coefficient of (5) is \(5\beta/\alpha=2\beta/\alpha\) in
characteristic three.  Stabilizing \(T^5+1\) therefore forces \(\beta=0\).
The constant coefficient then forces \(\alpha^5=1\).  Thus

\[
 \operatorname{Stab}_{\operatorname{AGL}(1,q)}(D)=\mu_5,
 \qquad |\operatorname{Stab}|=5.                            \tag{6}
\]

Here \(q=81^k\equiv1\pmod {10}\).  If \(g\) generates
\(\mathbf F_q^\times\), then \(\mu_5\) is generated by
\(g^{(q-1)/5}\), and this exponent is even.  Hence the entire stabilizer lies
in the square-scaling affine subgroup.

The full and square-affine groups have orders \(q(q-1)\) and
\(q(q-1)/2\), respectively.  Orbit--stabilizer and (6) now give

\[
 |\operatorname{AGL}(1,q)D|=\frac{q(q-1)}5,qquad
 |G_{\rm sq}D|=\frac{q(q-1)}{10}.                           \tag{7}
\]

Because \(G_{\rm sq}\) is normal of index two and the full stabilizer is
contained in it, the full orbit is exactly two square-affine orbits.

The twist sign can be checked directly.  For nonsquare \(\alpha\), choose
\(r\in\mathbf F_{q^2}\) with \(r^2=\alpha\).  Then \(r^q=-r\), and

\[
 (T,Y)\longmapsto(\alpha T+\beta,r^5Y)
\]

is an \(\mathbf F_{q^2}\)-isomorphism from the transformed curve to the
original one because \(r^{10}=\alpha^5\).  Frobenius changes \(r^5\) to
\(-r^5\), so the conjugate isomorphism differs by the hyperelliptic
involution.  This is exactly the nontrivial quadratic-twist cocycle and
negates all four Frobenius eigenvalues.  The two orbits in (7) therefore have
scalar signs \((-1)^kI\) and \((-1)^{k+1}I\).  The committed marked-stack
adapter independently confirms the same action and sign convention.

Finally,

\[
 |\mathcal H_5(q)|=q^5-q^4=q^4(q-1).
\]

Writing \(\rho_\pm(q)\) for the member-uniform densities of \(U=\pm I\),
dividing (7) by this exact family size proves

\[
 \rho_{+}(q)\geq\frac1{10q^3},\qquad
 \rho_{-}(q)\geq\frac1{10q^3},\qquad
 \rho_{+}(q)+\rho_{-}(q)\geq\frac1{5q^3}.                 \tag{8}
\]

The right sides are the exact densities of the constructed orbits.  There may
be other scalar-endpoint orbits, so (8) is deliberately a lower bound rather
than a classification.

## 4. Exact high-moment contribution

For an integer \(m\geq1\), normalize by

\[
 M_m(q)=\frac1{|\mathcal H_5(q)|}
 \sum_{D\in\mathcal H_5(q)}
 \left(\frac{r_D(10)}{q^5}\right)^m.
\]

Both constructed scalar signs have normalized value \(286\), because the
symmetric-power degree is even.  Their two-orbit union therefore contributes
exactly

\[
 \boxed{\frac{286^m}{5q^3}}                                \tag{9}
\]

to \(M_m(q)\).  Equation (9) is an exact additive subset contribution.  It is
a lower bound for absolute moments and even moments.  It is not, by itself, a
lower bound for a full signed odd moment, where the remaining family can
cancel it.

At every fixed \(m\), (9) tends to zero as \(q\to\infty\).  But for

\[
 m_q=\left\lfloor\frac{3\log q}{\log286}\right\rfloor,
\]

one has

\[
 \frac1{5\cdot286}
 \leq \frac{286^{m_q}}{5q^3}
 \leq\frac15.                                               \tag{10}
\]

Thus this explicit boundary layer disappears in every fixed moment while
remaining order one once the moment order grows logarithmically with the
field.  This is a rigorous noncommuting-limit warning for moment experiments,
not a claim that the full moment is determined by these orbits.

## 5. Literature consistency, not an input

[Howe's characteristic-three classification, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0604413)
lists, for even \(d\), the Weil polynomials

\[
 (X^2-2sX+q)^2=(X-s)^4,qquad s=\pm\sqrt q,
\]

only when \(q>9\).  It therefore excludes scalar endpoints at \(q=9\) and
permits them for every even-degree characteristic-three field with \(q>9\).
Our tower has \(d=4k\) and \(q\geq81\), so the exact construction above is
consistent with that classification.

Howe's theorem is not read, encoded, or used by the producer.  The seed point
counts, base-change identity, stabilizer, twist-orbit sizes, and densities are
proved independently in this packet.
