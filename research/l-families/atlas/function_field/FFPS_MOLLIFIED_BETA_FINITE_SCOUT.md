## 0. Outcome

The raw complete beta current has an exact atomic lower bound of order
`sqrt(Y)`.  The fixed-mollified current behaves very differently on the first
eighteen dyadic scales.

For the causal box width

\[
 \varepsilon={1\over2}\log2,
\]

the fine `512`-cell-per-doubling panel gives

| `Y` | mollified negative mass | mollified absolute mass | raw atomic lower bound | ratio |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `2^10` | 209.085 | 422.792 | 448.094 | 0.4666 |
| `2^14` | 287.634 | 580.765 | 1,858.573 | 0.1548 |
| `2^18` | 367.255 | 733.561 | 7,408.856 | 0.04957 |

Over the finite fitting window `2^10,...,2^18`, least squares gives

\[
 \text{negative mass}\approx19.32\log_2Y+19.22,
\]

and

\[
 \text{absolute mass}\approx38.38\log_2Y+42.41.
\]

The maximum residuals are `3.62` and `3.43`, respectively.  This suggests a
new finite-data conjecture:

> for this fixed mollifier the complete detector may have asymptotically
> linear logarithmic total variation, possibly with balanced positive and
> negative slopes.

That conjecture is deliberately **not** claimed.  The range is tiny on an
analytic-number-theory scale, the calculation is floating point, and RH
already predicts only the much weaker `Y^o(1)` upper envelope through the
equivalence packet.  The experiment is useful because it demonstrates that
the smoothing interface produces large cross-layer cancellation before the
Jordan part is taken; it is not merely hiding the raw atoms in notation.

## 1. What is evaluated

The inverse-Mellin packet writes the extra-notched kernel as finite dyadic
differences of

\[
 5\delta_0+(3-4e^{t/2})1_{t\ge0}\,dt.
\]

The producer convolves this exact formula with the causal log box, evaluates
the resulting compact density, and then forms

\[
 h_\varepsilon(t)
 =\sum_{n\le2^{18}}{\beta(n)\over\sqrt n}
   \kappa_\varepsilon(t-\log n).
\]

Because the kernel is causal, this truncation is exact for every displayed
horizon apart from log-grid placement.  The Mobius values are generated by a
linear sieve.  No zero, curve, character family, conductor family, or finite
field is enumerated.

The raw comparison is not a numerical estimate of total raw variation.  It
is the exact proved atomic subset lower bound

\[
 (50+35\sqrt2)
 \sum_{\substack{m\le Y/32\\m\text{ odd}}}
 {|\beta(m)|\over\sqrt m}.
\]

Thus the falling ratio in the table directly measures how far the mollified
negative part lies below one certified subset of the unmollified negative
variation.

## 2. Numerical controls

Two independent log meshes use `256` and `512` cells per doubling.  Across
all thirteen horizons their largest relative discrepancy in either mass is
`0.00321`; at `Y=2^18` the negative-mass discrepancy is below `0.00025`.

The midpoint quadrature respects every dyadic and box endpoint.  On both
meshes the numerical integral of `kappa_epsilon` rounds to zero at nine
decimal places, as required by the exact `D` factor in `K_ext`.

The mesh test controls discretization at these resolutions.  It is not an
interval-arithmetic certificate and does not turn the table into exact
finite mathematics.

## 3. Interpretation and next experiment

The nearly `2:1` absolute-to-negative slope means the signed mean is much
smaller than either Jordan side.  The alternating increments from one dyadic
scale to the next suggest retaining residue-class and `67`-duplicate panels
in a sequel rather than fitting a single smooth power law.

The next bounded experiment should compare several fixed box widths and
separate the native half-divisor reflection-even and reflection-odd energies.
If the apparent logarithmic slope is stable under those changes, the natural
theorem target becomes an `O((log Y)^C)` variation bound for the native
reflection gate.  Nothing in this scout proves such a bound.
