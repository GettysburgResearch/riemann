## 2. Exact midpoint-difference coordinates

For \(j<k\), write

\[
 d=k-j,\qquad
 x=j+{d\over2}-{n\over2}.
\tag{2.1}
\]

Then

\[
 y_{j+d}-y_j
 =\cos\!\left({\pi x\over n}\right)
  \sin\!\left({\pi d\over2n}\right).
\tag{2.2}
\]

At \(c=c_n(\lambda)\), the parity sign in \(q_jq_{j+d}\) combines with
the imaginary exponential to give the exact phase

\[
 \phi_n(d,x)
 =\pi d
 -2n\cos\!\left({\pi x\over n}\right)
       \sin\!\left({\pi d\over2n}\right),
\tag{2.3}
\]

and the exact damping

\[
 \eta_{n,\lambda}(d,x)
 ={\lambda n^{1/3}\over2}
 \cos\!\left({\pi x\over n}\right)
 \sin\!\left({\pi d\over2n}\right).
\tag{2.4}
\]

After the \(O(n)\) endpoint correction, the off-diagonal spectrum is

\[
 S_n(c_n(\lambda))
 =
 8\sum_{d=1}^{n-1}
 \sum_{j=1}^{n-d-1}
 e^{-\eta_{n,\lambda}(d,x)}
 e^{i\phi_n(d,x)}
 +O(n).
\tag{2.5}
\]

The factor \(8\) is the product of the two symmetric orientations
\(j<k\), \(k<j\), and the interior jump product \(2\cdot2\).

The only joint stationary point of the two-dimensional phase is

\[
 d=0,\qquad x=0.
\tag{2.6}
\]

It lies at the boundary of the difference coordinate and at the
Chebyshev midpoint. This boundary-stationary collision is the origin of
the cubic cusp.

## 4. Uniform tail closure

This section records the estimates needed to pass from the compact
Riemann sum to (3.6). They are included to distinguish the theorem from
the preceding numerical scout.

### 4.1 Stationary row lemma

For \(1\le d\le\delta n\), with fixed sufficiently small
\(\delta>0\), the \(x\)-row has the uniform stationary-phase expansion

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 &\sum_j
 e^{-\eta_{n,\lambda}(d,x)}
 e^{i\phi_n(d,x)}
 \\
 &\quad=
 \sqrt{n\over\pi\sin(\pi d/(2n))}
 \exp\!\left[
 -{\lambda n^{1/3}\over2}\sin{\pi d\over2n}
 +i\left\{
 \pi d-2n\sin{\pi d\over2n}+{\pi\over4}
 \right\}\right]
 \\
 &\qquad\quad
 +O_\Lambda\!\left({n\over d^{3/2}}+1\right),
 \qquad 0\le\lambda\le\Lambda.
 \end{aligned}
\tag{4.1}
\]

To prove (4.1), split the row into
\(|x|\le n/3\) and its two complements. On the central interval,

\[
 \partial_x^2\phi_n(d,x)
 ={2\pi^2\over n}
 \cos{\pi x\over n}
 \sin{\pi d\over2n}
 \asymp {d\over n^2},
\tag{4.2}
\]

and the unique stationary point is \(x=0\). Standard one-dimensional
stationary phase, followed by one Euler summation step, gives the displayed
main term and error. On the complements,
\(|\partial_x\phi_n|\) is monotone and bounded below by a constant
multiple of \(d/n\); discrete first-derivative summation supplies the same
error. The damping in (2.4) is a smooth amplitude with uniformly controlled
variation for \(0\le\lambda\le\Lambda\).

In particular,

\[
 \left|\sum_j
 e^{-\eta_{n,\lambda}(d,x)}
 e^{i\phi_n(d,x)}\right|
 \ll_\Lambda {n\over\sqrt d}.
\tag{4.3}
\]

Thus differences \(d\le\varepsilon n^{2/3}\) contribute at most

\[
 \ll n\sum_{d\le\varepsilon n^{2/3}}d^{-1/2}
 \ll \sqrt\varepsilon\,n^{4/3}.
\tag{4.4}
\]

This closes the integrable \(u^{-1/2}\) endpoint in (3.6).

### 4.2 Difference tail for positive \(\lambda\)

For \(\lambda>0\), the main term in (4.1) contains

\[
 \exp\!\left(
 -{\lambda n^{1/3}\over2}\sin{\pi d\over2n}
 \right)
 \le
 \exp\!\left(-c_\delta\lambda d/n^{2/3}\right)
\tag{4.5}
\]

for \(d\le\delta n\). Hence the part
\(d\ge L n^{2/3}\) is
\(O_{\lambda,\delta}(e^{-cL}n^{4/3})+o(n^{4/3})\).

### 4.3 Oscillatory difference tail at \(\lambda=0\)

At zero damping, the row phase in (4.1) is

\[
 g_n(d)
 =\pi d-2n\sin{\pi d\over2n},
\tag{4.6}
\]

with

\[
 g_n'(d)
 =\pi\left(1-\cos{\pi d\over2n}\right)
 \asymp {d^2\over n^2},
 \qquad
 1\ll d\le\delta n.
\tag{4.7}
\]

It is monotone. On a dyadic block \(D\le d<2D\), Abel summation and the
first-derivative estimate give

\[
 \sum_{D\le d<2D}
 {n\over\sqrt d}e^{ig_n(d)}
 \ll {n^3\over D^{5/2}}.
\tag{4.8}
\]

The accumulated error from (4.1) is

\[
 \ll nD^{-1/2}+D.
\tag{4.9}
\]

Starting at \(D=L n^{2/3}\), (4.8)--(4.9), after division by
\(n^{4/3}\), give

\[
 O(L^{-5/2})+o(1).
\tag{4.10}
\]

This proves the ordinary oscillatory limit at \(\lambda=0\), not merely an
Abel limit.

### 4.4 Macroscopic differences

For \(d\ge\delta n\), return to the original \((j,k)\) phase. Its two
coordinate derivatives are

\[
 \partial_j\phi
 =\pi\left(\sin{\pi j\over n}-1\right),
 \qquad
 \partial_k\phi
 =\pi\left(1-\sin{\pi k\over n}\right).
\tag{4.11}
\]

They vanish simultaneously only at \(j=k=n/2\), which is excluded by
\(k-j\ge\delta n\). Partitioning the triangular domain into the four
monotonicity regions around \(n/2\), at least one derivative is uniformly
separated from zero. Discrete summation by parts in that coordinate yields

\[
 \sum_{d\ge\delta n}\sum_j e^{i\phi_n(d,x)}
 =O_\delta(n\log n)
 =o(n^{4/3}).
\tag{4.12}
\]

Positive damping only improves the estimate.

Equations (4.4), (4.5) or (4.10), and (4.12) close all tails and prove
(3.6). The estimates are uniform for \(\lambda\) in compact positive
intervals.
