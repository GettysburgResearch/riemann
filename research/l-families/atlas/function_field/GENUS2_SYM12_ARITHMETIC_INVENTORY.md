## 1. Where the new scalar enters

The source-locked quintic squarefree sieve is

\[
 S_5(f)=C_5(f)-(q-\ell(f))C_3(f)
 +\left({\ell(f)+1\choose2}+k(f)-q\ell(f)\right)C_1(f).       \tag{5}
\]

At degree twelve the completed polynomial has degree ten and

\[
 C_5(f)=Q_5(f)-(1+C_1(f)+C_2(f)+C_3(f)+C_4(f)).              \tag{6}
\]

Let \(G_j^{(12)}\), \(\Lambda_3^{(12)}\), and \(Q_1^{(12)}\) have the same
Euler and marking meanings as in the `Sym^10` packet. Since the degree-zero,
degree-one, and degree-two aggregate rows vanish, (5)--(6) give

\[
 \sum_Dr_D(12)
 =H_{12}-(q+1)G_3^{(12)}-G_4^{(12)}
   +\Lambda_3^{(12)}+Q_1^{(12)}.                             \tag{7}
\]

The coefficient of \(H_{12}\) is exactly one. Every other row in (7) is
evaluated below. In the polynomial ring generated by the locked inputs and
one symbol \(\widehat H_{12}\), the remaining unknown module therefore has
rank one. Supplying \(\widehat H_{12}\) closes the trace, and without an
additional relation the locked dependency graph has no way to eliminate it.

This is narrower than an arithmetic non-determination theorem. An unknown
identity could express (1) through familiar modular traces; the present
argument says only that no such identity is among the locked inputs or the
one-step formal coefficient equations.
