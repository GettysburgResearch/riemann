# Classification of integrated prime biases in reduced residue classes with independent ordinates

Assume GRH for the Dirichlet functions modulo \(q\), including RH for the zeta function, and no central zeros. For necessity assume also that the positive ordinates in the full family of primitive inducing functions are rationally linearly independent and unshared between functions. Pair each zero with the reflected zero of the conjugate character at the **negative** ordinate. This forced pairing introduces one real phase per positive ordinate. Multiplicities within a function can be counted in its coefficient. Then
\[
 \int_2^X(\pi(t;q,a)-\operatorname{li}(t)/\varphi(q))\,dt<0
 \quad\hbox{for all sufficiently large }X
\]
holds exactly for the square reduced residue classes at the moduli
\[
 1,2,3,4,5,6,8,10,12,15,20,24,30 .
\]
The indicated persistent signs use only GRH. The converse in this all-modulus classification uses the ordinate assumptions. Counting moduli at least two gives 17 pairs. Of the 63 entries printed in [Table 1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40993-025-00640-y/tables/1), sixteen are on this list and 47 are excluded under the stated assumptions. The additional pair is \((6,1)\), which already follows on GRH from \((3,1)\) since their prime classes coincide.

### Exact spectral threshold

With the \(\varphi(q)\log X/X^{3/2}\) normalization in the [coset argument](zhao-progression-cosets-nov18.md), the constant is \(-2r_a/3\), where \(r_a\) counts the square roots of \(a\) in the group of units. Indeed subtract all prime squares from the prime-power explicit formula; each square-root class contributes \(\sqrt x/\varphi(q)\) before integration. Induced local corrections and higher powers contribute \(o(1)\) after normalization. Character orthogonality gives a Fourier series with coefficients \(-\overline{\chi}(a)/[\rho(\rho+1)]\), summed over the zeros of the primitive character inducing \(\chi\), including the principal zeta zeros. The once-integrated pole term is removed by the logarithmic integral; the standard partial summation and absolute convergence are as in the [quadratic calculation](zhao-bias-kernel-nov18.md). The limiting supremum therefore equals
\[
 -2r_a/3+S_q,\qquad
 S_q=\sum_{\chi\bmod q}\sum_{\rho_{\chi^*}}\frac1{|\rho(\rho+1)|},
\]
by Kronecker approximation. If \(a\) is nonsquare, \(r_a=0\), so its bias changes signs. If \(a\) is square, \(r_a=r(q)\) is the size of the kernel of squaring on the units. Without independence the triangle inequality still gives \(\limsup\le -2r_a/3+S_q\), enough for strict negativity when the right side is negative.

### Uniform finite reduction

Write \(f_\chi\) for the conductor of the inducing primitive character and \(M_\chi(c)=\sum_\rho(c^2+\gamma^2)^{-1}\). As in the [quadratic norm criterion](zhao-bias-classification-nov18.md), \(S_q\) is the sum of the monotone integral averages of \(M_\chi(\sqrt{1/4+2\sin^2\theta})\), and is at least \(\sum M_\chi(3/2)\). For the complex characters take the real part of the completed log derivative, pairing conjugate characters. Their real linear Hadamard terms cancel under the functional equation, or equivalently apply the real paired product. For the principal zeta factor the pole removal contributes \(1/s+1/(s-1)=3/2\) to the completed log derivative at \(s=2\). As \(\Re(L'/L)(2,\chi)\ge -0.57\), the conductor-discriminant formula for the cyclotomic field gives
\[
 3S_q\ge \varphi(q)\left[\log(q/\pi)-\sum_{p\mid q}\frac{\log p}{p-1}
                           -\gamma_E-1.14\right]+3 .
\]
Here \(\prod_{\chi\bmod q} f_\chi=|\operatorname{disc}(\mathbb Q(\zeta_q))|
=q^{\varphi(q)}\prod_{p\mid q}p^{-\varphi(q)/(p-1)}\), also valid for \(q\equiv2\pmod4\). For \(q\ge2000\) the average log conductor exceeds \(\log 130\): bound the sum by \((\log q+3\log2+\log3)/4\), separating primes 2 and 3. Since \(r(q)\le\varphi(q)\), the displayed bound exceeds \(2r(q)\). Apply the same displayed test to all smaller integer moduli. Only 52 require norm evaluation, all at most 120.

For these 52, group the inducing primitive characters by conductor. Compute the derivative and value of their Dirichlet series from deflated Hurwitz zeta jets at positive real arguments, with the pole canceled by summing over the period. The zeta term uses its own pole-removed expression, with \(M_\zeta(1/2)=2+\gamma_E-\log(4\pi)\). Equally spaced lower and upper rectangle sums of the monotone resolvent rigorously separate every modulus from its threshold. The [Arb code](../../experiments/X-nov18-quadratic-bias/classify_progressions.py) and [directed intervals](../../experiments/X-nov18-quadratic-bias/progression-norms.tsv) give the displayed classification.

### Unconditional counterexamples at the class one

For each \(q\in\{11,21,22,28,36,40,42,48,60,84,120\}\), the class \(a=1\) has positive excursions arbitrarily late without any independence or zero assumption. These entries occur in [Zhao's Table 1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40993-025-00640-y/tables/1). On GRH, at logarithmic phase zero the coefficient sum before its minus sign equals \(\sum_\chi[M_\chi(1/2)-3M_\chi(3/2)]/2\) by the completed product and functional equation for the aggregate. It is strictly below \(-2r(q)/3\) for each of the displayed moduli, e.g. below \(-2.9848\) at \(q=11\) versus \(-4/3\). The [interval check](../../experiments/X-nov18-quadratic-bias/classify_progressions.py) and almost-periodicity prove the positive excursions in this case.

If an inducing function has an off-line zero, by the functional equations some function has one at \(\Re\rho>1/2\). Since \(a=1\), all multiplicities in the logarithm of the collective Euler product add. Its Mellin transform for the integrated count thus has a logarithmic singularity there, with the pole of zeta at one removed by the logarithmic integral. The [signed Landau argument](zhao-bias-unconditional-nov18.md) gives both signs beyond any fixed normalized amplitude: all nontrivial real zeros to the right of \(1/2\) are excluded at these small conductors by [Platt's rigorous verification](https://doi.org/10.1090/mcom/3077), and zeta has no real zero in that interval. This establishes the counterexamples for the actual prime counts. For class one at each of the thirteen moduli in the initial list, the identical off-critical argument gives a converse to the GRH sufficiency statement. Eventual strict negativity at that single class is therefore equivalent to RH for zeta and GRH for every primitive nonprincipal character inducing a character of that modulus. The cases of moduli one and two reduce to the usual zeta assertion.

### The class one at all large moduli with GRH alone

Uniformly over moduli \(q\to\infty\) satisfying GRH for their inducing functions (and RH),
\[
 \limsup_{X\to\infty}J_1(X)\ge(1/2-o(1))\varphi(q).
\]
The opposite limiting infimum is at most \(-2r(q)/3\), by the nonpositive contribution of central zeros to the Bohr mean. Central vanishing and shared ordinates are permitted in both statements.

In the zero-phase identity, each nonprincipal inducing function contributes the real part of \((L'/L)(1,\chi^*)-(L'/L)(2,\chi^*)\), plus \(-\log2\) for even parity and \(\log2-1\) for odd parity. This follows either from the completed logarithmic derivatives or the digamma difference in the [prime-tail calculation](zhao-bias-kernel-nov18.md). An equal number of characters have each parity for \(q>2\). Separating the principal zeta factor changes the sum by \(O(1)\) after accounting for its own fixed gamma and pole corrections. It remains to show that the mean of either arithmetic logarithmic derivative over the nonprincipal characters is \(o(1)\).

Let \(V=(\log q)^6\). For each nonprincipal primitive inducing function the weighted prime-power series for \(-L'/L(1)\) can be truncated at \(V\) with error \(O(V^{-1/2}\log^2(2qV))=o(1)\), by partial summation from the uniform GRH estimate for the twisted Chebyshev function. For \(-L'/L(2)\) absolute convergence gives a smaller error. Include the principal character in the finite sum, costing \(O(\log V/\varphi(q))\) in the normalized mean. At any prime power \(n=p^k\le V\) with \(p\nmid q\), orthogonality gives zero, since \(1<n<q\). At \(p\mid q\) write \(q=p^e q_0\) with \(p\nmid q_0\). Precisely the characters with trivial \(p^e\)-component have inducing conductor prime to \(p\). Thus the mean of \(\chi^*(p^k)\) over all characters is \(\mathbf1_{p^k\equiv1\pmod{q_0}}/\varphi(p^e)\). A nonzero contribution requires \(q_0<V\), and at most one prime divisor has this property for \(q>V^2\). Its normalized factor is at most \(2V/q\), giving \(O(V\log V/q)\) in all. Hence the aggregate zero-phase coefficient sum equals \(-\varphi(q)/2+o(\varphi(q))\). Almost-periodicity transfers this single phase to arbitrarily large positive times. Finally \(r(q)/\varphi(q)\to0\) by the elementary divisor bound. This proves the displayed lower bound without any ordinate independence.

### Every nonsquare class at verified small moduli

For **every** reduced nonsquare class at **every** modulus \(q\le400,000\), the integrated prime-count deviation has both signs arbitrarily far out, unconditionally. For arbitrary \(q\) the same assertion follows from GRH and central nonvanishing for the inducing functions, without any independence. This rules out individual nonsquare classes in the stated ranges, extending the [quadratic-coset statement](zhao-progression-cosets-nov18.md). The finite range covers 37,644,857,281 modulus–class pairs, counting a pair separately at each modulus. Indeed the total is \(\sum_{q\le400000}(\varphi(q)-\varphi(q)/r(q))\), where the two sums are 48,634,207,310 and 10,989,350,029. These integer counts follow by sieving each odd prime divisor into the factor two of \(r(q)\), with one further factor at \(4\mid q\) and one at \(8\mid q\).

To see that a zero survives at each class, assign the weight \(w_\chi=\overline{\chi}(a)\) to each primitive inducing function, combining the multiplicities additively at a common zero. These weighted multiplicities cannot all vanish. Apply the Weil explicit formula for their weighted sum to a smooth log-space bump \(g_\epsilon(v)=g((v-\log p)/\epsilon)\) of fixed height \(g(0)=1\) and bounded support, centered at any prime \(p\equiv a\pmod q\). For sufficiently small \(\epsilon\) the only prime-power atom on either signed support is the positive atom at \(\log p\). As \(p\nmid q\) its coefficient is \(-\varphi(q)\log p/\sqrt p\) by orthogonality. The conductor term vanishes because the bump avoids zero. The archimedean term tends to zero since its log-space density is smooth away from zero; the two zeta pole terms are \(O_{q,p}(\epsilon)\) by direct integration. If all weighted zero multiplicities vanished, the zero side would be zero for every \(\epsilon\), giving a contradiction.

Now if a weighted off-critical zero survives to the right of \(1/2\), the derivative of the Mellin transform of the prime series has a pole there, exactly as in the [Landau proof](zhao-bias-unconditional-nov18.md). Subtracting \(\operatorname{li}(x)/\varphi(q)\) removes the zeta singularity at one. If the inducing functions have no off-critical real zeros to the right of \(1/2\), the transform is regular on the real interval there, so both normalized signs are unbounded. At the stated finite moduli [Platt's verified rectangle](https://doi.org/10.1090/mcom/3077) excludes any such real zeros and verifies central nonvanishing. If all weighted right-half zeros cancel, so do their left-half reflections \(1-\bar\rho\) by the same symmetry within each primitive function. In the once-integrated explicit formula the remaining critical-line profile is absolutely convergent and nonzero by the preceding atom test. Its mean is zero: all central points are nonvanishing and the prime-square constant vanishes in a nonsquare class. A nonzero uniformly almost periodic real function of mean zero has recurrent values of both strict signs. The same proof applies directly in the GRH and central-nonvanishing case at general moduli. More generally, even without GRH, it suffices at that particular nonsquare class that the weighted multiplicity vanish at every real zero in \([1/2,1)\). The abscissa obstruction then has regular continuation along \((1/2,1]\), and any surviving critical-line profile has mean zero.

### Two further square classes modulo eleven

At \(q=11\), the classes \(a=3,9\) also have unconditional positive excursions, as do their odd lifts \(3,9\pmod {22}\). These are further entries of Zhao's Table 1. With \(X=3079,27701\), respectively, the resulting lower margins for \(\limsup J_a\) exceed \(0.36888,0.02505\).

Here is the arithmetic check. Put \(b_j(\chi)=-(L'/L)(j,\chi)\), and let \(B_j=\sum_{\chi\bmod 11}\bar\chi(a)b_j(\bar\chi^*)\), omitting the principal term at \(j=1\) and using zeta at \(j=2\). In the profile with logarithmic phase \(-\log X\), the quantity before subtracting the square constant, divided by \(\sqrt X\), is exactly
\[
 B_1-XB_2+1+\log X-\gamma_E-1/(2X)
 +\sum_{p^k\le X}\frac{(X-p^k)\log p}{p^{2k}}w_a(p^k).
\]
Here \(w_a(n)=10\mathbf1_{an\equiv1\pmod{11}}\) at powers coprime to 11 and \(w_a(11^k)=1\). Indeed the negative-time [prime-tail identity](zhao-bias-kernel-nov18.md) for a complex character uses its conjugate on positive prime powers, by the functional equation. The conductor term vanishes and the gamma terms summed with \(\bar\chi(a)\) vanish separately in each parity because \(a\ne\pm1\). For the zeta pole contributions use the same kernel damped by \(e^{-\epsilon(v-\log X)}\) on its support. The two pole values are \(\sqrt X/[\epsilon(1+\epsilon)]\) and \(1/[\sqrt X(1+\epsilon)(2+\epsilon)]\). Combining them with \(X^\epsilon[-\zeta'/\zeta(1+\epsilon)]=1/\epsilon+\log X-\gamma_E+O_X(\epsilon)\) supplies the indicated normalization in the limit. The [Arb certificate](../../experiments/X-nov18-quadratic-bias/check_eleven.py) checks the two margins by Hurwitz jets and finite prime-power sums.

By Platt there are no off-critical real zeros at these moduli. Noncanceled off-line zeros give both signs by Landau's theorem. If they cancel, the same reflected explicit formula applies to the nonzero remaining critical-line profile, giving the stated positive excursion. The class mean is strictly negative since the centers are nonvanishing. Primes in either specified class modulo 11 and its odd lift modulo 22 coincide beyond the prime 2.
