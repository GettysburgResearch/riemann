# Dihedral octic Galois fields in Zhao's spectral test

Assume GRH for the Dedekind zeta function of the field under consideration. **No degree-eight Galois extension of the rationals with dihedral Galois group satisfies \(B_L<2\)**, with \(B_L=\sum_\gamma(1/4+\gamma^2)^{-1}\), counting signs and multiplicities. Thirteen fields survive the discriminant bounds, all totally imaginary. Each is excluded by a strict prime-ideal inequality using rational primes at most 800. [Inventory with field polynomials and lower bounds](zhao-dihedral-octic-nov18.json) · [Replay script](../../scripts/check_zhao_dihedral_octic_nov18.py)

Use the [discriminant inequality](zhao-number-fields-nov18.md) with \(s=7/2\). At degree eight the totally real bound for \(|\Delta_L|\) is less than 270,000,000, and the totally imaginary bound is less than 74,000,000, as checked in 96-bit Arb arithmetic. Since the extension is Galois, these exhaust its signatures.

Take \(D_4\) to be the group of order eight acting on the square. For a reflection stabilizing a vertex, let \(C\) be its quartic fixed field and \(E\) the unique quadratic subfield of \(C\). The biquadratic field \(A\) fixed by the center has the three nontrivial characters of degree one. The degree-four permutation representation on the vertices decomposes as the trivial representation, the quadratic character of \(E\), and the unique two-dimensional irreducible representation \(V\). The regular representation is the sum of the four linear characters and two copies of \(V\). Artin formalism and the conductor-discriminant formula therefore give
\[
 \zeta_L=\zeta_A(\zeta_C/\zeta_E)^2,\qquad
 |\Delta_L|=|\Delta_A|\,(\Delta_C/\Delta_E)^2 .
\]
These identities can equivalently be obtained by completing the permutation zeta functions and comparing their functional equations. The square root of the quartic discriminant gives the sign character of this square action. A vertex stabilizer is generated by a transposition, so this character differs from the quadratic character of \(E\). Accordingly the three signed fundamental discriminants of \(A\) are \(e=\Delta_E\), \(b=\operatorname{coredisc}(\Delta_C)\), and \(h=\operatorname{coredisc}(eb)\). Here coredisc is the fundamental discriminant for a quadratic square class; it takes the value 1 on squares. Then \(|\Delta_A|=|ebh|\). The character of \(E\) is the primitive product of the other two, so \(|e|\le\operatorname{lcm}(|b|,|h|)\le|bh|\). Hence \(|\Delta_C|\le\sqrt{|\Delta_L|}<16432\). The field \(L\) is totally real precisely when all four roots defining \(C\) are real.

The exhaustive PARI/GP command \(\texttt{nflist("D4",[1,16432])}\) lists 719 isomorphism classes of quartic fields. Using their exact quadratic subfield and discriminant in the two octic signature bounds leaves 26. For each, the script constructs its splitting field, checks the octic discriminant against the formula, and computes a reduced defining polynomial. This yields 13 distinct discriminants. Each occurs twice with identical octic polynomial, as expected from the two reflection classes. Thus deduplication requires only equality of polynomials on repeated entries and inequality of discriminants on the other entries. [PARI number-field enumeration and splitting-field functions](https://pari.math.u-bordeaux.fr/dochtml/html-stable/General_number_fields.html)

For each surviving octic polynomial, prime ideal decompositions from PARI give the residue degree of every prime ideal over each rational prime in the chosen range, including the ramified primes. The [finite prime-ideal inequality](zhao-field-prime-test-nov18.md) with 96-bit Arb arithmetic gives the strict lower bounds in the inventory, rounded downward to thousandths. The smallest displayed bound is 2.007 for the field with discriminant 1,750,329. The spectral conclusion is conditional solely on GRH for the tested field. It classifies the strict sufficient criterion, leaving the prime-ideal sign question separate.
