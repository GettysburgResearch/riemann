# Classifying the completed logarithmic derivative without GRH

Let \(b_K=2(\log(s(s-1)\Lambda_K(s)))'|_{s=1}\). Positivity of the symmetrized Hadamard summands gives an unconditional first cut for testing \(b_K\le2\). The [whole-strip derivative bounds](zhao-number-fields-nov18.md) make the [combined discriminant caps](zhao-field-catalogue-bounds-nov18.md) valid for this test without GRH: both summands have their required bounds on the symmetrized full strip with nonnegative coefficients. All the real abscissas used in those combined caps exceed the golden ratio. The direct evaluations below retain all nonreal zeros regardless of their real parts.

**Additional rejection at small primes.** Under \(b_K\le2\), every off-critical quartet has \(|\gamma|\ge1\), since its positive contribution is
\[
 4\left(\frac{\beta}{\beta^2+\gamma^2}+\frac{1-\beta}{(1-\beta)^2+\gamma^2}\right)\ge \frac4{1+\gamma^2}.
\]
A real off-critical pair costs at least 8, a central zero costs 4. Write \(a=s-1/2\), \(d=|\beta-1/2|\). Up to an overall constant, the paired resolvent divided by \(a\) equals \(v/(v^2+4d^2\gamma^2)\) with \(v=a^2+\gamma^2-d^2\). This decreases with \(a\ge1/2\) when \(v\ge2d|\gamma|\), as holds for \(|\gamma|\ge1\) or \(d=0\). Consequently \(D_K(s)\le(s-1/2)b_K\) for every \(s>1\) under the tested hypothesis. Replacing each local factor by the fully split degree-\(n\) upper bound for \(-\zeta_K'/\zeta_K\), then giving back the known savings at primes through 127, bounds the permitted discriminant. At primes dividing the index of the defining order, leaving the entire prime unrestricted is safe. Else Dedekind's factorization gives the true residue degrees, also at ramified primes. The [rejection checker](../../scripts/check_zhao_analytic_candidates_nov18.py) uses four rational abscissas, rounding the savings down in units of \(10^{-8}\). It compares the residue against an upward rounded starting budget. A rejection is valid without any assertion about unverified zero locations.

**A fast sign test for the remaining fields.** With \(H\) as in the [Dedekind contour argument](zhao-field-catalogue-bounds-nov18.md), the sign of \(b_K-2\) is the sign of \(H'(1)-(37/36)H(1)\), since the residue at the pole of the uncompleted zeta function is positive. The [fixed-point Fourier checker](../../scripts/check_zhao_field_threshold_nov18.py) computes this latter expression. It first truncates the ideal Dirichlet coefficients on the line 8 and evaluates the **finite** sum on the line 2. Each finite summand moves between these lines by contour deformation without crossing any pole. This order of operations gives an efficient approximation with a small absolute error.

To see the truncation bound, the coefficients \(a_K(m)\) count integral ideals of norm \(m\) and are nonnegative. Thus \(\sum_{m>N}a_K(m)m^{-8}\le N^{-27/4}\zeta(5/4)^n\). Multiply by the integrated modulus of the remaining completed kernel on the line 8, using the same real gamma and Gaussian norms as in the companion. For the finite sum the trapezoid on the line 2 uses \(h=1/16\) and \(|t|\le64\). The analytic strip boundaries now have real parts \(3/2,5/2\); at those abscissas the finite Dirichlet sum is bounded by \(\zeta(\sigma)^n\). The factor \(|1/[w(w-1)]-37/36|\) is below 3 on all lines used. Cotangent quadrature and the elementary Gaussian tail therefore give the explicit strip and tail terms in the checker.

Here are details of the real-variable interpolation, including its numerical error. After the positive factor \(c^2\), where \(c=\sqrt{|\Delta|}/\pi^{n/2}\), the quadrature has the form
\[
 \sum_{m\le N}\frac{a_K(m)}{m^2} K\left(\frac{Mh}{2\pi}\log(m/c)\right),\qquad
 K(y)=\Re\sum_{j=0}^{1024} z_j e^{-2\pi ijy/M},\quad M=8192 .
\]
The complex coefficients \(z_j\) include the exact two-sided trapezoid weights and are evaluated by Arb. At every integer coordinate modulo \(M\) a ball DFT tabulates the first seven Taylor coefficients of \(K\): for term \(j\), multiply by \((-2\pi i j/M)^k/k!\) for orders \(0\le k\le6\). Each real tabular value is replaced by an integer in units \(2^{-84}\), checked within two units by a separate ball inequality.

The log coordinates for \(m\) and \(c\) are individually approximated within two units of \(2^{-44}\). Thus the exact argument differs from the evaluated argument by at most \(4\cdot2^{-44}\), costing that quantity times \(\sum |z_j|2\pi j/M\). The nearest integer to the evaluated argument has distance at most \(1/2\). The real Taylor remainder costs at most \(\sum |z_j|[(1/2+4\cdot2^{-44})2\pi j/M]^7/7!\), by the integral remainder at real arguments. Fixed-point Horner evaluation costs at most \(4\cdot7\cdot2^{-84}\), since each multiplication rounds down by less than one unit and the offset has absolute value at most one half. Finally \(1/m^2\) is rounded down in units \(2^{-108}\). Since \(\sum_{m\le N}a_K(m)\le N^2\zeta(2)^n\), this costs at most \(c^2 N^2\zeta(2)^n(1+\sum|z_j|)2^{-108}\); the unit allowance dominates the verified interpolation errors for the enumerated signatures. All fixed-point sums and shifts are integer operations. PARI supplies ideal coefficients from the maximal order and the checker validates the input field discriminant. Tightening the tail tolerance increases the coefficient cutoff; it does not alter the mathematical quantity. Only strict interval signs decide membership.
