# Why counting zeros outside the Chasse sector does not bound Jensen failures

For a real polynomial $F$ with $F(0)\ne0$, the reciprocal degree-$d$ Jensen polynomial of its exponential Taylor coefficients is $F(D)x^d$, where $D=d/dx$. If $F$ has only finitely many nonreal roots, say $k$ counted with multiplicity, and all its remaining roots are real, then $F(D)x^d$ has at most $k$ nonreal roots: apply the nonreal factors first to $x^d$ (at least $d-k$ zeros at zero remain), then the real factors. Each $D+a$, $a\ne0$ real, preserves or increases the real-root count including multiplicities. This follows by applying Rolle to $e^{ax}p(x)$ between consecutive real roots, retaining the multiplicities minus one, and gaining the extra root on the side where the exponential tends to zero. The same argument passes to genus-zero products by locally uniform convergence.

Replacing the real-rooted factors in this argument by all factors inside Chasse's degree-$d$ sector fails. Even a single sector-safe factor may increase the nonreal-root count. Take $d=4$ and
$$
 B(t)=1+3t/8+25t^2/192,\qquad G(t)=(t+1)^2+25/81 .
$$
Each has a single conjugate pair of zeros, and both have strictly positive coefficients. The pair of $B$ is outside the sector, whereas $G$ has angular half-width $\arctan(5/9)$ around the negative real axis, with $4\sin^2(\arctan(5/9))=100/106<1$. In particular its finite Walsh symbol
$$
 G(D)x^4=x^2\big((106/81)x^2+8x+12\big)
$$
is real-rooted, and the Walsh additive convolution theorem gives real-root preservation on degree at most four. Yet
$$
 B(D)x^4=x^2\big((x+3/4)^2+1\big)
$$
has two nonreal zeros and $648(GB)(D)x^4=848x^4+6456x^3+14933x^2+9882x+2025$ has four. Its Sturm chain, generated by $q,q',-\operatorname{rem}(q,q'),\ldots$, has signs $(+,-,+,+,+)$ at $-\infty$ and $(+,+,+,-,+)$ at $+\infty$. The two sign variations at each endpoint certify zero real roots. Thus a count of two offending symbol zeros permits four failed Jensen roots even with a single additional safe quadratic.

This counterexample leaves open estimates using additional information specific to $\xi$, such as regularity of the real-root spacings of the safe part or the arithmetic heat-wave structure. The generic sector theorem by itself only handles the case of zero offending pairs. [Griffin et al., Lemma 5.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.01227)
