# Extending the third-product criterion to number fields

Fix a number field \(K\). Write \(C_K=e^{\gamma_E}\operatorname{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_K(s)>0\), and set
\[
 T_K(X)=\int_2^X\left(\frac1{\log x}
   \prod_{N\mathfrak p\le x}(1-(N\mathfrak p)^{-1})^{-1}-C_K\right)dx .
\]
Each of the 2,580 fields in the [strict completed-derivative census](zhao-results-nov18.md), and each of the [twelve quaternionic fields outside that census](zhao-quaternion-bias-nov18.md), has the following proposed equivalence without a zero-envelope regularity assumption:
\[
 \mathrm{RH}(\zeta_K)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 T_K(X)>0\quad\text{for all sufficiently large }X .
\]
More generally the proof works for a fixed field with no nontrivial real zeros in \((1/2,1)\) and a strictly positive lower limiting normalized harmonic profile on its own RH. In the case \(\sup\Re\rho=1\) the sign change mechanism itself allows real zeros of fixed defect. All constants may depend on the field. The finite endpoint statement for the ordinary zeta function remains separate.

Put \(E_{2,K}=\sum_{N\mathfrak p\le x}1/N\mathfrak p-\log\log x-M_K\), \(J_K(X)=\int_2^X E_{2,K}\), and \(Q_K(X)=\int_2^X E_{2,K}^2\). The number-field Mertens theorem identifies the logarithmic constant in the Euler product. Keeping the tail of the convergent prime-power sum gives precisely
\[
 C_K^{-1}T_K(X)=J_K(X)+O_K(Q_K(X)+\log X).
\]
Indeed the omitted higher powers from prime ideals of norm greater than \(x\) are \(O_K(1/x)\), bounding the multiplicity at each integer by the degree and then summing the elementary integer tail. On GRH, partial summation of the prime ideal theorem with the usual square-root log-power error gives \(Q_K(X)\ll_K(1+\log X)^C\). The strictly positive margin for \(J_K(X)\) on the scale \(\sqrt X/\log X\) gives the claimed eventual product sign. If \(1/2<\Theta_K<1\), the explicit formula gives \(Q_K(X)\ll_{K,\epsilon} X^{2\Theta_K-1+\epsilon}\). Since the real segment is clear, the [prime sum Landau argument](zhao-field-sign-equivalence-nov18.md) supplies both linear excursions beyond every fixed multiple of \(X^c\), where \(\max(1/2,2\Theta_K-1)<c<\Theta_K\). Thus both product signs recur in this case too.

For clarity, the reduction at \(\Theta_K=1\) uses the field's own prime-ideal theorem and explicit formula. The following checks specify the inputs needed to carry over the [direct proof](zhao-native-oscillation-nov18.md).

* Define \(F_K(u)\) by the dummy line \(u/2\) and the infimum of \((1-\beta)u+\log(2+|\gamma|)\) over nontrivial zeros of \(\zeta_K\). Adding 2 allows small or real ordinates. Bounded-height zeros are ultimately inactive since their defects are bounded below. The fixed-field classical high-ordinate zero-free region gives \(F_K(u)\gg_K\sqrt u\); also \(F_K=o(u)\). The same concavity argument selects arbitrarily large exposed scales with \(du\ge0.45F_K(u)\). At the chosen ordinates \(\log(2+|\gamma|)=\log|\gamma|+o(1)\).
* The fixed-field estimate \(N_K(1-h,T)\ll_K T^{A_K h}\) for large \(T\) and \(0<h<1/4\) follows by specializing Thorner–Zaman's Theorem 3.2 to the trivial Hecke character (enlarging the fixed-field constant or the exponent if needed). The ordinary truncated Dedekind explicit formula at a half-integer argument, with an admissible height comparable to its square, has log-power remainder: the underlying prime-power coefficients are bounded by \([K:\mathbb Q]\log n\), and the local zero count is \(O_K(\log(2+|t|))\). Splitting the rightmost strip as in the rational proof therefore gives \(|\vartheta_K(e^v)-e^v|\ll_{K,\delta} e^v e^{-(1-\delta)F_K(v)}\). All fixed real zeros also enter either the left strip or finitely many harmless terms. The same partial summations then give \(Q_K(e^v)\ll e^v e^{-1.96F_K(v)}/v^2\). [Thorner and Zaman, Theorem 3.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.01750)
* Set \(A(v)=\Pi_K(e^v)-\mathrm{lg}(e^v)\), counting prime ideal powers with weight \(1/k\) and using the *ordinary* comparator \(\mathrm{lg}(x)=\sum_{2\le n\le x}1/\log n\). The derivative relation \(v\,dA=d(\psi_K(e^v)-\lfloor e^v\rfloor)\) is exact away from zero. With \(g(v)=\int_v^\infty A(w)e^{-w}dw\), the same double integration yields \(J_K(e^v)/e^v=-g(v)+O_K((1+v)e^{-v/2})\). In the Laplace ODE the arithmetic term becomes \(-\zeta'_K(1+z)/\zeta_K(1+z)\); the comparator is still \(1-\zeta(1+z)\), so their poles at zero cancel. Choose any sufficiently small rectangle about zero free of other singularities and choose the starting positive abscissa small enough in terms of its height and the Gaussian width. This gives an exponentially small unmodulated mean as before.
* In the modulated explicit formula the pole at one has residue one, and the trivial zeros have bounded field-dependent multiplicities, including an allowable contribution at zero. All such secondary terms on the compact window are exponentially small; the term with ordinate zero from a possible nontrivial real zero is treated in the ordinary zero sum. The Poisson log-derivative bound at \(1+r+i\gamma\) is \(O_K(1/r+\log(2+|\gamma|))\), using the absolutely convergent Euler series and Stirling. All local counts, tail bounds and Kolesnik–Straus estimates in the [Gaussian argument](zhao-native-oscillation-nov18.md) now apply with field-dependent constants. Excursions of both signs cost at most \(1.57F_K(u)\) in the linear mean at these exposed scales, strictly less than the \(1.96F_K(u)\) saving on the quadratic term. The elementary \(O_K(\log X)\) finite-product correction is negligible.

This proves the asserted equivalence for the product over prime ideals, independently of whether the zero abscissa is attained. It combines the oscillation argument for Zhao's first question and the field-specific positive margins in the third.
