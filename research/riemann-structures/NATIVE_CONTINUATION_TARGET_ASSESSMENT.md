## 2. The order of operations is now a mathematical constraint

These four distinctions are load-bearing, not optional notation:

1. **Primitive source versus completed coordinates.** The raw source uses
   labelled prime exponents. Completion changes the physical exponent of
   nonowner labels. A physical square insertion after completion is not
   automatically the image of the same raw-source gauge operation.
2. **Common output versus retained atom coordinates.** Two signed mechanisms
   may cancel in the common physical field while retaining a positive sum of
   squares if their provenance labels remain distinct atoms. PLC gives exact
   numerical values for this difference.
3. **Homotopy integration versus inherited measure.** A full derivative has
   zero off-sector endpoint integral in its native Lebesgue parameter.
   A changing carrier density contributes its derivative. A Duhamel weight
   from a different parameter cannot simply be inserted into that identity.
4. **Forward restriction versus inverse restriction.** Deleting entries
   preserves the absolute Schur bound. It does not commute with matrix
   inversion. RMI makes the resulting inverse loss quantitative in a legal
   balanced source interval, while a single unchanged narrow shell has a
   different, trivial odd-square compression.

These distinctions explain why apparently conflicting results coexist.
The selector-amplifying transport column is real, and its complete native
homotopy connection cancels it. The protected global inverse is subpower,
and the newly inverted ratio-masked matrix need not be. The marked
class-forgotten linear count can collapse while its class-retained Wick
square still detects a coupled phase.
