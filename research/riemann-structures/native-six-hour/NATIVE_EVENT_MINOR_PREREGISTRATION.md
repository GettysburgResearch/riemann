## Exact events and coverage

For fixed reduced ratio a/b the row changes only at H=g^2 ab, where
g is supported on2,3,5. Include every such potential event, even when
its source update vanishes. The update in coordinate c is exactly
C_(ga,gb,c)/g. No derivative factor2 or1/g weight is dropped.

Start with a freshly reconstructed exact matrix at H=900 and compare
all its selected entries and full36 row coordinates to the frozen
source. Verify its retained exact inverse in both orders. This supplies
the base rank20 certificate independently of modular luck.

The only subsequent jobs have intervals(900,2^10], then
(2^(k-1),2^k] for k=11,...,48. The upper power is a literal integer
in10..48. Each job enumerates the complete supported aliases through
its upper horizon and retains exactly those with event H in its
left-open, right-closed interval. Events are sorted by(H,row,g).
All updates at the same H are applied before any rank test.

The enclosing alias box has at most25*16*11=4400 candidates per ratio
at the final horizon. Across twenty rows there are at most88000 updates.
Every distinct event is a2,3,5-supported integer at most2^48, so the
looser complete box49*31*21 bounds their number by31899. These are
theoretical bounds, not measured counts or a promised runtime.

A selected minor is constant between its event horizons, even if other
unselected physical rows change. Certification after every complete
group therefore certifies the corresponding full integer interval.

## Two fixed fields and complete group certificates

Use only65521 and1000003, in that order. Each modulus receives a
deterministic primality certificate by trial division through its exact
integer square root, retaining all nonzero residues. No extra modulus
is selected after observing a failure.

The local half coefficient b_n is dyadic:
b_n=-Catalan_(n-1)/2^(2n-1) for n>=1. Hence source products are dyadic,
and division by supported g adds only denominator primes2,3,5.
Both declared primes are eligible. Nonetheless check the exact
denominator of every retained rational entry before reduction and
retain its nonzero residue in each field.

Keep both complete20-by20 modular states, even when only the primary
field is needed to certify rank. At each event group, perform a fresh
modular elimination on the updated primary state. Retain all pivot
choices, swaps, elimination multipliers, final echelon matrix and
determinant. If its rank is below20, do the same in the second field
and retain both attempts. A verified nonzero determinant in either
prime proves rational rank20.

Intermediate single-row states at a simultaneous event are not actual
horizon observations. They are not tested as horizon states. In
particular no unguarded Sherman--Morrison step is used: an artificial
singular intermediate matrix could disappear after the other rows
at that same H have been updated.

If neither modulus certifies a group, record UNKNOWN_MODULAR_RANK.
This is not a rational-rank counterexample. Continue the complete panel
and retain all later certificates, but do not fill the resulting
uncertified integer intervals. Source, denominator, type, bit-cap or
resource failures are separate acceptance refusals.

## Retained source and witness data

For each update retain its row, ratio, g, actual source labels ga,gb,
complete sparse36-coordinate source curvature, exact selected
twenty-coordinate update vector, both denominator-residue vectors and
both modular update vectors. Retain every grouped horizon, both states
at panel boundaries and every group rank witness.

Each panel retains its complete event-address census, source and owned
file bindings, exact predecessor identity, and all certified closed
integer subintervals. Adjacent certified intervals may be merged only
when no integer is omitted. A panel may complete with some mathematical
UNKNOWNs; execution acceptance and rank coverage remain separate.

The base and every panel have a complete canonical JSON proof digest.
Boolean/integer/float aliases, duplicate JSON keys and noncanonical
rational strings are rejected.

## Frozen predecessor and final composition

Freeze this campaign's code, declaration, replay note and tests with its
base calibration before the first target panel. Every panel requires
the exact commit of the immediately preceding VERIFIED frozen result,
with the same owned bytes and source identities. Root owns acceptance
and freezing of these predecessor proof inputs.

The job authenticates that predecessor and takes its endpoint states
as already verified inputs. It does not claim to freshly reconstruct
or rerun all earlier panels. Its own interval's complete source updates
and witnesses are freshly calculated and, on check, freshly compared.
An interrupted job is not a completed panel or an accepted predecessor.

The final collector authenticates the ordered base/panel chain,
owned-code identity, predecessor links, complete source-derived event
censuses, contiguous dyadic intervals, endpoint-state equalities, and
the reported certified intervals. It composes previously verified
frozen proofs; it does not claim an unexecuted monolithic source or
modular-witness rerun. Its output states this distinction explicitly.
A partial consecutive chain is a valid bounded result.

The collector is itself streamed. Its first pass authenticates and
discards every component's bytes, retaining only immutable identities.
Only after all components have been authenticated does a second pass
reauthenticate and parse one component at a time. Between iterations it
retains only the compact predecessor endpoint/hash/coverage fields and
the accumulated interval summary, never all raw or decoded panels.

Root may freeze each accepted panel independently. There is no initial
claim that every panel takes less than ten seconds; worker timing and
the existing reserve can stop a job, with that refusal retained.
The initial implementation uses whole dyadic panels, without introducing
a second within-panel checkpoint contract.
