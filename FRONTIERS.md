# Frontiers and open problems

**Status date:** 2026-08-02  
**Global status:** RH remains unsolved.

This page lists load-bearing problems, not every active issue. A useful contribution should remove one of these burdens or make its exact failure visible.

## Priority 1 — one canonical explicit-formula source contract

### Problem

Several Weil, carrier, screw, terminal-prime, and operator branches rely on overlapping versions of:

- the completed-\(\xi\) normalization;
- the Guinand–Weil explicit formula;
- Fourier-transform orientation and \(2\pi\) factors;
- pole and archimedean terms;
- admissible test-function or form-core domains;
- support and prime-power cutoff conventions.

The review wave verified substantial algebra under declared conventions, but the repository still lacks one concise source packet that reconciles the common interfaces and identifies which later claims actually discharge the old D-0001 blocker.

### What would count

A readable theorem packet that:

1. starts from a primary classical formula or gives a complete derivation;
2. fixes every sign, transform, zero coordinate, multiplicity convention, and domain;
3. proves admissibility for the finite test families actually used;
4. maps each reviewed branch’s notation into the canonical convention;
5. records exact source qualifications and disagreements;
6. receives exact-SHA review.

### What would not count

- another numerical agreement test;
- citing a recent dictionary without matching its normalization;
- a checker that only propagates supplied matrix intervals;
- silently choosing one branch’s sign convention.

### Good first contribution

Audit the smallest scalar and one finite matrix instance in two independent derivations, then write the general transport theorem.

## Priority 2 — a strict finite Riemann-data violation, or an honest finite exclusion

### Problem

The repository has many mathematically valid RH-necessary finite predicates:

- negative \(\Re(\xi'/\xi)\);
- negative derivative-free secants or divided differences;
- negative Pick or cross-Loewner forms;
- negative direct-\(\xi\) modulus shapes;
- negative Weil or screw forms;
- terminal-prime band violations;
- arithmetic Robin/Nicolas violations.

No integrated Riemann-data object has a strict negative proof endpoint.

### What would count

One frozen finite witness with:

- a reviewed necessity theorem;
- exact points/test coefficients;
- authenticated primitive balls or exact arithmetic;
- complete coverage;
- strict separation from zero;
- a compact independent checker;
- appropriate independent reproduction.

### What would not count

- a negative midpoint;
- an interval touching zero;
- a surrogate/model score;
- a workflow trigger;
- a local zero census without a locality theorem;
- a truncated prime sum;
- a large coefficient condition number ignored in the error budget.

### Good first contribution

Build the smallest two-backend value-only \(\xi'/\xi\) secant or \(2\times2\) Loewner control using the exact positive boxes as regressions.

## Priority 3 — the infinite Robin tail

### Problem

The integrated Robin packet proves a finite barrier and a complete reduction to consecutive-prime nonincreasing exponent vectors. It also gives an exact finite shared-budget tail envelope. The canonical class remains infinite.

### What would count

Any of:

- one exact Robin violation;
- a theorem bounding support and exponents in every possible violation;
- a proof-producing argument that prunes every infinite branch;
- an asymptotic inequality strong enough to cover all remaining canonical integers;
- a new complete arithmetic equivalent with a tractable global tail.

### What would not count

- extending the finite endpoint alone;
- scanning only colossally abundant transitions without a completeness theorem;
- a hash of an unavailable traversal;
- independent exponent maxima that overspend the shared size budget.

### Good first contribution

Prove a support-dependent asymptotic ceiling that interfaces with the exact powered dynamic program.

## Priority 4 — square-screw and terminal-prime cofinal sign

### Problem

Reviewed branches reduce RH to one-sided growth or boundedness of explicit scalar prime/screw statistics. Finite positive ranges and small directed cells do not decide eventual behavior.

### What would count

- a proof of eventual nonnegativity for the reviewed square-screw sequence;
- a proof that its negative part is subpolynomial in the exact required exponent;
- a uniform boundedness theorem for a reviewed zero-free terminal-prime window;
- or a strict directed finite band violation with complete prime, selected-zero, trivial-zero, and high-zero terms.

### What would not count

- phase-blind PNT error bounds that discard the RH-bearing oscillatory term;
- finite ladders;
- FFT interpolation below the required moat;
- a hard-window computation substituted for a smooth target;
- a zero-tail estimate that does not cover every shell.

### Good first contribution

Independently replay one smallest complete terminal-prime cell with a direct window evaluator and proof-grade zero phases.

## Priority 5 — complete kernel/operator synthesis

### Problem

The reviewed operator stack has many valid finite lemmas. The missing statement is a cofinal corrected-kernel lower floor on a hierarchy proven complete in the relevant topology.

A representative burden is

\[
B_{K,j}-Z_{K,j}^*C_j^{-1}Z_{K,j}
\ge -\varepsilon_jG_{K,j},
\qquad
\varepsilon_j\to0.
\]

Under false RH, an off-line cardinal difference produces a persistent negative kernel direction. This means the final kernel estimate cannot be discharged by finite-dimensional bookkeeping alone.

### What would count

A theorem proving:

1. exact source/form-domain compatibility;
2. completeness of the packet hierarchy;
3. positivity of the complement;
4. complete capture of the selected-real-zero kernel;
5. a uniform residual/synthesis estimate;
6. a cofinal rate to zero;
7. transfer to global Weil positivity.

### What would not count

- a finite positive Schur block;
- fixed-rank tail convergence;
- selected-zero conditioning without complete low-index capture;
- a bare dimension comparison;
- a finite sequence of positive matrices;
- a principal-angle or density statement in the wrong norm.

### Good first contribution

Extract the smallest reviewed operator dependency spine, then attack the complete-kernel estimate with the off-line cardinal countermodel present from the start.

## Priority 6 — independent primitive and artifact assurance

### Problem

Many finite checkers are correct consumers of supplied JSON but do not authenticate the primitive files, source code, normalization, or coverage represented by that JSON. Some historical results depend on temporary Actions artifacts or missing bundles.

### What would count

- checked-in content-addressed primitives or durable independently retrievable objects;
- a producer-source binding;
- independent primitive recomputation;
- exact coverage replay;
- mutation tests for omitted cells, reused zero mass, endpoint semantics, and source mismatch;
- clear distinction among same-backend replay, independent algorithm, and independent backend.

### What would not count

- a 64-character digest string supplied by the same JSON;
- aggregate counts without the underlying stream;
- “verified: true” as evidence;
- a temporary artifact called immutable;
- two precisions from the same implementation called independent.

### Good first contribution

Choose one small reviewed finite result and reproduce it from primitive source through a separately structured checker.

## Priority 7 — source-qualified equivalences and literature repair

### Problem

Several imported RH equivalences or modern source claims are accurately labeled but not yet reconstructed at the exact normalization used by repository code. Historical overstatements include simplicity language, complex-center Li coefficients, and source-space density.

### What would count

- direct inspection of the primary theorem;
- exact statement with quantifiers and exceptional cases;
- normalization transport;
- proof extract sufficient for the repository use;
- explicit distinction between imported theorem and native proof.

### What would not count

- a later paper’s informal restatement when the original quantifier matters;
- changing a complex coefficient to its real part without a theorem;
- inferring density from an exact finite dictionary;
- citing publication as equivalent to repository verification.

### Good first contribution

Audit one imported theorem end to end and attach it to an integrated packet.

## Suggested entry points by skill

### Analytic number theory

- canonical explicit-formula source contract;
- square-screw growth theorem;
- terminal-prime Laplace/zero expansion;
- Robin or Nicolas asymptotic tail.

### Functional analysis or operator theory

- form-core and Mosco/density interfaces;
- complete kernel capture;
- cofinal Schur lower floor;
- Stieltjes/complete-Bernstein unification.

### Exact computation

- small two-backend \(\xi\) or prime-window proof cell;
- independent finite Pick-box replay;
- bounded canonical Robin certificate replay;
- provenance and coverage mutation tests.

### Formal methods

- finite exact theorem kernels;
- interval and exact-rational checkers;
- proof-object schemas that authenticate primitives;
- link and dependency validation for integrated packets.

### Research integration

- extract the reviewed operator spine without claim-ID collision;
- reconcile source conventions;
- preserve exact-SHA review boundaries while making the mathematics readable.

## Before opening a new problem

Check whether the proposed result crosses a real quantifier or source boundary. If it only adds another point to a finite positive ladder, state the limited value plainly and consider targeting one of the frontiers above instead.
