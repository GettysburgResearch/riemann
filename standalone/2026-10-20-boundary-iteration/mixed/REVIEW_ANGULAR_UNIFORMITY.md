Scope: the additional uniformity argument over the already reviewed
fixed-angular finite-profile and complete-dual proofs. This is an
analytic review, not a machine certification of those upstream
identities. No remaining gap was found in this additional argument.

The extraction matrices depend only on finitely many angular phases,
and their inverse norms are uniformly bounded. The geometric
ramified denominator is used on the reflected right line; it is
not expanded on the preliminary negative line. The paired gamma
ratios in (U4)--(U5) have fixed real shifts and cover both large
height and large angular order. For nonzero angular order the
smallest denominator argument on \(\Re s=-\eta\) has real
part \(1/3-\eta>0\).

The interpolation height exponent in (U9) is exactly

\[
 (2+4\eta)\frac{1+\eta-(1/2+\delta)}{1+2\eta}
                  =1-2\delta+2\eta.
\]

The Gaussian three-lines argument obtains uniform boundary constants
without assuming uniform bounds for an angular derivative of
increasing order. The finite row and angular suprema are therefore
finite before absorption. Reflection changes only the angular sign
and decreases the divisor row, so both cutoffs close.

The choice in (U13) makes the reflected contraction proportional to
\(C_0^{-4\delta}R^{-\delta^2+\eta}\). The original series has
angular powers \(2-4\delta\) and \(4/3-4\delta\), both within
the proposed normalization after allocating the sieve losses.
The nonconstant row powers are strictly below one. The derived
left-line height power is \(4-4p\), as stated in (U2).

The angular Fourier weighted sum for a bump of width \(\Delta\)
is \(O(\Delta^{-1-\epsilon})\). At balance the comparison
\(X^{3/4}\Delta^{-1}<X\Delta\) has precisely the claimed
threshold \(\Delta>X^{-1/8}\), with fixed power buffers.

The optional quadratic pair retains its three conductor terms.
Its zero angular mode additionally depends on
`../global/GUARDED_SPHERICAL_MEAN.md`; the present receipt does not
replace that separate constant-term proof. On its preliminary left
line the finitely many zero-order gamma arguments are finite for
\(0<\eta<1/6\), and introduce no changing height degree.
