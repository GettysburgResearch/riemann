## Constant terms and the inverse base

I read Liu's actual ordinary torus convention, local Fourier digits,
weighted norm-sheet identity, absolute Fourier majorant and Jacquet
exponents in Sections 4--5. The source's angular cancellation cannot be
quoted for the spherical vector. The new note instead restores both
constant terms, excludes the exponent two-thirds by the absolute growth
bounds, and transports the surviving term with its finite class cutoff.

The local unit phase used for the constant term follows from the torus
cocycle and compact invariance, not from evaluating a Whittaker identity
at zero. For an active prime it gives the digit-four restriction, with
modulus \(q^{1/2}(1-q^{-1})\). Rational torus transport scales the norm
sheet by \(q_r^2\), giving the resulting \(q_r^{-1/6}\) residue factor.
The shifted cutoff and Hilbert phase range over the actual finite class
group; an arbitrary row-dependent weight is not substituted.

An explicit digit-zero puncture kills the direct constant and multiplies
the reflected residue by \(1-q^{-1}\), without a torus shift. Subtraction
therefore gives the coefficientwise selected multiplier \(q^{-1}\).
The unmasked unit presentation is treated in the original bounded-row
terminal before completion. It is not assigned that selected law at a
possible negative Mellin pole. I checked this against the source bounded
row terminal and the divisor-bounded product mark in Ope26a.

The residue energy \(Z^{(2N+M)/3+\epsilon}\) has the displayed strict
reserve \(c/3\) under the original canonical margin. Nonconstant kernels
retain the allowed small-argument exponent \(a_0<1/3\), including the
source choice one-quarter. These are the changes needed for the inverse
terminal; the complete finite-depth recursion remains an imported input.

The principal inverse bound at eleven-twelfths follows by the source
sixth-power repetition count. Smooth dyadic continuation, rather than an
unproved implication about sharp partial sums, gives the holomorphic
reciprocal. No unitary zero-free premise is used to obtain this base.

## Guarded probe and the original target

I checked the adapted new-prime class decomposition and the exact
Poisson substitution in the guard. The source permits the new prime to
be chosen in a sufficiently deep fixed ray class. Reciprocity then makes
the old S-units local sixth powers there. The added unit line is a genuine
isotropic complement, so the allowed generator and cutoff conventions
are retained.

The reconstructed Gauss correction needs its own check after enlarging S.
The author added it during review: the good conductor-zero quadratic Weil
index is one on units, so the new unit line introduces no ramification
into that correction. It was not enough merely to say that the earlier
fixed character was unramified before changing the class construction.

The physical valuation minus one and inverse cubic twist combine with
the sextic row-unit correction to give digit three. Translation by a cube
unit preserves the actual cutoff and both Jacquet constants but reverses
the weight. The reflected Weyl identity includes the scalar factor
\(bI\); its action is trivial in this source convention.

The local Fourier support is exactly valuation minus one. After
\(t=t_0\ell_{q_sX}h/(sA)\ell_T^{-1}\), the two ideal twists cancel,
leaving only \(\lambda(h)^{-1}\) and the frequency restriction
\(v_*(h)=0\pmod6\). Thus the unique principal numerator class \(u=1\)
survives with nonzero Mellin factor. I checked uniqueness against Liu
Section 13.3, paragraph 12; there is no unexamined sum of principal
classes that might cancel the signal.

Both independent guard reviewers checked the sharper additive estimate,
including its principal exceptions, against the actual fixed-character
argument in Liu Section 13.2. The modification is not an arbitrary
coefficient insertion. All selected primes are outside the enlarged
fixed set, and the cancellation is coefficientwise in every compensated
term. The exterior Euler factors and the target \(L(s,\eta)\) are
therefore unchanged.

## Bootstrap, field scope, and limits

The bootstrap family is all actual unitary Hecke characters over the
fixed field, with the common norm component removed. It is closed under
the realized finite twists and whole-side conjugations. Its common
eleven-twelfths base supports the ordinary product estimates. The
character-dependent constants pose no supremum issue: a target is fixed
inside the strict scalar gap before its scale tends to infinity.

The initial comparison gives seven-eighths. The refined tangent argument
uses \(\kappa\in(2B_\flat-1,3/4]\), within the stated source range,
and keeps the source order of margins and small losses. The continuous
scalar certificates are imported. I did not independently certify their
entire continuum domain in this session.

For an arbitrary field, abelian base change to \(K(\zeta_{12})\) gives
a product with positive exponents. If the target is not finite order
times a common norm character, every factor is entire, so no pole can
hide a zero. Finite-order characters times norm twists are handled
directly by the inherited finite-order theorem. This exact dichotomy
also includes finite sign characters at real places; the author repaired
the earlier ambiguous phrase about nonzero balanced infinity type, and
I read the change before updating the two hashes above. The native
Eisenstein route is separately stated; no theorem
requiring twelfth roots of unity is applied directly to the Eisenstein
field.

The supported conclusion is a **proposed extension of the same
\(B_\flat\) to the full unitary family**, conditional on the named source
inputs and proposed fixed-type adapters. It is not a smaller numerical
bound for zeta, a proof of RH, or a claim that the whole upstream argument
has received independent certification here.
