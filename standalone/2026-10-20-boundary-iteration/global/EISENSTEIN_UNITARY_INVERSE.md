# All fixed unitary twists in the Eisenstein inverse moment

Status: proposed source-dependent analytic theorem, requiring independent
review. This adapts the full marked canonical induction, including its
initialization, in the pinned Ope26a source identified in
[EISENSTEIN_SPHERICAL_REFLECTION.md](EISENSTEIN_SPHERICAL_REFLECTION.md).
It imports that source's finite Gauss identities, masked Poisson theorem,

New input proved in this directory: spherical reflection with every

bound and selected-prime action. The inherited nonresonance restriction
is removed over the Eisenstein field.

## 1. Statement

Let \(E=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-3})\). Fix a unitary Hecke character \(\nu\)
and a finite set \(S\) containing its finite conductor, the primes above
\(6\), and the fixed moduli in Ope26a. Ideals are exterior to \(S\);
all Kummer powers retain their zero on nonunits, even at exponent zero.

For \(W\in C_c^\infty(0,\infty)\), put
\[
 M_u(D;W)=D^{-1/2}\sum_n\mu_E(n)\nu(n)\chi_n(u)^\varepsilon
                       W(q_n/D),\qquad \varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}.
 \tag{1.1}
\]
Both common orientations are permitted. Constants may depend on the
fixed character, field, \(S\), and test; no uniformity in growing angular
degree or fixed conductor is asserted.

**Proposed theorem.** The following statements hold under the identified
Ope26a inputs.

1. For every \(c,\epsilon>0\),
   \[
    \sum_{0<q_u\le CH}|M_u(D;W)|^2
      \ll H(HD)^\epsilon,\qquad
      H\ge\max\{2,D^{1+c}\}.                           \tag{1.2}
   \]
2. Let \(Q_u\) be a fixed number of the source's centrally normalized
   prime slots, with disjoint lists, bounded row-independent coefficients,
   total prime length \(z\), and the same character as (1.1). Then
   \[
    \sum_{0<q_u\le CZ^m}|M_u(Z^r;W)Q_u|^2
       \ll Z^{m+\epsilon}                              \tag{1.3}
   \]
   whenever \(r+2z<m\) and \(2r+8z<3m\) have fixed positive margins.
   All original zero masks, coprimality charges, surviving lists,
   finite test-seminorm orders, and polynomial norm-height costs remain.
3. For sixth-power-free rows \(u\) of size \(U\), and bounded \(r\ge0\),
   \[
    \sum_{q_u\asymp U}^{(6)}|M_u(U^r;W)|^2
       \ll U^{\max\{1,(1+5r)/6\}+\epsilon}.             \tag{1.4}
   \]
4. In particular
   \[
    L_E(s,\nu)\ne0\qquad(\Re s>11/12).                 \tag{1.5}
   \]
   A possible norm-shifted principal pole is allowed.

The new family in (1.5) includes the resonant characters
\(\alpha\xi N^{it}\) and \(\alpha^{-1}\xi N^{it}\), with arbitrary
finite-order \(\xi\). The conclusion concerns a fixed uniform
half-plane, with constants depending on each character; it is not RH.
No refined boundary below \(11/12\) is concluded in this note.

## 2. The finite character orbit does not require finite order

On primary ideals, every fixed unitary character over \(E\) has the form
\(\alpha^m\xi N^{it}\), with integer \(m\), a finite-order ray character
\(\xi\), and \(t\in\mathbb R\). One way to see this is to divide by the
angular primary-generator character and the norm character to remove
the entire infinity type. The resulting character factors through a
finite ray class group. The finite unit compatibility is carried by
\(\xi\), not discarded.

Absorb the fixed \(N^{it}\) into the test. Its seminorm costs are
polynomial. Work with \(\nu=\alpha^m\xi\).
At a canonical node, preserve the original coefficient
\[
 a_0(n)\nu(n),\qquad a_0(n)=\bar\alpha(n)\gamma_2(n).
 \tag{2.1}
\]
The possible fixed coefficients lie in
\[
 \{\alpha^{m}\theta,\alpha^{-m}\theta:\theta\in\Theta_0\},
 \tag{2.2}
\]
where \(\Theta_0\) is the finite group generated by the fixed finite
characters, Gauss corrections and their conjugates. This is a finite
list for fixed \(m\).

Here are the precise uses of the fixed coefficient in the source.

* In cube completion it gives \(\nu(b)^3\), and in any extraction
  \(n=Au\) it gives the separated product \(\nu(A)\nu(u)\).
* In the first Poisson step, the common gcd coefficient
  \(\nu(C)\overline{\nu(C)}\) cancels. The factors on the extracted
  \(\mathfrak A_i\) are outer coefficients. The residual \(\nu(u_i)\)
  remains on its respective column.
* The finite Fourier expansion at the source's
  eq:first-poisson-column concerns the Gauss/reciprocity correction
  \(G([u_1][u_2]^{-1})\mathcal R([u_1][u_2]^{-1},[P_T])\).
  For an angular coefficient expand only this finite correction.
  Its Fourier characters multiply the retained \(\nu(u_i)\).
  There is no need, and no attempt, to Fourier-expand \(\nu\) on the
  finite ray group.
* The second step's common \(\nu_i(v')\) again cancels between the two
  conjugate columns. Its finite correction is \(G([n_2][n_1]^{-1})\).
  The child coefficient is \(\nu_i\bar\vartheta\), precisely as at
  eq:inverse-moving-characters and eq:inverse-second-profile.
* A whole-polynomial conjugation reverses \(m\), giving (2.2).

These statements follow term by term from complete multiplicativity and
\(|\nu|=1\) on allowed ideals. Source punctures stay displayed moving
moduli; their primes are never added to the fixed finite group.
Thus every nonterminal coefficient is of the same admissible form.

## 3. All angular terminal reflections over \(E\)

The terminal theta angular degree for (2.1) is \(a=m-1\).
For \(a=0\), the spherical theorem in the companion file applies.
For \(a\ne0\), the needed higher angular reflection can be obtained
directly from the same classical theta function, without a new
automorphic input.

For \(r=|a|\), apply \(\partial_z^r\) or
\(\partial_{\bar z}^r\) to the finite translated theta sum according
to the sign of \(a\), and evaluate at \(z=0\). Its coefficient is a
constant times \(\mu^r\) or \(\bar\mu^r\).
Integrate against \(v^{2s+r-2}dv\).
The Bessel Mellin identity cancels the norm factor \(q_\mu^{r/2}\)
from that derivative. The coefficient Dirichlet series therefore has
exact angular factor \(\alpha(\mu)^a\) and norm factor \(q_\mu^{-s}\).
Its direct gamma factors are
\[
 \Gamma(s+r/2-1/6)\Gamma(s+r/2+1/6).
 \tag{3.1}
\]

To check the higher chain rule, use the exact coordinate map
\[
 (z,v)\longmapsto
 \left(-{\delta'\over c}
       -{\bar z\over c^2(v^2+z\bar z)},
       {v\over q_c(v^2+z\bar z)}\right).
\]
For pure \(\bar z\) derivatives at \(z=0\), the first coordinate is
linear in \(\bar z\), and the conjugate and vertical coordinates
are constant as functions of \(\bar z\) on the slice \(z=0\).
Hence the \(r\)-th pure chain rule has exactly the factor
\((-c^{-2}v^{-2})^r\), with no lower derivative terms.
The conjugate calculation handles pure \(z\) derivatives.
The reflected angular degree is \(-a\).
The constants at every cusp vanish after a positive-order derivative.

The gamma quotient of the transformed compact test is
\[
 \prod_{\pm}
 {\Gamma((r+1)/2+t\pm1/6)\over
  \Gamma((r+1)/2-t\pm1/6)}.                            \tag{3.2}
\]
It has no pole in \(\Re t>-1/3\). Stirling and the same finite-seminorm
argument give the identical admissible small-argument power \(1/4\).
Every finite-prime \(B_p\), natural zero, cusp sector, and selected-prime
phase is unchanged. The additional row phase and column phase are
separate unitary factors; they do not change the hybrid sieve.

This proves the terminal dual estimate for every integer \(m\).
Only \(m=1\) in the current orientation contributes the extra spherical
residue. Under whole conjugation this also covers the \(m=-1\) case
that was excluded in the inherited argument.

## 4. The residue at every marked canonical node

Use exactly the source's canonical energy, label weight and notation:
\[
 \mathcal E=Z^{-V}\sum_{f\ {\rm sf}}w(f)
   \sum_{0<q_k\ll Z^M}
  \left|Z^{-N/2}\sum_{n\ {\rm sf}}
   a_0(n)\nu(n)\rho(n)\chi_n(k)\chi_n(f)^4
                 \mathfrak d(n)W(q_n/Z^N)\right|^2.
 \tag{4.1}
\]
Here \(q_f\asymp Z^V\), \(F=N+V\),
\(\rho(n)=1_{(n,\mathfrak r_\rho)=1}\), and
\(Q=\log_Zq_{\mathfrak r_\rho}\). The two invariants are
\[
 F-M-Q-z_0\ge c_{\rm node},\qquad
 4F-3M-6z_0\ge c_{\rm node}>0.                         \tag{4.2}
\]

At the short-completion terminal, keep the actual \(h\) and completed
length \(X=Z^N/q_h^3\), as in eq:marked-completion-inversion.
The spherical companion proves that the completed residue has size
\[
 C_W X^{1/3}\mathfrak r(k,f),\qquad
 \sum_{q_k\ll Z^M}|\mathfrak r(k,f)|^2
       \ll Z^{M/3+\epsilon}q_f^\epsilon.               \tag{4.3}
\]
Reciprocity places its moving digit at
\(v_p(k)+4v_p(f)\pmod6\), up to one of finitely many fixed ray
corrections. Punctures contribute digit-zero primes, whose residue
factors are at most one. The squared weight has digits \(\{0,4\}\)
or its conjugate \(\{0,2\}\); the symmetric bound in (4.3) covers both.
The uniform label estimate is Lemma 1.1 of
[RESIDUE_INDUCTION_INTERFACE.md](RESIDUE_INDUCTION_INTERFACE.md).

Each surviving prime mark on the completed index has exact residue
weight \(1/q_p\); its fixed-ratio annular sum is \(O(1)\).
Assigned marks on \(h\) may be bounded by their divisor multiplicity.
The cube-completion weight is
\[
 q_h^{-1}X^{1/3}=Z^{N/3}q_h^{-2}.
 \tag{4.4}
\]
Thus the Hilbert-space triangle inequality sums all these \(h\)'s
absolutely, with only a small divisor loss. The label count
\(\sum_f w(f)\ll Z^{V+\epsilon}\) cancels \(Z^{-V}\). Consequently
\[
 \mathcal E_{\rm res}\ll Z^{(2N+M)/3+\epsilon}.
 \tag{4.5}
\]
The first invariant alone supplies the gap
\[
 F-(2N+M)/3
   ={N+3V-M\over3}
   \ge{2V+Q+z_0+c_{\rm node}\over3}
   \ge c_{\rm node}/3.                                \tag{4.6}
\]
The new terminal residue therefore has a fixed strict saving in every
state, including all selected states. It is not estimated only in the
initial family or only at \(f=1\).

The dual part retains the source's three terminal estimates
old-eq:4.9--old-eq:4.11. This follows from Section 3 and the identical
local table. Splitting the residue and dual square costs a fixed
factor. The source's finite-seminorm propagation lemma and bounded
induction depth absorb that factor in the constants.

## 5. Closing the induction and initialization

All nonterminal operations are the same masked Poisson identities.
Section 2 identifies their actual child coefficients; no modulus or
charge is removed. They retain the source's margins
\[
 F'-M'-Q'-z'_0\ge c_{\rm node}-7\eta,\quad
 4F'-3M'-6z'_0\ge c_{\rm node}-7\eta,
\]
the row decrease \(M'\le M-d\), and the step exponent cost
\(40\eta+\tau+\pi\). With the same bounded depth \(D\), choose these
parameters as in the source and also below the new residue margin
\(c_*/6\). This is possible because \(c_{\rm node}\ge c_*/2\).
Backward induction gives
\[
 \mathcal E\ll Z^{F+\epsilon}
 \tag{5.1}
\]
for every coefficient in (2.2).

Initialization also uses only complete multiplicativity.
For a selected tuple \(P\), set \(j=(n,P)\), \(n=jn_0\),
\(P=jP_0\), and \(c=n_0P_0\). The identities
\(\psi_u(n)\psi_u(P)=\psi_u(j)^2\psi_u(c)\) and
\(\mu(n_0)=\mu(c)\mu(P_0)\) hold for unitary \(\nu\).
The actual \(j\)-puncture and its original charge are retained.
With assigned length \(G\), the residual column length is
\(D'=r+z-2G\), the surviving cap is \(z_0=z-G\), and the outside
normalization \(Z^{-G}\) cancels the assigned tuple count.

The single initial Poisson step has fixed coefficient
\(\nu_*=\nu_0\bar\vartheta\) and canonical coefficient
\(a_0\nu_*\), as in the pinned eq:initial-formal-component. The finite
Fourier expansion again applies only to \(G([z_2][z_1]^{-1})\).
Its two margins are the source values

 m-r-2z+2G,\qquad 3m-2r-8z+10G+2P_1,

with the original small localization losses. Conditions in (1.3)

## 6. Sixth-power amplification and reciprocal continuation

For a sixth-power-free row \(u\) and any exterior ideal \(a\), the
exact source identity is
\[
 M_u(D;W)=\sum_{d\mid\operatorname{rad}a}
   \mu(d)\nu(d)\chi_d(u)^\varepsilon q_d^{-1/2}
   M_{ua^6}(D/q_d;W).                                 \tag{6.1}
\]
No finite-order condition on \(\nu\) occurs. At common primes, both
sides have the same natural zero. A scale Sobolev estimate from (1.2),
followed by averaging \(q_a\ll(H/U)^{1/6}\) and the injectivity of
\((u,a)\mapsto ua^6\), proves (1.4) exactly as in Ope26a.

Take \(u=1\), \(H=D^{1+c}\), and average \(a\) up to \(H^{1/6}\).
The scale version of (1.2) and (6.1) give
\[
 |M_1(D;W)|^2\ll D^{5(1+c)/6+\epsilon},
 \qquad
 \sum_n\mu_E(n)\nu(n)W(q_n/D)\ll D^{11/12+\epsilon}
 \tag{6.2}
\]
after choosing \(c\) small relative to the final \(\epsilon\).
A smooth dyadic partition produces a locally uniformly convergent sum
of entire Dirichlet polynomials on \(\Re s>11/12\), hence a holomorphic
function \(G\) there. This is a smooth dyadic continuation; ordinary
sharp-partial-sum convergence is not needed or asserted.
The partition of unity makes \(G=1/L_E^S(s,\nu)\) on \(\Re s>1\).
Meromorphic continuation and the identity theorem then give
\(L_E^S(s,\nu)G(s)=1\), excluding zeros in that half-plane.
Omitted unitary Euler factors are nonzero in \(\Re s>0\).
This proves (1.5).

## 7. The separate refined-boundary argument

The proposed inverse theorem fills a specific family gap in the latest
packet: the degree-one resonant Eisenstein characters now have the
same inverse and selected inverse estimates.

This inverse estimate alone does not give \(B_\flat\). The direct
compensated theta comparison has a separate spherical residue issue.
AUXILIARY_PRIME_RESIDUE_REMOVAL.md now removes that residue by a legal
fixed auxiliary-character choice, while retaining the original target
in the high scalar identity. EISENSTEIN_REFINED_BOUNDARY.md supplies the
ordinary angular product moment, native E numerator bridge, and scalar
closure at \(B_\flat\). Those additional arguments are separate from
the inverse theorem proved here.
