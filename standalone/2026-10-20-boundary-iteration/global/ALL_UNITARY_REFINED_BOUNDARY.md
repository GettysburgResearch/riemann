# The refined boundary for the full unitary Hecke family

Status: proposed source-dependent theorem, for independent review.
The new interfaces are proved in this subpacket; the complete upstream
analytic inductions and continuous scalar certificates remain imported
inputs. This is not a proof of RH and does not lower the numerical
boundary of the inherited nonresonant theorem.

Let \(K\) be any number field and \(\eta\) any unitary Hecke
character of \(K\). The proposed conclusion is
\[
 \boxed{\quad L_K(s,\eta)\ne0\qquad
             \Re s>B_\flat=0.874886647039964\ldots .\quad}
 \tag{1}
\]
Principal poles, including norm-translated poles, are allowed.
The boundary line is excluded. The advance over the preceding
packet is the inclusion of its formerly omitted resonant balanced
unitary types. Constants depend on the fixed field and infinity type;
no growing-degree or growing-type estimate is asserted.

The exact endpoint is the root in \((0.87488,0.87489)\) of
\[
 133488B^4-376596B^3+384534B^2-167541B+26231=0 .
 \tag{2}
\]

## 2. A single closed bootstrap family

Fix \(F\supset\mu_{12}\), and let \({\cal A}_F\) consist of all
unitary Hecke characters of \(F\), with their possible common norm
characters removed. Put
\[
 \beta_F=\sup\{\Re\rho:\rho\text{ is a nontrivial zero of }
                         L_F(s,\nu),\ \nu\in{\cal A}_F\}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Common norm twists translate only the imaginary coordinate.
The inverse theorem in ALL_UNITARY_BOUNDARY.md gives
\[
                         \beta_F\le11/12 .
 \tag{4}
\]
It uses no preceding zero-free assertion about \({\cal A}_F\).

Every subsequent ordinary product estimate with \(\kappa<1\)
uses the full-family premise
\[
                         \beta_F\le(1+\kappa)/2 .
 \tag{5}
\]
This includes arbitrary moving finite conductors and the realized
fixed balanced infinity types. A finite-order-only premise would
be insufficient for the new targets.

For each fixed target, its coefficient systems under both finite
Poisson steps, gcd extraction, and whole-side conjugation belong
to the fixed orbit generated by that infinity type, its negative,
and the finitely many fixed finite characters. The auxiliary
\(\psi\) of ALL_FIELD_AUXILIARY_GUARD.md is one additional such
fixed finite character. It does not increase any moving conductor
or original radical width. Finite Fourier expansion is applied to
the finite correction alone, while the infinity-type coefficient
remains on the entire column.

The fixed gamma factors in ordinary reflection have poles no closer
to the allowed small-norm strip than in the inherited proof, and
their vertical growth has fixed-type constants. The common-column
measure, natural zeros, and principal exceptional rows are retained.
Thus the source product estimates hold over \({\cal A}_F\) under
(5), with the original ranges \(13/18\le\kappa\le1\).
This is the fixed balanced-type proof transport already detailed in
the preceding packet and Section 3 of EISENSTEIN_REFINED_BOUNDARY.md;
the latter's field-specific theta formulas are not imported here.

## 3. The comparison remains about the original target

For every fixed target \(\eta\), the guard chooses one good
auxiliary prime and one cubic character. Its physical rows have
new-prime valuation \(-1\bmod6\), which combines with the cubic
theta twist and the exact sextic row-unit correction to give local
digit \(3\). Translation by a cube unit in the adapted finite
class complement annihilates both constant terms.

After Poisson, the two inserted ideal twists cancel coefficientwise.
The exact frequency family is
\[
  \{u:v_*(u)\equiv0\pmod6\},
 \quad\text{with coefficient }\lambda(u)^{-1},
 \tag{6}
\]
and every exterior Euler factor has the original target \(\eta\).
The principal frequency \(u=1\) survives with phase one and
nonzero local Mellin factor. The selected-prime compensation is
unchanged, because its primes are exterior to the fixed enlarged set.

Accordingly both sides of the scalar comparison refer to one
physical compensated probe. The low side uses (13.8), with the
same additive Gram estimate: its only new auxiliary coefficient is
a fixed S-supported unitary twist, which is permitted in the
common-support and principal-exception count in the proof of (13.7).
The high side uses (13.20), or its averaged version (14.20).
Restriction (6) and its unitary phases do not enlarge any of the
positive row bounds or alter any local contour. They retain the
principal term whose reciprocal is \(1/L_F(s,\eta)\).

## 4. From \(11/12\) through \(7/8\) to the endpoint

The initial source scalar comparison in Section 13.6 uses the
ordinary product moment under (5), together with the direct and
high estimates just retained. Its strict initial margin applies
from (4) and yields
\[
                          \beta_F\le7/8 .
 \tag{7}
\]
All auxiliary parameters are chosen in the source order: first the
strict scalar reserve, then contour margins and discretization,
then small losses and smoothing scales. Fixed local/type constants
are absorbed only after those positive margins have been chosen.

One may now use the tangent continuation in Liu Lemmas 14.8--14.10
directly from \(7/8\). If \(\beta_F>B_\flat\), set
\[
                     \kappa=2\beta_F-1\in(2B_\flat-1,3/4].
 \tag{8}
\]
The tangent certificates (14.3)--(14.10) cover this closed range at
the upper endpoint. Their strict comparison is exactly the one
used for the residue-averaged high bound (14.20).
The ordinary numerator average, disjoint prime-annulus partition,
actual local contour choices and retained dual lengths are those
of Section 14; they have not been replaced by a formal moment
bound outside its contour domain.

The strict gap gives the same contradiction to the definition of
\(\beta_F\) as the source continuation: if the supremum is not
attained, choose a zero with real part sufficiently close to it
inside the strict gap. The target is then fixed, so all its local
and infinity-type constants are fixed before the scale tends to
infinity. Hence
\[
                          \beta_F\le B_\flat .
 \tag{9}
\]
Equivalently, the source smoothed inverse estimates and Mellin
continuation give a holomorphic reciprocal on the open half-plane.
No passage from a finite ladder to its endpoint, or claim of
unsmoothed Dirichlet-series convergence, is required.

The continuous scalar certificates are imported source inputs.
The finite algebra checker in this subpacket is not an independent
certificate of those analytic inequalities.

## 5. Arbitrary number fields and possible poles

For an arbitrary number field \(K\), set \(F=K(\zeta_{12})\) and
\(\eta_F=\eta\circ N_{F/K}\). The extension \(F/K\) is finite
abelian, and the full local induction identity, including ramified
inertia-invariant Euler factors, gives
\[
 L_F(s,\eta_F)=
       \prod_{\tau\in\widehat{\operatorname{Gal}(F/K)}}
                                  L_K(s,\eta\tau).
 \tag{10}
\]
The setup of Liu Section 18 applies over \(F\), so (9) gives
nonvanishing of the left side.

If \(\eta\) is not a finite-order character times a common norm
character, every factor on the right of (10) is entire: a
finite-order twist cannot turn it into a pure norm character.
A zero of \(L_K(s,\eta)\) would therefore be a zero of the left
side, proving (1).

In the remaining case, \(\eta\) is a finite-order character times a
common norm character. The inherited
finite-order theorem at \(B_\flat\), followed by imaginary
translation, proves (1) directly, including the possible translated
principal pole. Thus the descent does not conceal zero-pole
cancellation.

## 6. What this result does and does not change

The preceding \(11/12\) proof remains the base of the bootstrap.
Its historical final section identified the missing direct-probe
interface; ALL_FIELD_AUXILIARY_GUARD.md supplies that interface
without changing the base proof.

The proposed result removes the resonant exception from the full
unitary family over all number fields. The numerical boundary is
the inherited \(B_\flat\), still strictly above \(1/2\).
It gives no proof of RH, no improvement of the Riemann zeta
boundary already covered by the source finite-order theorem, and
no uniform estimate for varying infinity type.
