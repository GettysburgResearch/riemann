## Local Rankin sums

I compared the four sums with the exact sixth-free local table and the
predecessor's fixed-divisor Cauchy/sieve expansion. Since the physical modulus
is `c=e^2 l c0`, the retained condition `Nl<<X` is valid in every local branch.
At a prime in h, every nonzero branch has local modulus exponent j>=2,
including the principal j=6 branches and the j=7 nonprincipal branch.

For S1, subtracting the Rankin exponent j/2 from its exponent `w+j-a`
gives `w+j/2-a<=-1`. For S2, subtracting j/4 gives
`w+5j/12-5a/6<=-1`. The stated S0 and S3 inequalities are equally correct.
An additional positive Rankin epsilon makes the prime contributions at most
a fixed multiple of `Np^(-1-2epsilon)`, so the Euler products converge.
Dropping H<=K only enlarges these positive sums. Thus their four costs are
`X^epsilon, X^(1/2+epsilon), X^(1/4+epsilon), X^epsilon`.

The exact inherited sixth-order sieve terms then give
`1+K X^-1/2+K^(5/6)X^-5/12+K^(1/3)X^-1/6`.
The last two are respectively the powers5/6 and1/3 of K/sqrt(X), proving
the claimed `1+K/sqrt(X)` multiplier. This uses the unchanged Möbius inverse
input, not a newly assumed stronger generic sieve.

## Completion on both sides

In L5, the four square-root terms at scale X=Y/b^6 have outside b weights
`b^-4, b^-5/2, b^-3/2`, and a constant times Y^-1/6 respectively.
The first three are summable; the last is summed only to `b<<Y^(1/6)`
because the original finite annulus is empty afterward. This produces the
plateau `D^(5/6)K`, with no missing infinite constant-weight tail.

For L6, the fourth finite-vector square-root term is
`D^(5/12)sqrt(H) X^-1/6`. Multiplication by the small-argument reflected
kernel `(X/C)^(1/6-epsilon)` cancels the critical X exponent up to epsilon.
The finite dyadic range 1<=X<=C therefore costs only a logarithm/subpower,
leaving `D^(5/12)sqrt(H) C^-1/6`. The other terms sum at the endpoint C.
Above C, rapid kernel decay controls every dyad.

Restoring all integral completion ideals gives the same three summable b
weights and the finite constant-weight range `b<=C^(1/6)`. Beyond it the
effective scale C/b^6 is subunit; choosing a fixed sufficiently large decay
order makes the remaining `b^-1-6A` sum converge. Its endpoint cost is at
most a constant times `(1+sqrt(H))(1+D^(5/12))`, absorbed by L6 for C>=1.
If C<1 every term is in that rapid-decay range. The common norm profile is
separated before fixing n or R, and the reflection retains the actual
numerator and the fixed finite local matrix. No sixth-power reduction of
the continuous numerator has been inserted.
