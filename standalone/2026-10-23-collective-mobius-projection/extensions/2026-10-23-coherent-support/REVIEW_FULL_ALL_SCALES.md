The small-scale argument is valid. For `U<=sqrt(DK)`, the coarse original finite-vector bound contributes at most `(DK)^(1/2)+D^(11/12)K^(1/12)+K^2+D^(5/6)K^(7/6)`. The full pole contributes at most `D^(5/6)K^(1/6)`. These are absorbed by the two terms of C1 for `K<=D^(1/7)`. Below the physical support threshold the finite term vanishes exactly; only the explicitly retained pole remains.

The weighted Euler-denominator argument is also valid. For fixed `0<delta<1`, the label-supported product of `(1-q^-delta)^-1` is subpower in the label norm, and the exchanged ideal sum with exponent `-2+delta` converges. Choose the input epsilon sufficiently small to absorb the additional `k^(6epsilon)` caused by scaling `U` to `U/k^6`. A general transformed primitive family must still satisfy the stated fixed-k hypothesis; the note correctly does not infer it.

No issue found. This strengthens the domain of the full-contour theorem, without proving a primitive or complementary-operator estimate.
