## How the questions changed the approach

The original question was exploratory, not a request to restate a known RH
criterion. Its pole/exponential mechanism suggested inverse geometry. The user
then pushed for an actual one-sided arithmetic estimate rather than attractive
spiral pictures. This led to a finite, arithmetically evaluable upper bound.

The user's next relaxations separated a minimal divergence requirement from an
unnecessarily strong uniform gain, and pushed computational complexity and
certification. The exact kernel calculation then invalidated the proposed
leading-power correlation shortcut. This was a substantive correction, not an
argument that the original question was uninteresting.

The next user proposal separated full spectral synthesis from constructing a
specific approximating family. The investigation chose the latter while keeping
the former as an open structural problem. The growth lemma tolerates much more
than vanishing error but needs control at all lengths.

The final pair of attacks sought arithmetic positivity or a rigid symmetry
rather than another asymptotic reformulation. Both exposed useful structure,
but neither supplied the source-dependent estimate. The last packet's exact
duals show that local coefficient signs are rigid, while the tail and one linear
mode carry what local matching cannot settle.
