# Source map, synthesis, and prior-art boundary

## Why this is the selected connection

The earlier research has several real achievements: complete-source
normalizations; valid local positivity; control of infinite arithmetic and
operator tails; and ways to force an individual hypothetical zero into an
arithmetic defect. It has repeatedly stopped at the all-scale sign or norm.
This pass does not extend a finite window or claim that another equivalent
endpoint is automatically easier. It asks whether our last requested
counting exponent was stronger than the analytic consumer actually needs.

The decisive link is between the *same* scalar's two descriptions:

    poles up to 2Theta-1 -> |D(m)|=O(m^(2Theta-1));
    sparse bad samples + that envelope -> negative-part convergence;
    real-axis regularity + Landau -> a strictly smaller pole boundary.

A fixed power saving in the count would iterate, or directly contradict the
supremum. It cannot stop at a partial zero-free strip. This is the mathematical
reason for replacing the previous square-root target by an arbitrary fixed
power saving. The count saving is still entirely unpaid.

The fixed-moment obstruction is respected: the proof does not ask harmless
critical-line oscillations to have sublinear raw moments. It also does not
misidentify the positive extension from the length-one theorem with the
actual kernel at larger distances. Local monotonicity and the positive
Euler counterfamily are not used to assert a global sign.

   of Oscillations of Error Terms*, arXiv:1512.03144v4 (26 July 2018),
   https://arxiv.org/abs/1512.03144v4 . Section 3 treats the positive/negative
   parts of a Mellin transform; section 3.3.2 explicitly uses a supremum of

## Corrected first open theorem

The following single assertion suffices, and is NOT proved:

    exists fixed delta>0,C>0 such that for every K>=2,
    #{2<=k<=K: B(k^2)<(135/2)k^8-240k^6} <= C K^(1-delta).

Its plausibility is not established by any finite sample count. A saving
in logarithms, a bound on a selected subsequence, or an assumed rightmost
zero is not substituted for the statement. The attainable-edge density
result in section 7 must remain conditional on edge attainment.
