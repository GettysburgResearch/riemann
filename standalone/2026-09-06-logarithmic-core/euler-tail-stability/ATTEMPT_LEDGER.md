# Direct attack and its stopping point

Goal: prove the native annular lower bound eventually at every integer m, not
merely certify a larger finite range or another equivalent statement.

1. Differentiate the literal finite sum and exploit the full prime-power
   Chebyshev bound before estimating it. Outcome: a scale-independent slope
   bound, and, with PNT, asymptotic flatness on every fixed additive m-window.
   Neither says the scalar cannot cross zero slowly.
2. Attempt to transfer a positive model through a vanishing relative error in
   prime coefficients. Outcome: an exact positive-kernel operator norm identifies
   the square-root precision threshold. A positive comparison with that accuracy
   would suffice, but no native source estimate at that precision was obtained.
3. Test whether genuine Euler products and positive Selberg hierarchies remove
   the parent's continuous-model obstruction. Outcome: they do not. A quotient
   of shifted tail zeta functions provides a meromorphic Euler counterfamily
   with positive ordinary coefficients, positive prime-power coefficients,
   all-order Selberg positivity, PNT, exact P2 and an arbitrary exact initial
   arithmetic interval. Its actual annular scalar has unbounded sign changes.
   The proof is analytic; no guessed parameters are labeled certified.
4. Check the surviving hypotheses. The counterfamily fails the Riemann
   functional equation at an explicitly identified reflected pair. It does not
   preserve the unit local Euler factor at large primes, or Lambda*1=log.
   It matches one safe moment, not all moments or a full analytic jet.

Surviving first open theorem:

    integral_(1/4)^4 w'(u)[psi(m^2u)-m^2u]du <= m/4
    at every sufficiently large integer m.

A second sufficient interface proved here is a uniformly positive comparison
with coefficient error o(Lambda(n)/sqrt n), or a suitably small critical
constant. That is a precisely quantified proposal, not a supplied comparison.

No unconditional native power saving, new sharp all-scale lower bound,
cofinal operator positivity, or RH proof is claimed. The inability of the
weaker bootstrap to distinguish the counterfamily is a scope-limited theorem,
not a declaration that the genuine arithmetic programme is impossible.
