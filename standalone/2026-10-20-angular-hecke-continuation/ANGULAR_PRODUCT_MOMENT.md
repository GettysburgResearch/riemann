# Product moments for a fixed pure-angular Hecke family

**Status:** Source-dependent analytic extension, submitted for independent review. This note proves the changes needed to extend the product-moment derivation in Baiying Liu, [arXiv:2610.26835v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26835v1), Section 12, to a family generated by one actual pure-angular Hecke character. It does not assume that the finite-order zero-free theorem applies to angular characters. The inverse base and the final compensated comparison are separate arguments.

The imported inputs are the source's finite Kummer/Gauss identities, orbit Poisson formula, common-profile estimates, and the explicitly derived scalar induction of Section 12, with its fixed-field extension in Part III. The changed Hecke and coefficient statements are proved below. No numerical induction inequality or moment length is strengthened.

## 1. The two character families and the precise theorem

Fix a number field `F` containing the twelfth roots of unity, with the source's allowed generators, fixed admissible set `S`, Kummer symbols, and zero conventions. Let `Omega` be an actual unitary Hecke character, unramified outside `S`, with pure angular complex components. In the same local-weight convention as the inverse note, write

\[
L_{\Omega,S}(z)=(z/|z|)^{a_v},\qquad a_v\in\mathbb Z,
\qquad L_{\Omega,S}=\prod_{v\in S}\Omega_v^{-1}.
\]

The corresponding idelic local component has exponent `-a_v`; only absolute angular degrees enter the gamma factors below. There is no radial norm exponent in `Omega`. Assume first that the vector `a` is nonzero; when it is zero, `Omega` has finite order and the original finite-group argument applies after enlarging that group.

Let `Theta_0` be a fixed finite group of finite-order Hecke characters unramified outside `S`, containing the source unit characters `Upsilon_S`, all initial finite coefficient characters, and their conjugates. Define the **fixed coefficient and exception group**

\[
\mathcal C_{\Omega,S}=\{\Omega^j\theta:j\in\mathbb Z,\ \theta\in\Theta_0\}.
\tag{1.1}
\]

This group can be infinite, but every member is unramified outside the fixed set `S`. Separately define the **global analytic family**

\[
\mathcal A_\Omega(F)=
\{\Omega^j\xi:j\in\mathbb Z,\ \xi\text{ any finite-order Hecke character of }F\},
\tag{1.2}
\]

and let `beta_Omega(F)` be the supremum of the real parts of its nontrivial zeros. The finite-order factor in (1.2) may ramify outside `S`. This distinction is essential: a moving Kummer row and a prime coefficient produce a member of (1.2), generally not of (1.1).

Fix, before the scale tends to infinity, a finite set `J` of initial angular exponents and a finite basis `B` of characters in (1.1), closed under conjugation. Prime coefficients are bounded scalar linear combinations of `B`. They are not arbitrary bounded arithmetic coefficients or arbitrary subsets of a prime annulus. Full exterior prime annuli, their original profiles, and every natural zero are retained.

The moving presentation has exactly the source form

\[
\psi_k(n)=\nu_{\rm fix}(n)
 \prod_j\chi_{a_j}(n)^{e_j}\chi_n(k),
\qquad
\nu_{\rm fix}=\Omega^h\theta,\quad h\in J,\ \theta\in\Theta_0.
\tag{1.3}
\]

Every displayed moving radical, including factors cancelled in the primitive character, is charged to the original width `q`; the row range is `Nk << Z^m` and `M=m+q`. A separate common coprimality radical of polynomial norm is treated by the original complete inclusion-exclusion, before a nonexceptional child or ordinary reflection. It cannot replace the charge for a cancelled moving factor.

Let `S_k(n_i)` denote the source's two centrally normalized smooth annular ordinary polynomials, and `Q_k` its product of full-annulus centrally normalized prime sums, with total prime length `z`. The common characters, exact two rectangles, and incoming norm modes have the source's scope.

### Theorem

The Section 12 product estimate extends to (1.3):

\[
\sum_{0<Nk\ll Z^m}
 |S_k(n_1)S_k(n_2)Q_k|^2\ll Z^{M+\varepsilon}.
\tag{1.4}
\]

For `z=0`, exclude only the principal primitive inducing character and allow arbitrary bounded nonnegative plain lengths. For `z>0`, exclude inducing characters in `C_{Omega,S}`, retain the original small mesh on each prime length, and require

\[
n_1+n_2+6\kappa z\le M,\qquad 13/18\le\kappa\le1.
\tag{1.5}
\]

When `kappa<1`, assume

\[
\beta_\Omega(F)\le(1+\kappa)/2.
\tag{1.6}
\]

For `kappa=1`, no zero-free input is needed. The original strict margins, boundary losses, seminorms, common height budgets, physical zero masks, and exclusions remain in force. The numerical mesh has the same independence from the subsequently fixed number of prime-sum factors as in source Lemma 12.15.

The constant and threshold can depend on the fixed `Omega`, `J`, `B`, `Theta_0`, field, source data, profiles, internal orders, and number of factors. There is no assertion uniform in unbounded angular degree. Uniformity in all the displayed moving Kummer factors and row conductors is retained.

## 2. Finite realized orbits inside an infinite exception group

The source's first transformed presentations are

\[
\tau_C=\tau\chi_\bullet(er)\xi_r,
\qquad
\tau_D=\tau\chi_\bullet(er)\bar\xi_r.
\tag{2.1}
\]

These extra factors have finite order. They remain displayed moving factors with their original radical charges. A whole-side conjugation changes the fixed coefficient to its conjugate. The frequency-unit operation multiplies both child columns by the same `xi_epsilon` in `Upsilon_S`; no independently twisted plain is created. Thus the realized fixed angular exponents lie in the finite set `J union (-J)` throughout the induction.

Because `a` is nonzero, the map `h -> h a` is injective. A transformed primitive inducing character has actual infinity type `h a` for one `h` in `J union (-J)`. If it belongs to (1.1), expressing it as `Omega^j theta` forces `j=h` by that infinity type; its remaining factor belongs to `Theta_0` by the definition of (1.1). Therefore every actually encountered exceptional character belongs to the finite set

\[
\mathcal E_J=
\{\Omega^{\pm h}\theta:h\in J,\ \theta\in\Theta_0\}.
\tag{2.2}
\]

The proof takes fixed-character maxima only over (2.2), not over all of the infinite group (1.1). This gives the necessary uniform constants for exceptional means and finite profile choices. The group (1.1) is used for its closure under multiplication by the prime-coefficient basis and the unit characters, not as a finite summation index.

More specifically, after the first Cauchy--Schwarz separation the two positive norms `N_C,N_D` are treated separately. Within either norm, source Lemma 12.8's second correlation produces two child polynomials with the same inducing character. Membership in (1.1) therefore agrees on those two sides. This step does not require the two different first-transform presentations in (2.1) to agree.

The fixed character is always evaluated on a **whole column product**. Raising a column product to the common norm mode does not raise its angular exponent by the number of factors. For example `Omega^h(n_1)Omega^h(n_2)=Omega^h(n_1n_2)`. The source common-coefficient invariant consequently passes `h`, not `2h`, to a child. Powers on extracted fixed primes and common divisors are outer unitary scalars.

## 3. Fixed-character means: the angular version of Lemmas 12.3--12.4

For every `vartheta` in (2.2), let its finite conductor and angular type be fixed. Let `R_*` be the complete common coprimality radical, with `NR_*<=Z^C`, and let `0<T<=Z^C`. For an annular smooth `W`,

\[
\sum_l\vartheta(l)1_{(l,R_*)=1}(Nl)^{it}W(Nl/T)
 =c_{\vartheta,R_*}T^{1+it}I_W(t)
  +O\bigl(Z^\varepsilon(1+|t|)^A p_A(W)\bigr),
\tag{3.1}
\]

where

\[
I_W(t)=\int_0^\infty W(y)y^{it}\,dy,
\qquad
c_{\vartheta,R_*}=
\mathop{\rm Res}_{s=1}L_F^S(s,\vartheta)
\prod_{p\mid R_*}(1-\vartheta(p)/Np).
\tag{3.2}
\]

The residue is zero unless `vartheta=1`.

**Proof.** For fixed positive `a,delta`, the deleted Euler product satisfies

\[
|E_{R_*}(s,\vartheta)|
\le\prod_{p\mid R_*}(1+(Np)^{-a})
\ll_{a,\delta,F}(NR_*)^\delta,
\qquad \Re s\ge a.
\tag{3.3}
\]

Only unit modulus is used. For sufficiently large prime norms each local factor is at most `(Np)^delta`; the remaining prime ideals form a fixed finite set.

The Hecke functions for the finite set (2.2) have meromorphic continuation and functional equations with gamma factors

\[
\prod_{v\mid\infty}\Gamma(s+|h a_v|/2),
\tag{3.4}
\]

up to their fixed conductor and exponential factors. They have polynomial vertical growth on fixed strips away from poles. A pure-angular character has a pole only when it is trivial, and then the pole is the usual simple pole at one. In particular there are no poles shifted by a fixed radial norm character; such characters are absent from this family.

Mellin inversion on `Re s=2` is exact:

\[
\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{(2)}
 L_F^S(s-it,\vartheta)E_{R_*}(s-it,\vartheta)
 \widehat W(s)T^s\,ds.
\]

Shift to a fixed `0<a<=min(1/4,epsilon/[8(C+1)])`, and choose `delta` equally small. The only possible crossed pole is `s=1+it`, with residue (3.2). The functional equation and (3.3), together with arbitrary decay of the annular Mellin transform, bound the remaining integral by

\[
T^a(NR_*)^\delta(1+|t|)^A p_A(W)
\ll Z^\varepsilon(1+|t|)^A p_A(W).
\]

The horizontal integrals vanish. For `T<1`, the inequality `T^a<=1` gives the same bound. All constants are simultaneous over the finite set (2.2), before `R_*`, `T`, and the row are selected. No zero-free hypothesis is used. This proves (3.1).

Now retain the same character, coprimality indicator, common `t`, and tests in the two source rectangles, with `T_1T_2=Y_1Y_2=P`. The leading product in (3.1) is `c_1c_2P^{1+it}` for both rectangles, so it cancels exactly. If all four scales are at least `Z^r`, the error is

\[
O\bigl(Z^\varepsilon(1+|t|)^A p_A(W_1,W_2)PZ^{-r}\bigr).
\tag{3.5}
\]

Indeed every linear cross term is at most a constant times one of `T_1,T_2,Y_1,Y_2`, each at most `PZ^{-r}`, and the product error is also absorbed because `PZ^{-r}>=1`. When no positive lower scale remains, retain raw absolute rectangle volume at the original scales, as in the source. For nontrivial angular characters the leading terms separately vanish; using the uniform cancellation bound (3.5) is sufficient.

## 4. Ordinary reflection: the angular version of Lemma 12.6

For a moving primitive inducing character `psi_k^*`, its angular type is one of the fixed vectors `h a`, up to sign. Put

\[
b_v=|h a_v|/2\ge0.
\]

The ordinary Hecke functional equation gives the source reflection identity with the transformed test replaced by

\[
\widehat W^{\sharp,h}(s)=
\widehat W(1-s)
\prod_{v\mid\infty}
\frac{\Gamma(s+b_v)}{\Gamma(1-s+b_v)}.
\tag{4.1}
\]

All fixed exponential factors are included in the same conductor scale and a fixed harmless normalization. The primitive root number has modulus one. The source conductor inequality becomes

\[
C_k N R_{0,k}\ll_{F,S,\Omega,J}Z^M,
\tag{4.2}
\]

because the angular coefficient has a fixed finite conductor at `S`, whereas the exterior moving Kummer conductor and every redundant radical retain the original charges. It is not legitimate to charge only the primitive conductor after cancellation.

For clarity, the source's exact natural-zero restoration has the same form

\[
S_k^0(X;W)=\varepsilon_k
\sum_{d\mid R_{0,k},\ \operatorname{rad}b\mid R_{0,k}}
\frac{\mu(d)\psi_k^*(d)\bar\psi_k^*(b)}{\sqrt{Nd\,Nb}}
S_{\bar\psi_k^0}\!\left(\frac{C_kNd}{XNb};W^{\sharp,h}\right).
\tag{4.3}
\]

The coefficient sum is bounded by an arbitrary small power of the polynomial radical. This is the same geometric Euler restoration; unit modulus, not finite order, controls it. Every natural nonunit zero remains in its original presentation.

**Kernel estimates.** The numerator gamma factors in (4.1) have poles only at `s=-b_v-n`, while reciprocal gamma factors introduce no poles. On the imaginary axis, only the places with `b_v=0` can contribute a pole at zero, of total order at most `r_F=[F:Q]/2`. Mellin inversion, shifting a little to the left of zero, therefore gives the small-argument bound

\[
W^{\sharp,h}(x)
\ll (1+|\log x|)^{r_F-1},\qquad 0<x\le1,
\tag{4.4}
\]

with a bounded interpretation when there is no pole at zero. Poles at negative real parts contribute positive powers of `x`, possibly times fixed logarithmic powers, and are no worse. On any fixed right line, Stirling and the annular Mellin decay permit arbitrary integrations, giving rapid decay as `x` tends to infinity. The same statements hold for all required Euler derivatives. Their seminorm and incoming norm-height dependence is polynomial, simultaneously over the finite angular list.

As in the source, pure norm modes are first absorbed into normalized test profiles, with their scalar scale phases removed. In the two-factor estimate this is done with its common parameter box. Formula (4.3), dyadic subdivision of (4.4), and the summable central masses `2^{-j/2}(1+j)^{r_F-1}` then reduce a factor of length `n` to factors of declared length at most `M-n+xi`, with an arbitrarily small discarded upper tail.

The row-dependent conductor and Euler-divisor scales are handled by the original single row-Hilbert-space Sobolev measure. For two factors one uses its two-dimensional version, including the mixed derivative, rather than multiplying separate rowwise suprema. Conjugating a reflected factor inside its own absolute value restores the original arithmetic character and conjugates its profile. The signed centered rectangle itself is not reflected. These operations preserve the source rank reduction and do not create different angular characters on its two ordinary factors.

## 5. Prime sums: the angular version of Lemma 12.7

Let the moving primitive inducing character be outside `C_{Omega,S}`. For a basis element `b_i=Omega^{ell_i}theta_i`, the primitive character inducing `psi_k b_i` is nonprincipal: otherwise `psi_k=b_i^{-1}` as complete Hecke characters, placing it in (1.1). The product belongs to the global family (1.2), with angular exponent `h+ell_i` or its corresponding conjugate. For the fixed input lists, only finitely many such exponents occur.

Under (1.6), choose `sigma_0=(1+kappa)/2+e`. On disks centered at `2+iv`, the Euler branch of `log L(s,psi_k b_i)` is analytic throughout a fixed buffered zero-free disk. The ordinary Hecke functional equation with the fixed finite angular list gives a convexity bound polynomial in the finite conductor and `1+|v|`. Borel--Caratheodory, then three circles from the smaller Euler disk, and Cauchy's estimate give

\[
|\log L(\sigma_0+iv,\psi_k b_i)|+
|L'/L(\sigma_0+iv,\psi_k b_i)|
\ll_{e,\epsilon'}
\{2+N\mathfrak f_{k,i}(3+|v|)^{[F:\mathbb Q]}\}^{\epsilon'}.
\tag{5.1}
\]

All dependence on the fixed angular degrees is in the constant and threshold. The natural deleted Euler factors have the same subpower bound on this fixed positive strip.

Mellin inversion of the logarithmic derivative gives a smooth prime-power sum of size

\[
P^{(1+\kappa)/2+e}Z^{\epsilon'}(1+|t|)^A.
\]

Higher prime powers contribute `O(P^{1/2+epsilon'})`, since their coefficients still have modulus at most one. The annular logarithmic factor is absorbed into the fixed smooth profile, and deleted primes contribute only their divisor-count cost with the original zero mask. After central normalization,

\[
|Q_{k,i}|^2\ll P_i^{\kappa+2e}Z^{\epsilon'}(1+|t|)^A.
\]

For the fixed list of factors, distribute subsidiary losses and multiply. The result is exactly the source estimate

\[
|Q_k|^2\ll Z^{\kappa z+\varepsilon}
\tag{5.2}
\]

with its finite profile and polynomial norm-height costs. For `kappa=1`, absolute prime counting proves the required bound directly. Full prime annuli and the specified finite basis remain essential.

Using only a finite-order zero-free hypothesis in this paragraph would be a gap. It is (1.6), for the global family (1.2), that justifies the contour. The theorem is useful without this issue at `kappa=1`; later applications with `kappa<1` require an independently justified preceding bound for (1.2).

## 6. Poisson, the common coefficient, and the sparse exceptions

The row character in the opened product square is a product of finite-order Kummer symbols. The angular factor is

\[
\nu_{\rm fix}(U)\overline{\nu_{\rm fix}(V)},
\]

which is independent of the summation row. Hence the original residue-class Poisson formula and all full prime-power Gauss correlations apply unchanged. Angular coefficients are not substituted for periodic row characters.

The whole-column common Fourier measure inserts `NU^{it}` on the full product `U`, with the same `t` on its two plains, all surviving prime factors, and both centered rectangles. The angular factor remains multiplicative on `U`; it is never converted to an unrelated row-dependent profile. All extractions, including valuations `1,6,7` at the amplifier prime, act on the whole product and divide the same two rectangle scales. Extracted powers of the fixed character are unitary outer scalars. Surviving prime coefficients retain their original basis components, up to whole-side conjugation; enlarge `B` by its conjugates at the outset.

The dual origin and diagonal-restoration identities also remain exact. On equal columns the angular factors multiply to one. On other principal configurations their modulus is at most one; the original full coefficient allocations, common supports, and signed restoration terms remain present. No new count of an angular character is introduced.

For the exceptional split, membership in (1.1) implies no ramification at any exterior prime. The source local condition is therefore unchanged. At an equal multiplicity-one common prime in the nonunit partition, the fixed frequency contains `p^2`. The earlier displayed moving support is coprime to this prime, and `Omega` and `Theta_0` are unramified there. Trivial exterior inertia consequently forces

\[
2+v_p(h')\equiv0\pmod6,\qquad v_p(h')\ge4.
\tag{6.1}
\]

At every exterior prime the required row valuation occupies one prescribed class modulo six. If consistent, the exterior ideal is one prescribed sixth-power-free ideal times a sixth power. The quotient of `S`-unit choices by sixth powers remains finite; the angular coefficient has not changed that quotient or supplied an additional row index. Thus, with the source's `f=2v_1` and actual row cap,

\[
\#\{h':h'\text{ exceptional}\}
\ll Z^{(m'_{\rm act}-2f)/6+\varepsilon}
\le Z^{(m'-f)/6+\delta_2/6+\varepsilon}.
\tag{6.2}
\]

The implied constant is finite by (2.2). It is not a count of all elements of the infinite group (1.1).

For each such row, reconstruct its exact presentation as one of the fixed characters (2.2), times its complete natural coprimality indicator. Applying (3.1)--(3.5) then performs the source exceptional centering at the original norm ranges. No angular character is discarded and no exceptional row is removed merely because its infinity type is nontrivial.

## 7. The unchanged normalized induction

We state the relevant numerical estimates to make the transport of the proof explicit. The source first transform uses full common product lengths `c,d`, common radical `p`, primitive common conductor `R`, and complementary divisor `E`. Its side allowance is

\[
N_C\ll Z^{A-c+\widetilde q+B_c-s_0+\varepsilon},
\qquad B_c=(3c-5d-R)_+/6,
\]

and its exchanged version. The local inequality making their outer exponent at most `M` depends only on the multiplicities and finite Kummer exponents, so is unchanged by Section 6.

After the one sixth-power Gauss amplification and second transform, the displayed child width and allowance remain

\[
M'=M+J-g-g_2+t_2\le M-\sigma,
\qquad
\Delta'=b_2-p_2+w+B_c+\ell\ge w+\ell.
\tag{7.1}
\]

Here each symbol has exactly its source Section 12.7 meaning. The coefficient set is enlarged only as described in Section 2; no support exponent is dropped. Actual row caps include the original small endpoint allowance before any positivity or child call.

The transformed excess over the prime-length condition is at most

\[
6(w+\ell)+\delta_1+2\theta_N.
\]

The removal of an initial segment of surviving prime factors, with one mesh overshoot, costs by (5.2) at most

\[
\kappa d_z\le\Delta'+\delta_1/6+\theta_N/3+\eta.
\tag{7.2}
\]

If all prime factors disappear, the already proved `z=0` assertion at smaller width is used. This is the same rank order as in the source.

For exceptions, the outer excess before centering is exactly the source's

\[
A-5M/6-F_1-F_2,
\qquad
F_1\ge2(c+w)/3-3\sigma-O(\xi),\quad
F_2\ge2b_2/3.
\]

If all four initial plain lengths are at least `L=M/4`, set `v=c+w+min(c_2,d_2)` and `r=(L-v)_+`. The fixed-character estimate (3.5), with the complete last-divisor allocation, saves the same `r` against the paired raw volume. Therefore the exceptional excess is at most

\[
A-5M/6-2v/3-(L-v)_++3\sigma+O(\xi)
\le(A-M)_++3\sigma+O(\xi),
\tag{7.3}
\]

since the supremum of `-2v/3-(L-v)_+` is `-2L/3=-M/6`. For nontrivial angular exceptions their two fixed-character means have no main term, but no extra saving is needed or claimed here.

Sections 3--6 establish exactly the analytic estimates needed in these formulas. Reflection (4.1)--(4.4) supplies the same arbitrary-length reduction and the same balanced comparison with shorter scale `M/4`. The inequalities `6kappa-1>=10/3` and `A_comp+(6kappa-1)z<=14M/15+xi` are numerical and unchanged. The source rank, augmented by its reflection phase, strictly decreases at every child call. Fixed lower widths use absolute volume and (5.2).

The positive-measure recurrence of source Lemma 12.12 now applies. Its seminorm weights have the same common Fourier integrability, since the new gamma kernels have the estimates of Section 4 and all realized angular types form a finite set. Increasing fixed derivative orders changes constants and height polynomials, not powers of `Z`. Choose the lower width, step, length margin, internal losses and mesh in the original order. This proves (1.4) on rows outside (1.1).

For `z=0`, add back any nonprincipal inducing rows in (1.1). Each is a member of the finite set (2.2), and its residue in (3.1) is zero. Each nonempty centrally normalized annular factor is therefore `O(Z^epsilon(1+|t|)^A p_A(W))`. There are at most `O(Z^m)` rows with the original finite unit multiplicity, so their contribution is `O(Z^{m+epsilon})<=O(Z^{M+epsilon})`. The principal inducing rows remain excluded. This proves the theorem as stated.

## 8. Scope of the new input

The product-moment adapter uses only that `Omega` is an actual pure-angular unitary Hecke character with fixed local data. It does not require the inverse theta condition on its infinity vector. That condition belongs to the separate inverse argument that supplies a preliminary bound for the global family (1.2).

In particular, the theorem applies with `Omega=Lambda^d` for fixed `d>=2`, and also with more general pure-angular characters. To use `kappa<1`, the preceding bound must hold for the **whole global family** (1.2), including the angular twists created by prime coefficients. No uniformity as the initial `Omega`'s finite conductor or angular weight grows is inferred from a finite realized orbit. The displayed moving row conductors retain the uniformity stated in Section 1.

The final zero-free boundary requires the theta average and continuation argument of Sections 13--14 with their actual angular vectors and principal Euler factor. This note supplies the previously unstated product moment for that argument; it does not replace those remaining verifications.
