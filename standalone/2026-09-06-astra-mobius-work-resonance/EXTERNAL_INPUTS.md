# External inputs and attribution

No external statement below is a proof of the remaining signed work bound.
Only source metadata/abstracts and official reference pages were consulted
on the web during this pass; a full PDF/literature novelty audit is not claimed.
No web-sourced code, tables or numerical zero ordinates are imported.

1. Standard zeta continuation, pole, real symmetry, functional equation and
   localization of nontrivial zeros. NIST DLMF, Chapter 25, especially 25.2,
   25.4 and 25.10. Used in the conditional implication to RH and in referring
   to actual critical-line zeros. The local arithmetic identities are also
   proved directly from the finite divisor convolution.
   https://dlmf.nist.gov/25.10

2. Existence of a critical-line zero, without simplicity. This is the
   classical Hardy theorem; a primary historical source is Hardy and
   Littlewood, "The zeros of Riemann's zeta-function on the critical line,"
   Mathematische Zeitschrift 10 (1921), 283-317, DOI 10.1007/BF01211614.
   Used ONLY to turn the per-zero resonance theorem into an unconditional
   cT (or c log N) lower bound. No claimed ordinate interval is needed.
   https://eudml.org/doc/167645

3. The classical Littlewood implication RH -> M(x)=O_epsilon(x^(1/2+epsilon)).
   Used ONLY for the converse direction of the growth criterion. The
   implication towards RH uses the native source and point evaluation.
   This premise is never asserted unconditionally.

4. Jean-Francois Burnol, "A lower bound in an approximation problem involving
   the zeros of the Riemann zeta function," arXiv:math/0103058 (2001).
   Attribution for the classical Hilbert-space approximation and
   zero/multiplicity lower-bound setting; its specific lower bound is not
   used as a black box in the finite-interval proof here.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0103058

5. Nathan Ng, "The distribution of the summatory function of the Mobius
   function," arXiv:math/0310381 (2003), and Shota Inoue, "Relations among
   Some Conjectures on the Mobius Function and the Riemann Zeta-Function,"
   arXiv:1705.00853 (2017). These place weak-Mertens growth, simplicity and
   reciprocal derivatives in context. Their conditional estimates are NOT
   imported as unconditional input bounds. The exact source-specific
   resonance theorem is proved in RESONANCE.md.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0310381
   https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.00853

6. Bettin, Conrey and Farmer, "An optimal choice of Dirichlet polynomials
   for the Nyman-Beurling criterion," arXiv:1211.5191 (2012).
   Attribution/context: quantitative near-optimal approximation can require
   hypotheses on reciprocal zeta derivatives in addition to RH. No such
   hypothesis is assumed here, and no optimal approximation rate is claimed.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.5191

Cauchy--Schwarz, causal Fubini on compact intervals, finite Gram projection,
finite-dimensional Lyapunov integration and elementary Fourier frequency
orthogonality are standard. Their exact applications, signs and constants
are all given in the manuscripts rather than hidden behind citations.
