## Answers to the ten research questions

1. **Conceptual transitions.** Riemann's analytic continuation, functional equation and explicit-formula viewpoint became rigorous entire-function/zero-free methods and the prime number theorem. Zero-density, mean-value and mollifier methods made partial progress quantitative. Hilbert-space and positivity criteria exposed equivalent obstructions. Finite-field geometry replaced analytic zero location by Frobenius weights under geometric hypotheses. Pair correlation, random matrices and quantum-chaos analogies organized statistics. Modern exact arithmetic and formalization make some proof components executable. These transitions have different source coverage; metadata inclusion is not a proof audit.

2. **Strategies nearest meaningful incremental progress.** This investigation supports concrete work on effective optimized approximation, arithmetic higher moments, sharper positive weighted density, and quantitative prefix control. None can be ranked reliably as closest to resolving RH. Each has a precise intermediate target and testable failure modes. The new formal density replay verifies an existing source bound rather than advancing that bound itself.

3. **Ideas worth reconsidering.** Integrated explicit formulas avoid invalid pointwise zero sums; phase cutoff cancellation avoids oscillatory infinite quadrature; fixed Laurent terms repair a compressed localization step; correct Hardy evaluations prevent transferring a pole cancellation to an entire function. These are reusable techniques with substantial antecedents, not claims that historical mathematicians overlooked them.

4. **Strongest connections established here.** Finite phase lower certificates match the adaptive upper theorem in the exact same norm; prefix costs become a determinate lattice moment problem; finite Euler-factor division and Hardy anchoring produce a sharp admissible-space classification; the disk operator is an exact arithmetic jump transport whose evaluation obstruction can be calculated. Each connection includes proofs, compatible hypotheses and its unresolved arithmetic step. Publication priority is not established.

5. **Repeated obstacles.** Finite information does not control an infinite positive measure without a tail condition. Fixed parameters do not imply joint uniformity. Conditional Parseval or purity cannot be imported to prove its own arithmetic premise. Closure does not control finite length. Pole cancellation changes the feasible target. Positive majorants can lose the cancellation needed by a signed theorem. Shared vocabulary is insufficient for theorem transfer.

6. **Credible source issues.** Four exact auxiliary assertions have explicit source-version counterexamples: two weighted-criterion endpoint/reverse assertions and two de Roton phase/Hardy assertions. Complete repairs and unaffected main results are recorded; this is not four false papers. Suzuki's signed-time problem already has an author erratum. Newer global zero-free claims retain an unresolved analytic transport ledger; scalar endpoint checks do not validate that transport. Other local observations remain pending rather than accusations.

7. **What was independently established.** The program has complete ordinary scoped proofs of finite cost formulas, moment recovery and its limits, phase certificates, character transfer/anchor controls and the operator-interface results listed above. Many results reconstruct sources or specialize classical theory. No proof/disproof of RH, unconditional simplicity theorem, new best bound or publication novelty was established. Prescribed Möbius convergence is stronger than arbitrary-coefficient existence. Generic Hardy kernels at nearby nonzeros do not annihilate the approximation space.
