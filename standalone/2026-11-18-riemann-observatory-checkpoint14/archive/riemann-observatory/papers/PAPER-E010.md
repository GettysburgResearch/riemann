Theorem3.4, internal pp16–17, proves for fixed α≤1 and a polynomial f with every zero outside the open unit disk that the optimal squared approximation distance is O_{α,f}(φ_α(N+1)⁻¹), where φ_α(x)=x^(1−α) for α<1 and log⁺x for α=1. If at least one zero is on the circle there is a matching lower bound with a positive f-dependent constant. In the Hardy case α=0 this is the familiar order1/N. This is an established predecessor of the observatory’s finite approximation exercises, not a new order-of-decay discovery.

Proposition3.2, internal pp12–15, constructs reciprocal-coefficient means for polynomials with simple zeros outside the disk, using bounded reciprocal coefficients and a ControlLemma. The paragraph immediately before Theorem3.4 explicitly notes that this proof does not extend directly to(1−z)² because its reciprocal coefficients are unbounded. The authors then overcome multiplicity by factoring f h=g^γ with g square-free and taking powers of approximants to g. Thus this is a stated limitation of one proof followed by a different valid construction, not an alleged gap in their theorem.

The direct scalar-window campaign uses a twisted finite-difference product rule to handle multiplicities without bounded full reciprocal coefficients and gives constants depending only on degree for roots on the unit circle. The paper inspected here gives f-dependent constants and does not, in those passages, assert that identical scalar-window construction or collision-uniform constants. No novelty follows from that observation: the prior summability literature remains to be investigated.

The paper uses equivalent integral norms when deriving particular optimal approximants in§2. Exact optimal coefficients depend on the norm convention. This record imports only the approximation **orders** and proof mechanisms, which survive equivalent norms; it does not copy those coefficient formulas into the observatory’s exact standard-H² results.
