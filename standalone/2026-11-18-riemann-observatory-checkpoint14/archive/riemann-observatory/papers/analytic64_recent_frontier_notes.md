## Complete pinned-edition readings

### Shengbo Zhao, two-sided oscillations of the iterated argument

Read arXiv:2611.19383v1, pp. 1–13, including the reference list. Theorem 1.1 assumes RH and supplies both signs for increments of the iterated argument S_n for fixed n≥1, any fixed 0<β<1 and shifts 0≤h≤1/log₂T. Corollary 1.2 yields both signs for S_n itself for n≥0 at the scale √(log T·log₃T)/(log₂T)^(n+1/2).

The kernel in (2.1) is positive and uses a phase depending on n. Proposition 2.3 applies the Chirre–Mahatab logarithmic convolution to the three frequencies and uses their integral representation for the iterates. The negatively centered Fourier support vanishes for positive integer frequencies. The phase of the surviving shifted band puts its real part into the desired signed main term for either parity. Section 2.2 recalls the sparse Bondarenko–Seip resonator and its Gaussian-weighted estimates. In §3 the unshifted band is supported at much smaller frequencies. The two omitted t-ranges are controlled by a pointwise resonator bound near the origin and Gaussian decay at the upper end. The specially chosen prime band has positive sine weights. Using the whole-line positivity lemma gives the lower bound in (3.10), which dominates the error in (3.9). The change from centered to forward increments uses h/2 and a smaller height parameter. Section 4 applies the increment estimate to S_(n+1), averaging S_n on the short interval. [Pinned PDF](../sources/analytic64_recent_frontier/two_sided_arg.pdf)

### Ramūnas Garunkštis and Julija Paliulionytė, shifted zero moments

Read arXiv:2512.03297v1, pp. 1–13. The finite sum in §1 pairs ζ(ρ+δ) with ζ(1−ρ+conjugate δ), so it becomes a sum of absolute squares under RH. Theorem 1 treats δ log T→0, where Taylor expansion at each zero and the Gonek derivative mean square supply the leading coefficient |δ|² T(log T)^4/(24π). Theorem 2 treats the larger complex shift range with an additive O(T^(1/2+ε)) term and a main term composed of zeta and logarithmic-derivative residues. Corollary 3 uses both regimes and the main-term expansion near zero to obtain the asymptotic.

Section 2 revisits the earlier unconditional contour bound. The moved line still permits growth of ζ(s), contributing the additional factor in its second error regime. Section 3 uses the Lindelöf consequence of RH and a Cauchy circle around each zero to bound the Taylor remainder. Section 4 uses the contour reduction (14), separates J₁ and J₂, and shifts the two integrals with different residue terms. Equations (19)–(20) retain the residue switches and the square-root error. Section 5 compares that additive error to the main term when the shift is at least of size T^(−1/4+ε), using the Taylor result for smaller shifts. The contour inputs quoted from the earlier paper were followed in this edition. The quoted original proof was not independently audited. ### Benjamin Durkan, Neea Palojärvi and Timothy S. Trudgian, fourth moment error

Read arXiv:2506.16766v2, pp. 1–14, including the October 2026 revision and its references. Theorems 1 and 2 reduce logarithmic exponents in the pointwise and mean-square bounds for the fourth-moment error E₂. Corollary 1 gives logarithmic improvements for moments of order 8 through 12, including the stated twelfth-moment bound. The revised introduction separately acknowledges the newer announced de Faveri–Pandey moment bounds.

Lemmas 1 and 2 compare the sharp interval with a Gaussian convolution. Lemma 2 retains boundary integrals of width Δ, which removes a logarithmic loss. Section 2 then applies the integrated spectral expansion, bounds its sum by the cubic Hecke-series moment, and balances Δ(log T)^4 against the spectral error with Δ on the scale T^(2/3)(log T)^(−2/3). Section 3 applies a localized spectral large sieve via duality. Lemma 4 Mellin-separates the Gaussian uniformly, splits the spectral blocks into four interleaved classes, and sums cross terms by decay in the difference of the spectral parameters. This limits the mean-square log loss to the sum of the diagonal dyadic bounds. The telescoping path in (21)–(25) uses the stronger local remainder from Ivić–Motohashi. To derive the moment corollary, the proof covers level sets of large ζ values with cells whose length depends on the level, absorbs the linear term by choosing the mesh constant small, and interpolates between the fourth and twelfth moments.
