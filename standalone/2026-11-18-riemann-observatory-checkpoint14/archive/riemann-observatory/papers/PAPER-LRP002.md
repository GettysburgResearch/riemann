For general frequency `lambda_n`, the means are `sum_(lambda_n<x) a_n exp(-lambda_n s)(1-lambda_n/x)^k`. Theorem4.1,p15, supplies the exact gamma/Perron integral; Remark4.2,p16, gives its complex kernel and mass. For `lambda_n=log n` these are first-kind logarithmic Riesz means.

Theorem5.1,p17, assumes **fixed** `k>ell>=0` and the uniform bound `sup_(Re s>0)|f(s)/(1+s)^ell|<infinity` from Eq22,p9, together with a Riesz germ. Its conclusion is almost-everywhere boundary Riesz convergence. The recalled classical Theorem1.2,p8, instead allows finitely many boundary poles under its stated large-height growth and pole-order assumptions.

These assumptions matter for RH applications. Under RH, `f(z)=1/zeta(1/2+z)` is holomorphic in the open right half-plane, but its boundary poles violate the uniform finite-order bound, and their infinite number violates the recalled finite-pole theorem. Thus the inspected statements do not directly imply the archive's moving-order, weighted zeta-product norm criterion. The kernel is prior machinery; the failed hypothesis is exact.
