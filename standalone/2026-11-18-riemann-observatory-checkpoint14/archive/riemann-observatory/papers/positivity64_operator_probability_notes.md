## Alexander, Baclawski and Rota, a discrete zeta process

Read the entire three-page publication, pp. 697–699, §§1–5. Theorem 1 identifies the hypotheses for Möbius inversion on a locally finite poset when the underlying upper-set series and the requisite double sum converge absolutely. Theorem 2 applies this in a homogeneous lattice of Dirichlet type. If the Möbius coefficient is bounded by a polynomial of degree $k$ in the order function and the unweighted Dirichlet sum converges on $\operatorname{Re}s>s_0$, its reciprocal Rédei series converges and the product identity holds on $\operatorname{Re}s>s_0+k$.

Theorem 3 uses normalized Haar measure on a profinite inverse limit of finite groups and a supremum of its quotient-identity events. Homogeneity relates the probability at any lattice element to the probability at the minimum. The theorem conditions on finite supremum and requires positive minimum probability. Corollary 1 couples the random variables by reusing the first $s$ coordinates of a single countably infinite product. In the divisibility-lattice case of Theorem 4, the inverse limit is the profinite integers. For each integer $s>1$, independent reductions at the primes give the probability of greatest common divisor one as $\prod_p(1-p^{-s})=\zeta(s)^{-1}$. Comparing this with the conditional formula proves finiteness almost surely and the unconditional law $n^{-s}/\zeta(s)$. At $s=1$ the paper remarks that the finite-divisor event has probability zero. This construction supplies the discrete precursor to Ehm's continuous-index coupling. [Inspected journal printing](https://ken.baclawski.org/pub/1993/01/public.pdf)

## Ehm, a continuous zeta process

Read the whole four-page note, pp. 279–282, §§1–3. In §1 the Euler factors give independent geometric random variables with parameters $p^{-\sigma}$ for $\sigma>1$. For each such $\sigma$, the sum is finite almost surely by Borel–Cantelli. The author's positive log-integer variable has the reflected characteristic function of $\zeta(\sigma+it)/\zeta(\sigma)$ because of the sign in the Fourier convention. Section 2 couples the geometric family with successive uniforms, stopping immediately before the first uniform exceeding the common threshold. It derives its transition probabilities, the independent but nonstationary increments, the characteristic quotient (4), and the distinct mean and variance time changes (6)–(7). In fact the logarithm of the increment quotient has nonnegative Poisson coefficients $(v^n-u^n)/n$ for $u<v$. The source's time-change obstruction concerns stationarity of increments.

Section 3 writes the centered zeta process as a backward martingale with independent decrements, mean of the uncentered process $-(\log\zeta)'(\sigma)$ and variance $(\log\zeta)''(\sigma)$. Theorem 3.1 uses the inverse variance clock $V(\sigma_T(u))=uT$. The reported Skorokhod limit of $\sqrt u+T^{-1/2}R(\sigma_T(u))$ has independent increments and exponential marginals of mean $\sqrt u$. Its increment from $u$ to $v$ is a mixture of an atom at zero and the exponential law of mean $\sqrt v$, with atom weight $\sqrt{u/v}$. In particular $(\sigma-1)\log N(\sigma)$ tends in law to a mean-one exponential. The note sketches the Brown–Eagleson argument with a truncated variance distribution and uses the prime number theorem for its single-jump part. Tightness is indicated through independence and a fluctuation estimate. Theorem 3.2 reports a distinct Brownian limit, for the centered total prime-factor count using its own variance clock. [Inspected journal printing](https://comptes-rendus.academie-sciences.fr/mathematique/item/10.1016/j.crma.2007.07.023.pdf)

## Burnol, Dirac and Schrödinger equations for the Fourier transform

Read the complete six-page journal note, pp. 919–924, the abridged English portion and French §§1–4. The note puts $u=\log a$ with $a>0$, truncates the cosine and sine Fourier transforms to $[-a,a]$ and relates the squares of the truncated operators to the even and odd Dirichlet kernels. Section 1 solves the cosine resolvent equations with boundary forcing $2\cos(2\pi ax)$ and differentiates in both the scale and spatial variables. Section 2 identifies the Dirac coefficient $\mu$ with the $u$-logarithmic derivative of the ratio of the two truncation determinants $\det(1+F_a^+)/\det(1-F_a^+)$. Theorem 2.1 gives $\mu^2=-\partial_u^2\log\det(1-(F_a^+)^2)$ and identifies the partner potentials $\mu^2\mp\mu'$ as second logarithmic derivatives of the individual determinants. Its proof uses the rank-one kernel derivative of the squared truncation.

Section 3 constructs Fourier-invariant and anti-invariant distributions with the two support gaps. Their completed right Mellin transforms are the Sonine structure functions. Theorem 3.1 reads the dilation operator as multiplication by the spectral coordinate, with $s=1/2+i\gamma$, and obtains the first-order Dirac system for the two structure functions in $u$. Equations (15) and (16) give the associated Schrödinger systems in the cosine and sine cases. Section 4 describes the isometric expansion, reflection at the confining end and the Jost solution at the other end. It identifies the indicated reflection coefficient with the corresponding gamma-factor ratio on the critical line, with the parity signs stated there. Some scattering assertions are presented with indications in this short note. The associated entire functions have zeros on the critical line by their Sonine structure. The article reports first-order zero-density agreement with zeta for these functions, which does not identify their zeros with those of the Riemann zeta function. [Inspected journal printing](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.1016/S1631-073X%2803%2900223-1.pdf)

## Nakamura and Suzuki, the central-zero extension

Read the whole six-page arXiv v1, §§1–3. Section 2.1 specifies the Lévy–Khintchine convention and its untruncated version when small jumps have finite first absolute variation. Section 2.2 specifies all five Selberg-class axioms and the completion, including the conjugate functional equation. Theorem 2.1 states a GRH equivalence via infinite divisibility. Theorem 2.2 states the triplet, with central multiplicity $m_0$ contributing the Gaussian variance and each nonzero ordinate $\gamma$ of multiplicity $m_\gamma$ contributing mass $m_\gamma/\gamma^2$ at $-\gamma$ under GRH. Section 2.4 constructs the law from its positive jump measure and then uses the conjugate Selberg function and one-sided transforms in the converse. The concluding examples use the sign of the functional equation for the normalized eigenforms of weights 18, 22 and 26 to force central vanishing. The claimed checks for other real zeros are described as numerical.

There is a literal issue in the inspected v1 formula for the intended central-zero case. The displayed (2.7) defines $B_F=i(\xi_F'/\xi_F)(1/2)$ although $\xi_F'/\xi_F$ has a pole at $1/2$ when $m_0>0$. The Hadamard form at the bottom of p. 4 indicates the regularized coefficient
$$
 B_F=i\left.\left(\frac{\xi_F'}{\xi_F}(s)-\frac{m_0}{s-1/2}\right)\right|_{s=1/2}
$$
with removable value. The functional equation implies that this regularized drift is real. The author’s formula for the Gaussian part follows from the transform of $-\tfrac12m_0t^2$ and the contribution of the pole. This is a reading qualification and proposed regularization, not a claim about another version. With real drift the law given by the triplet is a translated compound Poisson law when $m_0=0$, or its convolution with a centered Gaussian when $m_0>0$. A zero drift is an additional condition for an unshifted compound Poisson law. [Inspected arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.02027v1)
