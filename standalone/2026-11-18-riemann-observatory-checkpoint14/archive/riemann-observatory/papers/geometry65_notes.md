## Complete readings

**Deninger, “There is no ‘Weil-’cohomology theory with real coefficients for arithmetic curves,” arXiv:2204.02714v2, pp. 1–8.** Section 1 gives Serre's quaternion argument for a supersingular elliptic curve: a real realization of its two-dimensional first cohomology would represent the Hamilton quaternions in \(M_2(\mathbb R)\), which cannot happen. A grading with an antilinear structure squaring to \((-1)^i\) distinguishes this case from ordinary real descent. Section 2 proposes a derivation \(\theta\), an eigenvalue identification (2.3), top degree acted on by \(\theta=1\), and duality pairing \(\alpha\) with \(1-\alpha\) in (2.6). The stronger (2.7) gives an antilinear \(*\) commuting with \(\theta\), \(*^2=-1\), and \( \langle h,h'\rangle=\operatorname{tr}(h\cup *h')\) positive definite. The derivation rule then makes \(\theta-\frac12\) skew-symmetric and forces its eigenvalues to be imaginary. This is a conditional argument. Theorem 2.13 instead proves a negative result: choose a finite Galois field with a symplectic irreducible Artin representation of root number \(-1\), for example the cited octic quaternion fields. The sign makes its central vanishing order odd; by (2.12) the corresponding multiplicity would be odd. Complexification of a real representation always has even multiplicity for a quaternionic irreducible, giving a contradiction. On a real generalized \(\frac12\)-eigenspace complexification commutes with forming the eigenspace. This argument rules out a simultaneous functorial real theory with the Artin multiplicity axiom, and shows why the proposed complex or graded-quaternionic target remains relevant. The last pages' central vanishing conjecture and local-contribution suggestion are proposals. The quaternion field existence and Artin root number are cited inputs. The contradiction uses the specified test fields. [Primary manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.02714v2)

**Weil, “On Some Exponential Sums,” PNAS 34(5), pp. 204–207.** The note constructs an abelian ray-class character of \(k(t)\) from a character of local series at infinity trivial on monomials, multiplied by the value of a multiplicative character on a finite divisor disjoint from the modulus. The modulus has degree \(N+d\) from conductor \(T^N\) at infinity and the \(d\) geometric finite support points, with each exponent nontrivial for the chosen character. The cited degree theorem gives \(N+d-2\) reciprocal roots in the applicable nontrivial case. At degree one, monic polynomials \(t-x\) identify the linear coefficient with the mixed sum up to sign. Class field theory embeds the character's \(L\)-factor into the cover's zeta factorization. The function-field RH already supplies modulus \(\sqrt q\). In the odd-characteristic Kloosterman example the quadratic \(t^2-a\) and conductor \(T^2\) give the constant 2. The last paragraph extends the coefficient calculation via suitable characters at infinity for polynomial additive phases. This is a transfer *from* curve RH *to* bounds for local sums. An arithmetic counterpart needs the corresponding global factorization and all-zero trace, not square-root cancellation in local families alone. [Primary journal scan](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1079093/)

**Katz, “An Estimate for Character Sums,” JAMS 2(2), pp. 197–200.** Theorem 2 generalizes Theorem 1 from a degree-\(n\) field extension to a finite étale algebra of dimension \(n\). The Lang torsor of the unit group constructs the weight-zero rank-one sheaf. Norm pullback is compatible with extension of scalars and preserves nontriviality. Over a splitting field the regular element's coordinates are distinct, so one nontrivial character gives nontrivial inertia at its own point, with the other factors unramified there. Rank one then kills \(H_c^2\) by the coinvariant description. Openness kills \(H_c^0\), and tameness gives Euler characteristic \(1-n\), leaving \(n-1\) eigenvalues of weights at most one. If the character restricts nontrivially to the base units, the paper relaxes regularity and uses the smaller subalgebra generated by the element or ramification at infinity. The final question concerns non-étale algebras. Here the geometric proof supplies dimension and vanishing. It imports weight control expressly. [Author-hosted original journal scan](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/old/estcharsums.pdf)

**Katz, “A Note on Exponential Sums,” FFA 1, pp. 395–398.** For \(b\ne0,1\), Theorem 1 studies the Kummer character times the Artin–Schreier phase \(bx/(x^2+1)\). The rank-one finite-order sheaf is pure of weight zero, \(H_c^0=0\), and ramification at 0 kills \(H_c^2\). In odd characteristic the two simple poles each contribute Swan conductor one, giving \(-\chi_c=4\). In characteristic two, write \(b=\beta^2\). At the repeated pole, subtracting an Artin–Schreier coboundary reduces the phase to \((\beta^2-\beta)/(x+1)\), nonzero under the hypothesis. The open curve's Euler characteristic and one Swan conductor give \(-\chi_c=2\). Theorem 2 extends the estimate to the quadratic norm-one torus by splitting it over the quadratic extension. The trace becomes \(x+x^{-1}\), the induced character remains nontrivial by norm surjectivity, and the Euler characteristic and vanishing are geometric. The stronger weight-one purity remark uses nontrivial ramification at all missing points. The final question asks about higher degree. Conductor arithmetic and cohomology explain the different constants; the weight input is already finite-field RH. [Author-hosted original journal scan](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/old/noteexpsums.pdf)

**Katz, “The Work of Pierre Deligne,” Helsinki ICM, pp. 47–52.** This is a complete reading of an expository address. Sections I–II distinguish the consequences of the étale trace formula, duality and good reduction from the harder absolute-value statement. Weak Lefschetz first gives integrality of reciprocal roots. A proposed shortcut to rationality via a greatest common divisor of hyperplane factors needs the hard Lefschetz primitive decomposition. The successful argument fixes one coefficient prime, uses Lefschetz-pencil monodromy to isolate the variable pieces, controls the poles of their even tensor powers through the large symplectic monodromy group, then applies the result with a uniform half-weight loss to even Cartesian powers. Cohomological complete reducibility under the required weight hypothesis feeds back into hard Lefschetz. Sections III–IV discuss mixed Hodge structures, local invariant cycles, formal homotopy and further Deligne results. The address distinguishes the finite-field purity proof from genuine positivity of cycle pairings: one can prove purity here without an available positive classical-style scalar product on all étale cohomology. [Author-hosted proceedings](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/old/katzWorkofPDicm1978.pdf)

## Missing transfer lemmas

- **Frobenius trace and boundary.** A putative arithmetic correspondence needs a trace realization whose geometric side gives all completed explicit-formula terms, including the archimedean term and the polar part under one normalization. Mieda's translated-disc example is a concrete reason to state compactification and boundary conditions. Its interior fixed set is empty while the compact-support trace equals one. Zheng and Gaitsgory–Varshavsky extend local term formalism in settings with geometric or categorical functoriality hypotheses. None supplies this arithmetic correspondence. [Mieda, Theorem 1.1 and introductory example](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1011.1720v2) · [Zheng, Theorem 8.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1006.3810v5) · [Gaitsgory–Varshavsky, Theorems 0.4, 0.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.14257v2)
- **All-zero spectral identification.** A global generator on a specified cohomology or Hilbert space needs a trace/determinant identity that recovers the full completed zero divisor, with multiplicities and without discarded terms. The scaling-site construction in Connes–Consani explicitly uses the multiplier \(\zeta(\frac12+it)\) and gives critical-line spectral data. A separate theorem would have to detect hypothetical off-line zeros. Connes's heat expansion assumes RH at the starting point. A match of heat asymptotics alone would still allow distinct discrete spectra. [Connes–Consani, Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.10419v1) · [Connes, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.13082v1)
- **Positive adjoint structure.** With all zeros identified and the affine shift normalized to \(\frac12\), Deninger's (2.7) would suffice on the zero-bearing domain. The missing lemma is that a trace-compatible pairing in that domain is definite and makes the shifted generator skew-symmetric. Ancona–Marmora's index congruence modulo four does not yield definiteness. Agugliaro's positive intersections do establish definiteness for specified actual algebraic cycle spaces. They have no source map to the arithmetic spectral domain. This is complementary to the finite-field minimal hypotheses already in the archive. [Deninger, (2.3)–(2.7)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.02714v2) · [Ancona–Marmora, Theorem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.14001v3) · [Agugliaro, Theorems 1.2, 1.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.21562v1) · [Minimal polarization](../frontiers/geometry64_minimal_polarization.md)
- **A test-space inequality without residual corrections.** Connes–Consani–Marcolli's Proposition 6.2 identifies trace-pairing positivity as equivalent to RH for Grössencharakter \(L\)-functions. Wong's lower bounds have signed correction terms from normalization or the intertwining distributions. A sufficient new result would control their sum with the required sign on a core detecting every ζ zero, then justify passage to all tests needed for Weil positivity. A spectral/geometric identity and absolute convergence on restricted test spaces alone do not furnish this inequality. [Connes–Consani–Marcolli, Theorem 6.1 and Proposition 6.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0703392v1) · [Wong, Corollary 1.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1608.02296v2) · [Wong, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.02998v1)

The next concrete transfer question is whether one signed residual term in the \(SL_2\) formula of Wong (1.4) can be identified as a positive quadratic form on the same compact-support convolution tests as the ζ term, with explicit cutoffs and uniform boundary estimates. If not, an explicit negative test for that term would isolate the obstruction more sharply than a general assertion that positivity is missing.
