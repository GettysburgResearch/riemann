# Spectral, scattering and operator-domain primary sources

This batch adds eleven distinct works after checking the selected bibliography and paper tracks for names, titles and arXiv identifiers. Berry–Keating's 1999 SIAM Review article, Endres–Steiner on concrete self-adjoint realizations, the Gnutzmann–Smilansky quantum-graph review, and the Connes and Meyer formulations already have selected records. The 1990 Berry–Keating article here is a different primary work. The eleven PDF copies are retained with page counts, exact inspected versions, hashes and read scope in the source records. Bellissard's three-page note and the authors' two-page reply were read in full. [Batch records](prefix_skeptic62_spectral_records.jsonl)

## Periodic orbits and regularized spectral measures

- **Berry and Keating, 1990.** Equations (7)–(13), pp. 4840–4842, start from an already posited chaotic Hamiltonian and its regularized resolvent trace $g(E)=\operatorname{Tr}(E-H)^{-1}$. The ordinary spectral staircase is read from the imaginary part of $g(E+i0)$; the oscillatory expression is a semiclassical sum over primitive orbits and repetitions, with weights involving $T_p$ and $|\det(M_p^m-I)|^{-1/2}$. The authors identify the real-axis convergence problem and propose a Riemann–Siegel-inspired resummation to approximate the determinant zeros. The calculation supplies a prescription for existing chaotic systems rather than a domain or an exact trace identity having the zeta zero measure. [Published primary scan](../sources/prefix_skeptic62_spectral/berry_keating1990.pdf)
- **Sierra and Townsend, 2008.** The lowest-Landau-level approximation reduces the planar crossed-field model to a hyperbolic mode, with effective noncommuting planar coordinates. The regulated state count is a missing-state count relative to a continuum, designed to reproduce the smooth Riemann counting term. The higher Landau levels are suggested as a source of fluctuations. The closing discussion separates those suggestions from the derived smooth count. Neither operation gives an identified discrete point-spectrum measure for all zeta ordinates. [ArXiv v2](../sources/prefix_skeptic62_spectral/sierra_townsend.pdf)
- **Lax and Phillips, 1973–74.** The modular example uses the hyperbolic wave equation and incoming and outgoing subspaces in an energy Hilbert space. The wave group and its compressed semigroup are distinct operators. Equations (20)–(24) first factor the scattering operator to deal with causality, then identify a modular scattering coefficient
  $$
  \mathscr S'(\sigma)=\frac{\Gamma(1/2)\Gamma(i\sigma)}{\Gamma(i\sigma+1/2)}
        \frac{\zeta(2i\sigma)}{\zeta(1+2i\sigma)}
        =\frac{\Lambda(2i\sigma)}{\Lambda(1+2i\sigma)},
  \quad \Lambda(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s).
  $$
  On the real $\sigma$-axis the ratio has unit modulus by the functional equation wherever regular. If $1+2i\sigma=\rho$ is a nontrivial zero, the numerator is $\Lambda(\rho-1)=\Lambda(2-\rho)$, which is nonzero to the right of 1. The poles in this correspondence are at $\sigma=\Im\rho/2+i(1-\Re\rho)/2$. RH would put them on $\Im\sigma=1/4$, the location stated in the seminar. This is meromorphic scattering data, distinct from the real point spectrum of the self-adjoint Laplacian. The paper credits Faddeev–Pavlov for the related scattering criterion. [Seminar original, pp. XXIII.4–6](../sources/prefix_skeptic62_spectral/lax_phillips.pdf)

## Boundary conditions and the smooth spectral staircase

- **Sierra and Rodríguez-Laguna, 2011.** The quantization of $x(p+\ell_p^2/p)$ acts on $L^2((\ell_x,\infty),dx)$ with a Green kernel for $1/\hat p$, a fixed nonlocal boundary condition in (11), and a one-parameter extension described via equal deficiency indices. Equations (12) and (14) select decaying modified-Bessel solutions and give the spectral equation with a fixed phase. Matching the expansion of the smooth zeta zero count sets a model phase. The paper makes the average nature of the match explicit. [ArXiv v1, pp. 2–4](../sources/prefix_skeptic62_spectral/sierra_rodriguez.pdf)
- **Lagarias, 2009.** The half-line operator $-\partial_u^2+\tfrac14e^{2u}+ke^u$ is limit point at $+\infty$ in $L^2([u_0,\infty),du)$. A separated real boundary condition $\cos\alpha\,\psi(u_0)+\sin\alpha\,\psi'(u_0)=0$ fixes the self-adjoint problem. Theorem 2.1 gives pure discrete spectrum and the Whittaker solution. Theorems 3.2 and 4.1 give the two-term counting law and a corresponding entire model for Dirichlet boundary conditions. The zero law is for that model and its spectral measure, with a smooth Riemann-style counting law after changing the parameter from energy to its square root. [ArXiv v6, Theorems 2.1, 3.2, 4.1](../sources/prefix_skeptic62_spectral/lagarias_morse.pdf)
- **Sierra, 2019.** The Rindler Dirac model imposes matching conditions across mirror locations and a phase at the endpoint. It uses a transfer-matrix analysis with small reflection amplitudes and tunes the extension phase using the phase of zeta on the critical line. The proposal gives a mechanism for selected zero-associated levels in a limit. A Hilbert–Pólya claim for all zeros would require one fixed operator domain realizing the complete spectrum, together with a justified spectral limit. The source's tuning rule and its proposed route are recorded as such. [ArXiv v4, §§X–XIV](../sources/prefix_skeptic62_spectral/sierra_rindler.pdf)
- **Hwang, Jung, Kim and Park, 2026.** The preprint fixes a phase in an SR-type Bessel equation and compares 606 numerical model eigenvalues to zeta ordinates. It explicitly treats the missing fluctuating part of the zeta count and reports an index offset for its smooth prediction. The reported fit is numerical and concerns one chosen extension. The corpus record does not treat the saddle-point comparison as a rigorously uniform individual-zero error estimate. [ArXiv v2, abstract and numerical comparison](../sources/prefix_skeptic62_spectral/sierra_comparison.pdf)

## Full primary reading of the BBM exchange and a concrete domain obstruction

**The proposal.** Bender, Brody and Müller write the formal expression $H=\Delta^{-1}(xp+px)\Delta$, with $\Delta=1-e^{-ip}$. Their Hurwitz-zeta recurrence produces formal candidates $ -\zeta(z,x+1)$; at $x=0$ the proposed condition reads $\zeta(z)=0$. Their paper explicitly leaves the reality of the proposed eigenvalues unproved. It introduces a formal metric and discusses a biorthogonal inner product. A domain and state-space argument is needed to promote the formal similarity to an equivalence of closed operators. [ArXiv v4, pp. 2–4](../sources/prefix_skeptic62_spectral/bender_brody_muller.pdf)

**Bellissard and the reply.** I read all three pages of the Comment and both pages of the reply. Problem 1 of the Comment gives a reproducible obstruction on the ordinary half-line $L^2(dx)$. The minimal momentum $p_0=-i\partial_x$ on $H^1_0(0,\infty)$ has adjoint $-i\partial_x$ on $H^1(0,\infty)$, as follows from the weak derivative and the vanishing trace in the boundary form. Under the convention $n_\pm=\dim\ker(p_0^*\mp i)$, its defect solutions are respectively $e^{-x}$ and $e^x$. Only the former is square integrable, giving $(1,0)$. The unequal indices rule out a self-adjoint extension of this momentum. The zero-fill right translation instead satisfies $S^*S=I$, $SS^*=I-P_{(0,1)}$, as checked directly by substitution and its adjoint. It is an isometry that is not onto. Thus the relevant one-sided shift is not a unitary translation generated by a self-adjoint half-line momentum.

Problem 2 addresses membership of the Hurwitz candidates in ordinary $L^2$. The authors' reply agrees that the candidates are not in that space on the critical line and points to their own large-$x$ asymptotic. It also agrees that the bare half-line momentum is not self-adjoint. The disagreement is over whether these observations exclude their proposed biorthogonal setting, not over the two ordinary-space facts. The reply explicitly disputes the attribution of an eigenvalue-reality proof. Those boundaries matter when assessing the published criticism. [Bellissard, Problems 1–2](../sources/prefix_skeptic62_spectral/bellissard.pdf) · [BBM reply, Issues 1–4](../sources/prefix_skeptic62_spectral/bbm_response.pdf)

**Specified metric completion.** Liu's later preprint is explicit about the choice it analyzes. Take $S$ to be the zero-fill shift and $\Delta=I-S$ on the ordinary $L^2$ core $C_c^\infty(0,\infty)$. Independent checks give the central state-space conclusion. The map $\Delta$ is injective by recursion on the unit cells. The adjoint kernel consists of 1-periodic functions in $L^2(0,\infty)$, hence vanishes. Thus $\Delta$ takes the completion in norm $\|\Delta f\|_2$ unitarily onto ordinary $L^2$. This norm is noncoercive. For example, normalized indicator functions supported on an interval of length $n$ have difference norm $\sqrt{2/n}$, and smooth core approximations give the same limit.

The free transported dilation generator $xp+px$ on this target space is unitarily equivalent under $f(x)\mapsto e^{u/2}f(e^u)$ to $-2i\,d/du$ with domain $H^1(\mathbb R)$. Fourier transform gives simple continuous spectral measure, Lebesgue measure up to normalization, with no eigenvectors. More directly, the stipulated Hurwitz recurrence gives $\Delta\psi_z(x)=x^{-z}$ for $x>1$, regardless of which extension is used to define the shift below 1. At $\Re z=1/2$ the tail has divergent squared norm $\int_1^\infty dx/x$. So this particular metric completion cannot contain a candidate vector with that shift difference. The state-space obstruction is independent of the choice of Hamiltonian extension *within that completion*. I did not independently audit Liu's additional transported-core deficiency-index classification or rule out other metric completions. [Liu, equations (18)–(24) and the missing-eigenstates discussion](../sources/prefix_skeptic62_spectral/bbm_metric.pdf)
