# Beurling systems, multiplicative orthogonality, and restriction

18 November 2026. This source wave adds 32 research works for three specific gaps in the existing dossier. Each record gives the actual statement read, a precise locator, and the condition needed before applying it to a dossier problem. Of the 32 works, 21 concern Beurling systems and Mellin tools, seven concern multiplicative orthogonality, and four concern restriction or mean values. Three complete pinned editions received L1 readings. The other 29 are targeted L0 readings of accessible full texts.

## Transfer conditions

- **Generalized Mellin boundary control.** Révész's adapted Perron contour and Broucke–Debruyne's cluster-sensitive mean value theorem address distinct parts of an extension of the tapered residual. The contour shift needs \(N(x)=Ax+O(x^\theta)\) and a contour inside its region of continuation. The Dirichlet-polynomial estimate must charge local clusters of real norms. Before transferring the taper theorem one still needs analogue targets, an anchor, boundary \(L^2\) control, and a zero derivative or residue tail. [Adapted Perron contour](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.11463v1) · [Cluster mean values](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.08716v2) · [Ordinary taper](../audits/adversarial_reviews/director_tapered_prime_array_round9.md)
- **Generalized Euler products and off-line controls.** Broucke, Debruyne and Révész construct finite prescribed zero and pole configurations in generalized systems. Debruyne, Schlage-Puchta and Vindas separate all-order Cesàro remainder estimates from a logarithmically weighted squared error. These give tests of which features of the ordinary model really use the arithmetic of rational integers. Constructing a Beurling model is insufficient to assign an off-line zero to the ordinary zeta function, and Cesàro control is insufficient for a global squared boundary norm. [Prescribed zeros and poles](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.01567v2) · [Cesàro separation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1505.04174v2)
- **Arithmetic correlations.** The Kátai extension requires concrete \(p,q\) dilation correlations of the candidate sequence. Tao–Teräväinen's unweighted results allow exceptional scales, while Frantzikinakis–Host's structured results require the stated dynamical and averaging hypotheses. A fixed bounded phase test and a logarithmic-average theorem cannot substitute for a uniform estimate over the varying Gram-test family; the first thing to check is a genuine finite reduction to the specified scalar terms. [Kátai level-set theorem](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.07322v2) · [Almost-all scales](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.02518v2) · [Furstenberg systems](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.08556v3)
- **Frequency collisions.** Green–Tao's restriction theorem is on an additive cyclic group with a sieve majorant. Bourgain's coefficient form uses a phase lift with a nonzero Wronskian and separated blocks. Robert–Sargos counts four-term collisions for fixed power exponents. In particular, taking a formal small-exponent limit to obtain log-product collision control would require uniformity as the power curve degenerates. These restrictions are relevant to the unresolved off-diagonal and inverse-stability steps. [Selberg sieve restriction](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0405581v2) · [Wronskian decoupling](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.7862v1) · [Monomial collision count](https://doi.org/10.1515/crelle.2006.012) · [Inverse stability](../audits/adversarial_reviews/director_prime_array_inverse_round9.md)

## Complete readings

### Pintz–Révész, arXiv:2407.12746v1, eight pages

The ordinary reciprocal Dirichlet-series representation is where integrality matters. The density detector requires Ramanujan-type bounds on both arrays. The proof uses a Beurling growth exponent derived from Axiom A and estimates a separate Riemann-zeta growth parameter using Bourgain and elementary bounds. Their comparison condition in Lemma 1 is part of the detector and cannot be dropped. The first branch gives \(N(1-\eta,T)\ll_{\eta,\varepsilon}T^{2\eta/(1-\theta-\eta)+\varepsilon}\), for \(0<\eta<\min(1-\theta,4/29,(8+13\theta)/71)\).

The pinned draft states subsequent branches with the broad preamble \(\eta<1-\theta\). The publisher PDF repeats this, but its proof gives the last displayed comparison only through \(\eta\le1/4\). A literal reading for \(\theta=0,\eta=3/4\) yields the negative value \(2/(1-2\eta)\) and cannot bound the Riemann zeta zero count. The record therefore uses the independently checked first-branch range. The publisher PDF is retained as a targeted manifestation comparison and is excluded from the whole-work count. [Complete draft](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.12746v1) · [Publisher version, Theorem 1 and §5.4, equation (45)](https://www.impan.pl/shop/en/publication/transaction/download/product/116232)

### Vindas, arXiv:2406.00736v2, nine pages

Section 2 uses convolution of measures on \([1,\infty)\), writing multiplication by \(\log u\) as a derivation. The weighted variation hypothesis \(\int |dR(u)|/u<\infty\) makes the exponential of \(dR\) a finite-variation convolution multiplier in the weighted norm. Dominated convergence transfers \(o(x)\) in either direction under that perturbation. Section 3 controls the other perturbation by a positive majorant for the convolution exponential, with \(\int_{[1,x]}u^{-1}dF^+(u)\ll_\varepsilon \log^\varepsilon x\). This gives the loss from \(x/\log^a x\) to \(x/\log^b x\) with \(b<\min(a,1)\) before the weaker integrable residual is reapplied. Section 4 uses the even exponential sum \(2\cosh^*(dA)\) as a positive comparison for de Haan's Tauberian input, yielding the sharper \(M_K=o(x/\log x)\) in Kahane's model. The proof calls on that Tauberian theorem and leaves the auxiliary transform expansion Lemma 4.2 to the reader. The general implication from the Beurling PNT alone is left open in this source. [Complete pinned text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.00736v2)

### Debruyne, arXiv:2002.00927v1, five pages

The finite root-of-unity filter reduces the residue-class counts to the mean of a multiplicative twist. Its Euler product factors the prime-power phase with the higher powers absorbed into an integrable remainder. Lemma 2.1 uses positivity and \(|\sin(Kx/2)|\le K|\sin(x/2)|\) for the key trigonometric bound, then compact-uniform Dini convergence. The actual mean-value step invokes the Beurling Halász theorem. Remark 2.2 separates finitely many small primes when counting distinct prime factors. Theorem 1.1 assumes a Chebyshev upper bound and positive ordinary density.

For Remark 2.3, equation (2.15) in the arXiv pin omits \(1/\log x\) from the stated logarithmic density. Partial summation under \(N(x)\sim ax\) gives \(a\log x+o(\log x)\) for the unnormalized integral. The Ghent University repository copy has the normalization. The selected result remains Theorem 1.1 under ordinary density, with the repository copy retained as a targeted version check. [Complete arXiv pin](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2002.00927v1) · [Normalized equation in repository copy](https://backoffice.biblio.ugent.be/download/8687953/8711793)
