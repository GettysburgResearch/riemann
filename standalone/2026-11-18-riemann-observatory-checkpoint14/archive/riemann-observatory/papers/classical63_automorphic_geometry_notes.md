# Automorphic forms, trace formulae, and arithmetic geometry

The 41 records use primary texts. Three short works have complete reading entries: Weil's 1941 note, Grothendieck's standard conjectures chapter, and Katz–Messing. The other entries report targeted reading at the stated locations. Publication citations identify works; the version field identifies the copy consulted. The Grothendieck entry follows the original proceedings attribution, while bibliographic dates for preprints may differ from their downloaded revisions.

Finite-field weight arguments require a Frobenius correspondence or a pure sheaf. Trace identities concern automorphic kernels and spectral moments, even when they contain values of classical ζ. These inputs leave open the identification and location of the full classical ζ zero set. Each entry below records the particular missing step.

## On the Riemann Hypothesis in Function-Fields

André Weil. PNAS 27, 345–347. Accessed version is original journal pages 345–347, assembled from PMC page scans. [Primary text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1078336/)

For a separable one-variable function field over F_q in Weil's setup, positivity of the correspondence trace pairing and reciprocal pairing of eigenvalues imply that all reciprocal roots have absolute value √q. See pp. 345–347, especially the polynomial argument on p. 347.

The proof uses the Frobenius correspondence and its transpose, with product q times the identity, on a smooth projective curve. No correspondence with these properties is available here for the completed classical zeta function.

Read all three article pages, following the intersection numbers, positive form, Frobenius power sums, and real interpolation contradiction. The note defers full foundational details of the correspondence theory.

## Sur les courbes algébriques et les variétés qui s’en déduisent

André Weil. Actualités Scientifiques et Industrielles 1041, Hermann. Accessed version is digitized transcription of the 1948 monograph. [Primary text](https://denisevellachemla.eu/Weil-courbes-varietes.pdf)

The zeta-function numerator of the curve has roots on the circle abs(u)=q^(-1/2), by the preceding theory of correspondences. See § IV, no. 22.

The argument uses the polarization of a curve's Jacobian and the evaluation of point counts through Frobenius iterates over finite fields.

## Standard conjectures on algebraic cycles

Alexander Grothendieck. Algebraic Geometry, Bombay Colloquium, 193–199. Accessed version is scan of original proceedings chapter. [Primary text](https://download.uni-mainz.de/mathematik/Algebraische%20Geometrie/Lehre/WS23.Padische.1968.Grothendieck.StandardConjs.pdf)

Formulates C(X), A(X)/B(X) and Hdg(X). B(X) and Hdg(X×X) imply the Weil weight assertion for endomorphisms scaling the polarization by q, and hence its Frobenius case. See §§2–4, pp. 194–198.

Even the conditional argument requires an algebraic Lefschetz operator and a positive primitive intersection form for an appropriate geometric realization. The paper supplies these as conjectures about varieties.

Read §§1–4 and the conclusion, following Dwork–Faton's trace criterion, diagonal projectors, the equivalence argument via X×X, and the conditional positivity implication.

## Some consequences of the Riemann hypothesis for varieties over finite fields

Nicholas M. Katz, William Messing. Inventiones Mathematicae 23, 73–77. Accessed version is original journal PDF with archive cover sheet. [Primary text](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/old/katzmessing.pdf)

For smooth projective geometrically irreducible varieties over F_q, any cohomology satisfying the stated duality, weak Lefschetz and zeta formula has the étale Frobenius polynomials. With the extra cycle-class hypothesis, Künneth projectors are rational combinations of Frobenius graphs. See § II Theorem 1 and Corollary 1, § III Theorem 2.

The comparison starts with Deligne's established weight-stratified Frobenius polynomials and an alternating cohomological factorization. Neither input is furnished for classical ζ.

Read all five printed pages from the scan and OCR, including the Lefschetz pencil divisibility proof, hard Lefschetz corollary, and polynomial construction of cycle projectors.

## The Selberg trace formula for groups of F-rank one

James Arthur. Annals of Mathematics 100, 326–385. Accessed version is original journal PDF. [Primary text](https://www.claymath.org/library/cw/arthur/pdf/1.pdf)

For the adelic F-rank-one setup the discrete automorphic convolution operator is trace class under Assumption 3.5 (K-finite convolution factors and the prescribed vanishing on almost all ramified classes), and §9 gives the trace expansion after continuous and geometric divergences cancel. See §3 Assumption 3.5 and Theorem 3.6, §9.

The trace class statement concerns the discrete automorphic convolution operator; its spectrum and the parabolic correction terms give no operator whose eigenvalues are all classical zeta zeros.

## A trace formula for reductive groups I. Terms associated to classes in G(Q)

James Arthur. Duke Mathematical Journal 45, 911–952. Accessed version is original journal PDF. [Primary text](https://www.claymath.org/library/cw/arthur/pdf/7.pdf)

For sufficiently regular truncation parameter T the sum of integrals of the absolute values of the class kernels is finite. The resulting geometric distributions are indexed by rational conjugacy of semisimple parts. See §7 Theorem 7.1 and §8.

This is geometric control of a truncated reductive-group automorphic kernel. It does not identify an untruncated self-adjoint operator with classical ζ's explicit formula.

## L-indistinguishability for SL(2)

Jean-Pierre Labesse, Robert P. Langlands. Canadian Journal of Mathematics 31, 726–785. Accessed version is IAS typeset copy of published article. [Primary text](https://publications.ias.edu/sites/default/files/ll-ps.pdf)

Computes global automorphic multiplicities for SL(2) and its inner forms by distinguishing packet types and tracking contributions related to tori. See §§6–7, Propositions 7.2–7.4.

Packet multiplicities are for automorphic representations on a prescribed group. Stabilizing this trace formula yields no zero-location theorem for the degree-one trivial Hecke L-function.

## Gauss Sums, Kloosterman Sums, and Monodromy Groups

Nicholas M. Katz. Annals of Mathematics Studies 116, Princeton University Press. Accessed version is digitized original monograph. [Primary text](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/Katz-GKM.pdf)

Determines geometric monodromy of rank n≥2 Kloosterman sheaves: Sp(n) for even n, SL(n) for odd n and odd p, SO(n) for odd n≠7 and p=2, and G₂ for n=7 and p=2. §11.4 applies equidistribution to the normalized Frobenius classes. See Chapter 11, §§11.1, 11.4.

These compact laws use the computed monodromy of a pure finite-field sheaf. They do not construct a sheaf giving classical zeta zeros.

## Random matrices, Frobenius eigenvalues, and monodromy

Nicholas M. Katz, Peter Sarnak. AMS Colloquium Publications 45. Accessed version is digitized original monograph. [Primary text](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/RMFEM.pdf)

Equidistribution theorems for unitarized Frobenius in pure lisse families, including uniform control in §9.6, support the separate normalized spacing limits of Chapter 1. See §§9.2 and 9.6, especially Theorems 9.2.6 and 9.6.10.

The proof needs purity and finite-field averaging plus explicit geometric monodromy. Even an agreement of spacing limits would not rule out exceptional zeros of a single classical function.

## Zeroes of zeta functions and symmetry

Nicholas M. Katz, Peter Sarnak. Bulletin of the AMS 36, 1–26. Accessed version is original journal PDF. [Primary text](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/old/katzsarnakarticle.pdf)

Explains how Frobenius on curve cohomology gives a unitary conjugacy class and how symplectic monodromy for suitable curve families yields the corresponding family statistics. See §3 and §4, equation (30) and the analogy table.

The spectral interpretation in §3 depends on curve cohomology. Quantum-chaos or compact-group statistical similarity does not identify a classical ζ spectral operator.

## Functoriality for the classical groups

James W. Cogdell, Henry H. Kim, Ilya I. Piatetski-Shapiro, Freydoun Shahidi. Publications Mathématiques de l'IHÉS 99, 163–233. Accessed version is original journal PDF. [Primary text](https://www.numdam.org/article/PMIHES_2004__99__163_0.pdf)

Irreducible globally generic cuspidal representations for the paper's split classical groups over number fields admit the specified functorial lifts to GL(N). See §1 Theorem 1.1.

Functoriality identifies local parameters and analytic L-functions. It imposes no all-zero constraint for ζ, whose GL(1) realization is already known.

## Le lemme fondamental pour les algèbres de Lie

Ngô Bảo Châu. Publications Mathématiques de l'IHÉS 111, 1–169. Accessed version is arXiv:0801.0446v3, 2 May 2008. [Primary text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0801.0446)

The identities concern endoscopic orbital integrals. The Hitchin cohomology supplies Frobenius traces for that comparison, without realizing the completed classical ζ function.

## The subconvexity problem for GL2

Philippe Michel, Akshay Venkatesh. Publications Mathématiques de l'IHÉS 112, 171–271. Accessed version is arXiv:0903.3591v2, 9 Mar 2010. [Primary text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0903.3591)

An absolute δ>0 gives C^(1/4−δ) central-value bounds for GL(1), GL(2) with unitary central character and the stated GL(2)×GL(2) Rankin–Selberg family with one representation fixed; imaginary twists include t-aspect. See §1 Theorems 1.1 and 1.2.

The method gives quantitative upper bounds for values. Such a bound lacks the zero exclusion needed in Re s>1/2 for ζ.

## Galois representations arising from some compact Shimura varieties

Sug Woo Shin. Annals of Mathematics 173, 1645–1741. Accessed version is author-hosted manuscript with internal pagination. [Primary text](https://math.berkeley.edu/~swshin/StableGal.pdf)

For regular algebraic conjugate self-dual cuspidal GL(m) over CM fields with the stated slight regularity for even m, constructs semisimple ℓ-adic Galois representations realizing local parameters away from ℓ. See Theorem 1.2, assembled from Theorems 7.5, 7.11 and Corollary 7.13.

These Galois representations realize individual local parameters. Their existence gives no global Frobenius operator whose spectrum lists the classical ζ zeros.

## The fundamental lemma of Jacquet and Rallis

Zhiwei Yun, Julia Gordon (appendix). Duke Mathematical Journal 156, 167–227. Accessed version is author-hosted final journal text. [Primary text](https://math.mit.edu/~zyun/FLJR_published.pdf)

In equal characteristic >n, the local theorem identifies the specified graded Frobenius modules for matching invariants with trivial η on the discriminant. The corollary yields the Jacquet–Rallis orbital-integral identity, and Gordon's transfer gives large-residue-characteristic cases. See Introduction, Main Theorem (Local part), Corollary, and Appendix Theorem A.1.

The matched distributions are those of the relative trace formula over local fields. Transfer of local identities does not pass a finite-field Weil bound to classical ζ.

## The mean value of L(1/2, χ) in the hyperelliptic ensemble

Julio C. Andrade, Jonathan P. Keating. Journal of Number Theory 132, 2793–2816. Accessed version is arXiv:1208.1131v1, 6 Aug 2012. [Primary text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1208.1131)

For fixed q≡1 mod 4, obtains the first moment of L(1/2,χ_D) over monic squarefree D of degree 2g+1, with Euler-product main and secondary terms and power-saving error. See §2, Theorem 1 and equation (2.5).

The degree truncation from the curve L-polynomial, averaging over D and finite-field character cancellation are unavailable for a single classical ζ.

## The Variance of the Number of Prime Polynomials in Short Intervals and in Residue Classes

Jonathan P. Keating, Zeév Rudnick. International Mathematics Research Notices, 259–288. Accessed version is journal advance-access typeset PDF (2012). [Primary text](https://www.math.tau.ac.il/~rudnick/papers/KeatingRudnickIMRNfinal.pdf)

At fixed h<n−3, q^(−h−1) times the short interval variance tends to n−h−2 as q→∞. The progression theorem gives G(n;Q)∼q^n(deg Q−1) for fixed n≥2, Q squarefree and n≥deg Q−1. See §2, Theorems 2.1 and 2.2.

The varying-field limit uses matrix trace averages from finite-field equidistribution. Its integer analogue involves a separate limit tied to ζ pair correlation.

## Hardy–Littlewood Tuple Conjecture over Large Finite Fields

Lior Bary-Soroker. International Mathematics Research Notices, 568–575. Accessed version is arXiv:1206.3930v3, 4 Oct 2012. [Primary text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.3930)

For fixed n,r and odd prime power q, the number of monic degree-n f with all f+a_i irreducible for distinct shifts of degree<n is q^n/n^r+O_(n,r)(q^(n−1/2)). See Theorem 1.1.

The bound uses geometric monodromy over coefficient space and finite-field Chebotarev with q→∞ at fixed degree. Integer primes do not vary over that coefficient space.

## Conjectures for the integral moments and ratios of L-functions over function fields

Julio C. Andrade, Jonathan P. Keating. Journal of Number Theory 142, 102–148. Accessed version is arXiv:1407.3099v1, 11 Jul 2014. [Primary text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1407.3099)

## Witt Vectors and a Question of Rudnick and Waxman

Nicholas M. Katz. International Mathematics Research Notices. Accessed version is journal advance-access typeset PDF (2016). [Primary text](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~nmk/WVQRW.pdf)

For odd n≥7 and field sizes tending to infinity, primitive super-even Witt-vector characters have normalized Frobenius classes equidistributed in USp(n−1), allowing varying characteristic. The n=3,5 clauses restrict characteristics. See §5 Theorem 5.1.

The symmetry group arises from Witt-vector character sheaves and controls family sampling. The construction gives no classical ζ Frobenius class.

## The fourth moment of quadratic Dirichlet L-functions over function fields

Alexandra Florea. Geometric and Functional Analysis 27, 541–595. Accessed version is arXiv:1609.01262v1, 5 Sep 2016. [Primary text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.01262)

For prime q≡1 mod 4, the fourth central moment over monic squarefree degree 2g+1 is q^(2g+1) times an explicit degree-10 polynomial truncated through g^8, with error O(q^(2g+1)g^(15/2+ε)). See Theorem 1.1 and Appendix (9.19)–(9.21).

Polynomial Poisson summation and exact finite degree cutoffs control this quadratic family. Its arithmetic and asymptotic scale differ from the t-aspect fourth moment of ζ.

## Shtukas and the Taylor expansion of L-functions

Zhiwei Yun, Wei Zhang. Annals of Mathematics 186, 767–911. Accessed version is arXiv:1512.02683v3, 11 Apr 2017. [Primary text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1512.02683)

For an everywhere unramified cuspidal representation of PGL(2) over the function field, the normalized even rth derivative at 1/2 of the quadratic base-change L-function equals the self-intersection of its projected Heegner–Drinfeld cycle. See Theorems 1.1 and 1.2, proof §9.3.2.

This is a special-value identity for a base-change L-function on a moduli stack of shtukas. An identity at the center does not give the signs or spectral data for all classical ζ zeros.

## The equidistribution of L-functions of twists by Witt vector Dirichlet characters over function fields

Reciprocity exchanges level and twist in an averaged spectral moment. The identity imposes no spectral-location constraint on ζ.

## On the Ramanujan conjecture for automorphic forms over function fields I. Geometry

Will Sawin, Nicolas Templier. Journal of the AMS 34, 653–746. Accessed version is arXiv:1805.12231v4, 27 Sep 2020. [Primary text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.12231)

Temperedness controls local Satake parameters. For ζ these local parameters are elementary already, and a bound on their moduli does not locate global zeros.

## Symmetric power functoriality for holomorphic modular forms, II

## On the Chowla and twin primes conjectures over F_q[T]

## Short sums of trace functions over function fields and their applications

## Bounds for standard L-functions
