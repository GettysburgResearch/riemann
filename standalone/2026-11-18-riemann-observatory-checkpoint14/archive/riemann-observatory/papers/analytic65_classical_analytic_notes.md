## Whole-work readings

- **Hiary, arXiv:1507.01261v4, 10 pages.** Theorem 1.1 gives the \(0.63\,t^{1/6}\log t\) estimate for \(t\ge3\), plus the separate bound through height 3. Lemma 2.2 uses interval Euler–Maclaurin through height 200. Lemma 2.3 uses Riemann–Siegel through \(9.3\times10^7\), and Lemma 2.4 divides the main sum into blocks of length \(\lceil t^{1/3}\rceil\), with partial summation in (24) and the third derivative in (27)–(29). Section 3 derives the explicit differencing estimate by choosing \(M=\lceil\eta W^{1/3}\rceil\) in (40)–(47). The paper specifies computational checks for finite and transition ranges. Their independent numerical replay was outside this reading. Transfer to a strip or a derivative estimate requires additional interpolation or Cauchy bounds. [Pinned full source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1507.01261v4)
- **Johnston, arXiv:2411.13791v2, 8 pages.** Theorem 2.1 links the density shape and zero-free width via \(\omega(x)=\min_{t\ge1}(\eta(t)\log x+\log t)\). Lemma 3.3 supplies bounded reciprocal-height mass above its auxiliary line. Section 4 chooses \(T=e^{2\omega(x)}\) in the truncated explicit formula and splits zeros at \(\sigma_1,\sigma_2\). The derivative sign assertion after (4.4) is stronger than the unique-minimizer hypothesis. The stated near-line bound follows directly: each weight \(x^{-\eta(t)}/t\) is at most \(e^{-\omega(x)}\) by the minimization, and there are at most \(2N(\sigma_2,T)\) terms after both signs. The claimed bound therefore needs no monotonicity argument at that step. Corollaries 2.2–2.3 give the different classical and Korobov–Vinogradov outcomes. [Pinned full source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.13791v2)
- **Schlage-Puchta, arXiv:1105.1616v1, 3 pages.** Theorem 1 uses the minor-arc bound for the additive squarefree sum, and Theorem 2 applies it to ternary representations under GRH. The dyadic proof of Theorem 3 writes \(\mu^2(n)=\sum_{d^2\mid n}\mu(d)\). Lemma 1 counts near-resonances \(d^2\alpha\) by grouping the squares in cells of length \(q\), bounding repeated differences of squares by divisor counts and using the rational approximation at that scale. The short-\(d\) proof compares against \(ad^2/q\). Read the rational approximant throughout as reduced, as introduced immediately before Theorem 3. Without reduction, taking \(\alpha=0,a=0\) and arbitrary growing \(q\) would contradict the claimed unrestricted bound. The \(\mu^2\)-specific decomposition is essential to reuse. [Pinned full source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1105.1616v1)

## Transfer priorities

- For a derivative test, track the two-sided derivative envelope on every dyadic subinterval. Sargos’s near-monomial French paper and Bombieri–Iwaniec’s paired mean-value paper address different regularity inputs. Bombieri–Iwaniec explicitly use a monomial in the proof of Lemma 5. A modern deformation requires a replacement for that step, even if the final phase is locally close.
- For a density-to-primes transfer, separate a count on an individual vertical line from a conductor average, a weighted sparsity assertion and a per-interval lower bound. The all-interval, almost-all and large-gap results in this wave supply benchmarks with different quantifiers. The almost-prime theorem of Iwaniec–Laborde does not give the prime conclusion.
- For effective character control, the primary Ford–Green–Konyagin–Maynard–Tao proof gives the one-prime exclusion antecedent in Lemma 7.1 and Corollary 6. The separate derivative-tail argument requires that the omitted conductor actually multiply a vanishing coefficient, as in the HLZ character sum.
