# New exponent pairs, zero density estimates, and zero additive energy estimates: a systematic approach

Terence Tao, Tim Trudgian and Andrew Yang. arXiv:2501.16779v1 (28 January 2025). *Mathematics of Computation* 95 (2026), 2941–2990, DOI 10.1090/mcom/4138, published electronically 6 November 2025. [ArXiv version](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16779v1) · [Journal record](https://doi.org/10.1090/mcom/4138)

Targeted reading of the v1 introduction, large-value-to-density conversion in Corollaries 42–44, Theorems 49–51, and the 2025 evidence table on pp. 26–33. Complete PDF available. The full exponent-pair and additive-energy proofs and the typeset journal version were outside scope. Level 0.

With \(N(\sigma,T)\ll T^{A(\sigma)(1-\sigma)+\varepsilon}\) interpreted in the source's exponent conventions, Theorem 49 recovers Guth–Maynard's \(A(\sigma)\le15/(3+5\sigma)\). Corollary 42 takes the maximum of two suprema, \(LV_\zeta(\sigma,\tau)/\tau\) for \(2\le\tau<4\tau_0/3\) and \(LV(\sigma,\tau)/\tau\) for \(2\tau_0/3\le\tau\le\tau_0\). This is a conversion of large-value inequalities into density bounds, and it explains why comparing only a single polynomial scale is insufficient.

Theorem 51 proves for fixed \(17/22\le\sigma\le4/5\) that \(A(\sigma)\le\max(2/(9\sigma-6),9/[8(2\sigma-1)])\), switching at \(38/49\). The proof uses the twelfth zeta moment, existing large-value inequalities, and subdivision through Corollary 44. Table 2 records the smaller envelope of available results at its date, and neither branch should be promoted independently over the full range. Theorem 50 gives \(3/(10\sigma-7)\) for \(7/10<\sigma\le1\) using the exponent pair of Theorem 21, extending the range of a Heath-Brown bound. Table 2 shows where it actually enters the smaller envelope.
