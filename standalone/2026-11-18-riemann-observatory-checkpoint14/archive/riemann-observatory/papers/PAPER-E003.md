**Main result:** Assume RH and sum_{|Im rho|<=T}|zeta'(rho)|^(-2)<<T^(3/2-delta) for some delta>0. For V_N(s)=sum_{n<=N}(1-log n/log N)mu(n)n^(-s), N>1, its squared weighted critical-line error (1/(2pi))integral_R |1-zeta(1/2+it)V_N(1/2+it)|² dt/(1/4+t²) is asymptotic to (2+gamma-log(4pi))/log N (Theorem1,p.2). Moment hypothesis forces simple zeros. Theorem2,p.3: analogous triangular reciprocal Taylor window is asymptotically optimal for polynomials with simple roots on/outside the circle, if boundary roots are present.

**Proof architecture:** ["Perron representation of logarithmically weighted Möbius polynomial", "Residues at pole s, nontrivial zeros, and trivial zeros (Lemma2)", "Reciprocal derivative moment controls sums of zero residues (Lemma3)", "Shift norm contour off critical line", "Double pole residue supplies sum 1/|rho|² log N; remaining integrals are lower order"]

**Limitations:** ["RH alone is not the theorem hypothesis.", "Inverse derivative moment excludes multiplicities.", "Infinite-zero residue tails require the additional moment estimate.", "No finite-N inequality of the form d_N²>=C/log N is proved.", "Polynomial theorem with no boundary roots should be read as o(1/N), rather than literal asymptotic equivalence to zero."]

**Historical importance:** Under an explicit strong arithmetic hypothesis, a concrete logarithmic Möbius smoothing attains Burnol’s asymptotic lower constant.

**Reusable lemma:** Lemma3 pp.6–7 supplies a bound on the sum of zero-residue terms on Re(s)=1/2±epsilon using the negative derivative moment.

**Research questions:** ["Can collective residue cancellation weaken the exponent 3/2-delta?", "Which estimates replace inverse simple derivatives for higher principal parts?"]
