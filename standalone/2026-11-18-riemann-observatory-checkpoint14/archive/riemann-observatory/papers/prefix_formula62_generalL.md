# Prior works on Nyman–Beurling approximation for individual L-functions

The closest established lower bound for a fixed primitive nonprincipal Dirichlet character is de Roton's 2006 Selberg-class result. Mellin Plancherel and restriction of her continuous dilation range $\alpha\in[1/N,1]$ to $\alpha=1/n$ produce the exact ordinary Dirichlet cutoff and the sharp multiplicity-squared lower constant. Dimitrov–Oliveira address the same discrete critical-line norm and give qualitative convergence under the corresponding zero-free hypothesis. Their quantitative asymptotic belongs to a larger interpolation space. This distinction is essential when looking for the prior matching upper bound. [de Roton, 2006](https://numdam.org/articles/10.24033/bsmf.2516/) · [Dimitrov–Oliveira, arXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.07887v1)

The closest known general sequential predecessor is de Roton's 2009 paper. The journal and HAL metadata establish a Selberg-class approximation sequence under the generalized Riemann hypothesis. I did not retrieve a readable full text. That source therefore cannot be cited from this audit for an exact norm rate, coefficient window, or hypotheses beyond the accessible author statement. A secondary bibliography attributes a power of $\log\log N$ to the constructed sequence, which I have left outside the theorem statements pending a primary-text check. The 2007 full-length generalization was also located bibliographically, with the HAL and AMS PDFs inaccessible here. Its 2005 precursor was available, and the DFMR papers provide independently accessible general continuous versions. [de Roton, JNT 129 (2009), 2647–2658](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jnt.2009.05.017) · [de Roton, Trans. AMS 359 (2007), 6111–6127](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-07-04261-4) · [2005 precursor](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.crma.2004.11.023)

The inspected prior sources do not furnish a matching GRH-alone discrete upper in the exact fixed-character norm. This is a finding about the sources inspected. It does not certify novelty or verify any later construction. Bettin–Conrey–Farmer remain the useful exact-constant upper comparator for zeta under the extra reciprocal-derivative condition. Burnol's adelic closure theorem covers the family of abelian $L$-functions, but uses norm-bounded adelic test functions and does not give finite integer-cutoff approximation rates. [Bettin–Conrey–Farmer](https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.5191) · [Burnol's adelic theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0001013v3)

## Source boundaries

- De Roton's 2006 theorem requires the complementary function to be in $L^2$. This is elementary for a fixed nonprincipal Dirichlet character. The 2007 Acta Arithmetica mean-square theorem supplies this for eligible positive degree below four. This covers the degree-one examples and permits degree-two or degree-three applications once the source's coefficient and functional-equation hypotheses are checked. For a general high-degree automorphic $L$-function one must prove the required integrability in the unsmoothed norm separately. [Acta Arithmetica 126 (2007), 27–55](https://www.impan.pl/shop/publication/transaction/download/product/82881)
- DFMR I and II work with a continuous dilation class and auxiliary smoothing. The express purpose is Hardy characterization and explicit zero-free regions. Their continuous induction and kernel weights cannot substitute for controlling an ordinary length-$N$ upper. [DFMR I](https://arxiv.org/abs/1101.1199) · [DFMR II](https://arxiv.org/abs/1112.0166)
- Oliveira's single-fixed-Dirichlet-polynomial theorem is an arithmetic cutoff model with a Burnol-type lower bound, but its lower constant counts distinct target-line zeros without a squared multiplicity weight. It is useful for separating finite-prime polynomial phenomena from genuine $L$-function asymptotics. [Oliveira](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.01234v1)
- The analogues in Nikolski, Burnol's co-Poisson synthesis, and Báez-Duarte's strong-kernel theorem identify different continuous zero detectors, phase operators, or targets. They clarify the mechanism and the necessity of checking which norm and which dilation set occur. Their claims do not identify a finite character-distance upper. [Nikolski](https://doi.org/10.5802/aif.1451) · [Burnol's co-Poisson treatment](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0112254v1) · [Báez-Duarte](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0505453)
