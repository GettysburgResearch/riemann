# Verified statements and normalization for the general L-function source survey

## The exact lower-bound transfer

For a fixed primitive nonprincipal Dirichlet character $\chi$, put
$$
d_{N,\chi}^{\,2}=\inf_{A_N(s)=\sum_{n\le N}a_n n^{-s}}\frac1{2\pi}\int_\mathbb R
\frac{|1-L(1/2+it,\chi)A_N(1/2+it)|^2}{1/4+t^2}\,dt.
$$
The coefficients are unrestricted complex numbers. Let $\Psi_F(u)=\operatorname{Res}_{s=1}(F(s)u^s/s)-\sum_{n\le u}a_F(n)$ for $F(s)=\sum a_F(n)n^{-s}$. In the present primitive nonprincipal case the residue vanishes. Periodicity and the zero sum over a full period imply $\Psi_F(u)=O_\chi(1)$, while $\Psi_F(1/x)=0$ for $x>1$. Thus the integrability hypothesis of de Roton's Theorem 2 holds unconditionally. Mellin transformation gives
$$
\mathcal M[\Psi_F(\alpha/x)](s)=-\frac{\alpha^sF(s)}s,\qquad
\mathcal M[\mathbf1_{(0,1)}](s)=\frac1s.
$$
For the first formula, integrate the cumulative sum first on $\Re s>1$ and continue by the bounded partial sums to $\Re s>0$. With the unnormalized Mellin transform the Plancherel measure on $\Re s=1/2$ is $dt/(2\pi)$. Taking $\alpha=1/n$ for $n\le N$ identifies $d_{N,\chi}$ with the corresponding discrete approximation distance. Its subspace is included in de Roton's continuous span with $\alpha\in[1/N,1]$, for which she proves
$$
\liminf_{\lambda\to0^+}\log(1/\lambda)D_F(\lambda)^2
\ge \sum_{\Re\rho=1/2}\frac{m_F(\rho)^2}{|\rho|^2}.
$$
Consequently the same lower bound holds for $\liminf_N(\log N)d_{N,\chi}^2$. No conductor-uniform bound or simplicity assumption is used. Distinct critical-line zeros index the sum, with their multiplicities included as squares. For the principal primitive character the source handles $\zeta$ by the complementary function with the residue included. No condition $A_N(1)=0$ is needed for this lower transfer because the continuous span used in Theorem 2 is the unrestricted one.

De Roton's p. 420 theorem text says *bande critique*, whereas the accompanying discussion expressly says only critical-line zeros contribute and the vectors used in §§6–7 are indexed by $\Re\rho=1/2$. The line-sum assertion above agrees with that construction. The wording is harmless for this transfer. As §6.1 observes, if there is a zero to the right, the distance stays bounded below and the logarithmic lower bound is immediate. Otherwise the Selberg symmetry puts the interior strip zeros on the critical line. Specifically, the finite Gram limit in §7 is indexed by a finite chosen subset of critical-line zeros and their orders, and one increases that subset after taking the cutoff limit. [Published Theorem 2 and §§6–7](../sources/prefix_formula62_generalL/deroton_lower.pdf)

## Exact discrete distance versus relaxed interpolation

For a Dirichlet $L(s,\chi)$ and $1<p\le2$, Dimitrov–Oliveira Theorem 2 says that $L(s,\chi)$ is nonzero on $\Re s>1/p$ if and only if the ordinary cutoff distance tends to zero. Its squared norm is
$$
\inf_{A_N}\frac1{2\pi}\int_\mathbb R
\frac{|1-L(1/p+it,\chi)A_N(1/p+it)|^2}{p^{-2}+t^2}\,dt.
$$
Theorem 3 instead minimizes the same rational-weight norm of an ordinary polynomial $B_N$ constrained only by $B_N(1/p+it_j)=1$ for finitely many distinct fixed real $t_j$. It obtains uniqueness for all sufficiently large $N$ and squared error asymptotic to $(\log N)^{-1}\sum_j(p^{-2}+t_j^2)^{-1}$. The theorem supplies no factorization of $1-B_N$ by the given $L$-function. The distinction persists when the interpolation points are actual zeros. [Theorems 2–3, arXiv v1](../sources/threshold62_do/1608.07887v1.pdf)

Oliveira's 2019 paper instead takes a fixed Dirichlet polynomial $P(s)=\sum_{j\le m} a_jj^{-s}$, $a_1\ne0$, and $\kappa_r(y)=\sum_{j\le y}a_jj^{-(r-1/2)}$. For any real $r$, Theorem 1 characterizes nonvanishing on $\Re s>r$ by convergence to zero of the $L^2(0,\infty)$ distance $d_{N,r}$ from the indicator of $(0,1)$ to the span of $\kappa_r(1/(nx))$, $n\le N$. Theorem 2 obtains $\liminf_N d_{N,r}^2\log N\ge\sum_{\Re\rho_P=r}|\rho_P-r+1/2|^{-2}$. The theorem uses distinct interpolation zeros without multiplicity-squared correction. [Theorems 1–2](../sources/prefix_formula62_generalL/oliveira.pdf)

## Continuous generalizations and integrability

- **de Roton 2005, Theorems 2.3, 3.2 and 4.2.** For the stated general Dirichlet-series continuation and coefficient hypotheses, integrability of $\Psi_F(1/x)$ gives the zero-free equivalence in $\Re s>1/2$ with the constrained continuous dilates dense in $L^2(0,1)$. For Selberg $F$ her Theorem 3.2 includes integrability in the equivalent criterion. Theorem 4.2 replaces the complementary function by the order-$k$ Riesz-smoothed version, choosing integer $k>d/4$ for degree $d$, to avoid the unsmoothed degree restriction. That complementary function has Mellin denominator $s(s+1)\cdots(s+k)$. The test span uses continuous dilation parameters and the pole-cancellation conditions in equation (1). [Published note, §§2–4](../sources/prefix_formula62_generalL/deroton_note.pdf)
- **de Roton 2007, Acta Arithmetica, Theorem 2.2 and §10.** The extended Selberg class with the weak axiom $\sum_{n\le x}|a_n|^2\ll_\varepsilon x^{1+\varepsilon}$ has complementary summatory-error mean square on $[1,x]$ bounded by $O(x^{2-1/d})$ when $0<d<3$ and $O_\varepsilon(x^{3-4/d+\varepsilon})$ when $d\ge3$. For $0<d<4$ either exponent can be made strictly less than two. Dyadic summation gives $\int_1^\infty|\Psi_F(u)|^2u^{-2}du<\infty$, and the residue-polynomial behavior on $0<u<1$ is integrable with the reciprocal change of variables. This is an input into the lower-bound assumption, with no upper approximation rate in this result. [Published theorem and application](../sources/prefix_formula62_generalL/deroton_meansquare.pdf)
- **Delaunay–Fricain–Mosaki–Robert I, Theorems 2.1, 2.4, 2.5.** The setup assumes $a_n\ll_\varepsilon n^\varepsilon$, meromorphic continuation to $\Re s>\sigma_0$ with only a possible pole at one, finite order after its removal, and an auxiliary compactly supported test $\varphi$ with the stated endpoint bounds. No Euler product or functional equation is required for the general theorem. At a line $r>\sigma_0$, Theorem 2.1 equates the summatory-error norm $L^2((1,\infty),du/u^{1+2r})$ to $L(r+it)\widehat\varphi(r+it)\in L^2(dt)$, with $r=1$ excluded in the pole case. Under zero-free $\widehat\varphi$ on $\Re s>r$, its real-axis exponential-type normalization, and $a_1\ne0$, Theorem 2.4 equates zero-freeness of $L$ on $\Re s>r$ to vanishing Hardy-kernel distance in the pole-cancelled continuous span. Their nonprincipal-character application uses the $L^2((0,1),dt/t)$ distance from $t^{1-r}$ to finite sums of $t^r\sum_{n<\alpha/t}\chi(n)$, $0<\alpha\le1$, and deduces no real zero for $\sigma>1-C/\log q$ when the squared distance satisfies condition (2.4). This is an implication from a distance bound, with no conductor-uniform input bound asserted. [Author PDF](../sources/prefix_formula62_generalL/dfmr1.pdf)
- **Delaunay–Fricain–Mosaki–Robert II, Corollaries 4.4–4.5.** The unconstrained continuous-span Hardy distance is comparable to its pole-cancelled counterpart. Under the specified $\psi$ integrability and transform hypotheses, distance zero for one test power is equivalent to distance zero for every such power, inclusion of $L^2_*((0,1),dt/t^{1-2\sigma_0})$ in the closed span, and absence of zeros on $\Re s>r$. The stated range is $r_0\le r<1$. The conclusion is about the full continuous span and the smoothed Mellin kernel where needed. [Author PDF](../sources/prefix_formula62_generalL/dfmr2.pdf)

## Neighboring exact mechanisms

- **Burnol, 2000, Theorems 1.7 and 1.11.** For a global field $K$ the abelian Riemann hypothesis is equivalent to orthogonality of the incoming and outgoing subspaces $D_-$ and $D_+$ in the idèle-class Hilbert space. It is also equivalent to the equality of the closure of $V E(\mathcal S_{\le1})$ with the norm-supported subspace $H^2$. This addresses abelian extensions and Hecke sectors but the cutoff is adelic support, without the ordinary $n\le N$ residual or multiplicity rate. [arXiv v3](../sources/prefix_formula62_generalL/burnol_adelic.pdf)
- **Burnol, co-Poisson, Theorem 5.2.** For zeta, the continuous distance from $\mathbf 1_{(0,1)}$ to dilations of its fractional-part function over $\lambda\le\theta\le1$ has squared logarithmic lower constant $\sum_{\Re\rho=1/2}m_\rho^2/|\rho|^2$. This text restates the original Burnol bound and discusses the almost-orthogonal zero-indexed vectors. Its broader co-Poisson/abelian theory is different from a discrete approximation rate. [arXiv v1, §5](../sources/prefix_formula62_generalL/burnol_copoisson.pdf)
- **Nikolski, Corollaries 4.1–4.2.** For the prescribed continuous generator span in $L^2((0,1),dx/x)$, a single zero Hardy-kernel distance characterizes a zero-free right half-plane for zeta. A finite-dimensional subspace distance also supplies a zero-free disc. This provides a predecessor for the changed target used by DFMR, without an ordinary logarithmic cutoff estimate. [Published paper, §4](../sources/prefix_formula62_generalL/nikolski.pdf)
- **Báez-Duarte, Theorems 1.2–1.3.** For a good kernel $f\in C^1(0,\infty)\cap L^1$ with the weighted derivative integrability in Definition 1.1, RH implies approximation of $f$ in $L^2(0,\infty)$ by integer contractions of the Müntz transform $Pf$. For compact support the converse forces every off-line zero of zeta in the stated strip to be a zero of the Mellin transform of $f$. Choosing a Mellin transform free of these zeros yields the equivalence. This is a change of test function in the zeta problem, with no fixed-character optimal constant. [arXiv text, §1](../sources/prefix_formula62_generalL/baez_general.pdf)

## Existing upper-bound comparator for zeta

Bettin–Conrey–Farmer Theorem 1 concerns the same integer-cutoff \(dt/(2\pi(1/4+t^2))\) norm with \(F=\zeta\). It gives squared residual asymptotic \((2+\gamma-\log(4\pi))/\log N\) for \(V_N(s)=\sum_{n\le N}\mu(n)(1-\log n/\log N)n^{-s}\), conditional on RH and \(\sum_{|\Im\rho|\le T}|\zeta'(\rho)|^{-2}\ll T^{3/2-\delta}\) for some \(\delta>0\). The latter entails simplicity. The theorem is the prior exact-constant benchmark in the zeta specialization and has no fixed-character GRH-alone consequence as stated. [Theorem 1](../sources/ratio62_priority/bettin_optimal.pdf)
