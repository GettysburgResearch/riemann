# Historical transitions inspected in the spectral track

Scope: four primary works; this is a source-grounded partial history, not coverage of the whole period.

- **1859:** Riemann replaces a raw prime-counting problem by analytic continuation, functional-equation symmetry, inverse Mellin transforms and zeros. The crucial transition is an exact prime/zero relation rather than an estimate from primes alone. The half-value convention at jumps and ordering of zero sums already matter (PAPER-S001,p4–8).
- **1999:** Berry–Keating translate the explicit formula into requirements on an unknown dynamical spectrum. Their candidate XP expression explains homogeneous modes and gamma phases but leaves compactification/domain unresolved. Their own discussion warns that a formal ζ-factor does not justify selecting its zeros (PAPER-S002,§6).
- **2010/2011:** Endres–Steiner classify fixed finite-graph self-adjoint domains and prove the resulting spectral count has the wrong order. This is a productive narrowing of a model class, not a refutation of Hilbert–Pólya (PAPER-S003,Proposition9.1 and Theorem15.6).
- **2018/2020/2021:** Rodgers–Tao convert negative de Bruijn–Newman time into a quantitative local-relaxation contradiction with known gap statistics. The theorem is unconditional; RH remains the endpoint assertionΛ=0. A2021 repair explicitly addresses boundary-energy control in the passage from time-averaged to endpoint energy (PAPER-S004,§§7–9,v5).

The recurring obstacle in these otherwise different settings is control of the limiting object: a formal eigenfunction versus an L² spectral state, a finite truncation versus an infinite distribution, or an averaged-in-time energy versus a terminal-time energy. These are distinct mathematical statements; they require separate proofs.
