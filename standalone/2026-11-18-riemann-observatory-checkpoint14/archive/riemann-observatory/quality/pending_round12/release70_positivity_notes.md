The pending wave contains 46 new work identities screened by normalized title, DOI and arXiv identifier against the canonical bibliography, all contributor records and pending shards. It records 45 pinned full-text PDFs and one pinned publisher extract. Four sources were read completely at Level 1. The other 42 records retain Level 0 and explicitly mark incomplete targeted extraction. The selection covers historical root estimates, entire-function approximation, operator preservers and explicit sign conjecture obstructions. These entries report source results within their stated scope.

## Historical sign conjectures and operator limitations

- **Odlyzko and te Riele, 1985.** The actual published scan, pp. 138–160, is present. Sections 1 and 4–5 give \(\limsup M(x)/\sqrt{x}>1.06\) and \(\liminf M(x)/\sqrt{x}<-1.009\), refuting the unit-constant conjecture. The lattice calculation gives oscillation and existence without naming an integer violation. Best–Trudgian and Hurst have separate finite-data and oscillation results. Kim–Nguyen use Pintz’s implication to obtain an existential upper bound on the least counterexample. These facts concern different quantifiers. [Published scan](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/1823/1823D.pdf)
- **Haselgrove, 1958.** Cambridge exposes the paper's opening definition and Dirichlet-series identity. The accessible Cambridge record establishes edition identity. Full-text downloads from Cambridge, Wiley and the located facsimile mirror returned HTML or denied access. The substantive methodology is available through the later primary paper by Borwein, Ferguson and Mossinghoff, §1 equations (3)–(7). It records the positive smoothed unweighted Liouville value at height cutoff 1,000 and phase 831.847 using 649 zeros, and separately the negative weighted Turán sum implication. The off-line-zero and multiple-zero alternatives are treated through Ingham. Haselgrove’s record remains a publisher extract and does not claim the original proof was read. [Original publisher extract](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/mathematika/article/abs/disproof-of-a-conjecture-of-polya/F44F62DF2736A41AD4EEAA858DC952F5) · [Later primary source](https://www.cecm.sfu.ca/~pborwein/PAPERS/P208.pdf)
- **de Branges.** Conrey–Li’s critique and Suzuki’s canonical-system paper already had corpus records. The newly accessed 13 April 2017 author manuscript is a historical proposal. Its stated reduction uses quaternionic arithmetic zeta functions and an accretive Radon transformation. The specific earlier positivity conditions analyzed by Conrey–Li do not by themselves identify which prerequisites of the 2017 manuscript hold. [Author manuscript](https://www.math.purdue.edu/~branges/proof-riemann-2017-04.pdf) · [Conrey–Li](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9812166)

## Complete substantive readings

- **PAPER-REL70-INGHAM.** All seven printed pages, §§1–3 and references. Theorem 1 uses a symmetric triangular frequency window and its nonnegative sinc-squared kernel. Landau’s nonnegative-tail argument gives the needed one-sided Laplace control, and Riemann–Lebesgue gives the vanishing residual. Theorem 2 treats simple line zeros, using a rectangular contour and the \(T^k\log T\) main term to force divergence of the sum of residue magnitudes. Theorem A uses Kronecker phase alignment only for an independent finite segment or tail; its proof already handles off-line and multiple-zero alternatives. Integral endpoints and OCR signs were interpreted with the proof’s two separate limsup/liminf arguments. Independence of zeta ordinates remains an input of the relevant case. [Printed source scan](https://personal.math.ubc.ca/~gerg/teaching/592-Fall2018/papers/1942.Ingham.pdf)
- **PAPER-REL70-MERTENS-HISTORY.** All seven pages, §§1–4, acknowledgement and references. This is the disproof coauthor’s historical account. Section 2 states the analytic consequence of the conjecture and refers to the separate proof. Section 3 traces Stieltjes’ letter and incomplete claimed argument, Mertens’ computed range, and the distinction between an empirical induction and a proof. Section 4 concerns the collaboration and publication record. [Printed source scan](https://ir.cwi.nl/pub/2518/2518D.pdf)
- **PAPER-REL70-1504-01524.** All six pages, Theorem 1 and proof, including the negative and complex parameter cases. The proof compares the partial theta function to its bilateral Jacobi extension near the simple bilateral zeros and uses a product lower bound away from them. The geometric zero locations then give convergence of the normalized genus-zero product. Positive real spectral intervals use the imported count of nonreal conjugate pairs. For negative real parameters, the distant zeros follow alternating real signs. The order-zero factorization can be read directly from Hadamard’s theorem. The general phrase on products of equal-order functions is stronger than needed for this step. [Exact sourced preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1504.01524v1)
- **PAPER-REL70-ENTIRE.** All three printed pages and the archive cover. Pétrovitch starts from an entire function of finite genus, an upper bound on the weighted maximum modulus and the value at zero. A Cauchy-derived coefficient bound is inserted into his prior least-root estimate based on squared coefficient magnitudes. He then fixes a positive radius to obtain constants depending on genus and reduces a nonzero level-value problem to a zero of \(F-C\). When the relevant constant term vanishes, the zero at the origin gives the trivial radius bound. [Numdam scan](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.24033/bsmf.711.pdf)

## Selection and limits

The two different partial-theta uses are coefficient criteria on a particular family and location estimates for zeros in the parameter-dependent model. The multiplier sequence records specify the operator basis when the theorem is extracted. The exact contents of the less central openings remain marked incomplete. The selection ledger retains known duplicates and deferred candidates. In particular a conjectured Mertens bound was deferred, and the prime reciprocal sums named after Mertens were kept out of this sign-conjecture wave.

The new finite-order ξ positivity preprint is indexed at opening scope because it is directly relevant to the archive’s finite-versus-global distinction. Its large finite-order claim depends on verified finite zero height and additional arguments whose numerical certificate and transfer remain unassessed in this reading.
