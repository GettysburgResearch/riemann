# Primary analytic sources for zero counts, mean values and exponential sums

The proposed shard contains 54 distinct full-text works. Four have whole-work readings, and the other 50 have targeted readings. Every targeted record marks that extraction can be incomplete. Where a scan or a short reading supplied only the introduction or an earlier cited theorem, the source locator and result state that boundary. The source-stated results are not proof certifications.

The deduplication pass used the read-only SQLite index, the current bibliography, every paper shard including analytic65, geometry65 and release65, and the pending material present at the time of the pass. The SQLite count was 701, so it was supplemented with the JSONL inputs. The receipt records four near-title pairs checked manually. Two are pairs of different works in the same area, Hasanalizade–Shen–Wong versus Littlewood and Gao’s continuous zeta sums versus Milinovich’s discrete derivative moments. The other two compare Kobayashi’s general irrational-spacing progression with his earlier unit-spacing quantitative result, and the Cicek–Hamieh–Ng ordinary divisor theorem with Hamieh–Ng’s higher-divisor conditional theorem. ## Whole-work readings

- **J. Arias de Reyna, arXiv:2406.08890v1, seven pages.** Lemma 1 removes the additive \(1/2\) in the argument-variation estimate by applying the preliminary bound to every positive integral power of a holomorphic function nonzero on the segment. Its initial symmetric holomorphic function uses the reflected conjugate \(\overline{g(\bar z)}\). In Theorem 3 the top contour is controlled by this disk estimate, with the deformed left side at \(\sigma=1-At^{2/5}\log t\). Expanding the four phase factors there gives the square-root correction to the auxiliary-function zero count. The proof first takes a height without a boundary zero. The inclusive convention follows by approaching any endpoint from above and using local finiteness in the bounded horizontal range. The source depends on separate auxiliary-function bounds in its references [3]–[5]. The count is for the auxiliary function, so applying it to critical-line \(\zeta\) zeros would require the corresponding location information. [Pinned text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.08890v1)
- **Michel Weber, arXiv:0907.4767v1, six pages.** Lemma 2 expands a positive integer moment against translated triangular weights and uses their nonnegative Fourier transforms for coefficientwise majorization. Theorem 1 averages random signs and uses Khintchine–Kahane. For this theorem one can also integrate on a centered triangular weight directly. For nonnegative coefficients the frequency-paired terms in the nonnegative-transform expansion have nonnegative contribution. Keeping only the index-matched terms already gives an absolute lower constant relative to \((\sum a_n^2)^q\). This test isolates the positivity hypothesis. The unrestricted coefficient inequality in (5) cannot include coincident cancelling frequencies. In the Corollary 3 proof, the \(L^2\) mean of the already-powered sum is the \(q=1\) application of Theorem 1. Its absolute coefficients are multiplicative convolution counts and one must keep support to \(N^\nu\) before restricting back to \(m\le N\). [Pinned text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0907.4767v1)
- **Fan Ge, arXiv:1512.06419v2, nine pages.** Theorem 1 sharpens the derivative-zero count under RH using the argument of \(G(s)=-2^s\zeta'(s)/\log2\) in Lemmas 3–5. Lemma 8 combines Zhang’s integrated positive kernel over consecutive \(\zeta\)-zero ordinates with a lower bound for the subtended angle of each \(\zeta'\)-zero in its small rectangle. Section 3 splits the horizontal argument into this rectangle, critical-line derivative zeros, and a distant contribution using the argument angle. Section 4 uses concentric rectangles to prove Theorem 2; its angle argument extends Lemma 8 to the square-sized rectangles used there. The floor term in (5) of Theorem 2 is \(\sqrt{\log T}\,\log\log T\), with \(\log\log T\) outside the radical, consistent with the final estimate from (13). No claim about inverse-derivative masses is extracted. [Pinned text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1512.06419v2)
- **Michel Balazard and Oswaldo Velásquez Castañón, 2009, four pages in French.** Theorem and Proposition 1 characterize the extremal left location using a signed comparison sum and \(-\log 2\) as the limiting ratio. Proposition 2 constructs a completely multiplicative \(\{\pm1\}\)-valued function with the required sign at \(n\) and opposite signs through \(n-k\), for fixed \(k\) and sufficiently large \(n\). Proposition 4 controls the squarefree parts of these consecutive integers. If three have only prime factors \(\le k\), Proposition 3 reduces the possible triples to integral points on \(Y^2=X(X-\alpha)(X-\beta)\), with \(0<\alpha<\beta\), and uses Siegel finiteness. Section 4 assigns a separate large prime to every relevant neighboring squarefree part except for the specified small-prime cases. Bohr equivalence lifts the sign construction back to zeros of the finite sums in a half-plane. The onset is not computed, and the theorem concerns extremal zeros of finite sums. [Publisher facsimile](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.1016/j.crma.2009.02.008.pdf)

## Transfer and source boundaries

- In the explicit count results, several functions have different count normalizations. The Dedekind and primitive Dirichlet constants include archimedean or parity terms. The zeta count gives its own parameter range, argument branch and endpoint convention. A Turing conclusion needs the finite list of Gram blocks and a separate completeness argument.
- In the mean-value results, the same formal Dirichlet monomial can have different leading terms under ordinary height, arithmetic progression, zero counting, and mollified averaging. The record points to the actual sampling law. Particular weight assumptions in longer-polynomial theorems, such as additive-divisor and smoothing hypotheses, are carried separately from the length.
- In the historical facsimiles, exact formulas are not claimed where text extraction lost inequalities or special characters. Four Russian MathNet originals could not be retrieved as verified PDFs and were omitted. The two TIFR endpoints tested returned HTTP 404. Other archived staging candidates were not nominated when a precise useful transfer had not been extracted. The archive contains private source copies, identity receipts and PDF checksums.
