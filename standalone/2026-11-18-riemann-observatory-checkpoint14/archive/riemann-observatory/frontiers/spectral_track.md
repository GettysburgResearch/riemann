# Spectral and heat-flow frontier: inspected portion

Literature cutoff2026-11-17; search coverage is selective. No claim to identify the best theorem in every spectral program. Connes, de Branges, canonical systems, transfer operators and arithmetic cohomology remain to be read.

## Finite compact first-order graphs

Established source result: Endres–Steiner's exact self-adjoint-domain classification and secular determinant, with fixed-graph no-go from linear count versus TlogT. Precise missing ingredient for a successful related construction: escape the finite-channel, fixed-domain first-order model while retaining a single self-adjoint realization and a noncircular arithmetic spectral identity. This is structural, not a missing numerical fit.

Meaningful incremental result: a controlled auxiliary realization theorem for an energy-dependent scattering matrix, followed by exact explicit-formula matching on a stated test-function class. The independent campaign note derives a three-height necessary channel-complexity lower bound; it is an elementary calculation under review, not claimed new.

Next tests: use genuinely non-diagonal unitary couplings and verify the phase count bound; replace a scalar potential by an auxiliary dynamical channel and identify exactly which hypothesis fails. Bounded real scalar first-order edge potentials gauge away and do not help.

## de Bruijn–Newman flow

Established inspected result:Λ≥0 (Rodgers–Tao Theorem1), with RH equivalent toΛ=0. An upper bound tending all the way to0 would be consequential, but a positive upper bound does not establish RH. This track did not audit the current numerical upper-bound literature and supplies no world-record claim.

Bottleneck for technique transplantation: localized energy and boundary flux must be controlled before converting integrated dissipation to an endpoint bound. The corrected v5 source is especially instructive because it names this issue explicitly. A meaningful incremental theorem is a general sufficient criterion for that terminal-energy passage in a new zero-flow family, with a checked tail estimate.

## Explicit formulas and distributional trace identities

Riemann's memoir already warns that individual termwise integrals may fail to converge and that zero-sum order matters. Berry–Keating's prime sum on the critical line is explicitly divergent before smoothing. A candidate spectral formula must therefore state a test space and convergence mode. Equality of low zeros, smooth density or finite oscillatory plots is not equality of spectral measures.

Concrete problem: choose one Fourier convention and a strip-analytic test class; reconstruct both prime and graph sides, including trivial-zero/gamma contributions, then determine whether any proposed equality holds as distributions. Potential counterexample: a model with correct leading density but no K-scale dyadic count curvature.
