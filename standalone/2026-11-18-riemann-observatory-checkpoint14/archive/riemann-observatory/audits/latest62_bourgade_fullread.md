# Bourgade's mesoscopic fluctuations of the zeta zeros

Full reading of arXiv:0902.1757v1, all 20 PDF pages, including all proofs in §§2–4, diagrams and references. The author-hosted PDF was spot-compared for Corollary 1.3, outside the certified whole-read version. This audit concerns mesoscopic finite-dimensional distributions, so it supplies neither microscopic GUE convergence nor tightness of a function-valued process.

Theorem 1.1 samples \(\omega\) uniformly on \((0,1)\), fixes the finite number of strictly ordered bounded nonnegative shifts \(f_t^{(j)}\), and evaluates \(\log\zeta(\tfrac12+\epsilon_t+i(\omega t+f_t^{(j)}))\). For \(\epsilon_t\to0\), \(\epsilon_t\gg1/\log t\), the scale is \(\sqrt{-\log\epsilon_t}\) and the pair exponents are \(c_{ij}=\lim\log|f_t^{(j)}-f_t^{(i)}|/\log\epsilon_t\). The diagonal covariance is one, the off-diagonal \(1\wedge c_{ij}\), for a centered circular complex Gaussian vector. For \(\epsilon_t\ll1/\log t\), the scale is \(\sqrt{\log\log t}\) and \(c_{ij}=\lim-\log|f_t^{(j)}-f_t^{(i)}|/\log\log t\); that includes the critical line \(\epsilon_t=0\). Exponents must exist, allowing \(+\infty\). These regime statements use the paper's big-O comparison convention, not a uniform assertion over every varying offset. The zeta result uses no RH.

Section 2 proves the CUE counterpart in Theorem 1.4, with Haar dimension \(n\), radial displacement \(\epsilon_n\), ordered angles bounded away from a full revolution, and \(1/n,\sqrt{\log n}\) in place of \(1/\log t,\sqrt{\log\log t}\). Expand the exterior complex log as a sum of traces. Diaconis–Evans detects the variance with \(\min(j,n)\), and weights above \(j=\lfloor n/\log n\rfloor\) or its displacement-adapted analogue have vanishing normalized variance. Lemma 2.2 uses the geometric logarithm and separates the radial and angular scales. Lemma 2.3 replaces the full series at the unit circle by the harmonic sum up to dimension \(n\), controlling both its oscillatory tail and the case of angles not tending to zero. The finite-dimensional statement follows by Cramér–Wold.

The zeta proof in §3 starts with Selberg's unconditional mean-square approximation by a prime sum. After normalization the error is negligible. Proposition 3.1 requires coefficients with vanishing maximum, finite limiting sum of squares, and a tail condition \(\sum_{p>m_t}|a_{p,t}|^2(1+p/t)\to0\) with \(\log m_t=o(\log t)\). Montgomery–Vaughan kills the high primes in mean square. For low primes, finite mixed moments of their correlated phases and independent phases agree asymptotically because a nonzero log-product frequency is at least a negative fixed power of \(m_t\). The independent phase model satisfies the triangular-array Gaussian limit, including convergence of the fixed moments by bounded-coefficient moment estimates. One can use \(1/\max(u_1,u_2)\ge m_t^{-\max(a,b)}\) directly for the frequency gap in Lemma 3.2. Lemmas 3.3–3.4 compute the covariance from the Euler logarithm near its pole and from prime partial summation at the smaller offset. The moving shift appears in these estimates, so this is more than applying scalar Selberg independently at each point.

Corollary 1.3 takes \(K_t\) bounded below, with \(\log K_t/\log\log t\to\delta\in[0,1)\), and centers the zero count in intervals \((\omega t+\alpha/K_t,\omega t+\beta/K_t]\) by the Riemann–von Mangoldt main term. The scale is \(\pi^{-1}\sqrt{(1-\delta)\log\log t}\). The stated limiting centered Gaussian process is \(D_\beta-D_\alpha\) with independent \(N(0,1/2)\) endpoint variables. In particular the covariance is
\[
 \frac12\big(
 \mathbf1_{\beta=\beta'}+\mathbf1_{\alpha=\alpha'}
 -\mathbf1_{\beta=\alpha'}-\mathbf1_{\alpha=\beta'}\big).
\]
The display (1.9) on preprint p. 4 explicitly lists the \(\beta=\alpha'\) opposite-endpoint case; symmetry or the explicit process on p. 5 gives the reverse case \(\alpha=\beta'\) too. This is the same display in the author-hosted copy inspected. Completely distinct endpoints, even for nested intervals, give independence in the limit. The scale and \(\delta<1\) do not give microscopic nearest-neighbor spacings. Section 4 proves positivity of the hierarchical covariance by an explicit Gaussian construction and connects the minimum-along-an-interval covariance to branching trees.
