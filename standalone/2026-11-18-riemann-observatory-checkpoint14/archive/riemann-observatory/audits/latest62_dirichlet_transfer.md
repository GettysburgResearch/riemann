# Adversarial audit of the fixed primitive Dirichlet transfer

17 November 2026. Scope includes the fixed-character proof in [the transplant audit](dirichlet_transplant_skeptic62.md), rStation 48 v2 §§2, 4–7 and 9.2, the corresponding [effective zeta comparison](adversarial_reviews/effective_cluster62_proof.md), and de Roton pp. 418–420, §6.1 and §7.2. No counterexample or additional hypothesis was found for the qualitative fixed-character transfer. This verdict uses the reconstructed grouped upper construction and ordinary GRH for the single fixed primitive nonprincipal character.

## Reciprocal, signs and short windows

The Borel–Carathéodory argument works for a disk centered at \(2+it\) of radius \(3/2-\eta/8\). GRH excludes zeros there for the chosen primitive character and the entire nonprincipal function has no pole. The Euler logarithm fixes the branch. The full-strip polynomial upper bound controls its real part, and a concentric disk of radius \(1/2\) has bounded absolute logarithm. Three circles yield \(o(\log |t|)\) for the absolute logarithm on radius \(3/2-\eta/2\). The point \(1/2+\eta+it\) is strictly interior and Cauchy's estimate there gives \(L'/L=o(\log |t|)\). Thus both \(L\) and \(1/L\) have subpower growth a fixed positive distance to the right, with constants dependent on that distance and \(\chi\). On the left, the primitive functional equation reads \(\Lambda_\chi(s)=\epsilon_\chi\overline{\Lambda_\chi(1-\bar s)}\). It transfers the same-character right-hand bound and multiplies \(1/L\) by a height factor of order \(|t|^{\sigma-1/2}\) on a fixed left line. Compact heights on \(1/2-\delta\) are zero-free. On the critical line only the bound for \(L\) is required at arbitrary height, obtained from the same functional equation and Phragmén–Lindelöf using arbitrarily small fixed offsets.

For the completed \(\Xi_\chi(w)=\Lambda_\chi(1/2+w)\), Hadamard's product contains \(w^{m_0}\) if there is a central zero. Its logarithmic derivative contains \(m_0/w\), the exponential constant, and corrected summands \(1/(w-i\gamma)+1/(i\gamma)\). At a regular point \(it_0\) of the imaginary axis, the real parts of all these summands, including \(m_0/(it_0)\) if present, vanish. Normal convergence of the corrected series applies locally there. The functional equation gives \(e^{-i\arg(\epsilon_\chi)/2}\Xi_\chi(it)\) real, so the real part of \(\Xi'_\chi(it_0)/\Xi_\chi(it_0)\) vanishes as well. This sets the real exponential constant to zero. It requires the reflection \(w\mapsto-\bar w\) and does not require a symmetry \(\gamma\mapsto-\gamma\) within one character.

At distance \(\eta\) the real log derivative is then the positive sum of Poisson kernels over all ordinates. Stirling and the fixed-offset log derivative make its leading term \(\tfrac12\log(q|t|/(2\pi))\). In the count of ordinates \(|\gamma-T|\le h\), integration on \([T-h-\kappa,T+h+\kappa]\) pays \(2\arctan(\kappa/\eta)\) per counted zero, while the integral is \((h+\kappa+o(1))\log|T|\). The upper coefficient after taking \(|T|\to\infty\), then \(\eta\downarrow0\), then \(\kappa\downarrow0\), is \(h/\pi\). This is valid for a tiny *fixed* \(h\), and for either sign of \(T\). The moving cluster midpoint causes no problem since the limit estimate is uniform once \(|T|\) exceeds the fixed threshold. With \(h=2D/3\), the coefficient \(2D/(3\pi)\) fits the cap \(D\). In good-height selection, integrate the logarithmic kernel over each fixed interval of length \(D/3\). Near ordinates cost at most \(2\pi\eta\) each in the integral. Unit-window counts and the quadratic kernel decay control the remote ordinates. Let \(\eta\) be small after \(D\) and the target exponent are fixed. The good-height horizontal estimate follows with a single constant for each fixed character and with independently selected positive and negative heights.

## Sharp constant and integer polynomials

I checked the sign and the residue in the retained-edge replacement. With the left edge \(a=1/2-\delta\) oriented upward, Cauchy gives \(F'=\sum_C R_C'-J_0\). The Perron integral gives \((\log N)V_N=(\log N)F+F'+\sum_C B_C+J_N\), where \(F=1/L_\chi\) and \(V_N\) has reciprocal coefficients \(\mu(n)\chi(n)\) and first-order cutoff. Hence the leftover edge in the normalized residual is \(L_\chi(J_0-J_N)/\log N\), whose norm is \(O_\chi(1/\log N)\). No trivial zero lies in the deformation strip. Sending the right edge to infinity for the derivative identity costs a power of its real location by absolute convergence, rather than requiring any control far to the left.

The contour expression per cell gives the same indicator by the residue \(L_\chi(s)F(s)=1\). With asymmetric upper and lower endpoints, finite telescoping includes empty rectangles and gives the indicator on the union of the two tails. Equations (47)–(49) apply at each endpoint because their weights involve absolute ordinate. Bounds in (33)–(41) require only fixed-character constants on contours and near the critical line. The zeros are on the critical line so the translated convolution exponents have real frequencies; any repeated or central node is included by the simplex law. The global constant on the near-disk bound appears once, after the Cauchy factorial cancels the simplex volume. This preserves the numerical multiplicity envelope and summable delayed tail. At each fixed repaired node the top Laurent coefficient is \(m!/L_\chi^{(m)}(\rho)\); multiplication by \(L_\chi\) removes this unknown constant in the localized leading norm. The weight at a central node is \(1/|\rho|^2=4\).

There is no imposed coefficient reality or parity. The delayed and fixed repairs are sampled on all integers \(1\le n\le N\), including integers at ramified primes, which is permitted by the infimum in question. The fixed-Laurent arithmetic comparison uses the Euler sum for \(\zeta(1+s-\rho)\) because the sampling is unrestricted over integers, even though the residual multiplier is now \(L_\chi\). The critical-line bound for the latter suffices for the \(O_\chi((\log N)^{-1})\) norm error of each fixed profile. The uniform and fixed repairs yield the limsup by first taking \(N\to\infty\), and then exhausting fixed clusters. There is no normalization at \(s=1\) in this test. The elementary bound on squared multiplicities in a unit window by the square of the zero count supplies convergence of the target sum.

## Lower bound and the source wording

For the nonprincipal primitive Selberg-class function \(F=L_\chi\) the residue term at 1 in de Roton's definition is zero. The complementary function on \(x>0\) is \(\Psi_F^{(1)}(x)=-\sum_{n\le1/x}\chi(n)\), supported in \([0,1]\) up to null sets and bounded by the conductor because a full period sums to zero. Its Mellin transform is \(-L_\chi(s)/s\), checked initially by absolute convergence and then by analytic continuation to \(\Re s>0\) using boundedness. The \([0,1]\) indicator has transform \(1/s\). The subspace in the primary paper is the complex span of \(x\mapsto\Psi_F(\alpha/x)\) for \(\alpha\in[\lambda,1]\). Integer dilations correspond to \(\alpha=1/n\); the extra minus sign in the transform is absorbed in arbitrary complex coefficients. Mellin Plancherel therefore gives exactly \(D(1/N)\le\Delta_{N,\chi}\), with the \(dt/(2\pi|\tfrac12+it|^2)\) normalization. Inclusion is in this direction and requires no discretization of the lower bound.

De Roton's printed p. 420 says *bande critique* in the theorem, then states that only critical-line zeros enter. Section 6.1 reduces to nonvanishing to the right of the critical line, and §7.2 exhausts fixed zero sets by orthogonal projection with one multiplicity block per zero. Under GRH the discrepancy in wording does not affect this application. The displayed bound is a liminf for the unsquared distance times the square root of the logarithm. The fixed complex zero set needs no signed pairing, and squaring nonnegative quantities gives the target liminf with \(m_\rho^2/|\rho|^2\) per distinct zero. Thus the lower bound in the proposed comparison applies to the actual integer distance and does not require an additional reality, parity or simplicity assumption.

The result is conditional on GRH and on the grouped approximation argument checked with the cited reconstruction. This review gives no uniformity in the conductor or effective rate of convergence. A quantitative certificate would require explicit all-height thresholds and constants and, for a two-sided effective result, an effective lower estimate. These do not follow from the qualitative limit.
