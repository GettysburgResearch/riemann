# Adversarial check of Dirichlet and fixed Selberg-class approximation

The director's fixed primitive-character proof checks out at the analytic logarithm, completed functional equation, very short fixed-window count, signs of the retained edge, signed mesh, arithmetic cutoff, and use of the published lower bound. The proof uses arbitrary complex coefficients throughout. It includes a central zero once, and its zeta quadrature term comes solely from sampling over the integers. It assumes no RH for zeta when the fixed primitive character is nonprincipal. The proof has fixed-character constants and the publication priority of the upper statement remains open. [Director proof](adversarial_reviews/director_dirichlet_transfer_round6.md) · [De Roton, BSMF 134 (2006), Theorem 2](../sources/prefix_formula62_generalL/deroton_lower.pdf)

## Removing the primitive-character restriction

This step follows directly from the primitive theorem by comparing polynomial spaces. Let $\chi$ be induced by $\chi_*$, and let $P$ be the finite set of primes dividing the modulus of $\chi$ but not the conductor of $\chi_*$. Write
$$
L(s,\chi)=E(s)L(s,\chi_*),\qquad E(s)=\prod_{p\in P}(1-\chi_*(p)p^{-s}),\qquad Q=\prod_{p\in P}p.
$$
Every zero of $E$ lies on $\Re s=0$, so the critical-strip zeros and their multiplicities are unchanged. The polynomial $EA$ has length at most $QN$ when $A$ has length at most $N$. Hence $d_{N,\chi}\ge d_{QN,\chi_*}$ and $\log(QN)/\log N\to1$.

For the upper bound, $1/E=\sum_n b_n n^{-s}$ is absolutely convergent at $\Re s=1/4$ by the finite product of geometric series. Let $B_K$ be its truncation at $K=\lfloor\exp\sqrt{\log N}\rfloor$, and let $A_M^*$ be a near-minimizer for $\chi_*$ at $M=\lfloor N/K\rfloor$, with additive error $o((\log N)^{-1/2})$ in norm. The candidate $A_M^*B_K$ has length at most $N$. On $\Re s=1/2$,
$$
\sup|1-EB_K|\ll_E K^{-1/4},\qquad
\|1-L_\chi A_M^*B_K\|_*
\le \|1-L_{\chi_*}A_M^*\|_*+
 O_E(K^{-1/4})\|L_{\chi_*}A_M^*\|_*.
$$
The last norm is bounded, and $\log M/\log N\to1$. This proves the matching primitive constant for every fixed induced character, including principal characters induced by the conductor-one character under RH. It uses the inducing primitive completion only. It also avoids assigning the imprimitive $L$-function to the Selberg class. The estimate is for a fixed Euler product $E$. [Primitive case](adversarial_reviews/director_dirichlet_transfer_round6.md)

## A fixed Selberg-class function

Let $F$ be a fixed member of the Selberg class with no zeros in $\Re s>1/2$ and with its allowed pole at 1. Let $m$ be the actual pole order and $d=2\sum_j\lambda_j$ its degree, where $\lambda_j>0$ and $\Re\mu_j\ge0$ in the gamma factors. The same optimized norm and multiplicity-square constant follow under this hypothesis. The following checks concern the steps beyond the degree-one proof. [Class axioms and published lower theorem](../sources/prefix_formula62_generalL/deroton_lower.pdf) · [Reusable upper construction](../sources/ratio62_q1_priority/rstation_48_v2.md)

**Reciprocal and pole cancellation.** The Selberg Euler logarithm has first-prime coefficient $b_p=a_p$, by comparing Dirichlet series on a far right line. Ramanujan bounds make the first-prime part converge absolutely for $\Re s>1$. Higher powers converge there because $|b_{p^k}|\ll p^{k\theta}$ with $\theta<1/2$ and $k\ge2$, so the prime sum is bounded by a constant times $\sum_p p^{-2(\sigma-\theta)}$. Exponentiating the absolute series proves absolute convergence of the reciprocal Dirichlet series there. Boundedness on $\Re s\ge2$ and absolute convergence at the Perron line are the two requirements. Bounded individual reciprocal coefficients are unnecessary. The function $H(s)=F(s)((s-1)/(s+A))^m$, for fixed $A>0$, is analytic and zero-free in $\Re s>1/2$, including at $s=1$. Its logarithm is bounded on the inner disks about $2+it$ for large $|t|$, because both the Euler logarithm and the additional rational logarithm are bounded there. A crude polynomial bound for $F$ on bounded vertical strips follows from the functional equation, Stirling and finite-order Phragmén–Lindelöf interpolation after pole cancellation. Borel–Carathéodory, three circles and Cauchy's estimate then yield subpower growth of $F,1/F$ at fixed right offsets and $F'/F=o(\log|t|)$ there. The gamma factor ratio in the functional equation is $O_F((2+|t|)^{d(1/2-\sigma)})$ at large heights. This follows since the numerator and denominator imaginary parts in each gamma ratio have equal absolute value, also for complex $\mu_j$. Interpolation across a sufficiently narrow strip gives the critical-line subpower estimate. Reflection gives the reciprocal on the fixed left contour.

**Completion and multiplicity.** The completed function $\Lambda_F(s)=Q_F^s\prod_j\Gamma(\lambda_j s+\mu_j)F(s)$ has only possible poles at $0,1$. On $\Re s>0$ the gamma factors have no poles, and the functional equation reflects each remaining potential gamma pole on $\Re s\le0$ to a point on $\Re s\ge1$ where the only possible pole is at 1. At 0 and 1 the pole orders are both exactly $m$ by reflection and the absence of a gamma pole at 1. The now-entire $\Xi_F(s)=(s(1-s))^m\Lambda_F(s)$ has order at most one. On the far right this follows from absolute convergence, polynomial factors and the gamma growth $e^{O_F(|s|\log(2+|s|))}$; on the far left it follows by reflection; on a bounded real strip polynomial vertical bounds on $F$ and Stirling give the same growth bound. By the zero-free assumption and reflection its zeros are on the critical line, where they coincide with the zeros of $F$, including multiplicities. At $0,1$ there are no zeros of the completion under the hypothesis. Centering at $1/2$, the same fixed-phase argument as for primitive characters removes the real exponential constant in the genus-one real logarithmic derivative. The Poisson smoothing argument therefore gives the short fixed-window multiplicity count $(dh/\pi+o_{F,h}(1))\log|T|$ on radius $h$. The gamma term in the inverse's horizontal defect remains favorable at large signed height, with coefficient $d/2$.

**Why a narrow fixed mesh suffices.** Use $D=10^{-4}/(1+d)$, $e=10^{-4}$, $\delta=D/100$. The cell width is at most $4D/3$, so $k_C\le10^{-4}\log T_C$ eventually, using $2dD/(3\pi)<10^{-4}$ and fixed-window counting. The radius-$D$ Cauchy bound now has multiplier $T_C^{dD+e}$. Thus the near squared exponent correction is at most $2dD+5e+2\cdot10^{-4}\log1200<0.002119$. The far estimate has correction at most $2e+10^{-4}\log200$ in norm. Retaining the two actual signed edge heights gives the same telescoped estimate. The quadrature powers stay strictly negative since $0<\delta\le10^{-6}$; making $D$ smaller changes fixed constants, including mesh density, but does not affect the powers. A pole of $F$ at 1 is a zero of the reciprocal and contributes no reciprocal residue. The full boundary norm imposes no value condition on the polynomial at 1. This covers higher-order poles such as $\zeta^2$ under RH, with its zeros having twice the zeta multiplicities. The retained-edge remainder, integer realization, and fixed repairs use the same equations as the primitive proof. [RST048 equations (25)–(41), (49), (51)–(61), (94)–(97)](../sources/ratio62_q1_priority/rstation_48_v2.md)

**Square-integrability of the complementary function.** Write
$$
\psi_F(x)=R_F(x)-\sum_{n\le 1/x}a_n,\qquad
R_F(x)=\operatorname{Res}_{s=1}\frac{F(s)x^{-s}}{s},\qquad x>0.
$$
If $F$ has a pole, $R_F(x)$ is $x^{-1}$ times a polynomial in $\log(1/x)$ and remains in the formula for $x>1$, where the sum vanishes. Do not cut it off at $x=1$. To identify $\psi_F$ rigorously, use a test $\phi\in C_c^\infty(0,\infty)$ against $dx/x$, with Mellin transform $\widehat\phi$. On a line $c>1$ the inverse-Mellin expression for the partial sum pairs to
$$
\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{(c)}\frac{F(s)}{s}\widehat\phi(-s)\,ds .
$$
One may interchange the series by absolute convergence and the rapid decay of the test, and inversion of $n^{-s}/s$ then gives the tested indicator $x<1/n$. Endpoint conventions change no integrals. In the fixed strip from $c$ to $1/2$, $\widehat\phi(-s)$ is rapidly decreasing uniformly and $F$ has polynomial growth at high height. Shift across $s=1$; its only residue pairs with $R_F$ by compact support. It follows that $\psi_F$ equals the inverse Mellin transform of $-F(s)/s$ on $1/2$ as a distribution on $(0,\infty)$. The critical-line subpower bound makes $F(s)/s$ square-integrable there. Mellin–Plancherel supplies an $L^2(dx)$ inverse. Pairing that inverse against $\phi(x)\,dx/x$ agrees with the displayed distribution, so $\psi_F$ equals it almost everywhere and is $L^2$. This argument checks the hypothesis of de Roton's Theorem 2 even when no unconditional mean-square estimate is supplied by a degree restriction. Its continuous dilation space at $1/N$ includes all integer dilations, with exact norm and no cutoff exponent loss. [De Roton 2006, pp. 419–420](../sources/prefix_formula62_generalL/deroton_lower.pdf)

The 2007 Acta Arithmetica publication gives estimates for the error term of the extended Selberg class, including the integrability input, rather than the optimized finite-polynomial upper constant. ## Full reading of Báez-Duarte's 10-page general strong criterion

This paper varies the target kernel $f$, while its Mellin multiplier is still $\zeta$. Theorem 1.2 gives RH $\Rightarrow f\in\overline{B(Pf)}$ for all good kernels $f$, and a converse zero-inclusion when $f$ is also compactly supported. Here $B$ uses integer dilations and $Pf(x)=\sum_{n\ge1}f(nx)-x^{-1}\int_0^\infty f(t)\,dt$. Theorem 1.3 assumes the Mellin transform of $f$ has no zeros in $1/2<\Re s<1$, so the inclusion forces RH by the zeta functional equation. This is relevant precedent for transferring discrete approximation by a dilation-commuting operator, while it supplies no general-$L$ matching upper constant. [Báez-Duarte, arXiv:math/0505453v1, pp. 1–3](../sources/prefix_formula62_generalL/baez_general.pdf)

The first mechanism is explicit. A good kernel lies in $C^1_0\cap L^1$ with $\int t|f'(t)|dt<\infty$, which implies $tf(t)\to0$. Burnol's estimate gives a.e. absolute dilate summation via $\sum_n\int_\varepsilon^\infty |f(nx)|dx/x=\int_\varepsilon^\infty |f(y)|\lfloor y/\varepsilon\rfloor dy/y\le \|f\|_{L^1(\varepsilon,\infty)}/\varepsilon$. Multiplicative Young's inequality bounds $T_Fg(x)=\int_0^\infty g(x/t)F(t)\,dt$ on $L^p$ by $\int_0^\infty t^{1/p}|F(t)|\,dt$. Integration by parts gives $Pf=T_{f'}\rho_1$ for $\rho_1(x)=\{1/x\}$ and $f=-T_{f'}\mathbf1_{(0,1]}$. I found two sign slips in the displayed intermediate limits on p. 6. The right sides of (2.8) and (2.9) should both carry a minus sign. Indeed $\int_0^T tf'(t)\,dt=Tf(T)-\int_0^T f(t)\,dt$, and the second integral equals $\lfloor T/x\rfloor f(T)-\sum_{n\le T/x} f(nx)$. Substituting the corrected limits into (2.7) yields the stated $Pf=T_{f'}\rho_1$. For complex $f$, finite sums and absolute estimates justify the exchanges, with $\int t|f'|$ controlling the integrand. [Báez-Duarte, pp. 4–6](../sources/prefix_formula62_generalL/baez_general.pdf)

The rest of the proof uses these two identities in separate ways. Multiplicative convolution commutes with integer dilation, so applying $T_{f'}$ to a classical strong Nyman–Beurling approximant gives one in $B(Pf)$ with the same cutoff. Müntz's formula $(Pf)^\wedge=\zeta\widehat f$ follows by the fractional-part Mellin identity and an absolutely convergent Fubini exchange, since $\int t^\sigma|f'|$ is finite for $0<\sigma<1$. For the converse, apply $V=I-2K_2$ to the target and approximants. The new target $Vf$ has zero integral and compact support, so $P(Vf)$ is compactly supported and its integer dilations have a common upper support bound. Evaluation by $\int x^{s-1}(\cdot)\,dx$ on the common support is continuous in $L^2$ for $\Re s>1/2$. At a zeta zero this forces $(1-2^{1-s})\widehat f(s)=0$, and the factor is nonzero for $1/2<\Re s<1$. The proof is complete after correcting the two intermediate signs. It yields a norm comparison controlled by $\|T_{f'}\|$, with no multiplicity-dependent optimal constant for the new target. [Báez-Duarte, pp. 7–10](../sources/prefix_formula62_generalL/baez_general.pdf)
