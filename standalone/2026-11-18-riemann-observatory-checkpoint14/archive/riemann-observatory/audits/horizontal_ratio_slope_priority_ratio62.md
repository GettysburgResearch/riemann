# Priority audit for the horizontal zeta-ratio slope

Search executed on 2026-11-17. The closest exact-object antecedent located is Burnol's 2002 preprint. He proves convergence for the same quotient and measure. Balazard and de Roton prove the sharp order and explicitly raise the question of an asymptotic. In the inspected material, no source was found supplying the first-order constant with unrestricted multiplicities or a general theorem implying it under matching assumptions. Historical priority remains open.

## Matching test

With $s=1/2+it$, the object is
$$
J_\epsilon=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}\left|\frac{\zeta(s)}{\zeta(s+\epsilon)}-1\right|^2\frac{dt}{1/4+t^2}.
$$
The reconstruction assumes RH, sends the single positive horizontal shift to zero with the full Cauchy-weighted line retained, and gives the coefficient
$$
\sum_{\gamma\ \mathrm{distinct}}\frac{m_\gamma(1-4^{-m_\gamma}\binom{2m_\gamma}{m_\gamma})}{1/4+\gamma^2}.
$$
The sum includes both signs. An exact antecedent must retain the full-line limit and the dependence on every multiplicity. The simple-zero specialization is $1+\gamma_{\rm Euler}/2-\log(4\pi)/2$. The familiar Nyman–Beurling constant $2+\gamma_{\rm Euler}-\log(4\pi)$ is twice that value and belongs to a distinct limiting problem.

The closest methods supply pieces of the reconstruction, including Hadamard products, Hardy-space boundary averages and local tests near zeros. The checked sources do not identify a theorem providing the passage from the rescaled single-zero profiles to the exact sum over all ordinates. That passage is what distinguishes the current proof from a pointwise expansion or a bounded-window argument.
