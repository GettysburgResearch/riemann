# Deninger’s arithmetic-curve real-coefficient obstruction

Whole-work advocate reading, 18 November 2026. Theorem 2.13 of arXiv v2 and Theorem 2.1 of the journal version follow from a central Artin-multiplicity requirement, a negative root number for an explicit quaternion field, and the even multiplicity of a quaternionic irreducible in a complexified real representation. The obstruction applies at the quaternion test field itself and therefore to any functorial theory covering all arithmetic curves with that requirement. It does not rule out an isolated real spectral construction for classical \(\zeta\) over \(\mathbb Q\). The cohomological positivity that would imply RH remains conjectural.

## Finite-field comparison and proposed coefficient categories

In §1 the base field is \(\overline{\mathbb F}_p\). For a supersingular elliptic curve, the imported endomorphism theorem gives a rational quaternion algebra ramified at \(p\) and infinity. A contravariant Weil cohomology valued in real vector spaces would map \(\operatorname{End}(E)\otimes\mathbb R\) unitally into the opposite of \(M_2(\mathbb R)\), since \(H^1\) has real dimension two. A unital map has zero kernel by simplicity of the quaternion algebra. The dimensions agree, so it would identify a division algebra with the split algebra. At \(\ell\ne p\) it splits over \(\mathbb Q_\ell\). The field denoted \(Q_p\) in the manuscript's local paragraph is \(\operatorname{Frac} W(\overline{\mathbb F}_p)\), over which the ramified quaternion algebra splits after an unramified quadratic extension already contained there. It should not be read as the ordinary base \(\mathbb Q_p\). Extending either coefficient field to \(\mathbb C\) requires a noncanonical abstract embedding. The algebraic coefficient field on the standard-conjecture line is visibly \(\overline{\mathbb Q}\) in the rendered v2, rather than \(\mathbb Q\). The endomorphism description and existence of étale and crystalline Weil cohomologies are external ingredients. Serre's 1960 source cited for the approach in (2.7) gives a distinct Kähler eigenvalue theorem, using invariance of a positive Hermitian form after rescaling. [V2 §1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.02714v2#page=1) · [Serre 1960, Theorem 1](https://www.math.purdue.edu/~tongliu/seminar/Weil/Serre-1960.pdf)

On \(V=V_0\oplus V_1\) in \(K_{\mathbb R}\), the even piece has an antilinear involution and descends to its real fixed-point space. On the odd piece, ordinary multiplication by \(i\) anticommutes with \(\sigma\) and both square to \(-1\), giving the Hamilton quaternion action described. The odd realization of the supersingular \(H^1\) can therefore be complex two-dimensional and quaternionic one-dimensional. This avoids the two-dimensional real representation contradiction. The period-two functor out of \(V_\infty\) collects even and odd integer weight summands. Deligne–Milne Theorem 4.29 gives the parity-compatible faithful exact realization of a polarized algebraic Tannakian category, and Milne Example 1.7 describes the integer-graded real/quaternionic category with its canonical polarization. These are the cited motivic underpinnings and do not prove the standard conjectures. Scholze Conjecture 9.5 asks for a Kottwitz-category realization over the finite-field base compatible with the crystalline, étale and real restrictions. The conditional argument following it invokes standard and Tate conjectures over the finite-field base and the Hodge conjecture for CM abelian varieties. The proposed arithmetic coefficient target is separate. [Deligne–Milne, Theorem 4.29](https://www.math.columbia.edu/~dejong/tannakian/Deligne-Milne-Tannakian-Categories.pdf) · [Milne, Example 1.7](https://www.jmilne.org/math/articles/1994aP.pdf) · [Scholze, Conjecture 9.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.03708)

## The arithmetic axioms and their consequences

Here \(H^i\) denotes the hypothetical cohomology of compactified \(\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal O_k\). Properties (2.1)–(2.4) ask for a cup derivation \(\theta\), \(H^0=\mathbb C\) with eigenvalue zero, \(H^1\) with the nontrivial zeros of \(\zeta_k\) as eigenvalues of the same algebraic multiplicities, and \(H^2=\mathbb C(-1)\) by equivariant trace, whose eigenvalue is one. Algebraic multiplicity is \(\dim H^{1,\theta\sim\alpha}\). The analogy in this opening paragraph cites existing finite-field constructions from étale data and earlier proposed theories for flat arithmetic schemes. It does not furnish the arithmetic groups. By (2.5), the binomial expansion of \((\theta-\alpha-\beta)^j\) on a cup gives its expression as a sum of cups of \((\theta-\alpha)^r h\) and \((\theta-\beta)^{j-r}h'\). It vanishes for sufficiently large \(j\) on generalized eigenvectors. The product may be nonzero only if \(\alpha+\beta=1\). The perfectness in (2.6) is an additional proposed axiom and gives equal algebraic multiplicities for the complementary eigenvalues, compatible with the functional equation. [V2 pp. 2–3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.02714v2#page=2)

For (2.7), put \(\langle h,h'\rangle=\operatorname{tr}(h\cup *h')\) and assume this form is a Hermitian scalar product, with \(*\) antilinear, \(*^2=-1\), \(\theta*=*\theta\). Applying (2.5) to \(h,*h'\) gives \(\langle\theta h,h'\rangle+\langle h,\theta h'\rangle=\langle h,h'\rangle\). For an eigenvector \(h\ne0\), this gives \(2\Re\alpha\|h\|^2=\|h\|^2\). On each finite-dimensional generalized eigenspace the shifted operator is skew-Hermitian under the restricted positive form, hence diagonalizable. Together with (2.3) this would prove the RH for \(\zeta_k\). There is no assertion of automatic Hilbert-space self-adjointness or full spectral resolution. The height comparison is likewise conditional: in Deninger's cited Example 5 the conjectural Abel–Jacobi map identifies \(E(\mathbb Q)\otimes\mathbb C\) with a \(1\)-eigenspace, and the hypothetical form restricts to the negative of the author's negative-definite cycle-height convention. Existing Néron–Tate positivity on that sector does not produce the form on all spectral sectors. [V2 p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.02714v2#page=3) · [Height-pairing paper, Example 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1001.1621v1)

At the real eigenvalue \(1/2\), (2.3) yields (2.8) for \(V_k=H^{1,\theta\sim1/2}\). The \(*\) of (2.7) would preserve \(V_k\) and make its complex dimension even. Independently, the perfect alternating pairing of (2.6) yields (2.9), so also gives even dimension. This agrees with the even completed Dedekind-zeta functional equation, whose gamma and conductor factors are nonzero at the center. This whole-space parity gives no evenness on the multiplicity space of a particular complex irreducible representation. If \(k/\mathbb Q\) is Galois, field functoriality provides a \(G\)-action commuting with \(\theta\), taking inverses when passing from right to left action. In Morin's heuristic, a continuous equivariant cycle-class map kills the compact degree-zero Arakelov subgroup, since the additive group of \(\mathbb C\) has no nonzero compact subgroup. If the map is nonzero, any resulting nonzero real line forces the one-dimensional complex representation \(H^2\) to be trivial. Equivariance of the degree and the nonzero cycle-class hypothesis are needed in this argument. Theorem 2.13 itself needs no action hypothesis on \(H^2\). [V2 p. 4 and display (1)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.02714v2#page=4)

For each simple complex \(G\)-module \(V_\pi\), its central idempotent cuts out \(H(\pi)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_G(V_\pi,H)\otimes V_\pi\), including for an infinite-dimensional \(H\). The evaluation isomorphism respects \(\theta\); on the Hom factor it acts by postcomposition. Artin formalism gives \(\zeta_k=\prod_\pi L(s,V_\pi)^{\dim V_\pi}\) via the regular representation. That motivates, without forcing, (2.10), which identifies zeros with eigenvalues on each multiplicity space; there is an analogue for actual function-field cohomology as cited. On generalized eigenspaces at nontrivial zeros, (2.11) expresses these multiplicities, giving the central specialization (2.12). A zero multiplicity is a nonnegative dimension; a formula with the signed order of a pole would not be an axiom of this sort. The proof uses the single central requirement (2.12) at one specific holomorphic Artin \(L\)-function. [V2 pp. 4–5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.02714v2#page=4)

## Theorem 2.13, with the quaternion example checked in its original source

Let \(\chi\) be the two-dimensional irreducible of \(Q_8\). Its determinant is one, and it has invariant nondegenerate alternating form. Equivalently its standard generators can act as
\[
I=\begin{pmatrix}i&0\\0&-i\end{pmatrix},\qquad
J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
They anticommute and each squares to \(-1\). The antilinear \(j=J\circ\text{conjugation}\) commutes with both and satisfies \(j^2=-1\). This gives a direct check of quaternionic type. It is also the induced representation of an order-four character of the normal cyclic subgroup of order four. Over the quadratic fixed field, the character is nontrivial one-dimensional, and Artin induction and abelian Hecke theory give entire \(L(s,\chi)\). Omar Proposition 3.5 checks the same holomorphy by \(L(s,\chi)^2=L(N/K,\chi',s)\) for the quartic fixed field \(K=N^{\{\pm1\}}\), where the right side is the nontrivial one-dimensional quadratic \(L\)-function, and Artin meromorphy excludes a square-root branch. The completion is nonzero as a function and its extra factors are finite and nonzero at \(1/2\).

Omar's original *Experimental Mathematics* 10(2), 237–245, p. 243 Table 1 row 3 supplies the totally real quaternion field given by
\[
P(X)=X^8-205X^6+13940X^4-378225X^2+3404025,\quad
d_N=74220378765625=5^6 41^6.
\]
Its quadratic subfields have discriminants \(5,41,205\). The extension is tame because \(2\) is unramified and the Galois group has order eight. Use the exact Fröhlich–Martinet test as stated in Omar p. 239, Theorems 3.6–3.7. The field is real, so the signed product of ramified primes is \(+5\cdot41\equiv1\bmod4\), while \((1+5+41+205)/4=63\equiv3\bmod4\). Thus no normal integral basis exists, and the root number equals \(-1\). The field classification and discriminant are imported from Omar's table. The congruence and the parity argument are exact and use no GRH or numerical simplicity claim. In the self-dual functional equation with sign \(-1\) the completed function is odd about \(1/2\), so its order there is positive and odd. The phrase about sign in the later conjectural discussion should be read in this self-dual context. A functional equation comparing *different* dual factors does not by itself give this oddness. [Original Omar article, EMIS mirror](https://www.maths.tcd.ie/EMIS/journals/EM/restricted/10/10.2/omar.ps) · [Omar DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/10586458.2001.10504445)

Suppose a real form of the functorial \(H^1\) existed, with a real \(\theta_{\mathbb R}\), and obeyed (2.12). At a real eigenvalue, the generalized eigenspace complexifies correctly because scalar extension is exact and commutes with the increasing union of kernels of powers. Hence \(V_k\) would be a complexification of a real \(G\)-module. On \(M=\operatorname{Hom}_G(V_\pi,V_k)\), conjugation \(c\) of \(V_k\) and the quaternionic \(j\) of \(V_\pi\) induce an antilinear map \(A(f)=c\circ f\circ j^{-1}\). It satisfies \(A^2=-1\). In finite dimension, writing \(A=C\circ\mathrm{conj}\) gives \(C\bar C=-I\), so \(|\det C|^2=(-1)^{\dim_{\mathbb C}M}\), forcing even dimension. This recovers the same parity from which the printed proof proceeds by the three real irreducible types: complex type contributes \(V\oplus\bar V\) with complex-type factors, real type contributes one real-type simple, and quaternionic type contributes two copies of its symplectic simple. Condition (2.12) gives an odd dimension of this same \(M\), a contradiction. The argument neither assumes the conjectural vanishing condition for the other factors nor uses (2.7). It forbids an ordinary real equivariant form in the specified quaternion test sector. For the trivial Galois group over \(\mathbb Q\) there is no such sector on which to apply the argument. [V2 Theorem 2.13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.02714v2#page=5) · [Journal Theorem 2.1](https://journals.sns.it/index.php/annaliscienze/article/download/5908/2026#page=6)

## Remaining proposals and correction scope

On the hypothetical arithmetic cohomology, the \(*\) of (2.7) would supply a quaternionic structure on \(H^1\) instead of a real descent. To refine \(H^0,H^2\simeq\mathbb C\) in even degree, real structures would be required, with a candidate for \(H^2\) given by the image of the proposed cycle-class map. A number-field version of \(K_{\mathbb R}\)-valued theory thus remains logically possible. Ordinary complex leafwise cohomology of real forms carries a natural real structure, so a realization by that construction for all fields with the required Artin equivariance faces the obstruction. Deninger's cited flow construction for arithmetic schemes is evidence for some dynamical analogies, without that spectral cohomology identification. Twisted complex coefficients are a proposed way to avoid the forced real form. [V2 pp. 5–6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.02714v2#page=5) · [Arithmetic flows](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.06400v4)

The center conjecture ascribed to Serre predicts exactly one central zero for symplectic irreducibles with sign \(-1\) and none for the other irreducibles of the absolute Galois group. The earlier quadratic nonvanishing conjecture is a special direction. Fröhlich–Queyrut's orthogonal root-number theorem supplies sign \(+1\) for representations realizable as real representations, which does not give nonvanishing. The sign distinction uses the real-representation hypothesis rather than merely real-valued characters. Omar tests cases of the quaternionic central order, with test-dependent numerical and GRH qualifications in Propositions 4.3, 4.5. None is used in the obstruction. Under (2.12) and the center conjecture, the isotypic summands are exactly the negative-root-number symplectic irreducibles, once each, giving (2.14); since the summands are self-dual and nonisomorphic, \(G\)-invariant perfect duality is blockwise and Schur's lemma makes each alternating pairing unique up to scale. This motivates the covariant functor in (2.15) with the sum-of-dimensions formula and a positive antilinear star. It is a proposed functor from number fields, with covariance stated separately from the earlier use of contravariant cohomology to induce the action of field automorphisms. [V2 pp. 6–7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.02714v2#page=6) · [Queyrut’s account of the root-number result](https://www.numdam.org/item/MSMF_1974__37__133_0.pdf)

The leading-coefficient comparison, local decomposition of the alternating pairing, half-integral \(K_0\) Adams weight, \(\mathrm{CH}^{1/2}\), and an \(\operatorname{Ext}^1\) with a half-Tate twist are research suggestions, without a constructed motivic category over number fields. The supersingular elliptic half-twist in the function-field setting is cited as a qualified analogue, not an ordinary Tate object over \(\mathbb Q\). The tamely ramified Galois-module suggestion is supported by the role of symplectic root numbers in normal integral basis results. The note invokes exponential motives for the idea of nonintegral twists, not an identification of \(V_k\). Acknowledgments include the source of the Chow argument and the referee simplification from the v1 conjecture-dependent proof. The references distinguish the prior determinant and foliation proposals [2]–[9], root-number input [10], categorical background [1], [11], [15], square-root analogies and center context [13]–[14], Serre's Kähler argument [16] and finite representation theory [17]. The quaternion field input [12] was read at the decisive original pages. These citations are not collective independent proof audits. [V2 pp. 7–8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.02714v2#page=7)

The journal and v2 wording “For a number field \(k\)” is safest restated with the witness hypothesis explicit: for a finite Galois \(k/\mathbb Q\) with the indicated symplectic Artin sector of odd central order, an ordinary real equivariant theory satisfying (2.12) is impossible. Such fields exist, so a universal functorial theory is impossible. Rephrasing this way resolves the apparent fixed-\(k\) quantifier ambiguity without changing the proof. The earlier v1 is superseded in scope by the later proof. The publisher's current article record and exact-DOI correction search did not yield a separate correction.
