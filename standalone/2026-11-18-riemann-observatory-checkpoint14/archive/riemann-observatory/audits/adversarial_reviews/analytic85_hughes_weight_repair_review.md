# Hughes–Rudnick averaging weights and the logarithmic moment threshold

18 November 2026. The literal omission of tail control occurs in both 2002 versions, and some later restatements retain it. The prime-product moment calculation survives unchanged. Under the original Fourier support hypotheses, the logarithmic moment of the averaging weight through order \(m\) is sufficient for the entire zero-statistic conclusion, including odd orders. If \(\int f\ne0\), it is also necessary for a finite \(m\)-th absolute moment at each fixed height. If \(\int f=0\), no extra weight-tail hypothesis is needed. This is a scoped audit of the original continuous-height average. No canonical finding is proposed here. ## Corrected scope and exact necessity

Let \(L=\log T\), \(H=T^a\), \(0<a\le1\), and \(m\ge1\) fixed. Take even real \(f\) with \(\widehat f\in C_c^\infty\) supported in a fixed \([-\sigma,\sigma]\subset(-2a/m,2a/m)\). Assume \(w\ge0\) of mass one with \(\widehat w\) compactly supported, and set \(I=\int f\). The original Gaussian first \(m\) moments of the statistic sampled at \(\tau=T+Hu\), \(u\sim w(u)\,du\), hold provided \(I=0\) or
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R} w(u)\log^m(2+|u|)\,du<\infty.
\]
The target mean is \(I\), with variance \(\int\min(|x|,1)|\widehat f(x)|^2\,dx\) when the comparison includes the second moment. All statements are unconditional for the source's complex ordinates. Conjugate pairing across the critical line makes the statistic real.

For the necessity, the fixed-\(T\) explicit formula from (2)–(4) gives
\[
 N_f(\tau)=\frac{I}{L}\log(1+|\tau|)+O_{f,T}(1)
 \qquad(\tau\in\mathbb R).
\]
Indeed the prime sum is finite at fixed \(T\), and the evaluations at \(\pm i/2-\tau\) are bounded at fixed \(T\) by the Fourier integral. The smooth part, after \(r=\tau+2\pi x/L\), differs from \(I\log(1+|\tau|)/L\) by a uniformly bounded multiple of \(L^{-1}\int |f(x)|(1+\log(2+|x|))dx\). This follows from \(\Omega(r)=\log(1+|r|)+O(1)\) and the logarithmic triangle inequality. The zero series is absolutely convergent at each real center by the ordinary unit-window zero count and Schwartz decay of \(f\) on the fixed horizontal strip of arguments. Thus for \(I\ne0\) and \(|u|\) sufficiently large for the fixed \(T,H\), \(|N_f(T+Hu)|\) is bounded above and below by positive multiples of \(\log(2+|u|)\). The same holds after subtracting a fixed real constant. The finite \(m\)-th absolute moment condition is therefore equivalent to the stated logarithmic moment condition. If it fails the tails have a fixed sign for the signed odd power as well, with no cancellation between the two infinite ends.

There are genuinely non-Schwartz admissible weights with exactly this order of logarithmic integrability. With any nonnegative normalized Schwartz bandlimited \(w_0\), consider the sum of translates to \(e^n\) with weights proportional to \(n^{-m-3/2}\). The Fourier support is inherited from \(w_0\) by \(L^1\) convergence. The logarithmic \(m\)-th moment is finite by the log triangle bound and the \((m+1)\)-st is infinite by integrating over each translated copy of a bounded positive-mass interval. The corrected class therefore extends substantially beyond Schwartz, and the order of logarithmic integrability cannot in general be lowered.

## Adversarial check of the odd-moment repair

The finite oscillatory sum \(S_f\) is unchanged. In its \(j\)-th signed power the Fourier cutoff of a fixed averaging weight only retains \(\log(M/N)=0\) when \(j\le m\) and \(T\) is large. For \(M>N\), one has \(\log(M/N)\ge 1/(2N)\) and \(N\le\sqrt{MN}\le T^{j\sigma/2}\). The separation beats \(H^{-1}\) by a fixed power. Grouping balanced prime-power blocks shows that opposite-sign paired primes give Wick terms. Blocks of size at least three have balanced total exponent at least four, so their contribution is bounded by constants times \(L^{-\ell}\sum_p(\log p)^\ell p^{-2}\) for block size \(\ell\), with summable geometric prime-power tails. Nontrivial prime powers in the size-two blocks are negligible for the same reason. After the prime number theorem these are exactly the Gaussian prime-sum moments in the support window. In particular all available **even absolute** orders of \(S_f\) are bounded by a constant times the averaging weight's mass. For a weight shifted or contracted by a scale \(\delta\), the same statement holds if \(H\delta\gg T^{m\sigma/2}\) with a fixed exponent margin. Translations do not enter the zero-frequency coefficient.

Let \(\Delta\) be the smooth gamma integral minus \(I\), with the two polar evaluations treated separately. The uniform estimate above now gives a decomposition into
\[
 I L^{-1}\big(\log(1+|T+Hu|)-\log T\big)+O_f(L^{-1}).
\]
When \(I=0\) this gives \(\|\Delta\|_\infty=O_f(L^{-1})\). Every mixed term \(E_w|S_f|^r|\Delta|^j\) with \(j\ge1\), \(r+j\le m\) is then \(O_f(L^{-j})\) by Hölder using \(2\lceil r/2\rceil\le m\) for \(r>0\), with \(r=0\) immediate. No logarithmic moment is needed in that case. If \(I\ne0\), the difference of logarithms is bounded by the sum of a constant, \(\log(2+|u|)\), and the regularized local quantity \(\log_+(1/(|u+T/H|+H^{-1}))\). Two different techniques handle these terms without requesting the nonexistent bound for an odd highest absolute prime-sum moment.

First, for the large-\(|u|\) weight, choose a Schwartz reproducing kernel \(\psi\) with \(\widehat\psi=1\) on the compact support of \(\widehat w\). Then \(w=w*\psi\) almost everywhere by Fourier uniqueness. For any integer \(1\le j\le m\), the weighted density \(w(u)(1+\log^j(2+|u|))\) admits a pointwise positive bandlimited majorant
\[
 C_j\,[\kappa*(w(\cdot)(1+\log^j(2+|\cdot|)))](u),
\]
where \(\kappa\) is a fixed sum of two squared sinc functions with a half-unit relative shift. The shifted squared numerators add to one, giving \(\kappa(x)\gg (1+|x|)^{-2}\); its Fourier support is compact. The log triangle inequality and rapid decay make \(|\psi(x)|(1+\log^j(2+|x|))\ll_j\kappa(x)\), proving the majorization from the convolution identity. The majorant has finite mass by the logarithmic moment assumption, and its compact Fourier support comes from \(\kappa\), independently of the Fourier regularity of the logarithm-weighted density. Apply the even moment at \(2\lceil r/2\rceil\) to this fixed positive weight. The inequality \(2\lceil r/2\rceil\le m\) follows exactly from \(j\ge1\), even when \(m\) is odd.

Second, at the moving singularity \(v=u+T/H=0\), take \(0<\varepsilon<a-m\sigma/2\). For \(\delta\) between \(T^{-\varepsilon}\) and 1, majorize the indicator of \(|v|\le\delta\) by \(B(v/\delta)\) with \(B\ge1\) on \([-1,1]\), nonnegative, integrable and bandlimited. A scaled squared sinc with no zero on that interval works. Its mass is \(O(\delta)\). Its Fourier window in height is now \(H\delta\), preserving the strict separation even at the smallest allowed \(\delta\). Thus the even moment under this majorant is \(O(\delta)\) uniformly in the shift and scale. By Hölder this gives \(\int_{|v|\le\delta}|S_f|^rdu\ll\delta\). Compact support of \(\widehat w\) and \(w\in L^1\) imply \(w\in L^\infty\) by Fourier inversion, so this also bounds the weighted singularity terms. Exponentially shrinking annuli contribute the summable \(\sum_\ell (1+\ell)^j e^{-\ell}\). The innermost core \(|v|<T^{-\varepsilon}\) uses the interval bound at radius \(T^{-\varepsilon}\) and the regularizing ceiling \(\log H\), costing \(O(T^{-\varepsilon}L^j)\). Thus the centered smooth terms give additive \(O(L^{-j})\) for the relevant mixed products. This addresses both the missing weight-tail control and the inadequacy of a bound on the smooth **mean** alone.

Finally the polar terms are individually \(O_A(T^{\sigma/2}(1+|\tau|)^{-A})\). For \(|\tau|\le1\) the Fourier integral suffices; outside, integration by parts costs \(O(L^A)\) in derivatives of the imaginary shift multiplier, absorbed by \((L|\tau|)^{-A}\). The finite prime sum is \(O(T^{\sigma/2})\) using \(\Lambda(n)\le \sigma L\) on its support. Any mixed term with a polar factor and total degree \(\le m\) has integrated absolute size \(O(T^{m\sigma/2}/H)=o(1)\), since \(w\) has bounded density and smooth-background logarithms are integrable against the arbitrary polar decay. This step does not rely on cancellation in the signed first polar mean. Combining these estimates proves convergence of each raw moment through order \(m\). Centering to the actual mean follows from the raw moment comparison. The requirement \(m\sigma/2<a\) is the same strict window as in the original theorem.

**Disposition.** The weighted82 odd-order localization and majorant argument holds as stated. The nonzero-mean necessity and zero-mean exception sharpen the scope. The primary attribution, later restatements and sharp-average variants should remain separately described. This is a repair of the literal whole-line weight class, not a challenge to its finite-window arithmetic mechanism.
