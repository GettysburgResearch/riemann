# Primary-source comparison for derivative-weighted prime-phase limits

18 November 2026. The measure compared is the RH probability law on the strict atomic tail \(|\gamma|>Y\) with weights proportional to \(|\zeta'(1/2+i\gamma)|^2/(1/4+\gamma^2)\). The scalar assertion uses primes up to \((\log Y)^D\) for fixed \(D>0\). The joint assertion uses deterministic first-harmonic sine and cosine columns on that support, in fixed dimension, with infinitesimal normalized columns and finite limiting covariance. The process uses sharp nested cutoffs \(P_Y(u)=\exp((\log\log Y)^u)\), fixed \(0\le u_0<1\), individual cosine centering \(1/p\), and normalization \(\sqrt{\tfrac12\log\log\log Y}\). The limit is standard Brownian motion restricted to \([u_0,1]\), in \(D,J_1\). [Scalar proof](uniform64_exceptional_prime_clt_skeptic.md) · [Joint proof](uniform65_joint_exceptional_prime_clt.md) · [Process proof](uniform67_brownian_prime_phase_process.md)

## Primary comparisons

- **Vassaux**, arXiv:2505.07352v2, Theorems 1.1, 2.1, 2.3 and §§2.3–3. For uniform \(\tau\in[0,T]\), the process is \(\alpha\mapsto\log\zeta(\tfrac12+(\log T)^{-\alpha}+i\tau)/\sqrt{\log\log T}\), \(0\le\alpha\le1\). Theorem 1.1 proves uniform-topology convergence to complex Brownian motion, whose independent real components have variance \(\alpha/2\). The theorem states no RH assumption. Theorem 2.1 gives covariance \(1\wedge\alpha_i\wedge\alpha_j\). Theorem 2.3 and the estimate (2.16) establish tightness using a uniform fourth increment moment on a common high-probability event. Proposition 3.1 and Lemmas 3.2–3.3 control smooth Dirichlet polynomial differences. This is the closest path-space antecedent and a direct methodological precedent. Its observable is the full analytic logarithm along a horizontal line at a continuous random height. Its effective smooth cutoff scale is approximately \(\exp((\log T)^\alpha)\), since suppression depends on \((\log T)^{-\alpha}\log p\). The target uses a sharp cutoff on the shorter scale \(\exp((\log\log Y)^u)\) and samples derivative-weighted zeros. [Full text, v2, 1 June 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.07352v2)
- **Bourgade**, arXiv:0902.1757v1, Theorem 1.1, §1.1 and §§3–4. For \(\omega\) uniform on \((0,1)\), samples the full log at common horizontal displacement \(\varepsilon_t\) and several moving vertical offsets from \(\omega t\). The complex Gaussian vector has diagonal covariance 1 and off-diagonal \(1\wedge c_{ij}\), where \(c_{ij}\) is the logarithmic separation ratio. Theorem 1.1 treats \(\varepsilon_t\gg1/\log t\) with variance scale \(-\log\varepsilon_t\), and \(\varepsilon_t\ll1/\log t\) with scale \(\log\log t\). It gives a finite-dimensional Selberg-scale Gaussian with a Brownian-kernel interpretation. It supplies no sharp-cutoff path tightness for the target law. [Full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0902.1757v1)
- **Bourgade–Kuan**, arXiv:1203.5328v4, Theorems 1–2 and (3)–(6). Under RH and \(1\ll\lambda_t\ll\log t\), count zero ordinates with multiplicities in test statistics centered at a uniform height on \((t,2t)\). Theorem 1 gives a fixed Gaussian vector with \(H^{1/2}\) covariance under decay and variation assumptions. Theorem 2 gives a scalar normalized Gaussian with diverging truncated Fourier variance. Zeros enter the observable and continuous height supplies the sampling law. [Full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1203.5328v4)
- **Rodgers**, arXiv:1205.0303v5, Theorems 1.1–1.3, and **Maples–Rodgers**, arXiv:1404.3080v2, Theorem 1.2. Analogous mesoscopic zero linear statistics around a uniform height on \([T,2T]\), at \(n(T)\to\infty\) with \(n(T)=o(\log T)\). Rodgers assumes RH. Maples–Rodgers remove RH for the compactly supported bounded-variation case with diverging Fourier variance, which is asymptotic to \(\int_{-n(T)}^{n(T)} |x||\widehat\eta(x)|^2dx\). These again put zeros in the observable and use a continuous test law. Searching “Kuan–Rodgers” retrieved these separate lines and no joint Kuan–Rodgers zeta theorem was identified. [Rodgers full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1205.0303v5) · [Maples–Rodgers full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1404.3080v2)
- **Selberg**, 1946, through the modern full proof by **Radziwiłł–Soundararajan**, Theorem 1, Proposition 2 and §3. Ordinary continuous \(t\in[T,2T]\); zero-centered \(\log|\zeta(\tfrac12+it)|/\sqrt{\tfrac12\log\log T}\) tends to a standard Gaussian. Gaussian moments of short prime sums are central to the proof. This is a direct precursor to the scalar method. The original Selberg 1946 text was identified bibliographically in Vassaux [17]. The proof inspection here used the modern full proof. [Full proof](https://www.math.mcgill.ca/radziwill/CLTNotes3.pdf)
- **Fazzari**, arXiv:1912.08127v2, Theorem 1.1 and Proposition 1.2. For continuous \(|\zeta(\tfrac12+it)|^2dt\) on \([T,2T]\), Proposition 1.2 gives unconditional fixed-order Gaussian moments for \(\operatorname{Re}\sum_{p\le x}p^{-1/2-it}-\sum_{p\le x}1/p\), at \(x=T^{1/(k\log\log\log T)}\) for moment order \(k\), with leading variance \(\tfrac12\sum_{p\le x}1/p\). The RH full-log CLT has mean \(\log\log T\). The scalar polynomial and centering are close precedents, with continuous sampling and a much larger moment-dependent cutoff. [Full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.08127v2)
- **Fazzari**, arXiv:2101.08036v1, Conjecture 1, Theorem 1, Corollary 1. With continuous \(|\zeta^{(m)}(\tfrac12+it)|^{2k}dt\) on \([T,2T]\), the full real logarithm has mean \(k\log\log T\) and variance \(\frac12\log\log T\). The source assumes RH and its shifted/twisted integral-moment conjecture, available for \(k=1,2\) in the source. At \(m=k=1\), the measure still has a density in height. The discrete zero set has probability zero under that law. No separate Fazzari path-space theorem was identified in the inspected texts. [Full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.08036v1)
- **Fazzari–Gerspach–Minelli**, arXiv:2507.04150v1, Theorems 1.1–1.2, (1.9), Corollaries 1.3–1.4. Continuous dyadic weight \(|N_\phi(t)-\widehat\phi(0)|^kdt\) for fixed even \(k\). Theorems concern complex full-log Gaussian moments at Fourier support \((-2/(k+2),2/(k+2))\) and RH imaginary-part moments at support \((-2/k,2/k)\) for \(k>0\). Their weight is a local zero linear statistic evaluated at the continuously sampled height. [Full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.04150v1)
- **Bui–Gonek–Milinovich**, arXiv:1302.5032v1, Theorems 2.2–2.3 and §5 (13). Under RH, Theorem 2.2 averages \(|P_X(\rho)|^{2k}\) over ordinary zeros, and Theorem 2.3 averages \(|\zeta'(\rho)|^2|P_X(\rho)^{-1}|^2\), at \(X=O((\log T)^{2-\epsilon})\). The proof defines exactly the one-sided prefix twist \(I(m,n)=\sum_{0<\gamma\le T}|\zeta'(\rho)|^2(m/n)^{-i\gamma}\) and evaluates a combination with specific inverse Euler-product coefficients. This is a direct arithmetic predecessor for the tilted law and its rational twists. The aggregate asymptotic alone does not yield a uniform error for every normalized character. Its calculations may be an alternative starting point for obtaining one. [Full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1302.5032v1)
- **Çiçek**, arXiv:2007.10533v2, Proposition 4.1 and Theorem 1.3. Under RH, Proposition 4.1 gives moments of a real prime polynomial on ordinary zero counting \(0<\gamma\le T\), with \(p\le X^2\), \(X\le T^{1/(8k)}\), and \(k\ll(\log\log T)^{1/6}\). Its Landau–Gonek evaluation uses off-diagonal prime-power terms. Theorem 1.3 concerns a log-derivative statistic under RH and an extra spacing hypothesis \(H_\alpha\). This is a direct discrete zero-ordinate prime-moment precedent under ordinary zero counting. [Full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.10533v2)
- **Çiçek–Gonek**, arXiv:2310.10119v1, Theorems 1.1–1.2, 1.4 and Lemma 2.1. RH and a spacing hypothesis for ordinary counts sorted by a normalized \(\log|\zeta'(\rho)|\) interval and uniform distribution modulo one with fixed dilation. Its moving dilation \(\log T/(2\pi)\) in Theorem 1.4 also assumes a conjecture. Discrete selection by a log-derivative interval and a single phase do not supply joint asymptotics for derivative-square weights or growing prime arrays. [Full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.10119v1)
- **Saksman–Webb**, arXiv:1609.00027v3, Theorems 1.1–1.2 and 1.7. Gives continuous-height zeta limits in generalized-function spaces and a functional Gaussian approximation for an independent randomized Euler product in the spatial variable \(x\). The Gaussian term in Theorem 1.7 has independent complex Gaussian prime coefficients \(p^{-1/2}/\sqrt2\) with spatial phases \(p^{-ix}\). At fixed \(x\), its Gaussian cutoff sums directly have Brownian finite-dimensional laws in reciprocal-prime time. This is a close independent-prime model precedent. The model prime marginals, sampling law, and topology differ from the target. [Full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.00027v3)

## What follows by standard methods

Heap–Li–Zhao, *Lower bounds for discrete negative moments of the Riemann zeta function*, Algebra & Number Theory 16 (2022), Theorem 4 and Propositions 10–11, is the closest arithmetic input. Its shifted twisted discrete second moment is for positive prefixes and has coefficient hypotheses. The archive obtains polylogarithmic-range real symmetric character estimates by admissible polarization and differentiation in complex shifts. The reduced kernel \((m,n)/\sqrt{mn}\) is exactly the independent Poisson-circle expectation for radii \(p^{-1/2}\). The extraction and uniformity estimate are a consequence of the source and separate calculations, rather than the literal statement of Theorem 4. Simplicity is assumed in their negative-moment Theorem 1. Nonsimple zeros contribute zero to this derivative-square application of Theorem 4. [HLZ journal PDF](../../sources/classical63_derivative_moments/hlz2022print.pdf) · [Uniform extraction and source locators](uniform64_hlz_third_review.md)

Stieltjes summation gives the strict tail once the requisite prefix estimates are uniform. For a signed cumulative difference \(A_\Delta(T)\) and \(f(t)=(t^2+1/4)^{-1}\), the tail difference is \(-f(Y)A_\Delta(Y)+\int_Y^\infty(-f'(t))A_\Delta(t)\,dt\), provided the endpoint at infinity vanishes. The inclusive prefix at \(Y\) ensures strict exclusion. Coefficients and support of any block depending on \(Y\) stay fixed across all \(T\ge Y\). A weak law in a single dyadic block under different weighting supplies neither this uniformity nor the needed joint information with height.

The method of moments, Lindeberg in the independent model, and Cramér–Wold are standard once fixed-order character errors are controlled. At order \(k\) the numerator and denominator can reach \((\log T)^{Dk}\). The effective archive proof restricts each prefix to support avoiding a prime of a possible exceptional conductor and restores the omitted summand before Stieltjes transfer. This step does not make every isolated rational twist effective.

The extra path-space input is the local fourth centered moment \(\ll h_I^2\) for every prime interval of reciprocal mass \(h_I=\sum_{p\in I}p^{-1}\). The proof combines the relative character kernel, an absolute Fourier mass bound \(\ll P^2h_I^2\) at cutoff \(P\), and a fourth-power omission cost \(\ll h_I^2\). A bound independent of the interval mass would not yield the stated tightness. Reciprocal-mass interpolation, Mertens' estimate and Kolmogorov's criterion then give a standard probabilistic transfer.

Vassaux already proves Brownian functional Selberg behavior under continuous height sampling. The precise result checked here is the sharp-cutoff process under the derivative-weighted atomic tail, with its standalone scalar and fixed-dimensional consequences. None of the inspected theorem statements gives that exact combination directly. This bounded search does not establish publication priority.
