# Rank-one Hermite witness | Normalization and asymptotic check

For the rank-one formal series \(M(z)=e^{\sigma^2z^2/2}(1-dz)\), where \(\sigma^2=\log(b/a)/2\) and \(d=b-a>0\), differentiate the centered Gaussian exponential to get \(\int x e^{zx}\,d\nu_\sigma(x)=\sigma^2 z e^{\sigma^2z^2/2}\). Thus the signed polynomial functional is
\[
 \Lambda(f)=\int f(x)(1-dx/\sigma^2)\,d\nu_\sigma(x).
\]
The modifier has negative slope, the signed first moment is \(-d\), and the negative direction therefore comes from the **largest** multiplication eigenvalue. In particular the variable standardized to unit variance uses coefficient \(d/\sigma\), whereas unstandardized multiplication by \(x\) uses \(d/\sigma^2\). [Rank-one proposition under review](geometry102_finite_hankel_bound.md)

For \(h_k(x)=\operatorname{He}_k(x/\sigma)/\sqrt{k!}\), the multiplication recurrence gives matrix elements \(\sigma\sqrt{k}\) on the edge from \(k-1\) to \(k\). The full moment form restricted to degree \(L\) is congruent to \(I-(d/\sigma^2)T_L\). Terms in \(x h_L\) of degree \(L+1\) vanish upon taking inner products against the restricted span. The tridiagonal characteristic polynomials obey the monic Hermite recurrence after rescaling, giving
\[
 \det(tI-T_L)=\sigma^{L+1}\operatorname{He}_{L+1}(t/\sigma).
\]
The least eigenvalue of the moment form in this basis is \(1-(d/\sigma^2)\lambda_{\max}(T_L)\). Thus it is positive definite below the top-root threshold, positive semidefinite and singular at equality, and indefinite above it. The latter form has a positive value on the constant polynomial, so the negative direction is a strict witness. In the normalized fixture \(\sigma=d=1\), the monomial degree-one matrix is \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) with determinant zero. At degree two its determinant is \(-4\), and \(\Lambda((x+x^2)^2)=-2\). The fixture checks the equality algebra in normalized parameters. Weighted89 also supplied an admissible band fixture. Take \(b=\sqrt{2\log2/3}\), \(a=b/2\), so \(\sigma/d=\sqrt3\). The largest root of \(\operatorname{He}_3\) is \(\sqrt3\), giving positive semidefiniteness with equality at \(L=2\). The largest root of \(\operatorname{He}_4\) is \(\sqrt{3+\sqrt6}\), yielding the first strictly negative direction at \(L=3\).

An alternate lower Rayleigh bound avoids trigonometric eigenvectors. For \(q=\lfloor\sqrt L\rfloor\ge2\), use the unit vector constant on the last \(q\) coordinate vertices and zero elsewhere. There are \(q-1\) edges inside its support, each at least \(\sigma\sqrt{L-q+1}\). Its Rayleigh quotient is at least \(2(1-1/q)\sigma\sqrt{L-q+1}\). The two weighted shifts give the matching upper bound \(2\sigma\sqrt L\). Thus the dimensionless top eigenvalue lies between \(2\sqrt L-O(1)\) and \(2\sqrt L\), since both \(\sqrt L/q\) and \(\sqrt L-\sqrt{L-q+1}\) are bounded. Let \(h=\sigma/d\). The upper eigenvalue bound gives \(L_{\min}>h^2/4\), strictly because root equality gives a null direction. The lower eigenvalue bound gives \(L_{\min}\le (h+C)^2/4+1\) for an absolute \(C\) when \(h\) is large, choosing \(C\) strictly larger than the bounded deficit. Hence \(L_{\min}=h^2/4+O(h)\) as \(h\to\infty\). For \(a=b/2\), this is \((\log 2)/(2b^2)\), compared with the first raw-moment crossing \(\lfloor1/b\rfloor+1\). The Hankel matrix through witness degree \(L_{\min}\) uses moments through degree \(2L_{\min}\).
