# Longer prime cutoffs in the derivative-weighted HLZ law

18 November 2026. Assume RH throughout the distribution claims. Write \(L=\log Y\), and use the strictly two-sided probability measure on \(|\gamma|>Y\) with weights \(|\zeta'(1/2+i\gamma)|^2/(1/4+\gamma^2)\). Let
\[
 W_P=\sum_{p\le P}\frac{\Re(p^{-i\gamma})}{\sqrt p},\quad
 H_P=\sum_{p\le P}\frac1p,\quad
 V_P=\frac12\sum_{p\le P}(p^{-1}-p^{-2}).
\]
The reference model has independent Poisson-circle phases of radius \(p^{-1/2}\), so these are exactly the model mean and variance.

The scalar \((W_P-H_P)/\sqrt{V_P}\) has the standard Gaussian limit and convergent fixed moments for \(P=\exp(L^a)\), every fixed \(0<a<1\). For \(P=Y^\theta\), every fixed \(0<\theta<1/2\) still gives the weak scalar CLT. Moments through even order \(2m\) also converge if \(m\theta<1/2\). These scalar statements use RH only and the qualitative shifted HLZ theorem. They concern the derivative-weighted law. In particular a moving polynomial prime band has a different mean than the Poisson model, even though the cumulative normalized scalar still has a Gaussian limit. [HLZ journal, Theorem 5, equation (24), §§4–6](../../sources/classical63_derivative_moments/hlz2022print.pdf) · [Earlier polylogarithmic law](director_prime_phase_clt_round10.md)

## 1. Uniform aggregate comparison

Write \(\mathbb E_T\) for the positive-and-negative prefix average with derivative-square weights and no inverse-quadratic factor, and \(A(T)=\sum_{0<\gamma\le T}|\zeta'(\rho)|^2\sim T(\log T)^4/(24\pi)\). For fixed \(\delta<1/2\), let \(U,V\) be Dirichlet polynomials supported at integers \(\le N\le T^\delta\), with all coefficient moduli bounded by a fixed \(M\). Their mixed symmetric quadratic form is available from HLZ Theorem 5 by exact four-sign cancellation. Specifically, on the union of divisors of their supports place a real baseline \(B=8(1+M)\), zero outside. Each input \(Q_{\sigma,\tau}=B+\sigma U+\tau V\) on this divisor-closed support has modulus between \(B-2M\) and \(B+2M\), and \((B-2M)^2\ge B+2M\). Thus it has fixed submultiplicativity and fixed divisor/log bounds, independently of \(T\), with \(\log(2n)\) or a separate constant at \(1\). Declare length \(y=T^\delta\). The four signs with weights \(\sigma\tau/8\) extract \((U\bar V+V\bar U)/2\) under RH and the same symmetrized bilinear form in the main term. The journal coefficients include conjugation and reflected shifts in the second \(I\); its conjugation interchanges coefficient slots and leaves a sesquilinear polynomial form in the coefficients. The same polarization applies to the two-sided prefix. Setting \(V=U\) extracts \(|U|^2\) directly.

The uniformity here follows from fixed envelopes, as opposed to a theorem for each fixed polynomial. In the source §§4.1–4.3, the first contour and continuous errors use the common coefficient upper bound and declared length, as does the stationary-phase error. Keep \(n^{O(1/\log T)}\) in the infinite shifted Dirichlet series; on the finite parts it is bounded as \(n\le T^{O(1)}\). The principal Perron error in §4.3 has \(x=Tk/(2\pi h)\) with \(\log x\asymp\log T\). Its outer factors after cancelling \(x\) in the error majorant are harmonic or divisor-weighted harmonic, as displayed just before (50) in the arXiv version. In §§4.4–6 the only coefficient inputs in the two conductor estimates are the fixed submultiplicative and divisor/log envelopes. The errors in Proposition 11 at \(y=T^\delta\) save a power except for its \(\eta^{-1/2+\epsilon}T\) term, and that term gives any fixed log saving by choosing a sufficiently large polylogarithmic split \(\eta\). Proposition 10 then gives a uniform small-conductor bound, qualitatively using Siegel. The general proof is uniform in all arrays above. Its constants do not depend on the size of this support or on \(P\). [HLZ arXiv version, Theorem 2 and §§4–6](../../sources/classical63_derivative_moments/hlz2022.pdf) · [Earlier source reconstruction](uniform64_hlz_third_review.md)

For indices \(m,n\), put \(g=(m,n)\), \(h=m/g\), \(k=n/g\), and \(w_{mn}=1/(ghk)\). On the separated circles \(|\alpha|=2c/\log T,\ |\beta|=3c/\log T,\ |\gamma_{\rm aux}|=c/\log T\) the main kernel before differentiation is the untwisted kernel times \(w_{mn}\) times arithmetic factors. For \(p^e\Vert k\), the exact local ratio is
\[
  V^e+(U-1)(\textstyle\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}U^iV^{e-1-i})(1-Vz)/(1-z),
\]
where \(U=p^{-u},V=p^{-v},z=p^{-1-b}\) and the shifts are \(O(1/\log T)\). It differs from 1 by \(O(e\log p/\log T)\) since \(e\log p\le\delta\log T\); consequently the product differs from 1 by \(O_\delta(\log(hk)/\log T)\). The factors \(h^{-b}\) and the monomials in \(J\) have the same bound, including at complex shifts. In particular the arithmetic factors are uniformly bounded all the way up to \(T^\delta\). Cauchy differentiation through the auxiliary and two outer shifts, as in the earlier audit, yields for the mixed comparison, with polynomial coefficients \(u_m,v_n\),
\[
 \left|\mathbb E_T\Re(U(\rho)\overline{V(\rho)})-
    \mathbb E_*\Re(U(\rho_*)\overline{V(\rho_*)})\right|
 \ll_{\delta,M,A}
 \sum_{m,n}\frac{|u_m v_n|}{\operatorname{lcm}(m,n)}
       \frac{\log(mn/g^2)}{\log T}+(\log T)^{-A}.                 \tag{1}
\]
Here the model convention is \(m^{-\rho_*}=m^{-1/2}\prod_p U_p^{v_p(m)}\). The kernel comparison is ordered, so it remains valid with complex coefficients, and the symmetry relevant to the two signs is obtained by averaging the conjugated prime phases. The model is invariant under conjugating all phases. The additive error in (1) belongs to the entire polynomial pair. It has no absolute Fourier-coefficient sum multiplying it. Returning to arbitrary \(T\) from a good ordinate costs a power, since \(|U(\rho)V(\bar\rho)|\ll_M N\) on RH and the RH fixed-strip zeta derivative is \(O_\epsilon(T^\epsilon)\). Alternatively use the theorem as stated for general large \(T\).

## 2. Scalar and bounded-polynomial joint arrays

For a prime band \(S\), write \(D_S(s)=\sum_{p\in S}p^{-s}\). The powers \(D_S^b\) have coefficients bounded by \(b!\), and their lengths are \((\max S)^b\). Thus each raw moment \((\Re D_S)^j\) expands into \(2^{-j}\sum_{b=0}^j\binom jb D_S^b\overline{D_S}^{\,j-b}\), with conjugate terms paired for (1). The weighted absolute coefficient sum against \(1/\operatorname{lcm}(m,n)\) is the model raw moment for the positive-coefficient majorant. It is \(O_j((1+H_P)^j)\). For example, the independent model has mean \(H_P\) and bounded centered moments of order \(j\) after scaling by \(1+\sqrt{H_P}\), by its bounded summands and the usual tilted variance exponential bound. After binomial centering at \(H_P\) and normalization by \(\sqrt{V_P}\), (1) gives discrepancy for each fixed \(j\)
\[
 O_j\!\left((1+H_P)^{j/2}\frac{\log(2P)}{\log T}
              +(1+H_P)^j(\log T)^{-A}\right),                  \tag{2}
\]
where taking a larger \(A\) absorbs the harmless centering factors. No factor \(P^{j/2}\) appears. For \(P=\exp(L^a)\), (2) vanishes uniformly for \(T\ge Y\); so does it for \(P=\exp(L\exp(-(\log L)^{1/3}))\). In both cases \(P^j\le T^\delta\) eventually for any fixed \(\delta>0\), and \(H_P\asymp\log\log P\).

For a real projected joint array with coefficients \(\alpha_p,\beta_p\) as in the earlier joint theorem, take \(D(s)=\sum_p\sqrt p(\alpha_p-i\beta_p)p^{-s}\). If these Dirichlet coefficients have a common bound, the coefficients of each \(D^b\) have the fixed bound \(b!\max(1,\sup_p\sqrt p\,|\alpha_p-i\beta_p|)^b\), including complex phases. Apply (1) to its mixed powers. As before, the absolute Fourier majorant corresponds to replacing the projected weights by \(d_p=(\alpha_p^2+\beta_p^2)^{1/2}\) and retaining cosines. Its model raw moments are \(O_j((1+\sqrt{H_P})^j)\) when \(\sum d_p^2\) is bounded. Centering costs at most the same growth from the model mean. Thus the moment comparison also vanishes on these ranges, and the previous projected triangular-array argument gives the joint Gaussian limit when the covariance converges and the largest normalized column goes to zero. A common bound on \(\sqrt p(\|a_p\|+\|b_p\|)\) suffices for all directions in fixed bounded sets. This covers the usual reciprocal-square-root bands and harmonic statistics; an arbitrary normalized unit-phase coefficient need not meet this polynomial envelope. [Previous joint theorem and positivity/majorant argument](uniform65_joint_exceptional_prime_clt.md)

For the scalar polynomial cutoff \(P=Y^\theta\), fix \(P_0=\exp(L\exp(-(\log L)^{1/3}))\). Eventually \(P_0<P\). The omitted band \(S=(P_0,P]\) has \(H_S=O((\log L)^{1/3})\) by Mertens. For any even order \(2m\) with \(m\theta<1/2\), use \(|\Re D_S|^{2m}\le |D_S^m|^2\). The polynomial in this square has length \(P^m\le T^\delta\) for fixed \(m\theta<\delta<1/2\). Boundedness of the main multipliers in (1) and the model majorant give \(\mathbb E_T |D_S^m|^2\ll_m(1+H_S)^{2m}\). Indeed the real model sum has mean \(H_S\), the imaginary sum has mean zero, and both centered sums have moments bounded by powers of \(1+\sqrt{H_S}\). Centering the band at \(H_S\) and dividing by \(\sqrt{V_P}\) gives a vanishing \(2m\)-th absolute moment, because \(V_P\asymp\log L\). Also \(V_{P_0}/V_P\to1\). Transfer the near block by (2) and remove the omitted band by Hölder through order \(2m\). The case \(m=1\) gives weak convergence by the probability bound on the omitted band, for every fixed \(\theta<1/2\), without requiring arbitrarily long polynomial powers at that cutoff.

All comparisons were for prefixes with the same fixed \(Y\) and cutoffs across \(T\ge Y\). Signed moment differences bounded by \(A(T)\epsilon(Y)\) and nonnegative moment upper bounds bounded by \(A(T)C(Y)\) transfer to the strict tail by Stieltjes integration against \(f(t)=(1/4+t^2)^{-1}\), with discrepancy at most \(O(\epsilon(Y))\) after normalization. The endpoint contribution uses \(-f(Y)R(Y)\) and the integral \(-\int_Y^\infty f'(t)R(t)\,dt\). For bounds instead of discrepancies the same absolute estimate works. This is the earlier normalization and use of RH positivity. [Strict-tail proof](uniform64_exceptional_prime_clt_skeptic.md)

## 3. What an individual unit-phase argument can prove

For arbitrary normalized joint arrays without a common Dirichlet coefficient envelope, compare Fourier words individually. A unit phase associated with \(m\) is \(\sqrt m\,m^{-\rho}\), so applying a bounded-coefficient theorem to \(m^{-\rho}\) requires putting back \(\sqrt{mn}\) for the word \(m/n\). The absolute coefficient sum for degree \(j\) after projection is \(O_j(P^{j/2})\) under a bounded projected \(\ell_2\) norm, by Cauchy–Schwarz. The total \(P^j\) bound before an additive monomial error uses \(mn\le P^j\); centering only adds a power of \(1+\sqrt{H_P}\). A fixed arbitrary log saving from Theorem 5 cannot absorb this at \(P=\exp(L^a)\).

Under RH alone the prime-excluding sparse reconstruction in the earlier effective audit does prove the extension to \(\log P=o(\sqrt L)\), including every fixed \(a<1/2\). Fix a maximum even order \(j_0\), take \(N=P^{j_0}\), and at each prefix use \(Q=\lceil e^{\sqrt{\log T}}\rceil\) in FGKMT Lemma 7.1 and Corollary 6. The selected exceptional prime \(\ell_T\) is \(\gg\log\log T\) if present. Here \(N<Q\) eventually, and every nonzero conductor has \(q\le N\) by the HLZ factor \(a(kq)\). Omit \(\ell_T\) from the prime block. Padded monomials preserve this omission, so the primitive exceptional conductor contributes zero, also when arising from an induced character. With Perron height \(U=\exp(c_0\sqrt{\log T})\) and a small fixed \(c_0>0\), the effective zero-free rectangle for the surviving characters still has width \(c_1/\sqrt{\log T}\), including the \(O(1/\log T)\) shifts. The proof of the effective audit applies with each crude factor formerly bounded by a fixed power of \(\log T\) from support size, divisor factors and outer counting replaced by \(N^{O(1)}\). Absolute initial Dirichlet series still cost fixed log powers, times the divisor factors at \(d\mid kq\). The Perron short interval radius \(x/\sqrt U\) absorbs its elementary divisor-sum boundary term, since now \(\log x=\log T+O(\log N)\). The direct short-twist AFE estimate and the good-height or stationary-phase steps still save powers of \(T\), since their formerly polylogarithmic support factors are at most \(N^{O(1)}\). In particular the shifted residual before differentiating is
\[
 T\exp\{-c_2\sqrt{\log T}+O(\log N+\log\log T)\}.
\]
After normalization and word summation, the same type of bound holds, with other fixed constants and powers of \(N\). The model-weighted error is \(O((\log(2P)/\log T)(1+\sqrt{H_P})^{j_0})\). Both go to zero uniformly for \(T\ge Y\). Restore the omitted centered coordinate with the same largest-column or exceptional-supremum bound as before, using the uniform even retained absolute moment, before passing to the strict tail. For a non-Gaussian moment comparison the relevant supremum is now over \(p\ge c\log\log Y\), and need only go to zero on the potential omitted coordinates. Gaussian infinitesimal arrays automatically meet it. The available effective bounds and fixed-degree smoothing in the previous joint proof give the same effective interpretation for computable rates. [Effective sparse source reconstruction](uniform64_exceptional_prime_clt_skeptic.md) · [Joint restoration and smoothing](uniform65_joint_exceptional_prime_clt.md) · [FGKMT, Corollary 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.5029v3)

For \(P=\exp(c\sqrt L)\) the argument gives a fixed-order result with \(c>0\) sufficiently small after the maximum order and Perron constants are fixed. It supplies no uniform \(c\) for all orders. For \(a>1/2\), deleting a prime at a larger conductor cutoff leaves the absolute-coefficient cost untouched. In the crude proof the zero-free rectangle then has a weaker width \(O(1/\log(Q U))\); a narrower Perron contour cannot absorb \(\exp(C_j L^a)\) from the word sum. Nor does Siegel give a bound of the required size: for \(q=\exp(L^a)\), its lower bound is \(1-\beta\gg_\epsilon\exp(-\epsilon L^a)\). This does not force \(T^{\beta-1}\) to tend to zero. Proposition 11's large-conductor \(\eta^{-1/2+\epsilon}\) saving is accompanied by a small-conductor \(\eta^{3/2+\epsilon}\) cost in Proposition 10, which is written for polylogarithmic \(\eta\). Choosing \(\eta\) to beat the unit-phase word sum is therefore unsupported by these estimates. A sufficient missing input is a residual bound for the **sum against these unit-phase coefficients**, whose weighted operator norm is small, or an additive individual monomial estimate smaller than \(P^{-j}\) at each required fixed order. Aggregate bounded-coefficient polarization bypasses this demand in §2. A conditional improvement is described next.

Under GRH for Dirichlet \(L\)-functions use the source's explicit GRH remark, \(O_\epsilon(y^{3/2+\epsilon}T^{1/2+\epsilon})\) for the shifted error with \(|a(n)|\ll_\epsilon n^\epsilon\) and \(y=T^\delta,\delta<1/3\). The arXiv Theorem 2 remark states the resulting full asymptotic, and the journal Theorem 4 remark locates the conductor improvement at Proposition 10. The divisor-closed padded monomials have this envelope. Complex Cauchy differentiation costs logarithms. For degree \(j\) and \(P=Y^\theta\), after phase normalization and absolute summation the displayed power suffices for \(j\theta<1/5\), since one can take \(\delta>j\theta\) with \(3\delta/2+j\theta<1/2\). This concerns the additive remainder with the actual main kernel, whose relative difference from the Poisson kernel is only \(O_j(\theta)\) at that scale. It therefore does not prove the full unit-phase Gaussian comparison at a fixed polynomial cutoff. For \(P=\exp(L^a)\), every \(a<1\), choose a fixed \(\delta<1/3\); the \(P^j\) factor is subpower and the main relative error times the model absolute moments tends to zero. This does give general fixed-dimensional joint comparison at that larger cutoff under the extra GRH. Neither statement uses an unweighted zero statistic. ## 4. A polynomial-scale main-term discrepancy

Take \(0<\theta_1<\theta_2<1/2\), \(S=(Y^{\theta_1},Y^{\theta_2}]\), and \(W_S=\Re D_S\). Write \(x=\log p/\log T\). In the symmetrized \((p,1)\) kernel the limiting normalized factor at \(p\to\infty\) is
\[
 F(x)=1-4x+2x^3.
\]
Here is the calculation on the actual HLZ residue kernel, with lower-order errors uniform for \(p\in S,\ T\ge Y\). Set the shifts to \(a/\log T,b/\log T,c/\log T\) and use fixed separated scaled circles. An ordered \(I\) factor has leading zeta quotient \((\log T)\,v/((u+v)(v+w))\), for a tuple of scaled shifts \((u,v,w)\). If \(h=p\), its arithmetic factor is \(e^{-vx}\); if \(k=p\), the exact local formula for \(e=1\) gives \(e^{-ux}+e^{-wx}-1+O(1/p)\). Therefore their average is
\[
 \frac{e^{-vx}+e^{-wx}+e^{-ux}-1}{2}+O(1/p).
\]
The three tuples \((u,v,w,t)\) for \(I(a,b)\) are \((a,b,c,0)\), \((-b,-a,c,-a-b)\), \((a,-c,-b,-b-c)\), where the last coordinate is the limiting exponent of the time factor. Indeed, after dividing by \(T\), the time integrals of \((t'/(2\pi))^{t/\log T}\) equal \(e^t+O(1/\log T)\) by direct integration. Adding a factor \(\log(t'/(2\pi))/\log T\) in \(J\) leaves the same limit. Laurent expansions of the zeta factors have uniform \(O(1/\log T)\) errors after their indicated scaling on the circles.

Extract the outer \(ab\) coefficient and, for \(I\), the auxiliary \(c\) coefficient. Simultaneous common rotation of all the circles keeps only homogeneous total degree two in \(J\) and degree three in prederivative \(I\). Thus it suffices to take the cubic exponential terms divided by \(a+b\) in \(J\), and the quartic exponential terms times \(v/((u+v)(v+w))\) in \(I\). In \(J\) the retained rational expression is
\[
 \frac{(-ax)^3+(-bx)^3-(-a-b+ax)^3-(-a-b+bx)^3}{12(a+b)} .
\]
In \(I\) the term per tuple is
\[
 \frac{v[(t-ux)^4+(t-vx)^4+(t-wx)^4-t^4]}
      {48(u+v)(v+w)} .
\]
Add its reflected version with \(a,b\) exchanged. Taking \(\partial_c|_{c=0}\) simplifies the sum into a polynomial in \(a,b,x\). The coefficients of \(ab\) are respectively \((x-1)^2(x+2)/6\) and \((2x-3)/12\). Their sum, divided by the untwisted \(1/12\), is \(F(x)\). These are exact symbolic operations. Uniform bounds on the scaled circles give an error \(O(1/\log T+1/p)\) after differentiation in the scaled leading kernel. This extraction concerns each main-term coefficient. It does not invoke an individual relative asymptotic for an arithmetic prime character. [Exact symbolic check](../../scripts/prime70_hlz_polynomial_band_check.py)

Use aggregate polarization of \(D_S\) against 1, both of length at most \(Y^{\theta_2}\) with coefficient bound 1. The preceding exact factors and the common log-saving **aggregate** remainder prove
\[
 \mathbb E_T W_S=\sum_{p\in S}\frac1p F\!\left(\frac{\log p}{\log T}\right)+o(1)
\]
uniformly for \(T\ge Y\). The normalized strict tail expectation has the same leading expression with \(T\) replaced by \(Y\). To see this, subtract the target with \(\log Y\) from the prefix moment and use the same Stieltjes formula. The endpoint has the correct target, and the integrated difference of the two main expressions is bounded by a constant times \(H_S\log(t/Y)/L\). The weighted integral with this factor is \(O(H_S/L)\) after normalization, by substituting \(t=Y e^u\) in \(\int_Y^\infty t^{-2}(\log t)^4\log(t/Y)\,dt\). By Mertens and the continuity of \(F\),
\[
 \lim_{Y\to\infty}\left(\mathbb E_Y^{\rm tail} W_S-H_S\right)
 =\int_{\theta_1}^{\theta_2} (F(u)-1)\frac{du}{u}
 =-4(\theta_2-\theta_1)+\frac23(\theta_2^3-\theta_1^3)\ne0 .
\]
Moreover \(\mathbb E_Y^{\rm tail}|W_S-H_S|^2=O(1)\), by the same majorant as in §2 using \(|D_S|^2\) of length \(P\) and bounded \(H_S\). Thus the first moments are uniformly integrable. This excludes convergence of these model-centered moving bands to the mean-zero Poisson-model Gaussian. It does not determine the full limiting band law or its variance. The cumulative scalar at polynomial cutoff still has the weak Gaussian limit in §2 because the variance of the cumulative low block diverges and the omitted band's normalized contribution vanishes.


The finite-prime remainder can in fact be bounded by \(O(x^2/p)\) in the local factor: the exact correction to \(U+V-1\) is \((U-1)(1-V)z/(1-z)\). As \(\sum_p(\log p)^2/p^2<\infty\), this refined error can be summed starting at \(p=2\). The other scaled-circle errors sum to \(O(H_P/L)\). For the cumulative scalar \(P=Y^\theta\), \(0<\theta<1/2\), the same aggregate first-moment proof therefore yields the finite centering correction
\[
 \mathbb E_Y^{\rm tail}(W_P-H_P)\ \longrightarrow\ -4\theta+\tfrac23\theta^3 .
\]
For example, use Mertens on every band \([\varepsilon,\theta]\) in the scaled logarithm \(x\). The omitted \(x<\varepsilon\) part costs \(O(\varepsilon)\) by \(|F(x)-1|\ll x\) and the elementary bound \(\sum_{p\le X}(\log p)/p\ll\log X\). The fixed correction disappears after division by the diverging \(\sqrt{V_P}\).
