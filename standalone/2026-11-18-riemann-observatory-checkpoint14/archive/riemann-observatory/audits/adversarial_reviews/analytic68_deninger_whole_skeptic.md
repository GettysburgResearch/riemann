# Independent whole-work audit of Deninger’s real-coefficient obstruction

18 November 2026. I read all eight pages of arXiv:2204.02714v2, dated 27 January 2023, including the references and the categorical discussion. The cached PDF matches SHA-256 abef52f91f14b13f53602c5af2add8e657caffbcee984b288d3eaf8adca50e37. I also retrieved the published nine-page text and compared the obstruction in §2. It is numbered Theorem 2.1 on journal p. 1722, with the same assumption 2.12 and the same quantifier wording as Theorem 2.13 on v2 p. 5. The underlying obstruction is sound for fields with an odd central order in a quaternionic constituent. The printed opening “For a number field \(k\)” admits an overly strong reading. The proof chooses a specific \(k\) with that obstruction and rules out a universal functor taking in such a field. ## From the spectral desiderata to the central Artin multiplicity

- **§1, pp. 1–2.** The base of the finite-field argument is \(\overline{\mathbb F}_p\), as the overline in the PDF confirms. All geometric endomorphisms of a supersingular elliptic curve act contravariantly on \(H^1\). The rational endomorphism algebra tensored with \(\mathbb R\) is \(\mathbb H\). Its opposite is isomorphic to itself. A unital anti-homomorphism into \(M_2(\mathbb R)\) would be injective by simplicity and therefore an isomorphism by real dimension, contradicting division versus split. The claim depends on the usual two-dimensional \(H^1\) and geometric endomorphisms, which are not the endomorphisms over an arbitrary fixed finite ground field. Over \(\mathbb Q_\ell\), \(\ell\ne p\), the quaternion algebra splits. The crystalline coefficient field on the page is the fraction field of \(W(\overline{\mathbb F}_p)\), which splits the algebra at \(p\). The references to Kottwitz categories and standard conjectures are wider context. They do not enter the arithmetic obstruction.
- **2.1–2.6, pp. 2–3.** The generator \(\theta\) is a derivation, \(H^0\) is the zero-eigenvalue copy of \(\mathbb C\), and \(H^2\) has a trace isomorphism to the copy where \(\theta=1\). The middle degree has zeros, counted by dimensions of generalized eigenspaces. On a cup of generalized \(\alpha,\beta\)-vectors, a large enough power of \(\theta-(\alpha+\beta)\) vanishes by the binomial expansion of the derivation on the two factors. Therefore a nonzero cup into \(H^2\) requires \(\alpha+\beta=1\). Perfectness for those paired spaces is a separate desired property. It is consistent with the functional equation and does not follow merely from multiplicity symmetry.
- **2.7, p. 3.** A positive Hermitian form from \(\mathrm{tr}(h\cup *h')\) with antilinear \(*\), \(*^2=-1\), and \(*\theta=\theta *\) would give \(\langle\theta h,h'\rangle+\langle h,\theta h'\rangle=\langle h,h'\rangle\), using the derivation. This forces each eigenvalue of \(\theta-\tfrac12\) to be imaginary and excludes nontrivial finite Jordan chains. Indeed if \(A=\theta-\tfrac12\), \(Av=\lambda v\), \(v=(A-\lambda)u\), then \(\langle v,v\rangle=\langle u,(-A-\bar\lambda)v\rangle=0\). This suffices for the zero interpretation of 2.3. It supplies no boundedness, domain theorem, compact resolvent or description of continuous spectrum in a Hilbert completion. Deninger’s height-pairing comparison motivates positivity but does not construct this scalar product.
- **2.8–2.9, pp. 3–4.** \(V_k=H^1(\theta\sim1/2)\) is finite dimensional if its dimension equals the central order of the Dedekind zeta function. The functional equation with sign \(+1\) gives an even order there. An antilinear square \(-1\) or a perfect alternating cup product also forces its total dimension even. Total evenness is weaker than even multiplicity in every quaternionic constituent. The group action commutes with \(\theta\). To obtain Galois-invariant cup pairings into a trivial \(H^2\), Morin’s heuristic uses a continuous equivariant cycle-class map from the Arakelov Chow group. Its compact kernel maps to zero in additive \(\mathbb C\). Concluding a real copy in the image additionally uses a nonzero cycle-class map on the degree quotient. Theorem 2.13 needs neither the cycle-class map nor trivial Galois action on \(H^2\).
- **2.10–2.12, pp. 4–5.** For a finite Galois group \(G\), \(H(\pi)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_G(V_\pi,H)\otimes V_\pi\) with \(\theta\) acting on the first factor. The finite group algebra is semisimple, so this algebraic statement also holds for infinite-dimensional \(H\). The generalized \(\alpha\)-eigenspace on \(\operatorname{Hom}_G(V_\pi,H)\) is \(\operatorname{Hom}_G(V_\pi,H^{\theta\sim\alpha})\). To check the reverse containment, take the maximum of the nilpotence exponents for the images of a finite basis of \(V_\pi\). The Artin factorization of \(\zeta_k\) is consistent with assigning each individual Artin factor to its own multiplicity space. That assignment is a **new hypothesis**, 2.10, with its central case 2.12. The total Dedekind divisor alone leaves the allocation among constituents undetermined. There is also a pole caveat to interpreting 2.11 at all points for arbitrary Artin factors. The obstruction uses only the central order in a known entire Artin \(L\)-function, so it avoids any general holomorphy conjecture.

## Direct reconstruction of Theorem 2.13

Let an \(\mathbb R\)-linear \(\theta_{\mathbb R}\) commute with the real \(G\)-action, and use the algebraic complexification. Since \(1/2\) is real, complex conjugation commutes with \(\theta-1/2\). Every vector in the generalized central space is uniquely \(x+iy\) with both real parts killed by the same power. Hence
\[
(H_{\mathbb R}\otimes\mathbb C)^{\theta\sim1/2}
=(H_{\mathbb R}^{\theta_{\mathbb R}\sim1/2})\otimes\mathbb C.
\]
The equality needs no global diagonalizability, no decomposition over an infinite list of eigenvalues and no topology. Denote the induced conjugation on the central space by \(J\), with \(J^2=+1\).

A complex irreducible representation of a finite group has invariant Hermitian metric. Conjugate and dual are then isomorphic. For an irreducible quaternionic/symplectic representation \(V_\pi\), the equivalent conditions are Frobenius–Schur indicator \(-1\), a nondegenerate invariant alternating complex bilinear form, or an equivariant antilinear map \(j\) with \(j^2=-1\). The character is real-valued, but the representation is not the complexification of a real representation of the same dimension. For a complex-type irreducible, the conjugate differs from the original. Its realification complexifies to the dual pair \(V\oplus\bar V\). Orthogonal irreducibles have equivariant antilinear square \(+1\). Quaternionic irreducibles complexify from their underlying real irreducibles to two copies, since \(V\simeq\bar V\). This is the distinction behind the proof's three cases.

There is an equivalent parity proof that does not first decompose any real representation. On the complex multiplicity space \(M_\pi=\operatorname{Hom}_G(V_\pi,V_k)\), let \(K(f)=J f j^{-1}\). This is complex-linear in its argument \(v\in V_\pi\), antilinear in \(f\), and \(K^2(f)=J^2f j^{-2}=-f\). A finite-dimensional complex space admitting such \(K\) has even dimension. For a basis write \(K=A\circ\mathrm{conj}\), so \(A\bar A=-I\) and \(|\det A|^2=(-1)^{\dim M_\pi}\). Therefore a finite odd \(\operatorname{ord}_{1/2}L(V_\pi,s)\) is incompatible with 2.12 and real descent. The assumed finite multiplicity of this one constituent suffices even if the ambient \(H^1\) or central space elsewhere is infinite.

For a completed holomorphic self-dual \(L\)-function of sign \(-1\), \(F(1/2+z)=-F(1/2-z)\), so its first nonzero Taylor coefficient has odd index at least one. At \(1/2\) the conductor and archimedean factors of the witness are finite and nonzero, so the order is the same for the uncompleted Artin \(L\)-function. The sign equation on its own would force only parity of a Laurent order in a meromorphic setting. The known holomorphic witness makes this distinction immaterial to the no-go, and 2.12 would already exclude a central pole.

## Exact quaternionic witness and source scope

Shankar–Södergren–Templier give in Example 1.3(ii), p. 7, \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{(5+\sqrt5)(41+6\sqrt{41})})\), with Galois group \(Q_8\) and root number \(-1\) for its two-dimensional factor. Their §8, pp. 50–51, gives its irreducible character and explains entireness by induction from a nontrivial order-four Hecke character of a quadratic subfield. This is primary full-text corroboration independent of Deninger’s use of Omar. I did not obtain valid full text from the tested Omar endpoints. The Taylor publisher PDF returned 403 and the tested Euclid response was HTML. I use Example 1.3(ii) for the root-number sign, not the bibliographic abstract or an independent numerical zero count. Jacques Queyrut’s primary French exposition gives both signs for quaternion fields on p. 133 and states the global \(+1\) theorem for real-realizable characters, a stricter condition than real-valued characters. [Shankar–Södergren–Templier](https://doi.org/10.1017/fms.2019.18) · [Omar publisher](https://doi.org/10.1080/10586458.2001.10504445) · [Queyrut original](https://www.numdam.org/articles/10.24033/msmf.140/)

For an exact check of the field’s Galois group, set \(u=\sqrt5\), \(v=\sqrt{41}\), \(M=\mathbb Q(u,v)\), \(\theta=(5+u)(41+6v)\) and \(a=\sqrt\theta\). If \(s_u,s_v\) negate \(u,v\) separately in \(M\), then \(\theta^{s_u}/\theta=c_u^2\) and \(\theta^{s_v}/\theta=c_v^2\) with \(c_u=(5-u)/(2u)\), \(c_v=(41-6v)/(uv)\). For each flip \(s_i(c_i)c_i=-1\). This already excludes \(a\in M\), since applying the relevant flip twice to a square root in \(M\) would negate it. The extensions \(s_i(a)=c_i a\) each square to the nontrivial involution \(a\mapsto-a\) over \(M\). They anticommute up to this involution because \(s_u(c_v)=-c_v\) and \(s_v(c_u)=c_u\). Hence \(M(a)/\mathbb Q\) is \(Q_8\)-Galois. The four sign conjugates of \(\theta=205+41u+30v+6uv\) are distinct, so \(\mathbb Q(\theta)=M\) and \(\mathbb Q(a)=M(a)\). Expanding \(\prod_{s\in\mathrm{Gal}(M/\mathbb Q)}(X^2-\theta^s)\) gives \(X^8-820X^6+146780X^4-3362000X^2+16810000\).

The unique two-dimensional irreducible has generators \(\operatorname{diag}(i,-i)\) and \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\). Both have determinant one, so the standard wedge pairing is invariant. Equivalently the Frobenius–Schur sum is \((2+2-6\cdot2)/8=-1\). Induce the order-four character from any cyclic subgroup of index two. The global Artin induction relation with the corresponding finite-order Hecke character gives the needed entire \(L\)-function. The witness suffices for the universal obstruction with **no** simple-central-zero claim.

## Limits and possible escapes

1. The proof excludes real Artin-compatible central descent for any \(k\) with the indicated constituent, and thus a functor on all number fields or any family containing a witness. On the isolated object \(\mathbb Q\), the only irreducible is trivial and \(\zeta(1/2)=\eta(1/2)/(1-\sqrt2)\ne0\) by the positive alternating eta series. Assigning the central space zero therefore satisfies 2.12. Even the full zero multiset of \(\zeta\), taken as bare spectral data, has a real algebraic model: each nonreal conjugate pair gives a two-dimensional real rotation-dilation block and each real zero gives a real scalar block, using the observed multiplicities in the algebraic direct sum. This is not a geometric cohomology construction. It shows why the arithmetic no-go requires the Galois and Artin constraints. Even for the witness field, matching just the total Dedekind central dimension by a trivial real representation would not violate the parity obstruction, because that weaker assignment drops 2.12.
2. The proposed \(\mathcal K_{\mathbb R}\) category in §1 is real-linear with a grading and antilinear square \(+1\) in degree zero, \(-1\) in degree one. Its odd object can carry a single quaternionic constituent. The corresponding odd \(H^1\) uses the expected \(*\) of 2.7. This is a way of avoiding the parity hypothesis of ordinary real complexification. It is not a construction of the cohomology or of positivity. The \(\mathbb C\)-lines \(H^0,H^2\) must be in even grading because an odd quaternionic structure requires even complex dimension. Their natural structures, the compatibility of \(*\) with the functor, and the Arakelov class motivation remain proposals.
3. On pp. 6–7 the Vanishing Conjecture adds both absence of all unforced central zeros and order one for forced zeros. A negative root number gives odd order, not order one. The decomposition 2.14 uses this conjecture **and** 2.12. Cup uniqueness up to scale on each summand uses invariant perfectness, pairwise inequivalence and Schur's lemma. Cross terms between distinct self-dual types vanish under invariance. Suggestion 2.15 additionally asks for functoriality on field maps, nondegenerate alternating forms and a positive scalar product via \(*\), as well as a possible local decomposition. Those are independent requirements beyond the direct-sum dimension statement. The cohomological interpretation of field functoriality follows the reversal from field embeddings to maps of spectra and then contravariant cohomology, with conventions for Galois action chosen consistently.
4. Ordinary leafwise cohomology has a natural involutive real form, so any model identifying it equivariantly with all of these Artin multiplicities would inherit the obstruction. Twisted complex leafwise cohomology can evade that premise. The Abel–Jacobi and height comparison, half-Tate twist, motivic Ext interpretation, \(K_0\) Adams eigenspace and leading-coefficient analogies remain speculative. The paper does not rule on abstract real Hilbert–Pólya models for the Riemann zeta function alone, and the obstruction neither assumes nor proves RH.
