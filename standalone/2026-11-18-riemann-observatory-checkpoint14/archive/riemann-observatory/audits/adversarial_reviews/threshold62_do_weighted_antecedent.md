## Exact theorem and proof dependencies

Dimitrov–Oliveira Theorem 2, p. 3, is stated for a Dirichlet L-function L(s,χ) of a character modulo q and p_DO in (1,2]. The printed theorem does not impose primitivity or exclude a principal character. It states zero-freeness in the open half-plane Re s>1/p_DO iff the infimum norm tends to zero over ordinary Dirichlet polynomials A_N(s)=Σ_{n≤N} b_n n^(−s) with complex coefficients. There is no condition at s=1. Squaring the norm gives
    (1/(2π))∫_R |1−L(b+it,χ)A_N(b+it)|² dt/(b²+t²),  b=1/p_DO.
For χ the unique principal character modulo 1, L(s,χ)=ζ(s); a principal character at larger modulus introduces finite Euler factors. The range is precisely 1/2≤b<1, and zeros on the boundary are allowed. The physical space is Hilbert for all these values, even when p_DO<2. [Theorem 2 and (1.2)](../../sources/threshold62_do/1608.07887v1.pdf#page=3)

The proof of Theorem 1 on pp. 14–17 shifts the L-function to η(w)=L(w+b−1/2,χ) and uses κ(x)=B x^α−Σ_{n≤x}χ(n)n^(1/2−b), where α=3/2−b and B=φ(q)/(qα) for a principal character, and α=B=0 for a nonprincipal one. Then κ is bounded for x≥1 by partial summation and bounded periodic counting errors (principal) or bounded character sums (nonprincipal). For a principal character κ(1/(kx)) has the explicit tail Bk^(−α)x^(−α) for x>1. It is square-integrable since α>1/2. For a nonprincipal character there is no such tail. On Re w=1/2 the full Mellin transform of κ(1/(kx)) is −k^(−w)η(w)/w. In the principal case, the contributions B/(w−α) over (0,1) and B/(α−w) over (1,∞) cancel. Formula (2.3) on p. 5 is the corresponding restricted transform after the tail constraint.

For sufficiency, pp. 15–16 use damped reciprocal coefficients and send the polynomial cutoff to infinity at fixed positive damping, using Lemmas 1–2 and the boundary growth of L. Then they send damping to zero using the completed-function quotient and Stirling, after a principal-tail correction involving 1/L(1+ε,χ). The reciprocal coefficients before the line shift are μ(n)χ(n). For nonprincipal χ no residue-tail correction is needed. An independent way to justify the quotient for an imprimitive χ is to pass to its inducing primitive character and multiply by the quotient of finite Euler factors, uniformly bounded on Re s≥b>0. For a primitive completed L-function, the canonical factors all have horizontal distance to a zero nondecreasing from b+it to b+ε+it when Re ρ≤b. The exponential corrections are uniformly bounded, and Stirling on fixed strips bounds the L-quotient by O((1+|t|)^(ε/2)) for 0<ε≤ε₀<(1−b)/2. This also handles b=1/2 and any boundary zeros, which are a null set in the final pointwise limit. Lemma 2 prints a uniform-in-N partial-sum bound after a fixed positive gap and leaves its proof implicit. The zeta specialization may instead use the separately checked Balazard–Saias fixed-gap estimate, or uniformize the Perron shift in their Lemma 1 by keeping the dependence on the initial ordinate in the horizontal and vertical bounds. [Source §§2.2, 3](../../sources/threshold62_do/1608.07887v1.pdf#page=7) [Independent zeta sufficiency](gallardo_seco_sufficiency_threshold62_round5.md)

I visually checked the exact p. 16 equation (3.15). It prints the full error norm ≤ the tail norm, which is reversed. Restricting to x>1 gives the required inequality
    B |Σ b_k k^(−α)| / √(2α−1) ≤ ∥1_(0,1)−Σ b_k κ(1/(kx))∥₂.
Thus the coefficient sum tends to zero along approximants. Subtract that sum times κ(1/x), a fixed L² function, and use the anchored Mellin separation in (2.3). The p. 2 sentence claiming the individual generators vanish for x>1 also needs the nonprincipal restriction or the principal cancellation, as the paper's later pp. 16–17 explicit tail confirms. The correction norm on p. 17 also needs the square of B in its squared tail integrand. These local corrections leave the principal-tail argument available. [Source pp. 16–17](../../sources/threshold62_do/1608.07887v1.pdf#page=16)

The proof of Theorem 2 on p. 17 replaces A_N(1/2+it) by A_N(b+it) under an infimum. This makes sense by the bijection of coefficient spaces A_N(s)↦A_N(s−b+1/2). The boundary comparison satisfies 1≤|b+it|/|1/2+it|≤2b<2. In particular the source's strict lower comparison should be nonstrict, with equality at b=1/2. The two norms therefore give precisely the same zero-limit criterion. [Proof of Theorem 2](../../sources/threshold62_do/1608.07887v1.pdf#page=17)

## Independent check of rStation Proposition 3

Fix 1/2≤b<1 and take the full space H_b=L²((0,∞),t^(2b−1)dt). Its Mellin transform to the line Re s=b is isometric for boundary measure dt_frequency/(2π). Write χ_0=1_(0,1) for the target and ϱ_n(t)={1/(nt)} for the unconstrained generators. On this line their transforms are 1/s and −n^(−s)ζ(s)/s. Thus the error for a Dirichlet polynomial A(s)=Σ_{n≤N}b_n n^(−s) has spatial representative F=χ_0+Σ_{n≤N} b_n ϱ_n. The tail on t>1 is A(1)/t. If the error on (0,1) has norm U and V=|A(1)|/√(2−2b), then the full norm is (U²+V²)^(1/2) by disjoint supports. Anchoring the polynomial by A′=A−A(1) keeps the same length, sets A′(1)=0, and subtracts A(1)ϱ_1 from the representative. The new tail vanishes. The interior adjustment has norm at most |A(1)|/√(2b), because 0≤ϱ_1<1 almost everywhere. Therefore the anchored error is at most
    U + V √((1−b)/b) ≤ b^(−1/2)(U²+V²)^(1/2).
Take the infimum over all A, and use inclusion of the anchored polynomials for the other direction. This proves d_DO,N(b)≤d_N(b)≤b^(−1/2)d_DO,N(b) for every N≥1, even if an infimum is treated without a chosen minimizer. The anchored polynomials are exactly Σ_{2≤n≤N} b_n(n^(−s)−n^(−1)). Their errors are functions supported on (0,1), and Mellin–Paley–Wiener gives the stated Hardy norm realization. This verifies the finite-distance comparison independently of the textual issues in the earlier preprint. [rStation Proposition 3](../../sources/threshold62_do/rstation63v3.md)

## Weighted Hilbert core and exact overlap with E012

For b in [1/2,1), write η_k(t)={1/(kt)}−k^(−1){1/t} for k≥2. By the distance comparison and Dimitrov–Oliveira Theorem 2, zero-freeness in Re s>b gives 1 in the closed η_k span in L²((0,1),t^(2b−1)dt). Conversely, at a zero ρ of real part σ>b, f↦∫_0^1f(t)t^(ρ−1)dt is bounded by Cauchy–Schwarz with squared evaluation constant 1/[2(σ−b)]. It vanishes on η_k by the exact Mellin transform and takes value 1/ρ on 1.

On I_n=(1/(n+1),1/n], up to endpoints, η_k equals {n/k}=r_k(n)/k. Let D_m f(t)=f(mt), extending f by zero outside (0,1). It has operator norm m^(−b), and D_m η_k=η_{mk}−k^(−1)η_m with η_1=0, by direct expansion. Hence the closed span is dilation invariant. If it contains 1 it contains all 1_(0,1/m] and every cell indicator by differences, and therefore all members of the closed step-function space. This proves the three-way Hilbert closure equivalence stated in rStation Theorem 1 at p=2 and weight t^(2b−1). It does not require the platform proof's quantitative moving-line estimate.

The cell norm weights are (n^(−2b)−(n+1)^(−2b))/(2b) ≍ n^(−1−2b). Under coefficient index n−1, the result is the antecedent to f_0∈closure span{R_k} in D_(−1−2b). This arithmetic-core antecedent and its tail correction appear in the earlier proofs. Gallardo–Gutiérrez–Seco's approximation target instead lives in D_(α+2) with the factor (1−z)^(1−a). The transport to that norm uses the bounded fractional convolution D_(α+2(a−1))→D_α, the strict T_a inverse, and b=(1−α)/2−a. The necessity witness and the explicit pair (α,a)=(−3,7/4) concern this two-parameter transport. The rStation preprint identifies an earlier proof gap in Theorem 5.5 but does not state that weighted counterexample or the two-parameter corrected theorem. Dimitrov–Oliveira and rStation Proposition 3 both stop at b<1. The corrected E012 endpoint b=1 is the separate Bergman case.
