# Independent adjudication of square and inverse-prime savings

For the smooth signed remainder with coefficient \(d_2*(\Lambda\log^2)\), the direct quadratic estimate removes **all** square-prime factors when \(1/2<\theta=4b<9/17\). Baker and divisor Voronoi remove a power-width region around \(p=T/K\) for coprime genuine primes on modulus blocks bounded by fixed exponent gaps from \(1/2\) and \(2/3\). The remaining genuine-prime interval on those blocks starts above \(T^{1+\eta}/K\). Other blocks retain their separate short-factor cutoff. These are estimates for strata, with no conclusion for the entire fourth moment. [Square analysis](weighted107_quadratic_and_inverse_prime_savings.md) · [Baker analysis](prime107_inverse_prime_voronoi.md)

## Source checks and square estimate

I checked Baker’s primary Acta Arithmetica PDF, pp. 351–352, Definition of \(S_q(a;x)\) and Theorem 2, SHA-256 `2b1b0690e08489b077bb632fef2d6ea777ebe6684dc02efe5d6af9e67127cbda`. I checked Blomer–Harcos–Michel, *Bounds for modular L-functions in the level aspect*, §2.3, pp. 708–709, (2.25)–(2.27), SHA-256 `7a07387f1f9da1dcd20d9dda329d0ee02d6d8df5aa3af885227545cbc4149fb5`. For Ghosh I read the rendered Theorems 1–2 on printed thesis p. 4, PDF p. 12, SHA-256 `52c201db4b3f5f19c4a1efc38556aef4c97a700e52d2ab741f7fa17a267f2e55`. The direct quadratic estimate includes any integral prefix length \(L\) and exact reduced rationals with positive numerator and denominator. Its three losses are \(L(q^{-1/4}+L^{-1/8}+q^{1/4}L^{-1/2})\) up to \(L^\epsilon\). Ren’s (1.4) reproduces these powers. [Baker journal](https://www.impan.pl/shop/en/publication/transaction/download/product/82065) · [BHM journal](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.1016/j.ansens.2007.05.003.pdf) · [Ghosh thesis](https://repository.nottingham.ac.uk/bitstreams/8f8d2034-326a-4974-9c1f-e5b488b1fa4e/download#page=12)

Let \(k\asymp K\), \(T\asymp Y\), \(p\asymp X\) and write the square term as \(m=np^2\). The taper support gives \(n\ll KT/X^2\). For each fixed \(k,n\), reduce the numerator of \(np^2/k\) using \(g=(k,n)\) and \(q=k/g\). Every unit numerator over \(k\) becomes a unit numerator over \(q\), with equal fibers. The same quadratic estimate therefore bounds both the original phase and its unit average. If \(q=1\), their difference is zero. No coprimality condition on the divisor argument \(n\) is needed. Shared prime factors \(p\mid k\) can be excluded at the cost of at most four terms per prime prefix, or handled by the previous shared-factor estimate.

On subintervals of \([X,2X]\), take differences of Ghosh prefixes. Prime powers in the prefix variable itself can be removed with \(O(X^{1/2}\log^{O(1)}T)\), absorbed by the \(X^{7/8+\epsilon}\) component. The square-factor weight is \(4\log^3p\), with one log already in Ghosh’s \(\Lambda\) weight. Partial summation inserts the other two and \(w_T(2\pi np^2/k)\). If the taper has two monotone transitions, its composition with the increasing argument has bounded total variation independent of \(H/T\). Clipping a dyadic interval at a cutoff adds bounded endpoint terms. This gives the fixed-\(k,n\) estimate
\[
 T^\epsilon X(q^{-1/4}+X^{-1/8}+q^{1/4}X^{-1/2}).
\]
For each gcd \(g\mid k\), \(\sum_{n\ll KT/X^2,,g\mid n}d_2(n)\ll T^\epsilon KT/(gX^2)\) when the set is nonempty. The gcd powers in the three contributions are respectively \(g^{-3/4},g^{-1},g^{-5/4}\), whose divisor sums are \(T^{o(1)}\). Summing \(c_k/k\) over one modulus block costs \(O(1)\). The resulting estimate is
\[
 T^\epsilon (KT/X)(K^{-1/4}+X^{-1/8}+K^{1/4}X^{-1/2}).
\]
For \(X\lesssim T^{1-\nu}/K\), use instead the pointwise divisor bound \(kT^{\epsilon_0}\) and count the prime bases, with \(\epsilon_0<\nu\). This pointwise estimate is valid with \(\kappa\) chosen close to 1, rather than at every fixed admissible \(\kappa\): the centered Estermann difference has no numerator-dependent polar part, so shifting its tapered Mellin integral to any fixed \(-B<0\) costs \(q\Delta^{-2}(q^2/(M\Delta^2))^B\), where \(M=kT/p^2\), \(\Delta=H/T\). Empty original supports can be ignored, otherwise \(M\gg1\). First take \(1-\kappa\) small, then \(B>0\) small, to obtain the claimed weak pointwise power loss. Polynomial vertical bounds for the finite Hurwitz sum and rapid Mellin decay justify the shift. [Prior centered Mellin calculation](weighted101_single_mangoldt_strata.md)

The large-\(X\) block bound decreases with \(X\). At the split it costs, before \(T^\epsilon\), at most \(K^{7/4}T^\nu+K^{17/8}T^{-1/8+9\nu/8}+K^{11/4}T^{-1/2+3\nu/2}\). The three limiting thresholds on \(\theta\) are \(4/7,9/17,6/11\). Their strict minimum allows fixed positive \(\nu,\epsilon\) and a common taper sufficiently close to the height scale. The logarithmic number of blocks does not use up the margins. This proves \(O(T^{1-c})\) for the full square stratum in \(1/8<b<9/68\). It makes no assertion at equality.

## Baker and Voronoi bridge

With \(p\nmid k\), freeze the genuine prime and apply BHM’s smooth formula to the **unrestricted** divisor variable \(n\). The test \(F(x)=w_T(2\pi px/k)\) is smooth and compactly supported in \((0,\infty)\). The additive numerator is a unit for both the original twist and each unit-average summand. BHM’s polar term \(k^{-1}\int(\log x+2\gamma-2\log k)F(x)dx\) is independent of the unit numerator and cancels. The two kernels are \(-2\pi Y_0(4\pi z)\) and \(4K_0(4\pi z)\). Inversion permutes the units, so the dual subtraction is \(c_k^{\rm Ram}(l)/\varphi(k)\) and the two retained phases are \(e(\pm l\bar p/k)\). This use of Voronoi freezes the prime factor and avoids a contour shift of the entire character-twisted Mangoldt convolution. In the latter series, at a simple character zero, the residue of \(L(s,\chi)^2(-L'/L)''(s,\chi)\) is \(-2 L'(\rho,\chi)^2\). A zero of multiplicity at least two leaves no pole.

Take fixed \(1/2<\vartheta_-<\vartheta_+<2/3\), \(T^{\vartheta_-}\le K\le T^{\vartheta_+}\) and \(T^{1-\eta}/K\le P\le T^{1+\eta}/K\). With \(M=kT/p\), rescale the Voronoi integral by \(x=Mu\). The weight including the outer \(1/k\) contains \(M/k^2\), the fixed-support taper \(w_T(2\pi T u)\), and a kernel at \(\sqrt{lM u}/k\). For \(l>T^{6\eta}\) and \(\Delta\ge T^{-\eta}\), the product \(\Delta\sqrt{lM}/k\gg T^{3\eta/2}\). Exponential decay for \(K_0\) and repeated integration by parts in the separated oscillations of \(Y_0\) give arbitrary savings after summing \(l,k,p\). Every integration costs at most \(\Delta^{-1}\) on a cutoff derivative and gains one inverse Bessel frequency. The gain also decays as \(l^{-1/2}\) per integration, controlling the infinite tail.

For \(l\le T^{6\eta}\) choose \(6\eta<a\). All modulus primes exceed \(Y^a\), so the inverse numerator is a unit in the **original** modulus. The Ramanujan average there is \(\mu(k)/\varphi(k)\ll1/k\) for the four-prime support, including repetitions. The taper now has no prime-variable derivative. Since \(p\partial_p M=-M\), the weight and \(P\) times its first prime-variable derivative cost \(O(T^{3\eta}\log T)\), using \(|J(z)|+|zJ'(z)|\ll1+|\log z|+z^{1/2}\). In this range \(z\ll T^{7\eta/2}\) and \(M/k^2\ll T^\eta\), so the stated loss has slack.

Baker’s Theorem 2 is an average over all integer \(K<k\le2K\) of maxima over **unit numerators** for a fixed common annulus \(x<p\le2x\), with \(\sqrt K\le x\le2K\). At any fixed common prefix endpoint \(t\) in a prime block, dyadic subdivision gives the same estimate at scale \(P\); the leftover \(O(\sqrt K)\) per modulus is absorbed by \(K^{11/10}P^{4/5}\). The strict exponent margins ensure all needed top annuli lie in Baker’s range. Apply Abel summation on a common prime subinterval for each \(k\), use the common derivative bound, and move the modulus sum inside the integral over the endpoint. This uses Baker pointwise in that common endpoint. An estimate with a supremum over different prime endpoints for each modulus is unnecessary. One can slice blocks to the fixed \(K\)-dependent endpoints. If stating a separate \(k\)-dependent sharp cutoff, the same proof needs a conversion or a common partition and I do not assert such a bound directly.

The short dual sum then costs \(T^{9\eta+\epsilon}(K^{11/10}P^{4/5}+KP^{11/12}+P)\), where the last term is the principal subtraction. Substituting \(P\le T^{1+\eta}/K\) gives limiting exponents \(4/5+3\vartheta_+/10\) and \(11/12+\vartheta_+/12\), strictly below one. All choices of \(\eta,\epsilon,1-\kappa\) can be made together by first selecting sufficiently small \(\eta\) and then taking \(\kappa\) sufficiently close to one. The entire bridge has a power saving. [Independent common-endpoint check](uniform108_baker_bridge_qa.md)

The weighted review’s optional pointwise route via Korolev has the stated exponent \((64+17\theta)/74\) at \(p\asymp T^{1-\theta}\), assuming the homogeneous source range \(k^{7/10+\epsilon}\le P\le k\). I checked the publisher abstract and Korolev’s self-citation with the exact powers in the 2019 v1, p. 2, (7). My direct fetch of the 2018 full PDF returned HTML or HTTP 403. The later inhomogeneous theorem requires both coefficients to be units, and does not justify zero linear coefficient. The Mellin calculation in weighted107 has the correct loss for Abel summation on \(\Re s=-B\): one extra \(1+|t|\) changes the integrated cutoff loss to \(\Delta^{-3-2B}\). After absolute dual truncation by a common length below \(Y^a\), the retained coefficients can be shifted to \(-1\) and the tail to a larger fixed negative line. The bridge certified here relies on Baker and BHM, with no dependence on the optional Korolev route. ## Remaining arithmetic support

For each dyadic \(K\) block from the Baker range, choose \(2(1-\kappa)+\delta_0<\eta\). The previous small-factor criterion \(p\lesssim H^2/(KT)\,T^{-\delta_0}\) then overlaps the bottom of the new bridge. Thus, for a common block slicing and \(1/2<\theta<9/17\), the remaining terms on these blocks come from \(p\nmid k\) and
\[
 T^{1+\eta}/K\ lesssim\ p\ \ll KT.
\]
The upper bound is just compact support with \(n\ge1\). Outside the certified Baker blocks, the automatic lower deletion remains the prior short-factor cutoff, and this note makes no estimate for the remaining interval there. On top modulus blocks \(K\asymp Y^\theta\) this gives a lower residual exponent \(1-\theta+\eta\), with a fixed positive \(\eta\) depending on the exponent and band margins. All shared factors and powers \(p^j\), \(j\ge3\), were already removed. Squares are additionally removed throughout these modulus blocks and outside them for \(\theta<9/17\). At larger \(\theta<2/3\) the Baker prime step still applies within fixed exponent margins, and the missing square region is retained. Character estimates which take absolute values before the Gauss-weighted average still leave the long-prime cost; the short dual treatment supplies no cancellation in those remaining strata.
