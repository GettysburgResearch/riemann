# Independent shrinking-band process audit

18 November 2026. The shrinking-band process theorem withstands the audit under RH. Let \(L=\log Y\), fix \(0<r_0<1\), and assume \(b=b(Y)\to0\) and \(bL\to\infty\). Sample a strict-tail ordinate \(|\gamma|>Y\) with mass proportional to \(|\zeta'(1/2+i\gamma)|^2/(1/4+\gamma^2)\). The centered cumulative cosine prime sum from \(Y^{r_0b}\) to \(Y^{rb}\) converges for \(r\in[r_0,1]\) in \(D([r_0,1]),J_1\) to \(B_{\frac12\log(r/r_0)}\). The proof genuinely needs a fourth increment bound that sees arbitrarily small reciprocal masses. I checked this independently and then compared with the available proposal. The scalar CLT or arbitrarily large fixed logarithmic savings alone do not give tightness. [Proposal](uniform81_shrinking_band_process.md) · [Scalar reconstruction](analytic78_shrinking_band.md)

## Fine scale fourth bound from analytic sampling

Keep \(I\subset(Y^{r_0b},Y^b]\) fixed as the prefix endpoint \(T\ge Y\) varies. Write \(\lambda=\log T\), \(h=\sum_{p\in I}1/p\), \(D_I(s)=\sum_{p\in I}p^{-s}\). The band mass bounds \(h\) uniformly by a constant depending only on \(r_0\). Let \(S_I=\Re D_I(\rho)-h\). On RH take \(F(s)=\zeta(s)D_I(s)^2\), so \(F'(\rho)=\zeta'(\rho)D_I(\rho)^2\). This is an analytic identity at every zero, with zero contribution from any multiple zero. There is no simplicity assumption.

On a radius \(R=1/\lambda\) disk about a zero, write \(F(\rho+z)=\sum_k a_kz^k\). Orthogonality of angular powers gives
\[
 \iint_{|z|\le R}|F(\rho+z)|^2\,dA(z)
   =\pi\sum_{k\ge0}\frac{|a_k|^2R^{2k+2}}{k+1}
   \ge \frac{\pi R^4}{2}|F'(\rho)|^2.
\]
The disk is pole-free for large \(T\). The union of disks at \(0<\gamma\le T\) lies in \(|\sigma-\tfrac12|\le R,\ -1\le t\le T+1\). At any point the disk overlap, with multiplicities, is bounded by the number of zeros in an ordinate interval of length at most two. The Riemann–von Mangoldt formula with \(O(\log T)\) error gives \(O(\log(3+|t|))\) there, also allowing exact endpoints by enlarging to a fixed longer interval. Compact heights contribute a bounded constant. Thus the overlap cost is \(O(\lambda)\). This is a sharp-prefix inequality from the disks themselves, valid even if \(T\) is a zero ordinate. [Titchmarsh–Heath-Brown, Theorem 9.4 and (9.4.3)](../../sources/equivalence_round2/titchmarsh1986.pdf)

The continuous fourth moment uniform across the thin strip is justified by vertical localization and the exact functional equation. On the right let
\[
 G_T(s)=\frac{s-1}{s+1}\zeta(s)\exp(s^2/T^2).
\]
It is holomorphic on the strip \([1/2,2]\), including at \(s=1\), and decays at large imaginary height since zeta has polynomial growth there. At \(\sigma=1/2\), dyadic summation of the classical fourth moment gives \(\|G_T(1/2+it)\|_{L^4(dt)}\ll T^{1/4}\lambda\). The geometric-Gaussian weights make the dyadic sum bounded uniformly for \(T\ge3\). On \(\sigma=2\) absolute convergence of the Euler series gives \(O(T^{1/4})\). To apply three-lines, pair \(G_T(z+it)\) with a compactly supported \(L^{4/3}(dt)\) test of norm at most one. On an endpoint line an imaginary translation of \(z\) has no effect on the full \(L^4\) norm. Uniform growth and holomorphy allow Hadamard's theorem for each pairing. Taking the supremum of the pairings gives the \(L^4\) bound at all intervening real parts. On \(-1\le t\le T+1\) and \(1/2\le\sigma\le1/2+R<3/4\), the pole-removal and Gaussian factors have modulus bounded below. The opposite side uses
\[
 |\zeta(\sigma+it)|=|\chi(\sigma+it)|\,|\zeta(1-\sigma+it)|,\qquad
 |\chi(\sigma+it)|\ll(1+|t|)^{1/2-\sigma}\ll1
\]
uniformly in the thin strip. The bounded-height statement follows by gamma regularity on the compact strip and the large-height one by Stirling. Hence \(\int_{-1}^{T+1}|\zeta(\sigma+it)|^4dt\ll T\lambda^4\) on the entire strip. The critical-line input is classical Ingham or Heath-Brown, stated in (7.6.2) or (7.21.1) of the monograph. Theorem 7.5 in that edition treats \(\sigma>1/2\); it is not the critical-line statement. [Titchmarsh–Heath-Brown, §§7.6 and 7.21](../../sources/equivalence_round2/titchmarsh1986.pdf)

For the eighth moment of \(D_I\), write \(D_I^4=\sum_{n\le N}c_n n^{-s}\), with \(N=Y^{4b}\), \(0\le c_n\le4!\). In the disk strip,
\[
 \sum_{n\le N}c_n^2 n^{-2\sigma}
 \le 4!\Big(\sum_{p\in I} p^{-2\sigma}\Big)^4\ll h^4
\]
since \(p^{2/\lambda}\le e^{2bL/\lambda}\). Direct integration of pairs of distinct integers uses \(2/|\log(n/m)|\le2N/|m-n|\). Bound the product of two weighted coefficients by half the sum of their squares and sum the harmonic rows. The off-diagonal cost is \(O(N\log(2N))\) times the displayed coefficient norm. Eventually \(N\le T^{1/2}\) uniformly for \(T\ge Y\), so together with the diagonal \(\int|D_I|^8\ll T h^4\). The case \(I=\varnothing\) is exactly zero. Cauchy–Schwarz now gives \(\int |F|^2\ll T\lambda^2h^2\) on each vertical line. Multiply by strip width \(2/\lambda\), disk factor \(O(\lambda^4)\) and overlap \(O(\lambda)\), obtaining
\[
 \sum_{0<\gamma\le T}|\zeta'(\rho)|^2|D_I(\rho)|^4
 \ll T\lambda^6 h^2 .
\]
Dividing by the true positive prefix mass \(A(T)\asymp T\lambda^4\) gives \(E_T|D_I|^4\ll\lambda^2h^2\). The same scale holds for \(S_I\) since \(h\) is bounded and \(h^4\ll h^2\). No hypothesis on higher discrete derivative moments is used. [Untwisted Gonek asymptotic, Corollary 1](../../sources/classical63_lfunctions/gonek1984.pdf)

## Aggregate bound, strict endpoint and change of scales

A shorter independent check of the complementary bound uses \(|\Re D_I|^4\le|D_I^2|^2\) directly. The coefficients of \(D_I^2\) are bounded by 2, supported through \(Y^{2b}\), and divisor-closed padding gives the admissible quadratic estimate in HLZ Theorem 5. The exact pair weight is \(1/\operatorname{lcm}(m,n)\), times a uniformly bounded relative main coefficient. The local bound holds for repeats as well as distinct primes. In the Poisson circle model with radii \(p^{-1/2}\), the positive coefficient sum with these pair weights is exactly the fourth moment of \(D_I\). For centered complex independent summands, the sum of variances is at most \(h\) and the sum of fourth moments is \(O(\sum_{p\in I}p^{-2})\le O(h^2)\). Expansion for real and imaginary parts gives total \(O(h^2)\); adding the bounded mean \(h\) costs \(O(h^4)\). Therefore \(E_T|S_I|^4\ll_M h^2+\lambda^{-M}\) with uniform fixed-envelope constants. This argument avoids needing cancellations among signed centered powers. It also permits the earlier effective exceptional-prime removal: for a removed prime in \(I\) the fourth power of the omitted term is at most \(16/p^2\le16h^2\). A triangle inequality restores the full array for each \(T\) before tail integration. [HLZ (5), Theorem 5 and (24)](../../sources/classical63_derivative_moments/hlz2022print.pdf) · [Uniform full-length aggregate input](weighted70_hlz_cutoff_review.md)

For \(U_I(T)=A(T)E_T|S_I|^4\) and \(g(t)=(1/4+t^2)^{-1}\), the strict positive-tail numerator is exactly \(-g(Y)U_I(Y)+\int_Y^\infty -g'(t)U_I(t)\,dt\). The first term uses the prefix inclusive at \(Y\), hence excludes a zero equal to \(Y\). The boundary at infinity vanishes by either prefix bound. Upper bounds can discard the nonpositive endpoint term because the integrand is nonnegative. Divide by the actual strict mass, asymptotic to \(Y^{-1}L^4/(24\pi)\) on the positive side. The integral of an extra \((\log t)^2\) factor costs \(O(L^2)\) after normalization, by \(t=Yu\) and integrability of \(u^{-2}(1+\log u)^6\). The negative side gives the same cosine law. Hence
\[
 E_Y|S_I|^4\ll_M\min(h^2+L^{-M},L^2h^2).
\]
For \(h\le L^{-4}\), use the second bound, which is at most \(h^{3/2}\). For \(h\ge L^{-4}\), choose \(M\ge6\), so \(L^{-M}\le h^{3/2}\). The \(h^2\) term is also \(O(h^{3/2})\) by the global band mass bound. There is no lower bound on a nonzero grid mass in this argument. The power-length singleton can have \(h^2\) much smaller than every available fixed log saving. This is why the coarse estimate cannot be omitted.

## Interpolation, jumps and finite-dimensional laws

For the ordered band primes \(p_i\), let \(v_j=\frac12\sum_{i\le j}1/p_i\). Interpolate the statistic linearly from one \(v_j\) to the next, then extend flat to a fixed interval \([0,V]\) containing all the clocks for large \(Y\). At grid endpoints the subset bound is \(E|Z_Y(v)-Z_Y(u)|^4\le C|v-u|^{3/2}\), absorbing the factor two. A fractional interval of size \(\alpha\) in a single prime cell multiplies the fourth moment by \(\alpha^4\), smaller than \(\alpha^{3/2}\). Across cells split into two fractional pieces and the full intervening grid block, and use Minkowski's inequality in \(L^4\). This gives the same bound on all times with a fixed constant. Extension past the last grid point is composition with a 1-Lipschitz clamp. A dyadic union bound on adjacent increments at level \(j\) with threshold \(K2^{-\beta j}\) costs \(O(K^{-4}2^{-(1/2-4\beta)j})\), for \(0<\beta<1/8\). Summation and dyadic chaining give a uniform modulus and tightness in \(C([0,V])\). This estimate applies uniformly beyond the grid and does not require controlling a union over an increasing number of prime cutoffs directly.

The deterministic clock \(c_Y(r)=\frac12\sum_{Y^{r_0 b}<p\le Y^{rb}}1/p\) is uniformly within \(O_{r_0}(1/(bL))\) of \(c(r)=\frac12\log(r/r_0)\), by the elementary effective reciprocal-prime Mertens estimate. This is uniform because \(r\ge r_0>0\) and \(bL\to\infty\). At exact prime cutoffs the original process uses the new grid value, hence is right-continuous and equals \(Z_Y(c_Y(r))\). It starts at zero even if the lower endpoint is a prime, because that prime is excluded. Its jumps have deterministic magnitude at most \(2\exp(-r_0 bL/2)\). The uniform modulus and clock convergence give \(\sup_r|Z_Y(c_Y(r))-Z_Y(c(r))|\to0\) in probability. This controls \(J_1\) distance as well. The continuous approximation \(Z_Y(c(\cdot))\) is the tight family used in the identification.

For finite sets of cutoffs, apply a fixed real linear projection. The resulting coefficients on primes are bounded and piecewise constant. The absolute coefficients of each fixed-degree expansion are dominated by the coefficients from the full band with a fixed positive bound. Thus the relative \(O(b)\) HLZ main term comparison and log-saving errors give projected moment convergence to the corresponding Poisson sums, including odd moments. Centering is a bounded deterministic operation. Exceptional-prime restoration uses the removed coordinate bound \(O(e^{-r_0bL/2})\) times a bounded higher even moment and precedes partial summation. In the independent Poisson model each band's centered coordinate goes uniformly to zero while the variance sums along all disjoint intervals converge to the respective clock increments. Lindeberg, or addition of cumulants with the \(k\)-th cumulant bounded by the largest-coordinate bound to the power \(k-2\) times the sum of variances, gives the projected Gaussian limits with all moments. The covariance at \(r,s\) is the limit of \(\frac12\sum_{p\le Y^{b\min(r,s)},\ p>Y^{r_0b}}(p^{-1}-p^{-2})\), exactly \(c(\min(r,s))\). Cramér–Wold then identifies the finite-dimensional law. Tightness of the continuous approximation and equality in probability at every fixed cutoff identify all path limits, which gives the stated \(J_1\) result. No claim in a nonseparable uniform topology on arbitrary càdlàg paths is needed.

## Scope of effectivity

The crude fourth comparison, the mass normalization and the dyadic modulus estimates admit effective constants. The refined comparison uses the independently stated full-length effective aggregate estimate with one exceptional prime excluded and restored. The finite-dimensional bounded-Lipschitz comparison at a fixed mesh can be made effective from finitely many joint moments. For example add small independent Euclidean Gaussian noise to each vector, restrict smoothed measures to a large fixed ball by the uniform second moments, and bound the density discrepancy there by Fourier inversion. Gaussian damping controls the Fourier tail, and the finite joint moments control the Taylor polynomial on the remaining compact Fourier region. A sufficiently high even Gaussian moment, already eventually matched by the moment comparison, controls the Taylor remainder. The Euclidean smoothing argument works even if the limiting covariance includes the deterministic initial coordinate. Polygonal reconstruction at a fixed \(r\)-mesh then transfers the bounded-Lipschitz bound to path tests by the uniform modulus for the continuous surrogate and Brownian motion. Clock error and jumps are controlled in the same approximation. Thus an in-principle conditional onset follows for computably specified \(r_0\) and effective moduli of \(b\to0\) and \(bL\to\infty\), with the effective source constants understood in the proof-extraction sense. Pointwise computability of \(b\) alone is insufficient, and no numerical onset or uniform result over increasing numbers of projected coordinates is claimed.

**Disposition.** No counterexample or missing arithmetic condition was found. The result is a process theorem because the proof obtains the two-scale fourth bound and interpolates in the actual prime reciprocal-mass clock. The existing fixed-exponent non-Gaussian result has different assumptions and is unaffected.
