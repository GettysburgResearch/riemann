# Exact integer Toeplitz test for reciprocal purity

18 November 2026. Let \(q\ge 1\) be an integer and \(P\in\mathbb Z[X]\) monic of degree \(d\ge1\), whose multiset of roots \(\alpha\) is nonzero and invariant under \(\alpha\mapsto q/\alpha\). Set \(t_n=\sum_\alpha\alpha^n\), with multiplicity and \(t_0=d\). For indices \(0\le i,j<d\), define
\[
K_{ij}=q^{\min(i,j)}t_{|i-j|}.
\]
Then all roots have modulus \(\sqrt q\) if and only if \(K\succeq0\). Repeated roots and reducible \(P\) are allowed. In fact, if \(c\) is the number of distinct roots of modulus \(\sqrt q\) and \(b\) is the number of unordered pairs of distinct off-circle roots interchanged by \(\alpha\mapsto q/\bar\alpha\), the inertia of \(K\) in positive, negative, zero order is
\[
(c+b,\ b,\ d-c-2b).
\]
The upper-left \(r\times r\) matrix gives the same nonzero inertia for \(r=c+2b\). In particular, full positivity can be decided from traces through order \(d-1\), and, if \(r\) is known, through order \(r-1\). [Earlier finite trace formulation](director_finite_trace_round10.md)

## Independent proof

Write \(z=\alpha/\sqrt q\), \(s_n=\sum_z z^n\) for integers \(n\). Reciprocity gives \(s_{-n}=s_n\), and reality gives \(\bar s_n=s_n\). Thus \(T_{ij}=s_{j-i}=s_{|i-j|}\) is real symmetric. The diagonal matrix \(D\) with \(D_{ii}=q^{i/2}>0\) gives \(K=DTD\) because \(i+j-|i-j|=2\min(i,j)\). This argument is valid whether or not \(q\) is a square. Newton's identities show that the \(t_n\) used are integers.

The complex polynomial space of degree \(<d\) is the coefficient space for \(T\). If \(p(z)=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}a_i z^i\), then
\[
a^*Ta=\sum_{z\ \mathrm{distinct}}m_z\,\overline{p(1/\bar z)}\,p(z).
\]
The involution \(z\mapsto1/\bar z\) preserves the normalized roots and their multiplicities. The evaluation map from these polynomials to \(\mathbb C^r\) on distinct roots is surjective, by Lagrange interpolation, with nullity \(d-r\). At a fixed point of the involution the displayed form is \(m_z |p(z)|^2\); at a swapped pair its matrix is \(\begin{pmatrix}0&m_z\\m_z&0\end{pmatrix}\), with eigenvalues \(m_z,-m_z\). Thus the claimed inertia follows from surjectivity and Sylvester's law. The \(r\times r\) case uses the same evaluation map on polynomials of degree \(<r\), where it is invertible. The same real inertia follows from symmetry of \(T\). More explicitly, a negative complex witness \(a=u+iv\) yields \(a^*Ta=u^{\mathsf T}Tu+v^{\mathsf T}Tv<0\), so one of the real vectors already witnesses negativity. This makes no unit-circle assumption in either the interpolation or the representation, and the multiples of distinct roots enter as positive weights throughout.

## Exact algorithm and example

1. If reciprocity is part of input validation, require \(P(0)\ne0\) and check \(X^dP(q/X)=P(0)P(X)\) coefficient by coefficient. This identity is equivalent to reciprocal closure because \(P\) is monic and has no zero root.
2. Write \(P=X^d+a_1X^{d-1}+\cdots+a_d\). Start at \(t_0=d\). For \(1\le k<d\), use \(t_k=-k a_k-\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}a_jt_{k-j}\). Assemble \(K\) with precomputed integer powers of \(q\).
3. Test PSD by exact rational symmetric elimination. If a negative diagonal exists, reject. If a diagonal is zero and some off-diagonal entry in that row is nonzero, reject because the corresponding two-by-two principal determinant is negative. If a row with zero diagonal is entirely zero, it may be discarded. Otherwise select a positive diagonal pivot, take its Schur complement, and repeat. Completion of squares proves each positive-pivot equivalence. The zero-row and two-by-two checks apply to the current Schur complement after any number of pivots. This handles rank deficiency without ambiguities about zero leading minors.

These procedures are polynomial in the coefficient bit size, \(d\), and \(\log q\). For a simple bit bound, put \(M=\max(1,\max_j|a_j|)\). The Cauchy root bound gives \(|t_k|\le d(1+M)^k\), so \(K\) has entry bits \(O(\log d+d(\log(1+M)+\log(2q)))\). Forming the traces uses \(O(d^2)\) integer operations and elimination \(O(d^3)\) rational operations. The intermediate Schur complement entries after positive pivots are quotients of minors of the integer input (by the bordered determinant formula). Hadamard bounds numerator and denominator bits polynomially. Use reduced rationals or fraction-free elimination. An alternative exact sign rule is that every coefficient of \(\det(xI+K)\) is nonnegative iff \(K\succeq0\). If \(K\) has a negative real eigenvalue, the polynomial has a positive real root, impossible for a monic polynomial with nonnegative coefficients. Conversely each factor is \(x+\lambda_i\) with \(\lambda_i\ge0\) when \(K\succeq0\).

The case \(q=100\), \(P=(X^2-21X+100)(X^2+100)\), demonstrates that the low-index joint test is strictly stronger than the two-term tests through the same index. Here \(t_0,t_1,t_2,t_3=4,21,41,2961\), so \(|s_1|=2.1\), \(|s_2|=.41\), \(|s_3|=2.961\), all below 4. The leading principal determinants of \(K\) are \(4,\ 1159,\ 31663800,\ -31894884000000\). The first quadratic supplies an off-circle real reciprocal pair, while the second supplies two unit-circle roots after normalization. In contrast the pure repeated-root example \(q=4,\ P=(X-2)^2\) has \(K=\begin{pmatrix}2&4\\4&8\end{pmatrix}\) of rank one. Thus positive definiteness would reject some pure inputs.

## Antecedents and scope

The exact unscaled power-sum Toeplitz criterion appears in Heinig and Rost, *Bezoutians* (TU Chemnitz preprint 2008), §10 subsection 7, printed p. 62. For a conjugate-symmetric polynomial \(w\) they use \((w')^\#/w^\#=\sum_j(1-\bar z_j t)^{-1}\), explicitly identify its Hermitian Toeplitz matrix and state that its signature counts the distinct circle roots. Since our moments are real and reciprocal, their matrix is the displayed \(T\). Their Corollary 8.7 (printed p. 50) has the corresponding Vandermonde congruence with exchanged-pair blocks. Their Theorem 10.10 states the associated Toeplitz Bezoutian version. The matrix \(K\) here is the integer congruence adapted to the square-radius \(q\) normalization. The circle criterion and its inertia are documented antecedents, so the integer expression should be presented as a specialization. The ordinary Schur–Cohn form in Heinig–Rost §10 subsection 6, (10.6) and Theorem 10.9, is \(\operatorname{Bez}_T(w^\#,w)\) and its positive definiteness addresses the open disk. In the present self-inversive setting \(w^\#\) is a constant multiple of \(w\), so that form vanishes for both pure and impure spectra. Its derivative-based relative in Theorem 10.10 is the relevant one. The original circle-counting lineage includes Arthur Cohn's *Über die Anzahl der Wurzeln einer algebraischen Gleichung in einem Kreise*, Math. Z. 14 (1922), 110–148. The stated direct Toeplitz identification is from Heinig–Rost, rather than a claim that Cohn used this exact matrix. [Cohn original scan](https://gdz.sub.uni-goettingen.de/download/pdf/PPN314393552/PPN314393552.pdf)

There is also a direct link to the Hermite–Sylvester power-sum trace form. Remove the known unit-circle root \(z=1\) if present, and use the Cayley map \(x=i(1+z)/(1-z)\). It maps the unit circle minus \(1\) to the real axis and satisfies \(x(1/\bar z)=\overline{x(z)}\). The transformed monic polynomial has real coefficients, and its ordinary Hermite Hankel matrix uses the powers \(x^{i+j}\) in place of differences of powers of \(z\). Its evaluation form is \(\sum m_x\overline{p(\bar x)}p(x)\), with the same real-root and conjugate-pair inertia blocks. Restoring \(z=1\) adds one positive block when present. This is a parallel trace-form derivation. The integer Toeplitz congruence is more direct since it uses the original \(t_j\) without constructing the Cayley-transformed polynomial. Hermite's historical root-counting work is documented by Heinig–Rost §14 and [Hermite 1856, pp. 39–51](https://geodesic.mathdoc.fr/item/JRAM_1856__52/). The real-root trace-form criterion and its power-sum matrix are stated in [Yang, *Semidefinite Optimization and Relaxation*, Theorem 4.9](https://hankyang.seas.harvard.edu/Semidefinite/SOS.html).

The calculation assumes a compatible finite root multiset with positive multiplicities. It does not produce a low single-power witness and does not imply any result for an infinite trace limit. If \(q\) is only a positive real number, the congruence and purity equivalence still hold for a real reciprocal polynomial, with no guarantee that the assembled entries are integers.
