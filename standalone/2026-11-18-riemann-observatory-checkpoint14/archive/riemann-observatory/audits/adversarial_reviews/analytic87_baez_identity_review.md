# Work identity of Báez-Duarte's strengthened Nyman–Beurling criterion

18 November 2026. **Treat PAPER-E001 and PAPER-E014 as one research work with two separately registered arXiv deposits and a journal manifestation.** An E001→E014 work-level alias is justified. The author's own cross-references establish the chain in both directions, and the journal incorporates the later deposit's distinctive quantitative theorem. This is neither a claim of identical editions nor an instruction to collapse the arXiv IDs, edition locators or mathematical finding records. No canonical changes in this review.

## Primary identity evidence

1. The later deposit, [math/0205003v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0205003v1), is titled with a trailing “, 2” and has a separate arXiv registration. Its p. 1 abstract calls math/0202141v2 the “first version of this paper” and introduces its own difference with “This second version differs from the first”. Its reported addition is the RH-conditional norm-approximation rate. The exact arXiv landing abstract confirms the link and the denominator \(\log\log n\). The original main criterion and proof reappear in its §§1–2, rather than being cited as a theorem of a disjoint article which starts from it. Its printed manuscript date is 28 March 2002, while arXiv submission history is 1 May 2002. [Later deposit](../../sources/equivalences/baez_duarte.pdf) · [Pinned arXiv metadata](../../sources/equivalences/baez_duarte_metadata.html)
2. An independent primary self-citation settles the likely direction of the journal relation. Báez-Duarte's *Fast proof of functional equation for \(\zeta(s)\)*, [math/0305191v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0305191v1), p. 2 reference 1, gives the unnumbered strengthening title and states “posted in arxiv math.NT/0205003, to appear in Atti Accad. Naz. Lincei Cl. Sci. Fis. Mat. Natur. Rend. Lincei”. The note has a 13 May 2003 internal date and was posted in May 2003. Thus the author explicitly identified the May 2002 deposit as the preprint for the Lincei publication. This source is a contemporaneous bibliographic witness. Its mathematical result is separate. [Pinned self-citation PDF](../../sources/analytic87_baez_identity/math_0305191v1.pdf)
3. The original Lincei journal scan has the unnumbered title, **series 9, volume 14 (2003), no. 1, pp. 5–11**, with receipt on 21 August 2002 and final form on 6 September 2002, p. 11. Theorem 1 on p. 5 is the integer-dilation closure criterion. Section 3, p. 9, says “After the first version of this paper” in introducing the quantitative addition. Theorem 2 and (3.1), pp. 9–10, use exactly the new approximation with coefficient \(-\mu(a)\exp[-c\log(a)/\log\log n]\) and upper bound \(O((\log\log n)^{-1/3})\). They correspond to Proposition 3.1 and (3.2), pp. 6–8, of the May deposit. Both give the same splitting of the norm error, identical substantive shrinking-gap input, and the Burnol ratio bound with local labels (3.2) in print versus (3.3) in May. Both credit Balazard for the quantitative theorem and Bombieri for a parallel suggestion. The journal bibliography supplies no distinct published part 2. The precise result is an upper bound, regardless of the less specific wording “of order” in the May abstract. [Journal source scan](../../sources/analytic74_baez/lincei_2003.pdf) · [Digital library record](https://www.bdim.eu/item?id=RLIN_2003_9_14_1_5_0)
4. The early [math/0202141v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0202141v2) follows its v1 of 15 February 2002 with revision on 18 February. The archive says this seven-page revision corrected three typos and added one reference. The two February versions include Theorem 1.1, Corollary 3.1 and Lemmas 2.1–2.2, with no explicit quantitative rate theorem. The approximately nine-page May deposit repeats the criterion and adds Proposition 3.1. The journal retains both results. This is stronger evidence than similar titles and theorem statements alone. The two arXiv archive entries are real and independently citable. Therefore a count of deposits or edition artifacts can remain two arXiv records and one journal item. A count of unique selected **works** should reduce by one. The earlier E014 description of the later PDF as a “sequel” should be understood as separate deposit, or replaced by “expanded preprint under a second arXiv ID”. A trailing numeral and new result could signal a sequel in other cases, but here both author links, the repeated proof, and the journal's absorption of that exact result outweigh that surface indicator. A bounded title-and-ID search yielded no distinct publication of the numbered title; the conclusion rests primarily on the positive author evidence, not search absence.

## Merge safeguards

- Canonical preference must survive the alias. In the inspected archive builder, aliases are applied before merging track rows, and the first row retains existing nonempty title, arXiv, source and record-path fields. Because the E001 row in the equivalence track sorts ahead of the E014 row in the weighted-priority track, a bare E001→E014 alias can yield an E014 aggregate carrying the May title and record path. Prefer the true canonical E014 row explicitly while retaining the E001 row as an alternate manifestation, or verify an equivalent canonical override. This is an integration condition, not an objection to the identity conclusion. [Archive merge](../../scripts/build_archive.py)
- Retain document-specific read scope, dates and source hashes. Mathematical repairs verified in one manifestation must not be asserted of another merely by resolving the work alias. In particular the February gamma-sign issue and the corrected May/journal formula are distinct textual observations. Preserve reference targets such as May Proposition 3.1 and journal Theorem 2, and do not assume a canonicalized identifier suffix automatically matches the journal's locator.
- Keep aliases, manifestation entries and finding provenance separate. The qualitative criterion should count once as a result; the added quantitative result remains independently represented. Do not merge this work with *New versions of the Nyman–Beurling criterion for the Riemann hypothesis* (journal DOI 10.1155/S0161171202013248), the later general strong criterion, or Burnol's separate ratio paper.

**Disposition.** One preferred E014 work, E001 work alias with preserved original document and handles. The expected unique-work decrement is one provided both were selected before deduplication. The dated self-citation, the quantitative theorem match, and the distinct edition hashes provide independent evidence for that decision.
