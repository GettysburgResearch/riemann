## Main theorem and complete proof chain

For every positive integer n let H_n=sum_(j=1)^n 1/j and sigma(n)=sum_(d|n)d. Theorem 1.1 states that RH is equivalent to sigma(n)<=H_n+exp(H_n)log H_n for every n, with equality only at n=1. Both implications are valid with the elementary display correction below.

The finite sum-integral identity gives H_n=log n+gamma+delta_n, where delta_n=integral_n^infinity {t}/t^2 dt lies strictly between 0 and 1/n. Thus exp(H_n)log H_n>exp(gamma)n log log n for n>=3. The logarithms are positive in this range; n=1,2 are handled separately. The independent upper reconstruction establishes the May bound H_n+exp(H_n)log H_n<=exp(gamma)n log log n+7n/log n for n>=20. It also verifies the stronger July bound with 4n/log n for n>=3. The delicate May inequality log(1+1/log n)<=1/log(n+1) is checked in both reviews, not replaced silently by a stronger unsupported assertion.

Robin's original Theorem 1 and section 3 Proposition 3 supply, under RH, strict sigma(n)<exp(gamma)n log log n for every n>5040. This and the elementary lower bound prove the forward implication beyond the finite range. The director replayed the independent 192-bit Arb divisor-pairing check for all 1<=n<=5041; the minimum positive Lagarias gap occurs at n=2 and exceeds 0.3171685434. Equality at 1 is exact. The other reviewer uses a divisor sieve and separately directed intervals. These are distinct implementations.

If RH fails, choose a zero with real part b>1/2 and 1-b<beta<1/2. Robin's section 4 oscillation theorem applies because 1-Theta<=1-b<beta, where Theta is the supremum of the zero real parts. It supplies an unbounded colossally abundant subsequence with excess at least C n log log n/(log n)^beta. Its ratio to 7n/log n tends to infinity. Hence the elementary upper envelope is violated eventually. This imports the matched Robin theorem, including its Nicolas/Landau ancestry; it does not reprove all of Robin's paper or locate a first counterexample.

## Versioned local findings

1. May p.6's opening integral has infinite upper limits and diverges: floor(t)>=t-1 suffices to witness this. The outer endpoint must be n and each summand's endpoints r,n. Equation (3.4) immediately uses the correct finite endpoint; July p.7 repairs the display. This is an already repaired local display, not a gap in the equivalence.
2. Both accessible p.3 versions print the ordinary Mertens product with exp(+gamma)/log x. Its correct coefficient is exp(-gamma). The director visually checked the May PDF, rather than relying on extraction. The maximal-order reconstruction uses the correct inverse product.
3. Both p.3 versions print a strict universal Robin upper bound with the exact decimal 0.6482. At n=12 the equality-forced coefficient is rigorously in (0.64821364942179,0.64821364942181). Thus the literal displayed inequality fails. Robin's sharp constant has additional digits and equality at 12; the rounded strict coefficient 0.6483 is valid. Neither p.3 display enters section 3's criterion proof. These two false auxiliary assertions are one version-scoped finding group, not a verdict against the main theorem.
4. At epsilon=log(4/3)/log3, the two CA maxima are 2 and 6. Formula (2.3) chooses the larger tied exponents, whereas the stricter convention in footnote 1 selects the smaller maximum. Both agents independently checked this after the director's query. Read as equality of sets as epsilon varies, the footnote survives: the two endpoints are unique on adjacent parameter intervals. Pointwise identification at a fixed exceptional epsilon needs this qualification. It is not classified as an additional false global theorem.
