## Reconstruction checks

**Exact transform.** With \(x=e^u\), (2.6) is the unnormalized Fourier transform of \(g(u)=e^{u/2}f(e^u)\) with sign \(+it u\), and \(\int_{\mathbb R}|g|^2du=\int_0^\infty|f|^2dx\). Plancherel is therefore
\[
 \|f\|_2^2=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}|\mathcal Mf(t)|^2dt.
\]
In particular \(\mathcal M\chi(t)=1/(1/2+it)\) has ordinary squared integral \(2\pi\), so the printed density would give squared norm \(\sqrt{2\pi}\) rather than 1. Multiplying a norm by this fixed constant does not affect limit existence. For \(0<\Re s<1\), dilating and substituting \(u=1/x\) give \(\int_0^\infty\rho_a(x)x^{s-1}dx=a^{-s}\int_0^\infty\{u\}u^{-s-1}du=-a^{-s}\zeta(s)/s\). The last identity follows by integrating \(\lfloor u\rfloor\) over \([1,\infty)\) first on \(\Re s>1\), separating \(u-\{u\}\), then continuing and including \(\int_0^1u^{-s}du=1/(1-s)\). Thus (2.7) has \(s-\epsilon\) in the zeta factor and \(s+\epsilon\) in the Möbius polynomial precisely because the coefficients are damped by \(a^{-2\epsilon}\).

**Ratio and two different epsilon ranges.** The functional equation plus conjugation gives the absolute ratio \(\pi^{-\epsilon}|\Gamma(1/4+\epsilon/2+it/2)/\Gamma(1/4-\epsilon/2+it/2)|\), after removing common-zero singularities along the real \(t\) line. Uniform Stirling on a fixed compact positive gamma strip and continuity for bounded \(t\) give the bound \(C_{\epsilon_0}(1+|t|)^\epsilon\) for every \(\epsilon_0<1/2\). That uniform estimate handles the limiting analytic kernels. For the first limit, RH and Balazard–Saias give the transform error
\[
 O_{\epsilon,\eta}\left(n^{-\epsilon/3}(1+|t|)^{-1+\epsilon+2\eta}\right)
\]
using \((1+|t|)^\eta\) separately in the Möbius estimate and in the reflected Lindelöf bound. The displayed \(-1+2\epsilon\) follows by choosing \(\eta=\epsilon/2\). Its square is integrable only for \(\epsilon<1/4\), with logarithmic divergence at equality. Taking instead \(0<\eta<(1/2-\epsilon)/2\) yields the first \(L^2\) limit for every fixed \(0<\epsilon<1/2\). Neither use asks for uniform fixed-gap constants as \(\epsilon\downarrow0\). The endpoint \(1/2\) is outside the original weighted domain and is genuinely bad for a single weighted generator, since \(\int_1^\infty\{y\}^2dy/y=\infty\).

**Qualitative conclusions and converse.** The fractional-part floor formula gives both pointwise limits and the exact scalar tail \(x^{-1}\sum_{a\le n}\mu(a)a^{-1-2\epsilon}\). The weighted first norm limit agrees a.e. with the pointwise one by an a.e.-convergent subsequence and can be unweighted using the contraction \(x^\epsilon\) on \((0,1]\) and the scalar tail on \((1,\infty)\). The symmetric ratios tend a.e. to 1 and obey a uniform square-integrable transform envelope for any \(\epsilon_0<1/2\). This kernel limit is unconditional, but the identification as the transforms of the actual damped functions is conditional. The resulting norm limits put \(\chi\) in the closed integer span on RH. Off RH, Mellin evaluation at a zero \(\rho\) with \(1/2<\Re\rho<1\) is continuous on the span closure because its \((1,\infty)\) restrictions lie in the closed scalar span of \(1/x\). It vanishes on the dilates and gives \(1/\rho\) for \(\chi\). For the damped corollary, the \((0,1)\) Mellin integral initially on \(\Re s>1\) equals
\[
 [(s-1)\zeta(1+\epsilon)]^{-1}-\zeta(s)/[s\zeta(s+\epsilon)].
\]
If \(f_\epsilon\in L^2\), this extends holomorphically to \(\Re s>1/2\). An off-line zero with real part \(>1/2\) forces a pole at \(\rho-\epsilon\) for every sufficiently small positive \(\epsilon\), by isolation of numerator zeros. Thus one null sequence of dampings with norm convergence along an unbounded cutoff sequence for each damping suffices. This independently rechecks the Burnol obstacle without an unproved unconditional identification.

**Quantitative theorem.** Both reviews actually reconstruct both terms in the p. 10 split. The near-zero \(J_\epsilon\) part uses Burnol's one-sided ratio bound and local zero count. On \(|t-\gamma|>\delta\), integrate the local log derivative horizontally and bound \(|e^w-1|\le|w|e^{|w|}\) with \(|w|\ll(\epsilon/\delta)\log(2+|t|)\) for sufficiently small \(\epsilon\). The resulting \(J_\epsilon\ll\delta+\epsilon^2/\delta^2\) gives \(\epsilon^{2/3}\) with \(\delta=\epsilon^{2/3}\). The first integral needs the previously adjudicated genuine uniform estimate. Titchmarsh–Heath-Brown §14.14, pp. 354–355, has an equality-line result in (14.14.4), so adjacent regions and a fixed-strip bridge are needed. At finite height \(V\), use this near-line input above \(\exp(\sqrt{\log V})\), and below it use Borel–Carathéodory on the zero-free pole-removed \((s-1)\zeta(s)\). A Perron shift at height \((n+1/2)^4\) to \(-\epsilon/2\) yields \(n^{-\epsilon/2}\log n\) with reciprocal growth loss \(e^{O(\log n/\log\log n)+O(\mathcal L(t))}\). For large enough fixed \(c\) and \(\epsilon\ge c/\log\log n\), absorb the \(n\)-loss into \(n^{\epsilon/6}\). Endpoint truncation and horizontal edges contribute smaller errors. The first integral is then \(O(n^{-2\epsilon/3})\) by the RH critical-line bound and \(\mathcal L(t)=o(\log |t|)\). Setting \(\epsilon=c/\log\log n\) and taking the square root gives the theorem. This is THM-E-D202–D204, not a new dependency result. [Scoped director decision](director_round3_adjudication.md)
