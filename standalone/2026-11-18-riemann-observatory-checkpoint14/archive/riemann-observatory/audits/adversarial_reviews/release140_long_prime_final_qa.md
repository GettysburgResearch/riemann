# Independent QA of the long-prime phase and factorability adjudication

18 November 2026. The exact centered phase, sufficient square budget, row-model limitation and padded factorability theorem pass. The actual long-prime estimate remains open. I checked the cited Pascadi and Matomäki passages against the retained PDFs and verified their checksum manifest.

## Exact phase and square budget

For \((p,k)=1\), let \(g=(h,k)\) and \(q=k/g\). Reduction of units from \(k\) to \(q\) is onto with equal fibers. The remaining numerator \(hp/g\) is a unit modulo \(q\), so the average is \(\mu(q)/\varphi(q)\), including exact cancellation at \(q=1\). The ordered-pair convolution gives precisely the four-prime coefficient, including repeated primes. The CRT product requires coprime pair moduli and retains the other modulus's inverse in each factor. The original centered phase continues to cover shared factors.

The signs in Mellin inversion produce \(k^{-1+it}h^{-it}p^{-it}(2\pi/T)^{-it}\). On the actual support \(DP\asymp KT\). The coefficient square sum before the prime polynomial is \(O(T^{2\epsilon}D/K)\) by \(a_k\le24\), the divisor bound and the \(1/k\) weight. The proposed square bound \(T^{2-\eta}K/D\) therefore gives \(T^{1-\eta/2+\epsilon}\) by Cauchy–Schwarz. The taper's \(L^1\) cost is \(T^{1-\kappa}\); choose \(\epsilon\) and the fixed \(1-\kappa\) with strict room under \(\eta/2\), as well as \(1-\kappa<\epsilon/2\) for the Mellin tail. Fixed high derivative order handles the tail and the remaining dyadic losses are logarithmic. This is a sufficient power-saving input; no bound of that size has been proved for the genuine prime sum.

Ignoring logarithms, the model size \(KDP\) divided by the unsaved budget \(T^2K/D\) is \(D^2P/T^2\asymp K^2/P\). A model-scale bound could therefore suffice when \(P>K^2T^c\) for a fixed \(c>0\). At \(h=1\), \(P\asymp KT\), the ordinary additive large-sieve budget is of order \(P^2\) up to logarithms and does not meet the square target. The budget comparison supplies no lower bound on prime cancellation.

## Residue array scope

For a squarefree product of four odd supported primes, the two unit Ramanujan sums at frequencies 1 and 2 are both one. This gives the displayed centered cosine variance, asymptotic to \(k/2\) uniformly over the chosen moduli. The baseline masses dominate the row amplitudes because \(P/\varphi(k)\gg T\) and \(A\asymp\sqrt T\log^2T\). The maximum and square norms have the stated sizes. Orthogonality against the mean shows that the real projection is \(24A\sum V_k/k\asymp K\sqrt T/\log^2T\), and the imaginary part vanishes by negation.

For \(k_i=gv_i\), summing the character on a unit fiber over \(v_i\) gives \(\mu(v_i)\), leaving the cosine at \(a\overline{v_i}/g\). The constant terms reduce by multiplicativity exactly as displayed. The two marginals differ at \(a=1\) because distinct \(v_i\) with \(v_1+v_2<g\) have inverse residues distinct up to sign. A single sequence supported on units of both moduli would give the same marginals on (g). The genuine longest prime block has this unit property. The row construction refutes an implication from the specified scalar norms and row constraints alone. It provides no obstruction to an argument that uses the common sequence across moduli.

## Exact level and source boundaries

For a nonempty prime set the claimed level \(p_*^{m+r-1}\) is an exact if and only if for the full normalized coefficient. At or above the threshold, \(\sum_i\lfloor t_i\rfloor>\sum_i t_i-r\ge m-1\) guarantees an integer allocation of all \(m\) slots. The normalized factors have coefficients \(1/\prod_p v_p!\). Multiplying one convolution factor by \(\prod_i n_i!/m!\le1\) gives the target coefficient everywhere. A formal factor of capacity \(Q_i=1\) uses the unit at 1. When \(r=1\), the criterion is simply that the single factor can reach the last support point \(p_*^m\). Below the threshold the proposed split has \(t_i<f_i+1\) and \(\sum_i f_i=m-1\), so no allowable factor tuple can produce \(p_*^m\), whose target coefficient is \(1/m!\). Complex signs cannot change this support obstruction. I also checked the normalized convolution and below-threshold split with finite rational examples. The full coefficient is essential; the argument leaves sufficiency for a sharply restricted subset undecided.

Pascadi's pinned v2 p. 2 defines exactly the stated triple convolution with real \(Q_i\ge1\) and 1-bounded complex factors. Theorem 1.3(i) permits \(Q\le x^{5/8-\epsilon}\) for a fixed nonzero integer residue \(a\). Its implied constant is uniform over the qualifying weight sequences but may depend on \(a\). The signed sum is over moduli coprime to this fixed residue. At the longest scale, the comparison \(6b<(5/8)(1+4b)\) is exactly \(b<5/28\), so the padded level is allowed throughout the stated band. The smaller-scale condition is separate. Complete multiplicativity gives factorable \(k^{it}\) weights uniformly in real \(t\); Mellin or Fourier synthesis gives a fixed smooth dyadic modulus weight at fixed residue, with the transform's finite \(L^1\) cost. This transfer does not justify a sharp product restriction by itself or a sum over modulus-dependent residues with the required mode.

Matomäki's author manuscript (1)–(2), Theorem 1 and Corollary 3 use a common real frequency \(\alpha\), a fixed progression residue and, in Theorem 1, a separate divisor-bounded multiplier index. Those hypotheses give no reciprocal frequency \(\alpha j=-h/k\) tied to the same modulus. Both source comparisons preserve the stated limits. No canonical record or delivered checkpoint changed.
