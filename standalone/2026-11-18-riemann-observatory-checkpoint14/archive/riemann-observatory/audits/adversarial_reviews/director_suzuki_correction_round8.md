# Director adjudication: an existing signed-time correction

Date: 2026-11-17. I inspected the original nine-page Suzuki arXiv2209.04658v3 at the definitions, statements and signed expansions, the merged-paper passages, and the actual one-page author erratum. Two independent complete scoped checks are prefix_formula62_suzuki_signed_extension.md and witness62_suzuki_signed_time.md. The author bibliography links the erratum. Its exact SHA is 9c28b278874eb8242165ac3f8b3ab9ae44365cfba6958a4869e462eb057a1382; the journal article DOI10.4153/S0008414X25101739 identifies the corrected article, not a separately verified erratum DOI. This is a reconstruction of a pre-existing correction, not a new error discovery.

The old literal convention S_(−t)=S_t conflicts with its claimed full-real screw kernel G_g(t,u)=g(t−u)−g(t)−g(u): at u=−t it would force g(2t)=0, contrary under RH to the nonzero cosine sum for an appropriate t. An odd zero-mean smooth test likewise integrates the even path to zero while its screw quadratic form is positive. Restricting times to the positive half-line avoids that witness but changes the stated bilateral domain. It does not explain the old literal full-line formula.

For the zero-side meromorphic series P_t(z)=Σ mγ(e^(iγt)−1)/[γ(z−γ)] in the standalone convention, conjugation and γ↦−γ give

    P_(−t)(z)=overline(P_t(bar z))=P_t(−z).

The series is locally normally convergent away from the zeros, uniformly on compact real time sets, using |Imγ|<1/2 and Σmγ/|γ|²<∞. This reflection, applied to the known positive-time formula, defines the signed continuation without inserting negative t into its original prime-side special-function expression. The phase factors on the real axis have the same modulus, so the source's unconditional positive-time L² membership transfers. The author erratum prescribes precisely this conjugate extension for the merged convention and makes its signed zero sum valid for all real t. It also changes the prefactor i to −i in (1.5) and π to4π in one later inequality. These separate corrections must all be retained when comparing normalizations.

Under RH the signed basis coefficients in increments contain e^(iγu)(e^(iγt)−1)/γ (with the opposite exponential convention in the merged paper). The basepoint phase cancels in inner products, giving the screw kernel. In the merged corrected convention the pointwise line uses Fγ# whereas the integrated operator uses Fγ; conjugation is antiunitary and must not be dropped. Parseval yields the norm identities with the corrected π factor. Conversely the zero-mean smooth test domain is the derivative of compact smooth functions; its equality implies Weil positivity and therefore RH. Translation of zero-mean tests into the positive half-line also proves that a correctly restricted half-line formulation suffices. None of this establishes the norm equalities unconditionally.

The source's one-sided norm criterion is unaffected, as it uses only nonnegative time. The signed correction restores the bilateral assertions rather than refuting the RH equivalence as a research program. The corrected integrated operator has dense image in its RH model space: an orthogonal vector would define a tempered discrete distribution annihilating all Fourier transforms of compact smooth tests; Fourier injectivity forces every coefficient zero. The basis and thus this density conclusion remain RH-dependent. The exact source versions and author correction are linked in the canonical record; no whole merged-paper technical audit is claimed.
