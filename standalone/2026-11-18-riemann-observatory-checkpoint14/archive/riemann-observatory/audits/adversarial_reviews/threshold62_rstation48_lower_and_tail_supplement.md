## Exact cutoff and multiplicity normalization

Burnol's actual math/0103058v2, printed pp. 3–4, defines K=L²((0,∞),dx), χ=1_(0,1], and B_λ the finite complex linear span of ϱ(θ/x) for λ≤θ≤1, where ϱ is fractional part. There is no barycentric coefficient constraint in that definition. D(λ)=inf_{f∈B_λ}∥χ−f∥_K. Theorem 1.3, printed p. 4, and its proof as Theorem 5.5, printed pp. 15–16, give
    liminf_{λ↓0} [log(1/λ)]^(1/2) D(λ) ≥ (Σ_distinct m_ρ²/|ρ|²)^(1/2)
under RH, counting both signs of the nontrivial ordinates. The square form is justified because the distances are nonnegative. The proof may take any fixed finite set of zeros first, with multiplicities handled inside each zero's jet block. [Burnol primary PDF](../../sources/equivalences/burnol.pdf)

For an ordinary Dirichlet polynomial A(s)=Σ_{n≤N}a_n n^(−s), Mellin sends χ+Σ a_nϱ(1/(nx)) to (1−ζ(s)A(s))/s. At Re s=1/2 this Mellin map is isometric for dt/(2π), by x=exp u and Fourier Plancherel. Thus the optimized classical ∆_N is exactly the distance to the finite span of those integer dilates in K. The plus sign in the residual is immaterial as the coefficients are arbitrary complex numbers. Since θ=1/n belongs to [1/N,1], D(1/N)≤∆_N. The endpoint n=N is included in the ordinary Dirichlet coefficient space even though the base first-order taper's nth coefficient vanishes there. Using λ=1/N gives the exact log N factor. No coefficient count changes from N to a power of N, and there is no boundary-measure factor to reconcile.

For the anchored subspace the constraint is D_A(1)=0, where D_A denotes the polynomial actually multiplying ζ. Every such polynomial of length at most N is a linear combination of n^(−s)−1/n for 2≤n≤N. The corresponding full-space physical functions have zero tail for x>1 in the small-variable convention. Equivalently, under x↦1/x they are the floor differences on the source's x≥1 domain with measure dx/x², with the signs as in the main audit. Thus ∆_N≤d_N. Burnol's lower bound needs no separate anchored projection.

For a direct check of the multiplicity coefficient in Burnol §5, his block for a zero of multiplicity m tends to the Cauchy matrix H_(i,j)=1/(i+j+1), 0≤i,j<m. Its inverse top-left entry is the squared norm of evaluation at zero on polynomials of degree <m in L²[0,1]. The shifted Legendre basis gives Σ_{j<m}(2j+1)=m² because P_j(−1)=(−1)^j. Burnol's target pairings carry 1/|ρ| in modulus. Hence the contribution is m²/|ρ|² with the exact normalization, not m or a factor two times this. The full sum converges even by the usual unit-window bound, since the total of m² over j<|γ|≤j+1 is at most the square of the zero count there, O(log²(2+j)). No uniform convergence of Burnol's growing jet matrices is used.

## Keeping both vertical and horizontal boundaries

Fix first an integer N≥2 and write L=log N. For each nontrivial cluster let F=1/ζ and apply (46) with its whole rectangle. Away from the edges and the discrete zero ordinates, the integral of F(z)[exp(L(z−s))−1]/(z−s)² acquires LF(s) when s is inside. The other residues match the first-order rational error. This verifies that the indicator is needed with exactly the sign in (46). Insert the empty rectangles at their original locations. Each contributes exactly zero to the combined expression by Cauchy's residue at s, so insertion changes no residual. Telescoping adjacent rectangles strictly before taking absolute values leaves a strip with bottom and top edges in each of the positive and negative half-axes. The cutoff h(H)=H+O(D) exists from the full grid of midpoints, including empty cells. A root at a chosen edge would invalidate this representation, and the good-height construction gives a finite reciprocal bound there, excluding that possibility.

On either vertical side, the reciprocal is O((2+|y|)^e) uniformly above the cutoff. On the right this is the fixed-δ RH bound. On the left the functional equation adds decay, so the same bound holds. For p=2−2e, kernel k_δ(v)=(δ²+v²)^(−1), and h≥1, the squared norm of the vertical part after Cauchy–Schwarz against k_δ satisfies
    C N^(2δ)L^(−2) ∫_{|y|>h}(2+|y|)^(2e) [∫(2+|t|)^(−p)k_δ(t−y)dt]dy.
The inner integral is O((2+|y|)^(−p)). To verify, put t near 0, t near y, and the rest in three disjoint regions. Near t=0 the kernel gives O((2+|y|)^(−2)) and the p-weight has finite integral. Near t=y extract O((2+|y|)^(−p)) and integrate the kernel. In the rest extract O((2+|y|)^(−p)) wherever |t|≥|y|/2 and again use the finite kernel integral. Thus the outer integral is O(h^(−1+4e)). All exchanges use nonnegative majorants. In particular the vertical kernel bound is for the whole semi-infinite side, not just a compact t-set.

On a horizontal edge at h set w=x+i(h−t), |x|≤δ. The inequality |exp(Lw)−1|≤L|w|N^δ follows by integrating exp(uw), 0≤u≤L. The surviving kernel after integration in x is A_δ(v)=2 asinh(δ/|v|), which has square-integrable logarithmic growth near zero and A_δ(v)²=O_δ(|v|^(−2)) for large |v|. The same split gives ∫(2+|t|)^(−p)A_δ(h−t)²dt=O((2+h)^(−p)). Hence the edge squared norm is O(N^(2δ)h^(−2+4e)). Pointwise one excludes t=±h, a countable set if all grid heights are considered. The weighted indicator squared norm is O(h^(−1)). There is no hidden microscopic partner-count input in these bounds.

The contour argument allows a more explicit norm-limit order. Take finite telescoped rectangles with inner h and outer good H′. The two outer horizontal edges obey exactly the same O(N^(2δ)H′^(−2+4e)) squared norm. The omitted outer portions of the vertical sides have O(N^(2δ)L^(−2)H′^(−1+4e)) squared norm. The difference of finite and infinite indicators has O(H′^(−1)) squared norm. Hence the finite telescoped sum converges in the full weighted space for each N held fixed. Then the infinite complement beyond H=N^0.05 has squared norm from (49) with the slowest polynomial exponent 2δ−0.05(1−4e)=−0.049978. The prior limit sending H′ to infinity does not need to be uniform in growing N because an explicit estimate for the resulting infinite strip is available.

## Section 8 and the fixed-set exhaustion

RH gives the classical M(x)≪_η x^(1/2+η) for every fixed η>0, with 0<η<1/2 in the tail argument. F(1)=0 and F′(1)=1 from the Laurent expansion of ζ at 1. Abel summation bounds the omitted tails in Σμ(n)/n and Σμ(n)log(n)/n as O_η(N^(−1/2+η)) and O_η(N^(−1/2+η)log N), respectively. Hence the first-order base polynomial has V_{N,1}(1)=1/L+O_η(N^(−1/2+η)). The growing correction has a negative-power bound at 1 and the fixed corrections have O_F(1/L). Anchoring subtracts a constant term of that size. The critical-line norm of ζ is finite with this Cauchy weight by convexity, so anchoring changes the norm by O_F(1/L). Its effect on the squared norm after multiplication by L vanishes for fixed F.

Make the exhaustion explicit. Fix a finite set F of roots closed under clusters. For any fixed Y exceeding their heights, retain the clusters below Y when evaluating the continuous model. For all N with H=N^0.05≥Y, the omitted *corrected* clusters have √L-times norm at most C Y^(−0.495), uniformly in N, by (41). The omitted *uncorrected* high clusters have √L-times norm tending to zero by the strip bound just proved. The quadrature errors and trivial pole contribution also have √L-times norms tending to zero. The retained finite model has an exact limit from the fixed-local argument and its repair in the main audit. Let N tend to infinity first, then Y to infinity. This proves the source's equation (75) with F fixed and the tail series convergent. The upper bound for limsup L d_N² holds for every such fixed F separately, since each yields an actual sequence of degree ≤N approximants.
