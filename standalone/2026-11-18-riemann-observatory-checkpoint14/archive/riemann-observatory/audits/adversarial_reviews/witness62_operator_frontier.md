# Primary-source review of spectral and positivity criteria

Five works were absent from the canonical bibliography on inspection. This is a scoped reading. Page numbers refer to the inspected manifestation. The source and theorem records are alongside this audit.

## The positivity step

A signed trace identity can already sum over every nontrivial zeta zero, as Meyer’s theorem does. Such an identity alone provides no positive Hilbert-space realization. The specific de Branges shift positivity tested by Conrey and Li encounters published negative witnesses. Suzuki supplies an alternative canonical-system construction, with an explicit arithmetic kernel and a narrower range of construction than the range in which innerness is available. In the adelic model, Connes isolates a global cutoff identity that must retain the arithmetic information needed for positivity.

## Connes · `PAPER-OP62-CONNES`

ArXiv v1 §III, Theorem 1, p. 13. For the weighted quotient \(H_\chi\) and \(\delta>1\), the generator \(D_\chi\) has discrete spectrum in \(i\mathbb R\), with \(\rho\) in the spectrum precisely when \(L(\widetilde\chi,\tfrac12+\rho)=0\) and \(\rho\in i\mathbb R\). Its multiplicity is the largest integer \(n<(1+\delta)/2\) no greater than the multiplicity of that zero. This criterion selects zeros already on the critical line. In §VIII, Theorem 5, the literal field hypothesis is positive characteristic. For the cutoff projection \(Q_\Lambda\) using simultaneous support bounds on \(f\) and \(\widehat f\), the global trace identity (16), viewed as an identity on the compactly supported test functions \(h\), is related by equivalence to RH for all Grössencharakter \(L\)-functions of \(k\). The proof proceeds through positivity of the Weil distribution. Section IX, pp. 46–47, explains why the same exact simultaneous support cutoff has no nonzero archimedean vectors and describes an alternative using the prolate spectral cutoff. The earlier local and finite-place trace formulae do not give the global identity by simply exhausting the places. Scope is the preprint. [Primary source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9811068v1)

## Conrey–Li · `PAPER-OP62-CONREYLI`

ArXiv v1 §2, Theorem 1, and §3. For an entire \(E\) with no real zeros, strict Hermite–Biehler inequality, \(E^\#(z)=\epsilon E(z-i)\) with \(|\epsilon|=1\), and \(|E(x+iy)|\) strictly increasing for \(y>0\), the hypothesis is \(\Re\langle F,F(\cdot+i)\rangle_{\mathcal H(E)}\ge0\) for all \(F\) with both functions in \(\mathcal H(E)\). It forces all zeros \(w\) of \(E\) to satisfy \(\Im w=-1/2\) and \(\Re(\overline{E'(w)}E(w+i)/(2\pi i))\ge0\). Section 3.1 applies the necessary sign at the zero corresponding to \(\rho\) near \(\tfrac12+111.029535543i\) to rule out this positivity for \(E(z)=\xi(1-iz)\). The source reports a Mathematica evaluation in (3.2), without an independent interval certificate in this audit. Theorem 2 separately assumes a linear operator on all of \(\mathcal F(W)\) taking \(K(w,\cdot)\) to \(K(w+i,\cdot)\), with nonnegative real numerical range. It implies continuation of zero-free-in-the-upper-half-plane \(W\) to \(\Im z>-1/2\) and a nonnegative real part of the quotient \(W(z)/W(z+i)\) there. The authors also report failure of this quotient sign for \(W(z)=1/\xi(1-iz)\) at \(z=-282\) in (3.4), and analogous counterexamples for the real primitive character modulo four in §3.2. These observations address the specified operator hypotheses. [Primary source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9812166v1) [Journal metadata](https://doi.org/10.1155/S1073792800000489)

## Suzuki · `PAPER-OP62-SUZUKI`

ArXiv v2 §§1.4 and 2. Proposition 1.2, for every fixed \(\omega_0\ge0\), equates zero-freeness in \(\Re s>1/2+\omega_0\) with meromorphic innerness of \(\Theta_\omega(z)=\xi(1/2-\omega-iz)/\xi(1/2+\omega-iz)\) for **every** \(\omega>\omega_0\). Theorem 2.2 translates innerness at a single fixed \(\omega>0\) into equivalent conditions on the Mellin convolution, each quantified over all \(f\in L^2((1,\infty),dx)\). In particular, the third condition requires \(h_\omega*f\) to belong to \(L^2((0,\infty),dx)\) for every such \(f\). Theorem 2.3 constructs the explicit system only for \(\omega>1\), with diagonal Hamiltonian entries \(m(a)^{-2},m(a)^2\) and \(m(a)=\det(1+H_{\omega,a})/\det(1-H_{\omega,a})\). The discussion following the theorem gives compressed-kernel compact self-adjointness for all \(\omega>0\), and the Hilbert–Schmidt and invertibility conclusions for \(\omega>1/2\). Kernel continuity in the construction is the reason stated for the stronger \(\omega>1\) hypothesis. Thus mere compactness leaves the extension of that explicit construction open within these arguments. The 2012 Kyoto manifestation preserves Proposition 1.2 and the \(\omega>1\) threshold in Theorem 2.3. These are source-era claims. [ArXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1204.1827v2) [Kyoto publication](https://www.kurims.kyoto-u.ac.jp/~kenkyubu/bessatsu/open/B34/pdf/B34_023.pdf)

## Meyer · `PAPER-OP62-MEYER`

ArXiv v3 §4.2, Corollary 4.2, identifies the eigenvalues of the transpose \(^{t}D_-\) acting on the continuous dual of the quotient \(\mathcal H_-^0=\mathcal H_-/Z\mathcal H_\cap\), with the zero orders as algebraic multiplicities. The completed function here is \(\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)\), meromorphic with poles at 0 and 1. Theorem 5.8 equates the signed Mellin spectral sum with the character of a virtual representation and the sum of local distributions for every \(f\in\mathcal O(\mathbb R_+^\times)\). The convention is positive order for poles and negative for zeros. The density used at this step is density of convolutions of smooth compactly supported test functions in that test algebra. The representation category is nuclear Fréchet. This identifies the scope of the all-zero spectral statement and the topology in which density is proved. [Primary source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0412277v3)

## Weil · `PAPER-OP62-WEIL72`

French original in Izvestiya 36(1), pp. 3–18, §16, printed p. 18. Weil writes the final local distribution formula as \(S(\chi,F)=\Delta[F(|w|)\chi(w)]\) and states that positivity of \(\Delta\) on \(W\) is equivalent to the **conjunction** of RH and Artin's conjecture for all \(L_k(\chi,s)\). His positive-characteristic conclusion follows in the same paragraph. The selected mathematical locator is the MathNet scan of the journal original. The locally saved French transcription aids textual search. This is the Artin–Hecke generality of §16, a different work from Weil's 1952 explicit-formula paper.
