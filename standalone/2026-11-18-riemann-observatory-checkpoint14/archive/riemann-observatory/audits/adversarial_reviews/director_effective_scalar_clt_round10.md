# An effective conditional scalar CLT: director adjudication

18 November 2026. The director read and reconstructed the proposed exceptional-prime proof and its independent skeptic review. It establishes effectivity in principle for the scalar theorem, under RH, for fixed effectively specified D>0 and tolerance. No numerical onset has been calculated. The general uniform theorem for individual prescribed rational characters still has its original ineffective boundary.

Use exactly the strict two-sign tail probability weights |zeta'(1/2+i gamma)|^2/(1/4+gamma^2), normalized over |gamma|>Y. Set P=(log Y)^D, H=sum_(p<=P)1/p, V=(1/2)sum_(p<=P)(1/p-1/p^2), and W=sum_(p<=P)cos(gamma log p)/sqrt p. For every fixed moment order the standardized moments converge effectively to their Gaussian values. For every computable D>0 and epsilon>0 there is an effectively computable conditional threshold Y0(D,epsilon) beyond which the Kolmogorov distance to N(0,1) is below epsilon.

## Why the exceptional character can be removed here

At prefix height T>=Y, each fixed-order word involves integers of size at most P^k. The primitive-character error in HLZ section 4.4 contains a(kq). A divisor-closed support avoiding one prime ell therefore contains no conductor divisible by ell. Effective Landau–Page, in the form of Ford–Green–Konyagin–Maynard–Tao section 7, excludes the one possibly exceptional primitive real conductor by deleting one of its primes. A conductor with a prime already beyond P is absent without further deletion. The effective zero-free rectangle for all retained conductors has width a constant times 1/sqrt(log T), enough for every shift of size O(1/log T).

The proof bounds both L and its reciprocal effectively: periodic character partial sums, a finite Dirichlet truncation, and Borel–Carathéodory for the holomorphic logarithm yield polynomial bounds in sqrt(log T). Perron then gives T times a fixed log power times exp(-c sqrt(log T)), with effective constants. The divisor short-interval estimate is independently derived from a largest factor of each fixed-length tuple. No Siegel residue is estimated after deletion. The independent review supplies a direct fixed-ratio product approximate functional equation when a sharp continuous moment is required, avoiding an unjustified smoothing transition. The untwisted normalization contains no nonprincipal term at all.

Positive Fourier coefficients of raw cosine words let the relative rational-character error sum against the model's actual raw moments. Under the independent prime Poisson-circle model, centered increments have variance (1/p-1/p^2)/2, are bounded by 2/sqrt p and have bounded higher cumulant sums. Consequently all normalized fixed even moments are uniformly bounded. Restoring one deleted prime costs O_k(H^-1/2), uniformly at every prefix height. This restoration is done BEFORE Stieltjes integration: the full W,H,V are fixed as T varies. The bad prime may change with T; no derivative or integral of that changing prime is taken. This order is essential.

Partial summation against (t^2+1/4)^-1 transfers a uniform prefix error to the strict tail. Its boundary uses the inclusive prefix at Y. The effective untwisted asymptotic bounds the normalization from below. Reflection gives the same cosine statistic for the positive-only law.

Finally, the finite-moment bounds imply an effective CDF estimate. Gaussian smoothing, a finite Fourier cutoff, Taylor's remainder controlled by the next even moment, and the explicit Gaussian moments form a finite selection procedure: choose the smoothing width and cutoff from epsilon, the moment order next, then Y from finitely many effective moment errors. The independent Fejér argument gives a second smoothing route. This is stronger than merely invoking qualitative moment determinacy.

## Limits and provenance

The one-prime deletion method is classical and explicitly attributed to FGKMT. Its transplant uses the exact a(kq) support factor in HLZ. This is a conditional consequence of matched existing inputs, with priority unestablished. It supplies neither a practical numerical Y0 nor effective full-vector covariance at all prescribed coordinates, total-variation convergence, an ordinary-zero CLT, or zero-spacing information. The finite-dimensional extension currently has a separate candidate proof and is not promoted by this scalar adjudication.
