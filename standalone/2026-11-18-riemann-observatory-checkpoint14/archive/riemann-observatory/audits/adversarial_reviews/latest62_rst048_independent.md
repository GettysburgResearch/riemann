## Grouped analytic inputs and quotient bound

The fixed-scale good-height construction can take its auxiliary \(\eta\) arbitrarily small after fixing the cell length and reciprocal exponent. Indeed, the integrated zero kernel in (10) contributes \(O(\eta\log T)\) over nearby heights and \(O(\eta^2\log T)\) from the rest. Integrating the completed logarithmic derivative from \(\eta\) to the line pays one half the zero kernel and a favorable gamma sign. The fixed right-hand reciprocal estimate under RH, followed by the functional equation on the left, supplies the horizontal-edge majorant. The vertical edges are a fixed distance off line. The source's fixed-interval count (8) gives \(k_C\le D\log T_C\) for sufficiently high clusters with multiplicity included. It comes from the primary Balazard–de Roton Proposition 8. The finitely many exceptions can be absorbed because their shapes and multiplicities are fixed. No bound on individual residue coefficients is needed. [Balazard–de Roton Proposition 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0812.1689v1#page=10)

The Newton form (32) can be obtained by telescoping the expansion of \((s-z)^{-1}\) through the root nodes. Its last term is divisible by the cluster zero polynomial, and after multiplying by \(F(z)\) becomes analytic inside the contour when \(s\) is outside. This gives (33) including repeated nodes, and the contour length and root distance give \(|d_j|\ll T_C^e(2D)^{k-j}\). A potential failure would be an exponential in \(k_C\) from raising the constant in the contour majorant. The formula uses that majorant once for each coefficient, so the constant is a single prefactor.

For (35), all \(k+1\) nodes, including \(s\), lie in the critical line, and their convex hull lies in the near-height range. The repeated-node divided difference equals \(\zeta(s)/P_C(s)\), including removable points, because each previously interpolated value or derivative at the zero nodes vanishes. The integral representation of that divided difference has simplex mass \(1/k!\). Cauchy's derivative estimate on the \(D\)-circles has factor \(k!D^{-k}\); the factorial cancels exactly. The circles avoid the pole at 1 and remain in the fixed strip where (7) gives \(O(T_C^{D+e})\). This proves the collision-uniform \(D^{-k}\) in (35) with its stated height exponent.

For each denominator of degree \(m=k-j\), the removed product \(P_C/P_{C,j}\) costs \((6D)^{k-m}\). Then \(D^{-k}(2D)^m(6D)^{k-m}\le6^k\), so the near integrand after the profile estimate has squared norm \(\ll L^{-1}T_C^{2D+4e}k^4(1200)^{2k}\). The profile estimate itself follows by expanding \(P(D_x)(w_k h)\), with endpoint delta at zero only, and integrating its Fourier transform. The \(C^{k-1}\) matching at the other two knots suffices even at \(m=k\). The coefficient-norm estimate avoids differentiating any zero separation.

## What (41) actually proves

The near-height intervals have bounded overlap by the separation of the *representatives*, regardless of gaps between actual zeros. At far heights, \((2D/d)^m\) sums geometrically using \(d>13D/3\). Its first term and the extra \(1/d\) in (26) give a kernel \(\ll_D(1+|t-\tau_C|)^{-2}\). With \(p=2-2e>1\), the separated sum of these kernels is uniformly bounded and
\[
\int_{\mathbb R}(1+|t|)^{-p}(1+|t-\tau_C|)^{-2}\,dt
\ll (1+|\tau_C|)^{-p}.
\]
Splitting into neighborhoods of 0 and \(\tau_C\) and their complement proves the convolution inequality. For finite subsets, Cauchy–Schwarz at each \(t\), first on the near sum and then with the far kernel weights, gives (41). The proof works with termwise moduli inside the sum. Thus the tail beyond \(Y\) is \(O(L^{-1}Y^{-99/100})\) in squared norm uniformly over subsets. This is sufficient for exhaustion and convergence of the limiting local constants.

A stronger assertion \(\sum_C\|E_C^w\|_*<\infty\) does not follow. The implication already fails for disjoint normalized functions with coefficient \(j^{-199/200}/\sqrt{L}\), whose squared norm for every subset is exactly \(L^{-1}\sum j^{-199/100}\) while their individual norms have divergent sum. That control addresses a potential misreading, not the manuscript's actual Hilbert-space argument.

## The high strip and the meaning of (49)

For fixed \(N,s\) the grouped derivative and exponential residue terms are separately absolutely summable outside a fixed neighborhood, by the \(O(T_C^{-2+e})\) contour bound with a constant allowed to depend on \(N,s\). In the identity (46), a contour enclosing \(s\) contributes residue \(L F(s)\), and multiplying by \(\zeta(s)/L\) gives the indicator. In an empty rectangle the indicator cancels the contour integral exactly, including when \(s\) lies inside. Thus every original rectangle can be included before telescoping. Across adjacent rectangles the shared good-height sides carry identical contour integrands with opposite orientations. This step would not hold if the indicators and contours were estimated separately for each high cluster.

The vertical bound is a positive convolution with \(k_\delta(v)=(\delta^2+v^2)^{-1}\). Cauchy–Schwarz against \(k_\delta\), followed by Tonelli and the same convolution estimate as above, leaves
\[
\frac{N^{2\delta}}{L^2}\int_{|y|>h}(2+|y|)^{-2+4e}\,dy
=O(N^{2\delta}L^{-2}h^{-1+4e}).
\]
On each horizontal side, using \(e^{L(z-s)}-1=(z-s)\int_0^L e^{u(z-s)}\,du\) removes one singular power before taking absolute values. The integral over its real variable is \(2\,\mathrm{arsinh}(\delta/|h-t|)\), whose squared logarithmic singularity is locally integrable and whose squared tail is \(O_\delta(|h-t|^{-2})\). The weighted convolution is \(O((2+h)^{-p})\) and gives (48). The single remaining indicator contributes \(O(h^{-1})\). At \(H=N^{1/20}\), the slowest power from (49) is exactly \(2\delta+(-1+4e)/20=-0.049978\). The actual \(h=H+O(D)\) is a shared edge, so it introduces no estimate for a partial cluster.

## Theorem 1 transfer and the local detail

The grouped derivative identity (21) uses the regularization at 0 and \(-1\) and eliminates its remaining constant by taking real \(x\to\infty\). The grouped derivative bound is \(O(x^{-1+e})\) at such \(x\) by separation of the representatives. Perron's contour shift in (43) uses fixed \(N,s\), then remote heights and negative odd abscissae. The factorial growth in the functional equation controls the latter. These steps do not require uniform control of their contour constants as \(N\to\infty\), since they first give identities at individual \(N\).

The low-cluster correction has a vanishing profile on \([0,b]\) and at 1. The collective derivative bound in (57) permits Euler quadrature once for the sum. The exponents in (59) and (60) are indeed \(-0.1499595\) and \(-0.049958\) for the fixed parameters. Their squared errors are \(o(1/L)\), as is the high strip. The pole-removal or anchor at \(s=1\) incurs an \(O(1/L)\) norm error. The finite correction uses \(q(0)=q(1)=0\), and the regular \(Z^{(h)}\) contribution from later polynomial pieces cancels between the two cutoffs on the same piece. The noninteger knot term in (65) is required and present.

The sentence in §7.2 saying that (26) controls the norm outside fixed zero neighborhoods needs a termwise qualification. A point between two cluster ordinates can lie outside all their small fixed neighborhoods while having distance zero from the *full* ordinate hull. For a fixed set of clusters, expand each \(R_C\) into its finite actual Laurent summands \(a_{\rho,j}(s-\rho)^{-j}\) and use linearity of \(T_{L,w}\). Apply (26) to the denominator \((s-\rho)^j\), whose ordinate hull is the singleton \(\{\Im \rho\}\). On the stated complement, its distance is bounded below by the neighborhood radius, and far away there is additional decay. Thus the weighted norm is \(O_{\mathcal B}(L^{-1})\), by (7), even between the cluster zeros. Constants can depend on the fixed \(\mathcal B\). The uniform step for growing clusters has already been handled separately by (41). This repair does not assume a uniform zero separation.

On a zero neighborhood the top Laurent order produces (69) after cancellation of its exact coefficient against \(\zeta\). The remainder of the analytic factor and the lower Laurent orders vanish in the scaled norm by the \(O((1+|v|)^{-1})\) profile bound and the change of variable \(v=L(t-\gamma)\). The shifted-Legendre projection computes \(m^2\) for the profile \((1-u)^m\), and the prescribed finite modification changes each top Laurent profile without modifying the distant tail estimate. One takes \(N\to\infty\) holding the finite correction set fixed, then exhausts it. The terms \(m_\rho^2/|\rho|^2\) are summable under the ordinary fixed-width zero count with multiplicities, even without (41). The potentially larger adaptive constants are summable *by* (41) and the fixed finite limits (72), which is the needed stronger conclusion. Burnol's lower bound then gives the stated constant for both distances with the same normalization. [Burnol lower bound](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0103058)
