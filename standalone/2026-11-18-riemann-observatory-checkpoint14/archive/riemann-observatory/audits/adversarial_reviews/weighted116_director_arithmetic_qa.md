## Source conventions and common endpoints

Baker pp. 351–352, Theorem 2, defines an unweighted sum on \(x<p\le2x\) over primes coprime to the modulus and bounds the dyadic modulus sum of maxima over unit numerators. Its bounds are \(Q^{11/10}x^{4/5}+Qx^{11/12}\) times \(Q^\epsilon\) for \(Q^{1/2}\le x\le2Q\). Fouvry–Shparlinski §3.1 and Theorem 3.3, pp. 296 and 301, use the same annuli and unit convention with \(Q^{13/10}x^{3/5}+Q^{13/12}x^{5/6}\) times \(Q^\epsilon\) for \(1\le x\le Q^{3/2}\). The latter definition starts at \(x\ge2\), so very small scales are treated by a trivial bound. The weights \(\log^3p\) in the application come only from Abel summation. A common prefix endpoint \(t\in[P,2P]\) can be decomposed into annuli with largest base \(t/2\le P\). In the Baker range the bottom part below \(Q^{1/2}\) costs \(O(Q^{3/2})\) after the modulus sum and is covered by its first term. In the Fouvry–Shparlinski range a bounded bottom interval costs \(O(Q)\). The admissible powers sum geometrically. The argument uses the outside averaged bound at a common endpoint and does not claim a sum of modulus-dependent maximal prefixes. [Baker primary PDF](../../sources/prime107_references/baker2012_acta.pdf) · [Fouvry–Shparlinski primary PDF](../../sources/prime113_references/fouvry_shparlinski2011.pdf)

Blomer–Harcos–Michel (2.25)–(2.27), pp. 708–709, use the polar kernel \(\log x+2\gamma-2\log k\) with prefactor \(1/k\) for an additive unit numerator. This cancels in the unit average. With the original negative phase \(e(-pn/k)\), the \(J^-=-2\pi Y_0\) kernel has the phase \(e(+l\bar p/k)\), and \(J^+=4K_0\) has \(e(-l\bar p/k)\). The formulas in the director and prime113 reports account for both signs and for the outer \(1/k\) in addition to the Voronoi factor. ## Uniform kernel mass

Put \(M=kT/p\), \(U=KP/T\), \(r=(l/U)^{1/2}\), and \(W(u)=w_T(2\pi Tu)\). On the fixed compact \(u\)-support separated from zero, \(M/k^2\asymp U^{-1}\) and the Bessel argument is comparable to \(r\). An independent way to check the derivative in the common envelope is to differentiate the exact kernel. For \(J=J^+\) or \(J^-\),
\[
 B_{k,l}(p)=\frac{M}{k^2}\int W(u)J(\sqrt{lMu}/k)\,du,\qquad
 p\partial_p B_{k,l}(p)=\frac{M}{k^2}\int uW'(u)J(\sqrt{lMu}/k)\,du .
\]
Indeed \(p\partial_p M=-M\), and the derivative of the argument is the negative of \(u\) times its \(u\)-derivative. Integration by parts cancels the derivative of the exterior prefactor.

Both \(W\) and \(uW'\) have \(L^1\) norm \(O(1)\) for the standard two-transition taper. Their derivatives of order \(j\) have \(L^1\) norm \(O_j(\delta^{-j})\), where \(\delta=H/T\). For the second test this uses transition support of length \(O(\delta)\), which compensates for the extra derivative. The exact Bessel differential equation gives \(D_u J=\pm(4\pi^2 lM/k^2)J\) for \(D_u=\partial_u(u\partial_u)\); the sign is negative for \(Y_0\) and positive for \(K_0\). The operator is formally self-adjoint and repeated transfers onto the tests cost \(O_j(\delta^{-2j})\) in \(L^1\). Hence for \(r\ge1\) the common envelope can even use \(U^{-1}r^{-1/2}(1+\delta r)^{-A}\), with the large positive real Bessel bounds. At \(r\le1\) the small-argument logarithm is integrable and costs \(U^{-1}(1+|\log r|)\). The weaker prime113 envelope with \(r^{1/2}\) at large \(r\) also holds with its stated derivative argument.

The sum of the weaker envelope over \(l\ge1\) is \(O(\delta^{-5/2})\), since a mesh of width \(U^{-1}\) in \(v=l/U\) has density \(U\). Compare each point with the integral on \([l/U,(l+1)/U]\), where the ratio of endpoints is at most two, and integrate \(v=r^2\). This also works for \(U<1\) and for \(U\delta^{-2}<1\). On a sufficiently large polynomial cutoff in \(l\), the product \(d_2(l)\tau(l)\) costs \(T^\epsilon\) by the divisor bound. The tail can be controlled by increasing the fixed exponent \(A\), since \(U,\delta^{-1},K,P\) grow by bounded powers. Alternatively \(d_2(l)\tau(l)=\tau(l)^2\le d_4(l)\), by the prime-power inequality \((j+1)^2\le {j+3\choose3}\). On dyadic \(l\)-shells the partial sums of \(d_4\) cost only fixed log powers through the effective cutoff, and the rapid large-\(l\) decay absorbs the growing logs beyond it. No cap on the dual index at the least prime factor of \(k\) is needed.

## Gcd, fallbacks, and exceptions

For fixed \(l\) and \(d=(k,l)\), write \(q=k/d\). The inverse phase descends to numerator \(\pm l/d\), a unit modulo \(q\). The reduction of unit groups is onto with equal fibers, so the dual unit average is \(\mu(q)/\varphi(q)\). The \(q=1\) contribution cancels exactly before estimating anything. For a fixed \(d\mid l\), the remaining map \(k=dq\) is injective into a dyadic group with \(Q=K/d\). The source maximum over numerators bounds the image subset by the whole group. Excluding original primes dividing \(k\) but not \(q\) costs \(O(\log T)\) per modulus, even without a lower bound for the factors of \(k\). The contribution from the separate Ramanujan average is \(O(T^\epsilon P)\) per group since \(\sum_{q\asymp Q}1/\varphi(q)\ll T^\epsilon\). Empty groups with \(Q<1\) introduce no \(q\ge2\). The number of divisor groups is at most \(\tau(l)\). These steps do not assume any coprimality on the original divisor variable, only the already isolated coprime-prime stratum.

Abel summation is done for each \(k,l\) using common endpoints, after which the common suprema of both \(B\) and \(P|\partial_p B|\) factor out of the modulus average. Taking a separate maximal prime endpoint for each modulus would not be justified by the source. The derivation here avoids that. The growth of the factor \(\delta^{-5/2}\) is absorbed only after the strict power margins have been fixed, by a choice of \(\kappa<1\) close to one.

For the Baker region, \(P>\sqrt K\) with a power margin. If \(Q\ge P\) then the prefix estimate may use Baker. If \(P^{2/3}\le Q<P\), the Fouvry–Shparlinski source applies before one simplifies the resulting budget to \(P^{19/10}+P^{23/12}\). If \(Q<P^{2/3}\), the trivial count costs \(P^{5/3}\). The dominant fallback \(P^{23/12}\) saves a power because \(23\xi/12<\theta+11\xi/12<1\). In the Fouvry–Shparlinski region use its estimate when \(Q\ge P^{2/3}\) and the same \(P^{5/3}\) triangle bound otherwise. Its first inequality with \(\theta>1/2\) gives \(\xi<7/12\), so \(5\xi/3<1\). The additional \(K+P\) budgets from exclusions and centering save a power in both regions. These cases cover all reduced moduli.

The separate homogeneous Korolev theorem is quoted with the correct source, Mangoldt weight, unit condition and inclusive \(q^{7/10+\epsilon}\le X\le q\) range. The application uses strict power margins at both endpoints and the common cutoff within each modulus and prime block. There the cap below the least prime factor makes the retained dual indices units. No such cap is carried into the averaged results above. The later two-coefficient version would not allow the zero linear coefficient. The full exact 2018 PDF was available to the remote reader, with no verified local bytes. ## Disposition

The director's strict regions, including the sample endpoints and the unconditional scope of the arithmetic pieces, check out. The Ghosh thesis input, square-window threshold, common-factor elimination and cube-power estimates agree with the prior targeted reviews. At a simple zero \(\rho\), the stated residue of the character-twisted whole sequence is indeed \(-2L'(\rho,\chi)^2\), since \((-L'/L)''\) has leading coefficient \(-2\) at order three and \(L^2\) vanishes to order two. At a zero of multiplicity \(m\ge2\) the product is regular there. This supports freezing the prime factor for the ordinary divisor transform. There is no conclusion for the much longer prime scales and no complete fourth-moment result. No canonical file was edited.
