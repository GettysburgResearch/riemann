## What is counted

The public definitions are in comparator/ChallengeDeps.lean, and the public proof wrappers in comparator/Solution/Multiplicity.lean delegate to the internal Lean theorems. The [static counts check](../../experiments/results/formal_count_semantics_check.json) compares nine literal zeta count definitions with their internal counterparts. Read together with the successful wrapper build, this establishes the concrete counts used in the printed types.

- Mathlib's riemannZeta is hurwitzZetaEven 0, with zeta_eq_tsum_one_div_nat_add_one_cpow identifying its Dirichlet series on \(\Re s>1\), in Mathlib/NumberTheory/LSeries/RiemannZeta.lean. The counted set requires its value to be zero and \(0<\Re\rho<1\). It uses the open critical strip, excluding any artificial value at the pole and excluding trivial zeros. The window condition is \(T_1<\Im\rho\le T_2\), not a condition on \(|\Im\rho|\).
- The multiplicity is \((\mathrm{analyticOrderAt}\ {\rm riemannZeta}\ \rho).{\rm toNat}\). Mathlib's conversion sends the infinite value to zero, so the source's proofs that the order is finite and at least one are material. Zeta23/Statement/Seam.lean proves finite positive order using analyticity away from \(1\), a nonzero value at \(2\), and the zero condition. The same file proves finite windows using local finiteness and compactness, with the nonzero residue at \(1\) excluding an accumulation there. SeamClosed.lean provides the concrete closed zeta seam. Thus the finite sum and finite-set cardinality refer to actual finite zero configurations.
- Ncount sums analytic orders over all zeros in the window. Ndist takes the cardinality of the set of complex zeros in the entire strip, so two different real parts at the same ordinate count as different points. N0simple takes the cardinality of the intersection of that set with \(\Re\rho=1/2\) and analytic order exactly one. It is neither a union of the simple and critical-line sets, nor N0star (all distinct critical-line zeros), N0 (critical-line zeros with multiplicity), or Nsimple (all simple zeros in the strip). Reflection pairs used in the proof do not change these public counting definitions.
- The analogous Dirichlet definitions use Mathlib's DirichletCharacter.LFunction, with LFunction_eq_LSeries identifying the series for \(\Re s>1\), in Mathlib/NumberTheory/LSeries/DirichletContinuation.lean. Zeta23/ThmE/SeamL.lean proves positive finite order and window finiteness for the primitive characters in scope. Counts are for a single \(\chi\), with the same open strip and half-open ordinate windows, not a union over characters or an average over moduli.

## Exact statements and what the query leaves open

For zeta, the four fractions are \(2/3\) and \(2-1/c_{\rm MT}\) against N0simple, and \(5/6\) and \(3/2-1/(2c_{\rm MT})\) against Ndist. Each appears both for \((T,2T]\) and for \((0,T]\), totaling eight declarations. There are four further dyadic declarations for primitive Dirichlet characters. Writing \(\theta=1/\sqrt2\), the formal \(c_{\rm MT}=\sqrt2\tan\theta/(1+\theta\tan\theta)\) gives \(c_{\rm MT}^{-1}=1/2+\cot\theta/\sqrt2\), exactly the paper's constant. Approximate decimals are absent from the theorem types.

Every type quantifies over real \(\varepsilon>0\), allows a real threshold \(T_0\) depending on that tolerance, and asserts the bound for every real \(T\ge T_0\). This is the eventual-inequality form of the corresponding \(c-o(1)\) lower bound; with the positive eventually growing zero count, it is also the claimed lower bound on the limiting inferior proportion. No zeta declaration is conditional on RH, and none of the Dirichlet types includes GRH. The Dirichlet arguments are \(q:\mathbb N\), NeZero q, a single complex character, \(1<q\), and IsPrimitive. The nonzero instance is implied mathematically by \(q>1\). The ordering allows the thresholds to depend on the fixed modulus and character, matching Theorem B's fixed-character scope. The omitted modulus-one case corresponds to the separate zeta bounds via Mathlib's modulus-one L-function identity. There are no cumulative Dirichlet declarations in this query, so any cumulative Dirichlet conclusion in the prose is outside the direct query evidence. The displayed quantitative cumulative rate \(O(\log\log T/\log T)\) is also not encoded in these types.

This establishes a locally built proof matching the main asymptotic types in Theorems A and B as scoped above. It does not audit the full paper proof term by term. The bandwidth-one ceiling in §7.2 lies in a different theorem and uses hvalid and EnclOK. The paper explicitly states that the interval enclosures furnishing EnclOK are external to the cited kernel check, and that its main Theorems A and B do not use them. The derivative-zero results, higher-moment or family-average extensions, independent comparator/kernel checks, and Knausgård kernel-table certificates have separate evidence requirements. The proportion bounds leave the status of the remaining zeros open and prove no RH or GRH conclusion.
