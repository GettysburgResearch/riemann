## Exact statement

Fix \(0<a<b<\infty\). On \((U,j)\) use probability proportional to \(|Z_U'(\theta_j)|^2\) against Haar measure on \(U(N)\) times counting measure on the eigenvalue labels. Include the selected eigenvalue in
\[
X_N=-\sum_{aN<k\le bN}k^{-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^N\cos(k(\theta_\ell-\theta_j)).
\]
Let \(f(u)=-\int_a^b\cos(2\pi xu)\,dx/x\). Let \(P\) be the Fourier projection for \([0,1]\) with the orthonormal Fourier transforms \(e_0,e_1\) of \(1,\sqrt{12}(s-\tfrac12)\) on that interval removed. Denote its kernel by \(K\), and put \(d=|e_0|^2+|e_1|^2\). In the free DPP let \(S\) be the \(L^2\) limit of \(\sum_{|u|\le R}f(u)-\int_{-R}^R f(u)K(u,u)\,du\). Then \(X_N\Rightarrow\mu+S\), with convergence of every fixed moment and joint convergence for a fixed finite list of such bands. Here
\[
\mu=f(0)-\int f\,d=\int_a^b\big[-1+\mathbf1_{x\le1}(2-4x+2x^3)\big]\,dx/x.
\]
For real \(t\) its characteristic function is
\[
e^{it\mu}\det\nolimits_2(I+PM_{e^{itf}-1}P)
 \exp\left(\int(e^{itf(u)}-1-itf(u))K(u,u)\,du\right).
\]
The determinant acts on the projection range, or equivalently on the whole space with identity on its orthogonal complement. The theorem imposes no upper-band restriction besides finiteness. The polynomial mean simplification is for \(b\le1\). The arithmetic theory supplies neither this full law nor a proof that the limiting arithmetic law exists.

## Dependency chain and adversarial checks

1. **Haar-label weighting → finite projection.** In HKO (2.2)–(2.7), the derivative modulus at a labeled root equals the product of its distances to all other eigenvalues. Haar contributes the squares of those distances. Rotation and the global tilt give the free Vandermonde squared times \(\prod_i|1-z_i|^4\), which is circular Jacobi at \(\beta=2,\delta=2\) in BNR (1.6). A diagnostic is \(N=2\): the two within-matrix derivative moduli are equal, so the per-matrix normalizer would choose either root uniformly and yield a free density with \(|1-z|^2\); the global law gives \(|1-z|^4\). Multiplying the free Vandermonde rows by \((1-z)^2\) realizes the orthogonal projection of dimension \(N-1\) inside degrees \(0,\ldots,N\). The two removed coordinate vectors are the constant vector and \(j-N/2\), with squared norms \(N+1\) and \(N(N+1)(N+2)/12\). [HKO PDF](../../sources/classical63_lfunctions/hko2000.pdf) · [BNR finite-density PDF](../../sources/prime77_rmt/bourgade_nikeghbali_rouault_2009.pdf)
2. **Riemann sums → compact DPP convergence.** The coordinate \(u=N\theta/(2\pi)\) gives normalized basis modes \(N^{-1/2}e^{2\pi iju/N}\). Kernels converge uniformly on compact rectangles and \(0\le K_N(u,u)\le1+1/N\). Compact continuum compressions are trace class because Fourier extension from \([0,1]\) into a finite interval is Hilbert–Schmidt. The local Hilbert–Schmidt convergence and diagonal trace convergence of positive compressions imply trace-norm convergence: a finite-rank projection captures all but a small trace in the limit, the complementary traces converge by subtraction, and the off-diagonal norms are bounded by the geometric mean of the two diagonal traces using the square-root factorization. Alternatively Hadamard dominates the compact Fredholm series coefficients by a summable exponential series. No global trace-norm convergence is used.
3. **Compact convergence → full centered law.** Abel summation on \([-N/2,N/2)\) gives \(C/[N|\sin(\pi u/N)|]\le C'/|u|\) using \(k>aN\); the harmonic band sum gives a uniform bound. Integration by parts gives these bounds for \(f\). For any real test \(h\) on a bounded region the projection-DPP variance equals \(\int h^2K(u,u)\,du-\operatorname{Tr}((PM_hP)^2)\). The square is self-adjoint even for signed \(h\). Centered tail variances are thus \(O(1/R)\), uniformly in finite \(N\) and for continuum annuli. Compact convergence followed by \(R\to\infty\) gives the centered law. The absolute point sum genuinely diverges: the endpoint sine difference in the leading \(1/u\) coefficient has mean square bounded below on large dyadic intervals and is bounded, hence has positive mean absolute value there. The absolute intensity integral diverges, while absolute sums over increasing windows have uniformly bounded variance. Chebyshev and monotonicity give almost sure divergence. There is a valid alternative **symmetric** uncentered \(L^2\) limit with the root because \(\int_{-R}^R f\to0\). For finite means the constant diagonal gives exactly zero by Fourier orthogonality and the removed diagonal has mass two, giving \(O(1/R)\) mean tails. The Fourier autocorrelation of the two removees yields the stated \(\mu\).
4. **Compact determinant → regularized determinant and exponential integrability.** For compact truncation the ordinary Fredholm determinant is trace class by factoring through the square root of the absolute multiplier, with its phase retained in the other factor. For general \(g=e^{itf}-1\), the identity \(\|M_gP\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2=\int |g|^2K(u,u)\,du\) and the tail bound give Hilbert–Schmidt convergence. The centering correction uses the integrable quadratic remainder \(e^{itf}-1-itf\). Continuity of \(\det_2\) gives the formula above. For real \(t\) in the MGF, the finite compression of \(e^{tf_N}\) is bounded below on the projection range by \(e^{-|t|\|f_N\|_\infty}\). Applying \(\log(1+x)\le x\) bounds the centered log MGF by \(\int(e^{tf_N}-1-tf_N)K_N(u,u)\,du=O_{t,a,b}(1)\). The bounds for both signs of \(t\) and bounded raw means give uniform integrability of all fixed powers. These same estimates hold for fixed linear combinations, giving Cramér–Wold and joint moment convergence.
5. **Palm description is lineage.** Bufetov, Proposition 2.16, gives the reduced-Palm projection for almost every tuple of distinct points. For the sine kernel the Schur complement supplies a continuous version at distinct \(0,h\), since its evaluation vectors \(1,e^{-2\pi ish}\) are linearly independent when \(h\ne0\). Removing the constant projection and dividing their difference by \(h\) yields a nonzero multiple of \(s-\tfrac12\) in \(L^2[0,1]\). The removed projections converge in operator norm, and their kernels locally. The confluent reduced double Palm therefore removes both Palm points. The statistic deliberately puts one deterministic point back. It does not condition on an exactly coincident pair. No external confluent-kernel theorem is needed for steps 1–4.

## Finite experiment

All three files in the prime78 hash list validated. The default replay with Python and NumPy produced byte-identical JSON to the archived result, including the independent dense rational case at \(N=20\). The trace formulas follow from the cubic expansion of the determinant logarithm; \(EM_{f^j}E\) are compressions of powers of the multiplier itself, and differ from \((EM_fE)^j\). For the recorded band \((1/20,1/10]\), any two positive band frequencies sum to more than \(N/10\), so no signed triple sums to zero. The vanishing unperturbed cubic term follows also by this direct check. The numerical agreement checks the finite trace implementation and corroborates the limiting numerical values. The limiting cubic formula is a separate analytic input, and the experiment gives no convergence rate or arithmetic distributional inference.
