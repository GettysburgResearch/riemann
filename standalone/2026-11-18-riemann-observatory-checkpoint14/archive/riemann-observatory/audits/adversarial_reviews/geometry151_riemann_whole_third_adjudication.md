**P. 677, lower simple-pole display.** The first print has \(+1/(2\pi i)\) in the sense of a positive prefactor with no unary minus. ## One historical normalization correction

Write \(\Xi(t)=\xi_*(1/2+it)\) and \(\xi_*(0)=1/2\). Then
\[
1+(s-\tfrac12)^2/\alpha^2
=(1+1/(4\alpha^2))(1-s/\rho)(1-s/(1-\rho)),\qquad
\rho=\tfrac12+i\alpha.
\]
Replacing the center-normalized factors by factors normalized at \(s=0\) adds the absolute real constant \(D=\log(1/2)-\log\Xi(0)\). This is positive from the p. 674 theta formulas. An additive constant in \(\log\zeta(s)\) contributes exactly that constant to the arithmetic inverse at \(x>1\). Hence the printed \(+\log\Xi(0)\) in the p. 678 formula and the closing p. 680 comparison has replacement \(-\log 2\) in the exact formula. The unnormalized zero-only comparison on p. 678 likewise has a \(-D\log x\) shift, because its displayed positive derivative integral contains \((D/s)'=-D/s^2\). Count these as the same normalization defect. The logarithmic-integral branch offsets are purely imaginary and cancel pairwise, so they cannot supply \(D\). Genocchi’s actual pp. 57–58 already derive this correction and equation (11) includes \(-\log2\). The collected note p. 155 states the same replacement and speculates about the source of the error. The speculation carries no independent evidence of origin. ## External proof dependencies and comparison

- **Zero count and RH.** The contour sketch forecasts the two count main terms and a relative remainder scale. Its boundary and horizontal argument control are absent. A displaced right edge, the argument principle, Stirling, and a Jensen count for real-part crossings give the modern total count and the bounded-width \(O(\log T)\) local count. Von Mangoldt’s later rigorous count and sharper bound are separate from the source’s additional assertion about how many roots are real and from the conjecture that all are real. Euler nonvanishing and the functional equation suffice for a closed strip; nonvanishing of its vertical boundary is a later result and is unnecessary for the product repair.
- **Product.** The two proofs are equivalent routes. The advocate uses order at most 1 in \(t\), pairs the two genus-one factors with opposite zeros and uses evenness to eliminate the residual linear exponential. The skeptic applies genus zero directly to the entire function of \(w=t^2\) of order at most \(1/2\). The same theta bound \(\log M_\Xi(r)=O(r\log(r+2))\) proves the requisite order. Both count multiplicity, distinguish reflection from conjugation and avoid using the count asymptotic as a premise. Neither extends a global branch of a logarithm through zeros.
- **Ordered inverse.** The advocate supplies the more complete modern bridge: polynomial bounds on the paired \(s=0\)-normalized logarithms on a fixed vertical line justify distributional inversion against compact smooth tests in \(\log x>0\). They are insufficient by themselves for pointwise convergence. The ordinary symmetric-height von Mangoldt formula for the half-weighted Mangoldt prefix, together with \(E_\rho(x)=x^\rho/(\rho\log x)+O_I(|\Im\rho|^{-2})\) on compact \(I\subset(1,\infty)\), supplies the pointwise representative, including every jump midpoint. A shell comparison uses local \(O(\log T)\) counts and individual \(O_x(1/T)\) terms to identify height order with first-print \(|\alpha|\) order. The earlier skeptic gave the same branch, convergence and normalization, and relied directly on von Mangoldt’s grouped tails and p. 299, eq. (64), for the historical pointwise proof. The 1892 p. 154 note specifically identifies the ordered-series match as a result Riemann connected to an unpublished expansion.
- **Rearrangements.** The advocate’s supplemental argument adds evidence beyond the skeptic’s intentionally narrower result. With fixed-\(x\) Landau sums and a first moment bound for \(|\beta-1/2|\) of \(o(T\log T)\), Weyl’s criterion gives equidistribution of \(\gamma\log x/(2\pi)\) modulo one for fixed \(x>1\). Both sine signs have positive density on dyadic ranges. The real conjugate blocks have these signs at leading order; a quartet preserving the reflected pairing adds positive amplitude at the same positive ordinate. Block multiplicities do not weaken the lower mass, and the \(O_x(\gamma^{-2})\) errors are summable. The real rearrangement theorem can then change the sum by regrouping symmetry-preserving blocks. This is a later proof of the warning, not an input to the ordered identity.
- **Final application.** Dirichlet–Jordan inversion gives the printed arithmetic midpoint convention because the exponentially tilted step function is integrable and has finite variation. Möbius inversion is locally finite and exact. The last differentiated finite approximants do not establish a classical derivative of the prime staircase or an unconditional square-root counting error. The quoted empirical comparisons, including the English rendering of “from the first hundred thousand onward,” are kept separate from theorem claims.

## Disposition

No mathematical disagreement between the two readings requires a separate finding. The rearrangement proof extends the skeptic’s scope. The older edition already repairs the two local display slips, and the translation-only interval issue cannot be assigned to the original. Genocchi’s historical correction is the consequential formula repair and includes the centered-contour shift. Recommend a whole-work historical proof adjudication after the director’s own complete reading, with no additional author-error flags from these local checks.
