## Signed joint comparison and necessity

For a fixed real projection \(u\), let \(\alpha_p=u^\mathsf Ta_p\), \(\beta_p=u^\mathsf Tb_p\), and \(d_p=(\alpha_p^2+\beta_p^2)^{1/2}\). In the Poisson model \(E U_p=r_p=p^{-1/2}\), \(EU_p^2=r_p^2\), hence the coordinate vector \((\Re U_p-r_p,\Im U_p)\) has mean zero and covariance \(v_p I_2\), \(v_p=(1-1/p)/2\ge1/4\). A bounded trace of the proposed covariance bounds \(\sum d_p^2\) uniformly on bounded \(u\)-balls. The symmetric derivative prefix expectation of a complex character at \(m/n\) is the real positive-prefix cosine expectation. The observable can include sines because its Fourier expansion is tested against this symmetric prefix, with no need for an imaginary one-sided character estimate.

The raw Laurent coefficients of an individual signed projection have moduli \(d_p/2\) at each of \(p,p^{-1}\). The coefficientwise absolute majorant of its \(j\)-th raw power is therefore \((\sum_p d_p\Re U_p)^j\). Every word before cancellation has \(mn\le P^j\). Cancellation can only decrease the absolute coefficient bound and does not create the omitted prime. The Poisson coefficient \(\kappa=(m,n)/\sqrt{mn}\) is nonnegative, so the weighted coefficient mass is exactly the expectation of the majorant. Its mean is at most \((\sum d_p^2)^{1/2}(\sum_{p\le P}1/p)^{1/2}\). The independent bounded centered model increments have a sub-Gaussian scale bounded by \((\sum d_p^2)^{1/2}\). Thus the \(\kappa\)-weighted mass grows at most like \((1+\sqrt{H_P})^j\). The unrestricted coefficient mass is at most \((\sum d_p)^j=O(P^{j/2})\) for bounded \(u\). The centering constant has the same \(O(\sqrt{H_P})\) bound. After binomial centering, these two masses pair with the relative \(O(\log(2P)/\log T)\) and absolute \(O((\log T)^{-B})\) errors from the prefix estimate. For a fixed even maximal order \(r\) choose the support exponent \(Dr\) and \(B>Dr/2+2\). The retained even moment is then bounded uniformly at all \(T\ge Y\) and all \(u\) in a fixed ball.

The missing summand is pointwise bounded by \(2\|u\|\lambda_Y\). The elementary increment bound for powers and Hölder restore the full moments at every prefix, before Stieltjes integration. If using the largest prime of the exceptional conductor, \(\ell_T\gg\log\log T\), the same argument uses the exceptional-range supremum. For \(T\ge Y\) this is controlled by the displayed large-prime parameter in the joint proof. The inclusive-prefix term at \(Y\) gets the negative sign required by the strict tail. The ratio of the sum of the absolute boundary and integral weights to the actual tail mass is effectively bounded by the untwisted derivative asymptotic. This works for sine/cosine products and arbitrary order-\(r\) signed projected moments.

For qualitative necessity, the unrestricted, possibly ineffective prefix estimate compares fixed moments even when one large prime cannot be restored by a small-error bound. Uniform even moment bounds imply uniform integrability, so a convergent arithmetic Gaussian law also forces the model moments to converge to the matching Gaussian moments. If a column pair is noninfinitesimal on a subsequence, extract limits of that pair and of \(r_p\in[0,1/\sqrt2]\). In a fixed direction detecting a nonzero limiting column, the selected independent summand converges to a bounded nondegenerate law with variance at least a quarter of the limiting sum of squared projected coefficients. The remaining independent sum is tight by its variance bound. Independence survives subsequential convergence by factorization of characteristic functions. Cramér's decomposition theorem then contradicts Gaussianity of the sum. This necessity argument needs the covariance bound and unrestricted **qualitative** comparison. It supplies no effective test for arbitrary normalized column arrays without a computable deletion bound.

## Two cutoff calculations

The effective form of Mertens summation needed for the finite-variance annuli is stronger than a bounded remainder. To obtain it, the elementary floor identity and an effective Chebyshev upper bound give
\[
  \sum_{n\le x}\frac{\Lambda(n)}n
  =\frac{\log(\lfloor x\rfloor!)}x+O(1)=\log x+O(1).
\]
Subtracting prime powers with exponent at least two changes this by \(O(1)\). Now write the weighted prime sum as \(\sum_{p\le x}\log p/p=\log x+E(x)\), \(|E(x)|\le C\). Partial summation against \(1/\log x\) produces a constant \(M\), with remainder
\[
 \frac{E(x)}{\log x}-\int_x^\infty \frac{E(t)}{t\log^2t}\,dt
 =O^{\rm eff}(1/\log x).
\]
Hence for fixed \(0<a<b\), on \(L^a<p\le L^b\) the exact cosine variance is \(\tfrac12\log(b/a)+O^{\rm eff}_{a,b}(1/\log L)\), since the removed \(\sum p^{-2}\) tail is \(O(L^{-a})\). The maximal column is \(O(L^{-a/2})\). Disjoint such windows give independent Gaussian limits. The initial standardized block is independent in the limit because its support is disjoint. By contrast cumulative cutoffs \(L^{D_i}\) have reciprocal sums \(H+\log D_i+O(1)\), with \(H=\log\log L\). Their separately standardized covariance tends to one. They do not produce a nontrivial Brownian clock.

For the Brownian cutoffs use \(\log P_Y(u)=(\log L)^u\). Then \(\log\log P_Y(u)=uH\) exactly, including \(u=0\). A bounded effective Mertens remainder suffices for the uniform clock error \(H(P_Y(u))/H-u=O(1/H)\), because \(P_Y(u)\in[e,L]\). The initial block at zero contains only the prime 2 and its normalized size is \(O(H^{-1/2})\). At \(u_0>0\) the limiting variance equals \(u_0\).

## Local fourth moment and arbitrary interpolation times

For an arbitrary subset \(I\) of primes up to \(L\), let \(h_I=\sum_{p\in I}1/p\) and \(S_I=\sum_{p\in I}p^{-1/2}(\Re U_p-p^{-1/2})\). Fix the subset before using the prefix argument. For a retained set of mass \(h\), the range length \(2/\sqrt p\) gives an independent model Hoeffding parameter \(h\), from the maximal tilted variance \(1/p\). Thus \(E_*|S|^j\ll_j h^{j/2}\) holds for every positive \(h\), without a lower bound on the subset size. Under the relative character estimate, the exact \(j=0\) expectation and the positive raw-power majorant make the discrepancy in the centered fourth moment at most
\[
 O^{\rm eff}\left[
 (\log(2L)/\log T)(h^2+h^4)
 +(\log T)^{-B}L^2h^2\right].
\]
The first term follows because the centered expansion uses \(h^{4-j}\) times raw moment \(O_j(h^j+h^{j/2})\), \(1\le j\le4\). The second follows from \((h+\sum p^{-1/2})^4\le16L^2 h^2\). Bounding \(h\le1+\log L\), \(T\ge Y\) and \(B>2\) makes both \(O^{\rm eff}(h^2)\). There is no additive global error at tiny \(h\).

If the omitted prime belongs to \(I\), its fourth power is at most \(16/\ell_T^2\le16h_I^2\). The fourth-power triangle inequality gives the full inclusive-prefix fourth bound, even when the retained set is empty. Because the statistic is nonnegative, the negative lower-endpoint term in Stieltjes summation can be dropped for the upper bound. The remaining integral divided by the strict untwisted tail mass is bounded effectively, including when the endpoint is itself a zero ordinate. Consequently the strict-tail fourth bound is \(C h_I^2\), uniformly for all subsets at the same height \(Y\).

On the grid \(v_j=H^{-1}\sum_{i\le j}1/p_i\), normalized fourth differences are bounded by \(C(v_j-v_i)^2\). A linear interpolation fraction \(\theta\) on a single cell has fourth moment at most \(C\theta^4(\Delta v)^2\le C(\theta\Delta v)^2\). Across cells there are at most three parts, counting the possibly empty middle grid increment. Their \(L^4\) norms sum to at most a constant times \(\sum_{i=1}^3\sqrt{\Delta_i}\le\sqrt{3|t-s|}\). Constant extension after the last grid point can only reduce the time lengths used in the proof. Thus the fourth bound holds on the whole real clock interval \([0,2]\). On dyadic intervals of length \(2^{-j}\), union and Markov bounds make the probability of any adjacent increment exceeding \(A2^{-\alpha j}\) at most \(CA^{-4}2^{-(1-4\alpha)j}\). On the event that all bounds hold for \(j\ge k\), continuous dyadic chaining gives modulus \(O_\alpha(A2^{-\alpha k})\) at distance \(2^{-k}\). This gives a uniform effective modulus in probability by a geometric sum.

## Process topology and effective tests

The original process is right-continuous with left limits because its increasing cutoff includes a prime at the instant of crossing. Its value is exactly the continuous interpolation evaluated at the step clock \(c_Y(u)=H(P_Y(u))/H\). With \(c_Y(u)=u+O^{\rm eff}(H^{-1})\), the uniform modulus shows that replacing the step clock by \(u\) has a vanishing effect on the full path in sup norm in probability. Equality at the crossing is important for identifying the chosen version of the process.

At a finite deterministic mesh, the model variance matrix converges effectively to \((\min(u_i,u_j))\) and maximum normalized column is \(O_d(H^{-1/2})\). Thus the joint comparison applies also when the first mesh value is asymptotically zero. The tests needed here are bounded Lipschitz, which remain valid for this degenerate marginal. The fixed-dimensional smoothing and Taylor procedure has a finite order because compact frequency is chosen before that order. In the path comparison, choose the mesh after the effective modulus estimate and before that finite-dimensional procedure. On a mesh interval every point's clock value and both endpoint clock values lie within the mesh width plus \(2\|c_Y-\mathrm{id}\|_\infty\). Consequently the polygonal interpolation through the actual mesh values differs from the actual path by at most the continuous process modulus at this deterministic distance. Mesh interpolation is 1-Lipschitz from vector sup norm to uniform path norm. The \(J_1\) distance is bounded by uniform distance using the identity time change, so any bounded-Lipschitz test for the stated \(J_1\) metric pulls back to a bounded-Lipschitz mesh test. Brownian paths satisfy the same modulus estimate from their fourth moments \(3|t-s|^2\). Combining the two modulus probabilities and the fixed mesh estimate gives an effective bounded-Lipschitz theorem on \(D\) for each fixed computable \(u_0\). The result neither demands continuity of the random step process nor substitutes finite-dimensional convergence for tightness.

The limiting Brownian law at this cutoff and the finite-variance annulus law are different consequences of the same comparison. No publication-priority or numerical-onset assertion follows from this audit.
