# Atomic traces and sharp Fourier inversion below the critical weight

The one-packet obstruction in [weighted160](weighted160_negative_log_counterexample_campaign.md) is already present for one isolated atom. Banach–Steinhaus gives a continuous compactly supported counterexample to the abstract atomic problem. The zeta construction uses a further step. Centers escape to infinity to retain smoothness at every finite location, and a good-height contour identifies the sharply truncated spectral functional with the atomic expression up to bounded terms. In this smooth example each fixed spatial point has convergent Fourier inversion.

## Isolated-atom lemma

Fix \(q>0\), \(w(t)=\sqrt{1+t^2}\log^{-q}(e+|t|)\), and the Fourier sign \(\widehat F(t)=\int F(u)e^{itu}du\). Let \(\mu\) be a finite signed real measure with an isolated nonzero atom \(c\delta_x\), meaning \(\mu=c\delta_x+\nu\) and \(\operatorname{dist}(x,\operatorname{supp}\nu)>0\). The claim includes a finite or summably weighted discrete set of atoms with this gap at \(x\). Write \(I_T\) for inversion with multiplier \(\mathbf 1_{[-T,T]}\) and inverse measure \(dt/(2\pi)\). Choose a compact interval \(J=[x,x+d]\) with \(d>0\) inside the isolation gap. On the real Banach space
\[
 X=\{F\in C(\mathbb R)\cap H_w:\operatorname{supp}F\subset J\},
 \qquad \|F\|_X=\|F\|_\infty+\|\widehat F\|_{L^2(w\,dt)},
\]
the functionals \(U_T(F)=\int I_TF\,d\mu\) have unbounded norms. The space is complete since uniform limits and the weighted Fourier limits of a Cauchy sequence agree as distributions. Fixed \(U_T\) is bounded, for example by \(\|\mu\|_{\rm TV}T\|F\|_1/\pi\). Every \(F\in X\) vanishes on the whole measure support, including \(x\) by continuity.

To see unboundedness on smooth inputs, take the one-sided packets of weighted160 at \(x\) with height \(T\), width \(W=(\log T)^A/T\), \(0<A<q\), and fixed amplitude 1. The squared \(H_w\) norm is \(O(W\sqrt T+TW\log^{-q}T)=o(1)\) by the low-band Plancherel and high-band \(H^{1/2}\) interpolation estimates. The sup norm is at most 1. The Dirichlet kernel and the two cutoff transitions give
\[
 I_TF_T(x)=\frac{\log(TW)}{2\pi}+O(1).
\]
For the remaining measure, \(|I_TF_T(y)|\ll W/\operatorname{dist}(x,\operatorname{supp}\nu)\) uniformly on its support for large \(T\). Thus \(U_T(F_T)=c\log(TW)/(2\pi)+O(1)\). One may even multiply \(F_T\) by \((\log\log T)^{-1/2}\) to obtain a vanishing \(X\) norm and unbounded atomic witness. The same proof survives a fixed smooth tilt nonvanishing at \(x\) on \(J\); bounded tilted derivatives preserve the two norm estimates and the leading term is multiplied by the tilt at \(x\). The bounds are uniform for exponential tilts in a compact parameter interval. An additional functional bounded uniformly by \(C\|F\|_X\) cannot remove this operator obstruction.

Banach–Steinhaus along integer \(T\) now yields a single real \(F\in X\) with \(\sup_{T\in\mathbb N}|U_T(F)|=\infty\), even though \(\int F\,d\mu=0\). The conclusion of Banach–Steinhaus does not give differentiability. In fact, for the fixed compact support and the isolation gap, any such divergent test fails local \(C^1\) at \(x\). Off the support neighborhood the inversion is \(O(\|F\|_1/\text{gap})\), and local \(C^1\) at \(x\) suffices for convergence there.

## How the smooth Mellin construction differs

For any exponentially value-decaying \(F\) smooth at every finite real \(u\), and each fixed interior tilt \(G(u)=e^{(\beta-1/2)u}F(u)\) with \(G\in L^1\), one has \(I_TG(x)\to G(x)\) at every fixed \(x\). Indeed for a small \(\delta>0\), subtract \(G(x)\) in the Dirichlet kernel integral over \(|v|<\delta\). The difference quotient is integrable by local \(C^1\), and the exterior integrand \(G(x+v)/v\) is integrable by the tail bound. Riemann–Lebesgue kills both, leaving the elementary Dirichlet integral on the constant part. The argument applies to each isolated atom and its reflected argument. In the smooth example all their limiting values are zero.

For any fixed finite atomic sum, convergence then follows termwise. Even the off-support remainder of the isolated-atom lemma has an integrable uniform bound (using total variation). For the infinite prime sum in weighted160, successive packets are supported near successively escaping prime centers. The factor \(b_j\log p_j/\sqrt{p_j}\) tends to zero but is nonzero at each stage, and \(A\log\log T\) can overcome it for the stage's chosen height. The modulated packet has small norm and small fixed-height Mellin trace, permitting induction. This shows failure of interchange with summation over infinitely many atoms, while maintaining pointwise inversion everywhere. The good-height contour and the gamma primitive are additional ingredients that pass this failure to actual zeta zeros. The finite atomic statement alone has no spectral conclusion.

## Primary-source checks

- S. Banach and H. Steinhaus, *Sur le principe de la condensation de singularités*, Fund. Math. 9 (1927), 50–61. I inspected the ICM journal scan, printed pp. 52–57. Section 2 Lemma 3 is the second-category boundedness argument, with the condensation theorem in §2 Theorem I. Section 3 makes the connection to divergent orthogonal expansions of continuous functions, using the sup norm and pointwise partial sums. The results apply as a functional-analytic mechanism once the packet estimates give unbounded operator norms. They do not provide the logarithmic Sobolev bound or a smooth escaping-center test. I searched the specific intersections of logarithmic Sobolev regularity, ordinary Fourier interval truncations and the Guinand–Weil formula. The executed scope establishes a classical boundedness mechanism and related sufficient inversion classes. It does not establish a matching prior statement of the smooth escaped-atom obstruction, nor any priority claim. The lemma and its use here concern only the explicit logarithmic weights above.

The diagonal corollary in weighted160 gives one smooth escaped-center witness in every negative logarithmic space, since it imposes the stronger stage norm at exponents \(q_j\downarrow0\) and each fixed weaker norm eventually inherits the same summable tail bounds.
