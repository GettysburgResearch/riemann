# Independent check of the fixed Selberg-class transfer

I checked the source theorem needed for the lower bound in de Roton’s French original, pp. 419–420, and reconstructed the complementary-function identity for arbitrary pole order. I also checked the upper-interface estimates and their parameter substitution into the fixed-character adaptation. This is a conditional review for each fixed \(F\) under the stated zero-free-half-plane form of its GRH. It does not establish uniform constants in \(F\), unconditional square integrability at degree at least four, or priority for the sharp upper bound. [De Roton’s original](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.24033/bsmf.2516.pdf) [Candidate proof](effective_cluster62_selberg_transfer.md)

## Higher-order pole and Mellin transform

Set \(x=-\log t\), \(A(x)=\sum_{n\le e^x}a_n\), and
\[
 R(x)=\operatorname*{Res}_{s=1}\frac{F(s)e^{sx}}s,\qquad
 H(x)=A(x)-R(x).
\]
Here \(R\) is defined for every real \(x\). In particular it usually survives for \(x<0\), where \(A=0\). If \(F\) has a pole of order \(p\), \(R(x)=e^x\) times a polynomial of degree \(p-1\). If \(F\) has no pole, \(R=0\).

Take \(\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)\), set \(B(s)=\int \varphi(x)e^{sx}\,dx\), and fix \(c>1\). For each \(n\), the line-\(c\) Fourier transform of \(e^{-cx}\mathbf 1_{x\ge\log n}\) is \(n^{-s}/s\) for \(s=c+iy\). Summing against \(a_n\) in \(L^1(dx)\) is valid because the absolute sum of these weighted \(L^1\) norms is \(c^{-1}\sum_n |a_n|n^{-c}<\infty\). Fourier inversion against the compact test function times \(e^{cx}\) gives
\[
 \int A(x)\varphi(x)\,dx=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{(c)}\frac{F(s)}sB(s)\,ds.
\]
There is no pointwise Perron assumption. The test transform \(B\) decays faster than every inverse power of the height, uniformly in \(1/2\le\Re s\le c\), by integration by parts. Polynomial growth of \(F\) across the fixed strip at large height then permits a rectangular contour shift to \(1/2\). The point \(s=0\) is outside the rectangle and the pole at \(s=1\) gives exactly \(\int R(x)\varphi(x)\,dx\) by commuting the compact integral with a small-circle residue. This proves
\[
 \int H(x)\varphi(x)\,dx=
 \frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}\frac{F(1/2+iy)}{1/2+iy}
     B(1/2+iy)\,dy .
\]
The conditional bound \(|F(1/2+iy)|\ll_{F,\varepsilon}(2+|y|)^\varepsilon\) for any fixed \(\varepsilon<1/2\) puts the right multiplier in \(L^2(dy)\). Plancherel identifies \(e^{-x/2}H(x)\) with its \(L^2\) inverse Fourier transform. For the identification, test with \(\varphi(x)=e^{-x/2}\psi(x)\) for arbitrary \(\psi\in C_c^\infty\), so that \(B(1/2+iy)=\int\psi(x)e^{iyx}\,dx\). The resulting equality of distributions identifies the locally integrable \(e^{-x/2}H\) with the \(L^2\) function almost everywhere.

Since \(dt=e^{-x}|dx|\), de Roton’s \(\psi_F(t)=\Psi_F(1/t)=-H(-\log t)\) lies in \(L^2(0,\infty;dt)\) and its \(L^2\) Mellin transform on the critical line is \(-F(s)/s\). The step values at \(x=\log n\) are irrelevant. For \(F=\zeta^2\), one gets \(R(x)=e^x(x+2\gamma_{\mathrm E}-1)\) from the double pole. Thus for \(t>1\), \(\psi_F(t)=t^{-1}(-\log t+2\gamma_{\mathrm E}-1)\). It is square integrable, and omitting it would alter the transform. The same reasoning treats \(\zeta^p\) conditionally for every fixed integer \(p\), including \(p\ge4\).

The original de Roton Theorem 2 assumes membership \(\psi_F\in L^2(0,\infty)\), with no degree restriction in its statement. The adjacent degree-\(<4\) remark concerns a known sufficient route to membership. Her continuous family contains the discrete dilates \(\psi_F(nt)\), \(1\le n\le N\). Their Mellin factors are \(n^{-s}\) and the target \(\mathbf 1_{(0,1)}\) transforms to \(1/s\) by direct integration. These normalizations give \(D(1/N)\le\Delta_{N,F}\). Squaring the monotone nonnegative liminf lower bound yields the stated reverse constant. The critical-line completion zeros and critical-strip zeros of \(F\) agree under the stated GRH, since the ordinary \(F\) and completed functions differ there by nonzero regular factors.

## Stress checks on the upper interface

- The Selberg completion may first be estimated on strips by applying Phragmén–Lindelöf to \((s-1)^p F(s)\), removing the pole before interpolating. Fixed-strip finite order is enough for the strip principle because finite order gives log growth \(O(|\Im s|^M)\), smaller than the allowable exponential strip barrier. Outside that fixed strip, Stirling applies on the right with positive real parts of gamma arguments, and the functional equation supplies the left estimate. This gives the order bound needed for the genus-one product. The multiplier \(s^p(s-1)^p\) removes the actual completion poles exactly, so it introduces no extra endpoint zeros. Use the actual order, including zero when \(F\) is entire.
- The analytic logarithm is of \(F(s)((s-1)/(s+2))^p\). This function is nonzero at \(s=1\) by the actual pole order and nonzero elsewhere on the assumed half-plane. Its branch matches the Euler logarithm plus the rational-factor logarithm on the right. Its values at \(2+it\) are bounded uniformly because both logarithms are bounded there. Borel–Carathéodory and three circles in the proposal give \(o(\log |t|)\) at each offset with a fixed positive margin for Cauchy’s estimate. Under the conjugate functional equation reflection sends \(\sigma+it\) to \(1-\sigma+it\), so no evenness of the ordinate set is needed.
- The horizontal avoidance estimate follows from the real Hadamard identity after centering at \(1/2\). At \(u\in[0,\eta]\), the integrated kernel is \(\frac12\log[(\eta^2+(t-\gamma)^2)/(u^2+(t-\gamma)^2)]\). The gamma ratio contributes \(-d(\eta-u)\log(2+|t|)/2+O_F(1)\) to \(\log|1/F|\) between these offsets, so no omitted positive exponent is needed. The remote kernel tail after averaging is summable by an \(O_F(\log T)\) unit-window count. At high heights choose the good point in a prescribed fixed interval before moving the contour. Finite intervals admit off-zero choices and a larger finite constant.
- With \(D=10^{-4}/(1+d)\), the needed disk exponent is \(dD+e\le 2\cdot10^{-4}\). In the near-cell estimate replace the fixed-character \(D\) in the growth contribution by \(dD\), and replace \(D\) multiplying \(\log 1200\) by the multiplicity exponent \(10^{-4}\). The near exponent loss is therefore at most \(2dD+5e+2\cdot10^{-4}\log 1200<0.0022<0.01\). In the far estimate use \(\beta=2e+10^{-4}\log 200\), giving \(2\beta+2e<0.0017<0.01\). The delayed correction uses \(10^{-4}\log 200<1\) for the taper derivative. The Euler-discretization losses quoted for fixed characters remain negative with these replacements. Constants involving \(D^{-1}\) are allowed to depend on \(F\), since the mesh is fixed during the \(N\)-limit. Finitely many exceptional large multiplicities are absorbed in those constants. No coefficient Ramanujan bound on the approximants themselves is used at these steps.
- For the fixed-window count and summability, every critical zero is counted with its multiplicity. Bounding the sum of squared multiplicities in a unit window by the square of its total multiplicity suffices. Both signs and a possible central zero enter the constant. The cutoff orders are \(N\to\infty\) at fixed cell height \(Y\), followed by \(Y\to\infty\). Neither the local Laurent profiles nor their constants need uniformity as \(Y\to\infty\).

I found no higher-pole obstruction to the stated fixed-function lower transfer or a loss of the upper-interface exponent margins at large fixed degree. The all-degree extension still uses the retained-edge reconstruction from the prior transfer for its discrete upper approximation. Scope is the specified unanchored distance with complex coefficients on the ordinary integer grid. [Fixed-character estimates](effective_cluster62_dirichlet_transfer.md)
