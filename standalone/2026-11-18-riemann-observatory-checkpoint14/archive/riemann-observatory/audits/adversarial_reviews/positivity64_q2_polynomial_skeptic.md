# Independent audit of the fixed $Q_2$ moment polynomial

18 November 2026. I independently extracted the quartic from HLZ's shifted residues before comparing the companion script. The density, cumulative polynomial and strict tail all agree coefficient by coefficient. The RH-conditional power-saving tail follows once the sharp-endpoint continuous $J$ estimate is supplied. An independent fixed-ratio argument for it is below. The polynomial alone supplies no certified numerical enclosure. ## Arithmetic specialization and mixed extraction

Use the primary HLZ journal pp. 1592, 1605, 1607–1615, especially (5), (6), (24) and (29)–(36). The theorem sums zeros with multiplicity in the shifted product and explicitly works with complex shifts in the proof. For $Q_2(s)=2\,2^{-s}-1$, pad by zeros up to $y=T^{1/8}$. The coefficient inequality $|a(mn)|\le|a(m)a(n)|$ is immediate for $mn=1,2$ and its left side vanishes otherwise. The isolated coefficient at 1 is treated separately from the asymptotic divisor-log bound. The additive modulus before primitive-character rearrangement divides 2, so there are no nonprincipal terms. Equivalently §4.4 contains $a(kq')$ with $q'>1$, and the sole possible conductor 2 has no primitive character. In this specialization $E=0$ identically, including for complex shifts. Thus the Siegel step drops out of this calculation.

For the principal terms, the common-divisor weights at reduced $(h,k)=(1,1),(2,1),(1,2)$ are $3,-1,-1$. Directly in the original principal sum (29)–(30), letting $x=T/(2\pi)$ and $S(u)=\sum_{n\le u}c(n)n^{-b}$, I get
$$
3S(x)-2^{1-b}S(x/2)-2^b\sum_{n\le 2x}(-1)^n c(n)n^{-b}.
$$
The parity sign is $+1$ when $2\mid n$ and $-1$ otherwise because $\mu(2/(n,2))$ has those values. The Dirichlet series of $c(n)n^{-b}$ is $C(s)=\zeta(s)\zeta(s+a+b)\zeta(s+b+g)/\zeta(s+b)$. With $C_{(2)}(s)$ its local factor at 2, the parity series is $(1-2/C_{(2)}(s))C(s)$. Thus the exact Perron kernel has the stated correction $G(s)=3-2^{1-b-s}-2^{b+s}(1-2/C_{(2)}(s))$. This also provides a fixed small contour enclosing the three near-one residues, so their sum is holomorphic in the shifts even at coincidence. For the $J$ term the two $a+b$ polar residues cancel because the arithmetic weights agree when $a+b=0$. Integrating the time-dependent densities down to zero is legitimate in a small shift disk, where the powers of $t$ and every finite logarithmic derivative are integrable.

For a second derivation directly from (6) and (24), let $d=\log2$, $U=2^{-u}$, $V=2^{-v}$ and $W=2^{-w}$. The series of $f_{u,w}(2^j)$ is $(1-x)/[(1-Ux)(1-Wx)]$. Dividing its coefficient-shifted series by itself gives $(U+W-1-UWx)/(1-x)$. The three reduced weights give
$$
\mathcal W(u,v,w)=3-V-\frac{U+W-1-UWV/2}{1-V/2}.
$$
Differentiating in the auxiliary third shift gives $dW(1-UV/2)/(1-V/2)$. Write $Z(v)=\zeta(1+v)$ and set
$$
D(u,v)=Z(u+v)\left[\partial_w\mathcal W(u,v,0)
                         +\mathcal W(u,v,0)\frac{Z'(v)}{Z(v)}\right].
$$
Equation (24) then contributes $D(a,b)+e^{-(a+b)L}D(-b,-a)-e^{-bL}\mathcal W(a,0,-b)Z(a)Z(-b)$ to the $I(a,b)$ density with $L=\log(t/(2\pi))$. In its third term, $1/Z(-g)=-g+O(g^2)$ supplies the minus, and the differentiation of the exponential contributes zero because the undifferentiated local term vanishes at $g=0$ for generic $a,b$. The $J$ density is
$$
L\left[Z(a+b)(3-2^{-a}-2^{-b})
       +e^{-(a+b)L}Z(-a-b)(3-2^a-2^b)\right].
$$
In the rendered Theorem 5 the other term is $\overline{I(\bar b,\bar a,T)}$. With real coefficients the conjugation gives exactly the analytic transpose $I(b,a,T)$. In particular, the shifts are independent holomorphic coordinates during differentiation. Only after both derivatives have been taken and RH assumed does the summand become $|\zeta'(\rho)|^2|Q_2(\rho)|^2$. The polynomial $Q_2$ remains unshifted in the theorem.

I used two Laurent rays $(a,b)=(v,v)$ and $(v,2v)$ for the symmetric combined density. If their degree-two coefficients are $r_1,r_2$, then $5r_1-2r_2$ is the mixed coefficient. This differs from the companion script's rays. I expanded the exponential and local analytic factors through degree four, $Z$ through degree three, and $Z'/Z=-1/v+(\log(vZ(v)))'$ through degree three. The negative powers canceled for both rays and for both twists. The output in standard Stieltjes notation was
$$
\begin{aligned}
D_1(L)={}&L^4/12+2\gamma_0L^3/3+(\gamma_0^2-2\gamma_1)L^2\\
&+(-2\gamma_0^3-10\gamma_0\gamma_1-\gamma_2)L
 +2\gamma_0^4+12\gamma_0^2\gamma_1+8\gamma_0\gamma_2+14\gamma_1^2+10\gamma_3/3,\\
D_2(L)-D_1(L)={}&2dL^3/3+(d^2+4d\gamma_0)L^2\\
&+(-16d^3/3+4d^2\gamma_0+4d\gamma_0^2-8d\gamma_1)L\\
&+49d^4/6-32d^3\gamma_0/3+2d^2\gamma_0^2-4d^2\gamma_1
      -4d\gamma_0^3-20d\gamma_0\gamma_1-2d\gamma_2 .
\end{aligned}
$$
I also compared the six polynomials (density, cumulative and tail for each twist) by exact symbolic subtraction from the companion script after applying $c_j=(-1)^j\gamma_j/j!$. Each difference was zero. The normalization is $T/(2\pi)$ times $P_{i,-}(L)$ for the cumulative and $1/(\pi Y)$ times $P_{i,+}(L)$ for the two-sign tail, where $P_{i,\pm}=\sum_{j=0}^4(\pm1)^j D_i^{(j)}$. In the untwisted case $P_{1,-}/(2\pi)$ agrees with the five coefficients in Milinovich's proved (5.22), including his separately displayed $B_2,B_1,B_0$ on p. 62. I inspected the rendered thesis page. Its subsequent Conrey–Snaith remark has individual coefficients that differ from those giving the proved $B_j$, so the proved constants supply the comparison. ## Sharp continuous $J$ input from a fixed-ratio product AFE

Theorem 1.1 of Pratt–Robles is printed with a smooth weight whose derivative bounds are powers of $\log T$. HLZ §4.1 writes the sharp integral after a rephrasing. For the power saving, one can avoid assuming that the smoothed error is uniform for a rapidly shrinking transition. It suffices to apply the primary product approximate functional equation (2.1)–(2.4) at the three fixed ratios in $Q_2$. Here are the sharp-interval estimates.

Use the two completed factors at $s_1=1/2+a+it$, $s_2=1/2+b-it$. Integrate their simultaneous shift with the even weight $H(v)=e^{v^2}(1-4v^2/(a+b)^2)$ divided by $v$ on $\operatorname{Re}v=2$, normalized by the initial gamma factors. Moving to $-2$, taking the origin residue, and using the functional equations on the shifted-left integral gives the two Dirichlet sums of the product AFE. The completion poles away from $v=0$ are at shifts of heights $\pm t+O(1)$ and give rapidly decreasing errors because of the Gaussian. This also fixes the phase from first principles as $(n/m)^{it}$ for the term with $m^{-1/2-a}n^{-1/2-b}$. The dual term at $(-b,-a)$ has the same phase after relabeling. On the nested shift circles $|a|=r/\log T, |b|=2r/\log T$, the factor $(a+b)^{-2}$ costs only $(\log T)^2$. The conjugate gamma ratio of the dual term is $(t/(2\pi))^{-a-b}(1+O(1/t))$ uniformly, with its logarithmic derivative $O(1/t)$.

On any dyadic interval $X\le t\le2X\le2T$ with $X\ge10$, each AFE weight $V(mn,t)$ and $X\partial_t V(mn,t)$ are
$$
\ll (\log T)^2 \min\{(X/(mn))^\eta,(X/(mn))^A\},
\qquad 0<\eta<1/4<A,
$$
for fixed $\eta,A$ with $A>2$. To see this, move the weight's Mellin line to $\eta$ or $A$ respectively. The gamma ratio on either line is $O(X^{\operatorname{Re}v}(1+|\operatorname{Im}v|)^C)$ for $|\operatorname{Im}v|\le X/4$ by Stirling. On the same range its $t$ derivative has an additional factor $O((1+|v|)^C/X)$, by applying the digamma difference along the interval between the two gamma arguments. The Gaussian handles the remaining heights, since there the growth from the normalized gamma factors is at most exponential in $X+|\operatorname{Im}v|$ times a fixed power. The same bounds hold for the dual weight with its gamma ratio. Shift exponents multiply the coefficient bound by $(mn)^{O(1/\log T)}$, absorbed on $mn\le X$ and in the decaying range by taking $A$ larger if necessary. The ranges $t\le10$ can be bounded on a fixed compact strip without any AFE.

For reduced $h,k$, multiply by the fixed phase $(k/h)^{it}$. Off the condition $hm=kn$, one integration by parts on the sharp time interval bounds the oscillatory integral by the variation and endpoint bound divided by $|\log(kn/(hm))|$. This requires no time smoothing. Close to the diagonal $m\asymp n$, the weight $(mn)^{-1/2}$ cancels the extra factor $n$ in the inverse frequency, leaving a sum over $1/|kn-hm|$. For fixed $n$ this is $O_{h,k}(\log(2n))$. Summing the weight bound through and beyond $n=\sqrt X$ gives $O(\sqrt X\log^C T)$. Outside a fixed neighborhood of the ratio 1, the inverse frequency is bounded. Grouping by the product $mn$ and summing the divisor function against the same weights gives again $O(\sqrt X\log^C T)$, using $\sum_{j\le u}\tau(j)\ll u\log(2u)$. Endpoint logs and the $\log(t/(2\pi))$ weight add at most fixed log powers.

On the diagonal, $m=k\ell,n=h\ell$. Mellin summation inserts $\zeta(1+a+b+2v)$. The pole at $v=-(a+b)/2$ is killed by $H$. Shifting to $\operatorname{Re}v=-1/4$ crosses only the pole at zero from $1/v$. No gamma pole is crossed because the real parts of the shifted gamma arguments stay positive. The origin residue gives the first $J$ diagonal zeta factor with coefficient $h^{-b}k^{-a}$, and the dual gives the second with $h^a k^b$. The new Mellin line has the bound $O(X^{-1/4}\log^C T)$ pointwise from Stirling and polynomial growth of the zeta factor on the fixed positive strip. Replacing the dual gamma ratio by its leading power costs $O(X^{-1}\log^C T)$. Integrating and summing dyadic intervals thus gives a sharp $J$ error $O(T^{3/4}\log^C T)$ on the nested circles. The $O(1/t)$ from HLZ's $\chi'/\chi$ expansion is lower order by the same estimates or direct convexity on the working strip. The proof uses only fixed $h,k$ and does not claim a sharp version of the full long-polynomial theorem. [Primary product AFE, Pratt–Robles equations (2.1)–(2.4)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.04593v3)

## Remaining error, strict endpoints and effectivity

In the principal Perron calculation, take $U=T^{1/4}$ and use Liu–Ye with window parameter $\sqrt U$ at $x,x/2,2x$. The window has length $O(T^{7/8}+1)$ at each endpoint. The coefficients there are bounded by a fixed multiple of $\tau_4(n)$ for shifts within fixed multiples of $1/\log T$. The classical effective $\tau_4(n)\ll_\varepsilon n^\varepsilon$ is enough, and its constant can be bounded by separating a finite initial set of primes and using the prime-power formula. On $\kappa=1+1/\log T$ the absolute coefficient sum is at most a fixed power of $\log T$ if the three shift circles are small enough. All of the Perron allowances are therefore $O_\varepsilon(T^{7/8+\varepsilon})$ before or after Cauchy losses. One can take the auxiliary $g$ circle four times the inner $a$ radius so the separated contours avoid all coincidences. Differentiating the principal kernel first leaves $\zeta(s)\zeta(s+a+b)$ times the displayed combination of $G\,\zeta'/\zeta(s+b)$ and $\partial_gG$. The common residue circle can be differentiated without splitting coincident poles.

On RH the line $\operatorname{Re}s=3/4$, after the small shift $b$, stays uniformly to the right of all zeros. Pairing logarithmic derivatives at $s+b$ and $2+i\operatorname{Im}(s+b)$ leaves a summable inverse-square zero difference. The unit-window count gives an $O(\log(2+|\operatorname{Im}s|))$ bound for the log derivative where the contour stays away from $s+b=1$. The effective elementary Euler estimate on $\operatorname{Re}s\ge3/4-O(1/\log T)$ bounds each zeta factor by $O((2+|\operatorname{Im}s|)^{1/4}\log(2+|\operatorname{Im}s|))$ up to a fixed shift factor, also away from its pole. The left edge then gives $T^{3/4}U^{1/2}$ times log powers, and the horizontal edges give at most $TU^{-1/2}$ times log powers. Both are $T^{7/8}$ before the permissible log losses. There is no additional nonprincipal or exceptional-zero bound. The earlier contour reduction and HLZ's stationary-phase Lemma 7 have displayed polynomial orders below this with $y=T^{1/8}$. To move to an arbitrary $T$ on RH, the bounded endpoint displacement has $O(\log T)$ zeros and the earlier $O(T^{7/16})$ critical-line derivative bound gives error $O(T^{7/8}\log T)$. The cumulative main polynomial shifts by only $O(\log^4T)$ over that displacement.

For the strict tail use the inclusive cumulative sum at $Y$ in the subtracted boundary term, even if $Y$ is an ordinate. With cumulative error $O(Y^{7/8+\varepsilon})$ and kernel $(t^2+1/4)^{-1}$, the boundary and integrated errors are $O(Y^{-9/8+\varepsilon})$ for small fixed $\varepsilon$. Reflection doubles the result. Differentiating the smooth cumulative gives density $D_2/(2\pi)$, and the substitution $t=Ye^u$ in the tail integral of $D_2/t^2$ yields exactly $P_{2,+}/Y$ since $\int_0^\infty e^{-u}u^jdu=j!$. The replacement of the kernel costs $O(Y^{-3}\log^4Y)$ on this smooth density. The unconditional log-saving cumulative identity follows directly from HLZ Theorem 5. RH is needed for interpreting the mixed moment as the full nonnegative derivative moment and for this proof of the conditional power saving.

The proof now isolates an effective route. The fixed-ratio $J$ replacement removes the unspecified smoothing transition, and the principal Perron and RH line estimates use elementary or computable zeta-count constants. A numerical implementation still needs explicit versions of the completed-product Stirling and contour bounds, the stationary-phase factor from HLZ Lemma 7 and its cited Conrey–Ghosh–Gonek proof, explicit shift radii and a full accounting of the factors in the Cauchy and endpoint steps. Picking a symbolic $\varepsilon$ and removing Siegel's theorem do not assign a useful constant or threshold. I have allocated neither. The formal tail evaluations at 100 or 400 therefore remain scale estimates.
