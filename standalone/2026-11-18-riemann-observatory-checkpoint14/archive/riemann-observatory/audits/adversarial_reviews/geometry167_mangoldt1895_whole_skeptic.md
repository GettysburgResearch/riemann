# Whole-source skeptical review of von Mangoldt 1895

2026-11-18 · PAPER-H406. I read the actual fifty-one German printed pages 255–305 in order, using the cached OCR only to assist with prose. The governing equations, displayed assumptions and page numbers were checked on the rendered scan. The canonical access record is Level 0 with targeted prior reading of the grouped remainder, so this is the first complete skeptic reading in the present campaign. This note does not promote the record. The main inclusive zero count and the height-ordered explicit formula reconstruct. A global interpretation of the uniformity sentence on p. 293 fails; its compact version supplies the proof's downstream needs. Several smaller strictness, domain and decimal details are separated below.

## Section I: exact total count and its margin

Use \(\Xi(t)=\xi_*(1/2+it)\) and write the source's positive-real-part roots as \(\alpha_\nu=\beta_\nu+i\gamma_\nu\), with \(\beta_\nu>0\), \(|\gamma_\nu|\le1/2\). Thus \(\rho_\pm=1/2\pm i\alpha_\nu\), and \(\beta_\nu\) is a positive ordinate in the modern \(s\)-plane. The source retains multiplicity and orders by \(\beta_\nu\). This order does not assume RH. Equation (1) imports the paired Hadamard product. The theta representation excludes zeros with \(\Re t=0\), e.g. its second kernel is positive on the imaginary \(t\) axis. The closed strip is enough here.

Along \(t=\tau-3i/2\), every linear-factor argument increases with \(\tau\). A zero above the segment whose horizontal projection lies in \([a,b]\) subtends at least \(\arctan((b-a)/2)\), the corner minimum. The paired product converges normally with no zeros on this path, and its finite products have convergent continuous argument increments. Consequently \(A\arctan((b-a)/2)\le S<\Phi\), for the counted set and total increment of pp. 259–260. The printed (3) incorrectly uses a strict left inequality even when \(A=0\); the weaker comparison still gives (4). The same zero-count convention includes endpoints of the real interval.

On this exterior line \(s=2+i\tau\), the normalized logarithms are continuous from \(\tau=0\), and \(\log\zeta(2+i\tau)\) is its absolutely convergent Euler logarithm. Stieltjes' remainder is a named external estimate, not proved in the article. For \(\tau>0\) it is bounded in modulus by \(1/(3\tau)\); its termwise integral in p. 262 has negative imaginary part. Hence the remainder in the argument is between \(-\log\zeta(2)-1/(3\tau)\) and \(\log\zeta(2)\). At \(h=12\) isolating the \(2,3\) terms gives upper bound \(-0.151288\ldots\) for the argument remainder, enough to violate the necessary inequality (17). The local window bound of pp. 265–266 is \(A<k\log h\) for \(\tan1\le k\le h-4\), by combining the positive angle bound with the exterior increment and the mean-value estimate. In particular fixed-width zero windows cost \(O(\log h)\), independent of the global estimate.

At symmetric exterior endpoints the main increment is \(h\log(h/(2\pi))-h+5\pi/2+O(1)\), with absolute error below \(1.548\) for \(h>12\) by (18). Group across the imaginary \(t\)-axis, i.e. pair \(\alpha\) with \(-\bar\alpha\) for the equal geometric increments. The arctangent formula gives \(\Phi=2\pi H-2S_1+2S_2\), \(H=\#\{\beta_\nu\le h\}\). At equality \(\beta_\nu=h\), the reciprocal arctangent is interpreted as \(\pi/2\); \(\alpha_\nu\) is included in \(H\). The sum \(S_1\) is nonnegative with bound \(2H/h+(\log h)^2+(\pi/2)\tan1\log h\). Its far terms use local shells of fixed width \(2\tan1\) and harmonic summation. The difference of the two arctangents in \(S_2\) has a near term of size \(1/({\beta_\nu-h})\) away from the first shell, with an extra far factor \(h/(\beta_\nu+h)\). Its cubic correction is summable. Splitting the harmonic range at \(\lfloor h\rfloor\) and integrating the far tail gives (26). The signed combination of the two positive errors can be bounded by their maximum, not the sum of their leading \((\log h)^2\) bounds. Solving for \(H\) gives source (27):
\[
 H=h\log(h/(2\pi))/(2\pi)-h/(2\pi)+5/4
  +\eta\,[0.34(\log h)^2+1.35\log h+1.33],\qquad |\eta|<1.
\]
Thus the uncentered error bound has constant \(2.58\) for \(h>12\). There is no inference about the critical-line proportion. The subsequent density applies in windows with \(k\gg(\log h)^2\) in the asymptotic sense \(k/(\log h)^2\to\infty\), and \(k/h\to0\). The initial root is bracketed by 12 and 53 by (18), (22), (26); the same particular numerical comparison fails at 52. Two decimal substitutions in the source can be rounded outward without altering any margin. For example \(2\log\zeta(2)+1/12=1.078733938\ldots\), so use \(1.07874\) in place of \(1.07873\) at p. 266, and the expression computed at 53 on p. 273 is \(66.3239685\ldots\), safely above \(66.3239\), still exceeding the p. 274 far bound \(65.146064\ldots\). These checks are numerical reconstructions, not a full interval certificate for all decimals.

The 1905 paper gives a different count proof with \(O(\log h)\) and a \(7/8\)-centered main term, away from zero ordinates in its printed explicit bound. Do not transfer that scale or endpoint definition to (27). [Independent 1905 primary reading](geometry85_mangoldt1905_whole.md)

## Section II: arithmetic inverse and grouped contour limits

Let \(x>1\), \(u=\log x>0\), and \(a>0,\ a+r>1\). The Perron inequalities (30)–(31) bound the finite error by \(O(e^{\pm au}/(hu))\), and (37) gives the midpoint \(1/2\) at \(x/n=1\). At finite height the Euler series for \(-\zeta'/\zeta(s+r)\) is absolutely convergent on the line. Fix an integer \(G>x\). For \(n>G\) the denominator \(\log n-\log x\) is bounded away from zero, so that Perron tail tends to zero uniformly after summing with \(\Lambda(n)n^{-a-r}\). The remaining finite terms yield (38) for \(A(x,r)=\sum_{n\le x}'\Lambda(n)n^{-r}\), where the prime-power term gets half weight. No zero-height exclusions occur on this fixed vertical line.

The printed hypotheses of auxiliary A on p. 274 use \(g\ge0,\ v\ge0\). At \(v=0\) the displayed bounds have a zero denominator, and at \(g=c=0\) the segment itself starts at the pole. P. 276 uses (29) at \(g=-b\), which is outside the stated sign range. The derivation in p. 275 in fact bounds either sign of \(g\) through \(\sqrt{g^2+c^2}\), and either nonzero sign of \(v\) through \(|v|\). The required extension is immediate by the same integration by parts on a nonsingular segment; (29) needs \(g\ne0\). This local textual defect has a direct repair.

Hadamard's logarithmic derivative and the gamma product give (39) at finite height. Normal convergence suffices at fixed height: the paired zero summands are \(O_C(|\alpha_\nu|^{-2})\) on compact subsets away from poles. The pole part separates for \(r\ne1\); at \(r=1\) the double-pole inverse gives \(u\). For the trivial zeros, pair the constant term with the resolvent term as on pp. 280–282. Choose \(G\) sufficiently large that \(r+2G>0\), then the shifted Perron estimate bounds the integrated Gamma tail uniformly by \(O_{x,a,r}((r+2G)^{-1})\) before any height limit. The term at a collision \(r=-2m\) is replaced by its explicit quotient limit \(1/(2m)-\log x\). These cases are in the source, and (42) evaluates the integral limit of the full zero sum.

The next step is separate. Put \(c=r-1/2\), \(D_\nu=c^2+\alpha_\nu^2\). The individual integrated limit is
\[
 W_\nu(x,r)=2c/D_\nu-2x^{-c}(c\cos(\alpha_\nu u)-\alpha_\nu\sin(\alpha_\nu u))/D_\nu.
\]
Equation (49) expresses the difference between the sum of these limits through \(\beta_\nu\le h\) and the finite-height integral of the full series. The bounded coefficient sum \(2c\sum D_\nu^{-1}\) multiplies a Perron error tending to zero. The two finite growing sums involve errors of the shifted Perron segment, not the individual residues alone. The paired shift has real part \(b_\nu=a+r-1/2+\gamma_\nu\) or its conjugate-root counterpart, always at least \(b_*=a+r-1>0\). Equations (45), (50) and (54) have the uniform positive denominator furnished by \(b_*\). The finite estimates reduce to
\[
\sum_{\beta\le h}\frac1{\beta(h+\beta)},\qquad
\sum_{\beta\le h}\frac1{\beta(b_*+h-\beta)}.
\]
The near half of the second sum has prefactor \(1/h\) and harmonic shells of local \(O(\log h)\) counts. Its far half uses \(1/(b_*+h-\beta)=O(1/h)\) and \(\sum_{\beta\le h}1/\beta=O(\log^2 h)\). The first sum has at most this same cost. This gives \(O(\log^2h/h)\) for each, even at equality \(\beta=h\). In (51)–(54), the infinite side first separates the absolutely summable reciprocal-square tail. For \(\beta>h\) the shifted contour stays on one side of the real axis; the oscillatory tail is bounded by \(\sum_{\beta>h}1/[\beta(b_*+\beta-h)]\). Split into \(h<\beta\le2h\), giving the same harmonic estimate, and \(\beta>2h\), costing \(O(\log h/h)\). This is a complete grouped majorant for the infinite tail, so the exchange in (55) survives. The conjugate argument is for the entire cutoff set, invariant under \(\alpha\mapsto\bar\alpha\), rather than claiming each nonreal root is its own conjugate. No RH or simplicity assumptions enter.

The ordered convergence for \(x=1\) is trivial since \(W_\nu(1,r)=0\). For \(0<x<1\) the decomposition in (57) gives the same ordered sine series at \(\log x<0\) by oddness, plus absolutely convergent corrections for fixed \(x\). The source's ordered series by individual indices also follows from the height-cutoff limit: a partial tie block is bounded by \(O(\log h)\) terms of size \(O_{x,r}(1/h)\). Equation (58) at \(r=0\) is the symmetric von Mangoldt formula for half-weighted \(\psi_0(x)\), with \(-\zeta'(0)/\zeta(0)=-\log(2\pi)\) and \(-\tfrac12\log(1-x^{-2})\). This is distinct from the \(-\log2\) constant in \(J_0\). The primary p. 294 footnote recognizes the sign \(\zeta(0)=-1/2\) in response to the 1892 editorial wording.

## Interpretation of the uniformity sentence on p. 293

Immediately after (57) the first part of the decomposition is described as unconditionally and uniformly convergent “für alle positiven Werthe von \(x\).” The all-positive-domain uniform reading fails. The regular remainder summand is
\[
R_\nu(x,r)=\frac{2c}{D_\nu}
 \left[1-x^{-c}\left(\cos(\alpha_\nu u)+\frac c{\alpha_\nu}\sin(\alpha_\nu u)\right)\right].
\]
At \(r=0\), \(c=-1/2\), each fixed \(\alpha_\nu\) with \(\Re\alpha_\nu>0\) gives an unbounded function on \(x>1\). If \(\Im\alpha_\nu\ne0\), one of the two exponential components dominates and its coefficient is nonzero since \(\alpha_\nu\) is not purely imaginary. If \(\alpha_\nu\) is real, take \(u=2\pi j/\alpha_\nu\) to make the bracket \(1-e^{u/2}\). Thus the summands fail the uniform term criterion. Normal convergence holds on compact subsets of \(x>0\), since the imaginary shifts are bounded and the coefficient tails are \(O(|\alpha_\nu|^{-2})\). This localization is enough for everything subsequently used. The sentence may intend compact-uniform convergence with all positive points in scope. Its wording is recorded as an interpretation question, without an author-error flag. P. 295 makes the relevant separate, valid assertion for fixed \(x>1\) with \(r\) in a bounded interval. The comment on slow convergence near jumps should be read as failure of uniform convergence across a jump, not a quantitative pointwise convergence rate.

## Parameter integration, branches and (64)

The half-weighted \(f(x,r)=\sum_{n\le x}'\Lambda(n)n^{-r}/\log n\) on pp. 295–296 satisfies \(\partial_r f=-A\), taking the \(n=1\) term as zero or starting at \(n=2\). At \(x=p^m\) its jump is \(p^{-mr}/m\), and the assigned value includes half of that jump. Thus \(f(x,0)=J_0(x)\). For fixed \(x>1\), decompose the zero series as in (57). Its only conditionally summable part is \(2x^{-c}\sum_\nu\sin(\alpha_\nu u)/\alpha_\nu\); it is already known to converge and depends on \(r\) through a scalar factor. The remainder is normally convergent for \(r\) in a compact interval. For integration from finite \(r\) all the way to infinity, the unsplit \(2c/D_\nu\) contributions must first be combined with the gamma and other constant terms as on p. 296. In the remaining cosine part use \(|D_\nu|\gg c^2+\beta_\nu^2\) for real \(c\): factor \(D_\nu\), use \(|\gamma_\nu|\le1/2\), and \(\beta_\nu>12\). The majorant for the cosine term is a multiple of \(|c|e^{-cu}/\beta_\nu^2\). In the sine part the source splits
\[
\alpha_\nu/D_\nu=1/\alpha_\nu-c^2/(\alpha_\nu D_\nu);
\]
the remainder has integrable \(\nu\)-majorant \(O_x(c^2 e^{-cu}/\beta_\nu^3)\), and the leading scalar factor is integrable for \(u>0\). These bounds justify (59)–(61) without using global uniformity in \(x\) or interchanging a conditionally summable series with an infinite interval without control.

The bracket in (61) is grouped at real poles. At \(\rho=1\) cancel \(\zeta'/\zeta\) by writing the two terms in terms of \(x^{1-\rho}-1\) and the logarithmic derivative of \((\rho-1)\zeta(\rho)\), as p. 298 does. For \(r>-2\) the latter product is positive and regular along the integration interval after removal, so it has a real continuous log. For \(r\le-2\) choose \(-2(\lambda+1)<r\le-2\lambda\); divide also by \(\prod_{n\le\lambda}(\rho+2n)\). The quotient \(Z(\rho)\) of p. 302 is positive on the interval after removing its apparent singularities, by continuity from \(\rho>1\) and the absence of any other real zeros there. The exact cancellation puts \(x^{-\rho-2n}-1\) in each removed trivial resolvent numerator. The unsplit tails have positive denominators, validating the elementary integrations and (67) including a trivial-zero endpoint. For \(r>1\) every uncanceled denominator is nonsingular and the integrations on pp. 297–298 apply directly.

At \(r=0\), (63) explicitly contains \(\log[(r-1)\zeta(r)]=-\log2\). The first two integrals in (64) combine into \(\operatorname{PV}\int_{-\infty}^{\log x}e^v\,dv/v=\operatorname{li}(x)\); the change \(y=e^v\) agrees with the usual PV at \(y=1\) because the two cutoffs differ by a relative factor tending to one. The trivial-zero integral is the positive smooth term \(\int_x^\infty dy/[y(y^2-1)\log y]\). On pp. 300–301 the complex logarithmic integral is defined in the \(w\)-plane, slit along the negative axis, by horizontal rays and \(+i\pi\) on the upper half, \(-i\pi\) on the lower half, with the real PV on the positive ray. In a pair \(w=(1/2\pm i\alpha_\nu)\log x\), the signs cancel since their imaginary parts are \(\pm\beta_\nu\log x\); the arguments are \(w\) itself, never a principal log of \(e^w\). Translating each horizontal ray to a real variable with upper limit \((\log x)/2\) and setting \(v=-\rho\log x\) proves the displayed paired identity on p. 301 and gives the minus sign for the zero terms in \(J_0\). Equivalently use the vanishing ray values and remove the imaginary offsets per pair. The final cosine series is absolutely convergent by its \(\beta_\nu^{-2}\) bound, and the sine series uses the established order plus its summable remainder; combining the two series preserves that order. Returning to Riemann's \(|\alpha_\nu|\) cutoff changes only a shell of width \(O(1/h)\) and hence \(O(\log h)\) terms of size \(O_x(1/h)\). It does not license arbitrary reordering. The p. 299 footnote explicitly calls the constant a known old correction rather than a discovery of the article; Genocchi's 1860 (11) is the primary antecedent. ## Historical boundaries

The p. 302 remark about the two assertions from Riemann's draft letter explicitly withholds the reality of the zeros. The proof supplies the total count and ordered formula, not a separate asymptotic for real centered zeros. The pages 304–305 hypothetical RH or fixed narrower strip and the criticism of Cahen's rectangle do not establish PNT in this paper. A modern implication of a fixed gap from \(\Re s=1\) can be proved using a once-integrated Perron contour, whose nontrivial zero terms are absolutely summable, then using monotonicity to remove smoothing. The source's unweighted asymptotic conclusion is expressly conditional here. The full 1895 paper predates de la Vallée Poussin's 1896 criticism of the 1894 extract and explicitly controls the growing sums. Avoid describing its proof as a response to that later criticism. No canonical finding or promotion was made.
