## Fiori–Jaskari v2 and an explicit Gonek substep

Andrew Fiori and Mikko Jaskari, *Explicit bounds for the prime number theorem*, arXiv:2609.23222v2, submitted 19 September and revised 8 October. Provider identity CAND-C214EEA5C23D, OpenAlex W7214051944. The targeted check covers Theorems 1.1 and 1.4 on PDF pp. 4–5 and the explicit-formula bounds and assembly in Theorems 5.2–5.3 and §§5.1–5.2 on pp. 44–45. This is a 50-page preprint. The specialized interval calculations and Sage code were outside this check. [Pinned primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.23222v2) · [Version history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23222)

Theorem 1.1 at \(x_0=e^3\) gives, unconditionally for classical \(\psi\) and \(x\ge e^3\),
\[
 \frac{|\psi(x)-x|}{x}
 \le 0.2390\exp\left\{
  -D\left(1+\frac{\log\log\log x}{15\log\log x}\right)
       (\log x)^{3/5}(\log\log x)^{-1/5}\right\},
 \qquad D=0.1982767\ldots .
\]
The introduction defines and writes a signed \(E_\psi\), but §5.1 explicitly assembles the bound for \(|\psi(x)-x|/x\), as does Theorem 5.2. The authors compare the result with the earlier \(0.1853\) exponent constant and explain the respective zero-free-region and optimization inputs in Remark 1.2. This is a numerical explicit-formula improvement for the PNT. The method consumes zero-free and density estimates.

Theorem 1.4 assumes every \(\zeta\)-zero with positive real part has real part at most a given \(\sigma\in[3/4,1]\). It gives a table-based transfer of the shape
\[
 |\psi(x)-x|/x
 \le A(x_0)x^{-1/4}\log^2x+C(\sigma,x_0)x^{\sigma-1}.
\]
The text explicitly specializes at \(\sigma=7/8\) to \(A(e^3)=0.04934\), \(C(7/8,e^3)=0.02636425\). Section 5.2 joins finite-height results of Büthe and Chirre–Helfgott to Theorem 5.3. That specialization inherits the half-plane hypothesis and is a separate implication from the unconditional Theorem 1.1. [Fiori–Jaskari, Theorems 1.1, 1.4, 5.2–5.3](../../sources/prime122_frontier_pending/2609.23222.pdf)

There is a concrete unconditional use in Gonek's Lemma 8. Set \(r(u)=|\psi(u)-u|/u\). For \(v=\log u\ge3\), \(\log v\le\sqrt v\) and the extra logarithmic factor in Theorem 1.1 is at least 1. Thus \(r(e^v)\le0.239 e^{-0.198\sqrt v}\), and
\[
 \int_1^\infty r(u)\frac{du}{u}
 \le \int_0^3 (1+v)\,dv+0.239\int_0^\infty e^{-0.198\sqrt v}\,dv
 =7.5+\frac{0.478}{0.198^2}<20,
\]
where the first bound uses \(\psi(u)\le u\log u\). Put \(m=\mu+\kappa+1\) in Gonek's notation and use real \(|\delta|\le1/2\). Partial summation with \(u^{i\delta-1}\log^m(x/u)\) then gives a bound \(31\log^m x+20m\log^{m-1}x\) for the error in replacing \(d\psi(u)\) by \(du\) in that step. This holds for real \(x>1\) and integers \(m\ge0\), with the second term absent at \(m=0\). The derivative contributes \(|i\delta-1|\le\sqrt5/2\) and \(m\), the lower endpoint costs \(\log^m x\), and the upper endpoint vanishes for \(m>0\) or costs \(r(x)\le4\) at \(m=0\). The bound \(4+1+10\sqrt5<31\) covers both cases. The finite divisor convolution and other contour constants in Gonek remain separate. This calculation supplies no new bound for the derivative series \(C_2\) or an effective numerical threshold for the full Gonek asymptotic. [Gonek Lemma 8 interface](geometry88_gonek_whole_advocate.md)

## Inumaru–Sugiyama v1 and the continuous/discrete boundary

Naoto Inumaru and Shingo Sugiyama, *Twisted second moments of Dirichlet L-functions with applications to weighted one-level density*, arXiv:2610.41394v1, submitted 30 October. The PDF's author date is 2 November. Provider identity CAND-AC026DA96A1D, OpenAlex W7225102923. Targeted theorem and proof reading covers §§1.1–1.4, the AFE setup in §2, normalized moments in §3, the explicit-formula argument and Theorems 4.1 and 4.3 in §4, and the support-removal distinction in Appendix A. The underlying AFE citations and all component bounds were not independently audited. [Pinned primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41394v1) · [Version history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41394)

Theorem 1.3 fixes a primitive Dirichlet character \(\chi\bmod q\), \(q\in\mathbb N\), and a real \(1/2\le\sigma\le1\). It uses a Schwartz test \(\varphi\) with strict Fourier support inside \((-\sigma/(\sigma+1),\,\sigma/(\sigma+1))\), giving \((-1/3,1/3)\) at the center. No GRH is imposed for this test class. Here \(q\) is fixed in the limiting statistic and the analytic conductor of the translated family is \((3+|t|)q\). The primitive modulus-one case is explicitly included in §1.4 and Remark 3.4, so the theorem applies to classical \(\zeta\). The zero sum is over strip zeros counted with multiplicity, with \(\gamma=(\rho-1/2)/i\) potentially complex off the line. Compact Fourier support extends \(\varphi\) to an entire function by Fourier inversion. Thus this statistic does not project the zero set onto points on the critical line. Appendix A uses GRH for removing a different support constraint \([-2,2]\) in the **unweighted** Theorem 1.2. [Inumaru–Sugiyama, Theorems 1.2–1.3 and Appendix A](../../sources/prime122_frontier_pending/2610.41394.pdf)

The weight is a continuous height density \(|L(\sigma+it,\chi)|^2\,dt\) on \(T\le t\le2T\). For the same character the limiting density under the stated tests is \(1-(\sin(\pi x)/(\pi x))^2\) at \(\sigma=1/2\), and \(1\) for fixed \(\sigma>1/2\), with \(O(1/\log T)\) error for each fixed \(q,\sigma,\varphi\). Theorem 1.4 yields density 1 for two distinct primitive characters under the same support constraint. For \(q=1\), the support gain is from \(\sigma/(2\sigma+1)\) in the earlier Sugiyama–Suriajaya 2610.05227 to \(\sigma/(\sigma+1)\) here. In §§3–4 the diagonal prime term for \(\sigma>1/2\) is bounded by absolute convergence. At \(\sigma=1/2\), the main normalized prime twist is \(\chi(p)p^{-1/2}(1-\log p/\log T)\) for \(p^{it}\), and the conjugate for \(p^{-it}\). In the equal-character case this gives the triangular Fourier kernel. Distinct characters leave a nonprincipal character prime sum. The largest error in the first-prime arm leads to \(T^{a(\sigma+1)/2-\sigma/2}\) for support in \([-a,a]\), so the strict support margin is required. [Earlier classical source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.05227v1) · [New normalized moments and density proof, §§3–4](../../sources/prime122_frontier_pending/2610.41394.pdf)

Two local points in the proof are easily repaired and should not be repeated as estimates in an audit. In the dual prime-square error preceding (4.9) the paper bounds a sum of \(p^{2\sigma-3}\log p\) by an expression tending to 0 when \(\sigma<1\); the correct bound is \(O_\sigma(1)\) for fixed \(\sigma<1\), or \(O(\log T)\) for \(\sigma=1\). The prefactor \(T^{1-2\sigma}\) still makes this term negligible. In the different-character use of Lemma 4.2, \(\chi_1\bar\chi_2\) need not be primitive. Replace it by its nonprincipal primitive inducing character and account for finitely many primes dividing the common modulus. These checks address specific proof interfaces and are not a general audit of the preprint.

The basic moment Theorem 1.1 concerns one unshifted square on a real line, rational twist \((m/n)^{it}\), \((m,n)=1\), \(m,n\le T\), and \(\sqrt T\le H\le T\), with explicit \(q\)-dependence in the error. The normalized Theorems 3.1 and 3.3 also state ranges for growing \(q\), while Theorem 1.3 fixes the conductor. At \(q=1,\sigma=1/2,H\asymp T\), inserting \(m=h,n=1\) termwise against a polynomial coefficient \(a_h/\sqrt h\), \(|a_h|\ll1\), gives a total error bound
\[
 O\big((T^{1/2}y^{3/2}+T^{3/4}y^{3/4})\log^2 T\big)
\]
for length \(y\). This particular use saves a power only for \(y<T^{1/3-c}\). Our asymmetric \(J\) comparison saves a power for every fixed length exponent below 1 and retains two independent complex microscopic shifts, which Theorem 1.1 does not supply. The long-prime obstacle is an additive phase at a varying reciprocal modulus in a discrete, derivative-square-weighted sum over roots. Neither the continuous squared-height law nor the weighted one-level statistic estimates that additive sum.
