# Independent contour check at critical Mellin H¹ᐟ² regularity

The contour proof in [weighted152](weighted152_signed_p1_campaign.md) works at p=0. Assume the original value bounds f(x)=O(x^δ) at zero and O(x^(−1−δ)) at infinity, continuity, and F(u)=e^(u/2)f(e^u) in H¹ near zero. Let h be the Mellin transform and assume its area norm with weight w₀(t)=sqrt(1+t²) is finite on β∈[−ε,1+ε], 0<ε<δ. Restrict ε to be below 1/2 if needed. The ordinary inclusive symmetric height sum tends to the ordinary compensated formula. All p≥0 follow by comparison with w₀. This proof does not assert one-sided signed convergence at p=0, and does not give a necessity claim.

## Uniform sharp inversion is the endpoint step

Write F_β(u)=e^(βu)f(e^u), hence h(β+it)=∫F_β(u)e^(itu)du. For the closed inner strip β∈[−ε/2,1+ε/2], fixed disk submean gives the pointwise bound C N₀ w₀(t)^−1/2. Integrating the submean inequality gives a uniform vertical L²(w₀) norm. The same integration restricted to |t|>R bounds its squared tail by C times the original area integral over |t|>R−r, with r fixed. Thus the vertical weighted tails tend to zero uniformly in β. Two fixed lines suffice for the residue argument, but the uniformity follows directly.

Set m_T(t)=1_(|t|≤T)−exp(−t²/T²). Then ∫|m_T|²/w₀ is uniformly bounded: use w₀≥|t|, change variables t=Tv and bound by ∫|1_(|v|≤1)−exp(−v²)|²dv/|v|. The integrand is O(|v|³) near zero and rapidly decreasing at infinity. Cauchy–Schwarz after splitting at fixed R makes ∫m_T h(β+it)e^(−itu)dt tend to zero uniformly in u and β. The low range has multiplier tending uniformly to zero and bounded inverse weight integral, and the high range uses the small area-derived trace tails. Do not use Cauchy–Schwarz against 1/w₀ over the entire line without the difference multiplier. The Gaussian inversion is convolution with a normalized Gaussian approximate identity. The F_β are uniformly equicontinuous on the inner strip by compactness on bounded u intervals and uniform exponential tails. Hence Gaussian inversion tends to F_β uniformly in u and β. In particular the sharp integral itself tends to F_β; the proof uses Gaussian damping only for the comparison.

## Fold and remove the good-height restriction

Choose a=1+ε/2. The quotient ξ′/ξ has only the nontrivial zero poles, simple as poles with residues equal to multiplicity. A positive good height in [T,T+1] can be chosen at distance at least c/log T from all ordinates, by deleting small intervals around the O(log T) nearby ordinates, counted with multiplicity. Its negative is good by reflection. The local partial fractions in [Titchmarsh, second ed., Theorem 9.6(A), printed p. 217](../../sources/equivalence_round2/titchmarsh1986.pdf) give ζ′/ζ=O(log² T) on the two entire horizontal segments because 1−a≥−1 and a≤2. Stirling and the rational terms give the same bound for ξ′/ξ. The bounded horizontal lengths and the uniform trace bound give O(N₀ log² T/√T).

The contour is counterclockwise. The upward right side contributes h(a+it)ξ′/ξ(a+it). The downward left side has a minus sign. Substitute t→−t in the left integral and use ξ′/ξ(1−s)=−ξ′/ξ(s) to obtain a plus, so the folded test is h(a+it)+h(1−a−it). The prefactor is 1/(2π) after canceling ds=i dt. For any target height T the good height differs by at most 1, so the intervening spectral mass, including every possible exact endpoint atom, is O(N₀ log T/√T) by the unit-interval count. Thus full ordinary symmetric convergence follows once the folded integral converges. The argument need not move individual spectral terms to the critical line.

On the right line the absolutely convergent series ζ′/ζ(a+it)=−ΣΛ(n)n^(−a)e^(−it log n) gives, at each finite height, the sharp inverse integrals with coordinates (β,u)=(a,log n) and (1−a,−log n). The signs come from changing t→−t only in the second piece. Uniform inversion and ΣΛ(n)n^(−a)<∞ give the limit by dominated convergence. The endpoint factors are F_a(log n)=n^a f(n) and F_(1−a)(−log n)=n^(a−1)f(1/n), hence the prime term is exactly −ΣΛ(n)[f(n)+f(1/n)/n]. Absolute convergence of this series also follows directly from the value bounds.

## Gamma contour and identification

On the right line write ξ′/ξ=ζ′/ζ+Q, with Q=1/s+1/(s−1)−log π/2+ψ(s/2)/2. Stirling gives
```text
Q(a+it) = (1/2)log(|t|/(2π)) + (iπ/4)sgn(t) + O_a(1/|t|).
```
The constant on each half-axis matters at p=0: it cannot be absorbed by the inverse-weight integral because that integral diverges. It is handled with the same local split as the log term. The O(1/|t|) part is absolutely integrable by weighted Cauchy–Schwarz, and bounded compact t intervals are immediate since a>1.

Multiply F_β near u=0 by a fixed compact smooth χ supported within the local H¹ neighborhood. Its transform is absolutely integrable against 1+log(2+|t|), since H¹ is stable under the local exponential tilt. For the remaining part take m(u)=(1−χ(u))/(iu), extended smoothly at zero, and G_β=Fourier(mF_β). Its derivative is the transform of the exterior F_β. Every derivative of m, including order zero, is in L², so Fourier(m) is in polynomially weighted L¹ by Plancherel and Cauchy–Schwarz. Moderation of w₀ then gives G_β,G_β′ in L²(w₀), with bound C N₀. Local unit-interval Sobolev gives |G_β(t)|≪N₀ w₀(t)^−1/2. The logarithmic integral of G_β′ converges on each half-axis by integration by parts, with boundary G_β(t)log|t|→0 and integrable remainder G_β(t)/t. The constant term converges by the vanishing boundary of G_β alone. This gives continuity of the Q functional in area norm plus local H¹. The reflection t→−t only changes the half-axis used for the second test.

To identify the limit with the compensated archimedean expression, it is enough to use the compact smooth explicit formula and approximate physically in u. Smooth cutoffs tending to one give uniform weighted Fourier approximate identities for both exterior lines and convergence in the full area norm. Small compact mollifiers act by the Fourier transforms of exponentially tilted mollifiers, uniformly bounded on the fixed strip and pointwise tending to one. Choose a diagonal sequence with local H¹ convergence as well, using a smaller fixed neighborhood of zero. The approximants preserve the exponential value envelope and converge pointwise, so the Mellin pole terms and prime terms converge by dominated convergence. Local uniform H¹ gives a 1/2-Hölder bound at zero in u and point-trace convergence, enough to dominate the compensated kernel near x=1. The tails are dominated by the original value envelopes. The contour gamma continuity proved above therefore identifies the limit with the ordinary formula. No separate pole passage is necessary in this route. If the Q contour is also moved to 1/2 for comparison, each tested Q integral crosses only its right pole at s=1 and contributes respectively h(1) and h(0).

## Literal source class and scope

In the pinned author manuscript, (A) gives finite one-sided limits of both f and f′ at exceptional points, with midpoint values for f. At p=0 the central disk trace and Plancherel give F∈H¹ᐟ². A genuine finite jump is incompatible with its Gagliardo seminorm: for points at distances u,v on opposite sides in small intervals, the numerator is bounded below while ∫₀^b∫₀^b (u+v)^−2 du dv diverges. Thus the piecewise original F is continuous after identifying the midpoint values, and the finite one-sided derivative limits give local H¹. This replaces the p>1 argument via absolute Fourier integrability, which is unavailable at p=0.

The earlier Selberg route proves separate half-height convergence for p>1 and remains an independent approach. The contour result proved here is a symmetric result for p≥0, and the smooth p=3 absolute-divergence witness lies in its domain. The continuous cusp without local regularity is outside literal (A) and the local H¹ theorem. Its signed divergence remains consistent with the contour proof. No priority conclusion, negative-p conclusion, or canonical change is made.
