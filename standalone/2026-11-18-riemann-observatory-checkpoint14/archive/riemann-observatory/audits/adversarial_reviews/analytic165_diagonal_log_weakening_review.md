# One smooth witness for every strict logarithmic weakening

The diagonal conclusion follows from the [packet bound](analytic160_negative_log_packet_lemma.md) and the [genuine zero-sum construction](weighted160_negative_log_counterexample_campaign.md). It also preserves the bounded subsequence in the [independent two-subsequence construction](geometry160_negative_log_skeptic.md). All norms here use the same fixed strip, and the logarithmic exponent is fixed separately at each finite stage before choosing its frequency.

Fix \(\delta>0\), \(0<\epsilon<\min(\delta,1/4)\), and the norm strip \([-\epsilon,1+\epsilon]\). Write \(u_j=\log p_j\) for separated increasing prime logarithms tending to infinity, and use packet amplitudes \(a_j=e^{-(1/2+\delta)u_j}\). On the \(j\)-th stage use \(q_j=1/j\), \(A_j=q_j/2\), and \(w_j(T)=T^{-1}(\log T)^{A_j}\). Write \(h_{j,T}\) for the Mellin transform of \(x^{-1/2}F_{j,T}(\log x)\), and \(N_{-q}\) for the area norm with weight \(\sqrt{1+t^2}(\log(e+|t|))^{-q}\). Translation, tilt and the fixed finite strip introduce only a finite factor at this stage. Thus
\[
N_{-q_j}(h_{j,T})^2\ll_j (\log T)^{-q_j/2}+(\log T)^{-q_j}.
\]
At fixed \(j\) each term tends to zero. At a good height within 1 of \(T\) the matching prime contribution has leading term
\[
-\frac{a_j\log p_j}{2\pi\sqrt{p_j}} A_j\log\log T.
\]
The off-target contributions tend to zero and the archimedean and horizontal contributions obey the earlier packet bounds. They may use constants for the fixed \(q_j\); no uniformity as \(q_j\to0\) is needed. At each stage \(A_j\) is strictly positive. Consequently the divergent matching term and the vanishing norm bound hold simultaneously for all sufficiently large \(T\), even if the required threshold grows very rapidly with \(j\). Keep each packet strictly between its prime logarithm and the next integer logarithm. Its transform also tends uniformly to zero on \(0\le\Re s\le1\) by the shrinking physical \(L^1\) envelope. Therefore, after fixing all stage constants and protected heights, one can enforce \(N_{-q_j}(h_{j,T_j})\le 2^{-j}\), spectral spike domination over the previous finite packets, and an absolute total effect at most \(2^{-j}\) at each earlier protected height. The earlier finite packets have absolutely bounded spectral sums by integration by parts and the local zero count.

For the optional bounded subsequence, every finite packet sum is smooth with compact physical support and has zero prime samples. Its explicit-formula limit is uniformly bounded as the number of packets grows, because the physical envelope is common, its support avoids a fixed neighborhood of \(x=1\), and the remaining pole and compensated archimedean terms are dominated. Alternatively the exterior \(Q\) estimate is available with the single fixed exponent \(q=1\), since \(q_j\le1\) and its weight is no larger than the stage weight. After fixing each spike height, choose a later finite-sum approximation height for the bounded subsequence and protect it together with the spike height. This uses no uniform-in-\(q_j\) control of the \(Q\) constants.

The resulting function \(f(x)=x^{-1/2}\sum_j F_{j,T_j}(\log x)\) is real and smooth by local finiteness. It vanishes near zero and one and is \(O(x^{-1-\delta})\) at infinity, since the disjoint packets lie less than 1 to the right of their centers. These statements give the literal (A) and (B) conditions. The exponential decay after tilting is uniform on the entire fixed strip because \(\epsilon<\delta\). Hence the transform is the pointwise sum of the packet transforms there by dominated integration. At a fixed protected height, the sum involves finitely many zeros and later packets contribute at most \(\sum_{k>j}2^{-k}\) by construction. Thus the same function has a subsequence of inclusive symmetric sums tending to \(-\infty\), and, under the two-height variant, a bounded subsequence. Its formula's arithmetic and compensated archimedean expressions are well-defined.

For any fixed \(q>0\), eventually \(q_j\le q\). Since \(\log(e+|t|)\ge1\), the corresponding tail norms satisfy \(N_{-q}(h_j)\le N_{-q_j}(h_j)\le2^{-j}\). Every earlier packet is compact and smooth in log coordinates, so repeated integration by parts gives finite \(N_{-q}\) on the same strip for each member of the finite prefix. The triangle inequality followed by Fatou proves \(N_{-q}(h)<\infty\). This argument holds for each \(q>0\) with the already constructed \(h\). It asserts no norm bound uniform as \(q\downarrow0\). Indeed, \(N_0(h)=\infty\) by the [critical signed formula](director_signed_mellin_round16.md), since the local regularity and value hypotheses hold and the raw symmetric sums have an unbounded negative subsequence. Monotone convergence then gives \(N_{-q}(h)\to\infty\) as \(q\downarrow0\).

For any \(s<1/2\), \((1+t^2)^s\ll_s\sqrt{1+t^2}/\log(e+|t|)\). The same witness therefore also belongs to every polynomial strip norm below the critical exponent \(1/2\). No change of contour strip, physical packet supports, or spectral heights is required when varying \(q\) or \(s\). This review concerns the stated strict logarithmic and polynomial weakenings.
