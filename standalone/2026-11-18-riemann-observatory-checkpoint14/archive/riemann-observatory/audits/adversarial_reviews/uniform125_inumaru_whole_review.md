## The theorem being compared

Theorem 1.3 fixes a primitive Dirichlet character \(\chi\) modulo \(q\in\mathbb N\), including \(q=1\) with \(L=\zeta\), and fixes a **real** \(\sigma\in[1/2,1]\). Let \(\phi\) be Schwartz with Fourier support compact in \((-\sigma/(\sigma+1),\sigma/(\sigma+1))\), with Fourier convention \(e^{-2\pi ixu}\). The measured family is \(L(s+it,\chi)\) at \(T\le t\le2T\) with normalized measure
\[
 \frac{|L(\sigma+it,\chi)|^2,dt}{I_1(\chi,\sigma,T)},\qquad
 I_1(\chi,\sigma,T)=\int_T^{2T}|L(\sigma+it,\chi)|^2dt.
\]
Its one-level statistic counts nontrivial zeros \(\rho=1/2+i\gamma\) with multiplicity and tests \(\phi((\gamma-t)\log T/(2\pi))\). Off RH, \(\gamma\) can be complex. Compact Fourier support gives the entire continuation. The result is the integral against density 1 for \(\sigma>1/2\), and against \(1-(\sin(\pi x)/(\pi x))^2\) for \(\sigma=1/2\), with error \(O_{q,\sigma,\phi}(1/\log T)\). Theorem 1.4 permits distinct fixed primitive characters in the statistic and weight, with the same support and density 1 including \(\sigma=1/2\). These weighted conclusions within their support are unconditional. The dependence on fixed \(\sigma\) is material. The side limit \(\sigma\downarrow1/2\) gives no uniform crossover law.

The t-window is hard and compact, and the weight is an ordinary continuous special value per parameter. Even at \(q=1\) its possible pole is outside \([T,2T]\). Its denominator is finite and positive since the L-function is analytic there and not identically zero. By Fourier inversion and integration by parts, the entire test decays faster than any power in the real coordinate on each bounded imaginary strip. For fixed \(T\), the zero count and bounded t-window give normal convergence of the zero sum and integrability with the weight. There is no external weight-tail or width hypothesis on this interval. The support width parameter used in the proof is an exponent \(a<\sigma/(\sigma+1)\), distinct from the short-interval length in Theorem 1.1.

## Twisted second moment and normalization, §§1–3

Theorem 1.1 evaluates the continuous integral with twist \((m/n)^{it}\), for coprime \(1\le m,n\le T\), real \(1/2\le\sigma\le1\), and \(\sqrt T\le H\le T\). The two diagonals off the center are
\[
 \chi(m)\overline{\chi(n)}
 \left[(mn)^{-\sigma}L(2\sigma,\chi_0)
 +(qt/(2\pi))^{1-2\sigma}(mn)^{\sigma-1}L(2-2\sigma,\chi_0)\right].
\]
The first diagonal imposes \(n\nu=m\mu\) in the direct product. In the reflected product the conjugations and roles of \(m,n\) swap, giving the same character factor. The source's Euler summation in Lemma 2.1 is valid from the principal-character discrepancy \(O(d(q))\); at argument zero it uses \(L(0,\chi_0)=0\) when \(q>1\) and \(\zeta(0)=-1/2\) when \(q=1\). If a diagonal cutoff \(x=\sqrt{qt/(2\pi mn)}\) is below 1, the summation-by-parts formula extends directly using \(S(q,y)=0\) for \(0<y<1\) and the same bound on the discrepancy. This handles empty sums rather than treating a positive upper limit as a guaranteed term.

The proof accepts the stated Rane and Titchmarsh approximate functional equations, then uses balanced moving cutoffs \(x_1=y_2=\sqrt{qtm/(2\pi n)}\) and \(x_2=y_1=\sqrt{qtn/(2\pi m)}\). Stirling produces the mixed oscillatory phases. The second derivative handles the equal-factor term, and the first derivative and a reciprocal logarithmic gap handle unequal factors. A minor range slip in Lemma 2.4's derivation bounds \(\mu^{2\sigma-1}\) by the cutoff power, which works as printed for \(\sigma\ge1/2\), the relevant application, but not for all stated \(\sigma\). A uniform alternative is to split the mixed off-diagonal at \(\nu=\mu/2\) for \(\nu<\mu\). For comparable factors each weight is \(O(1/\mu)\), and the inverse log costs \(O(\mu/(\mu-\nu))\). For separated factors the log costs a constant and each of \(\nu^{-\sigma}\mu^{\sigma-1}\) and \(\mu^{-\sigma}\nu^{\sigma-1}\) is at most \(1/\nu\) for \(0\le\sigma\le1\). This yields the needed \(O(X\log(2X))\) unequal-factor sum at cutoff \(X\), uniformly. The direct and reflected truncation compensators cancel at fixed \(t\), and the two \(L(s,\chi_0)\) poles cancel as \(\sigma\to1/2\), giving \(\varphi(q)/q\) times \(\log(qt/(2\pi mn))+2\gamma_q\), with \(\gamma_q=\gamma_E+\sum_{p\mid q}\log p/(p-1)\). The source's seven AFE error terms are grouped by oscillatory pair, one remainder, and two remainders. This reads the complete derivation while relying on the stated upstream AFE inputs.

Theorem 3.1 divides the \(H=T,n=1\) formula by its untwisted value. For fixed \(\sigma>1/2\) and fixed \(q\), the denominator is \(L(2\sigma,\chi_0)T+o(T)\), and the main normalized twist is \(\chi(m)m^{-\sigma}\). The three errors, suppressing fixed \(q,\sigma\), are \(T^{1-2\sigma}m^{\sigma-1}\), \(T^{-\sigma}m\log^2T\), and \(T^{-\sigma/2}m^{\sigma/2}\log^2T\). For Theorem 3.3 at the center the main normalized expression is \(\chi(m)m^{-1/2}(1-\log m/\log T)\). The errors in (3.4) for fixed \(q\) are \(m^{-1/2}\log m/\log^2T\) and \((T^{-1/2}m+T^{-1/4}m^{1/4})\log T\). The proof's broader conductor hypotheses are \(q\ll T^{4\sigma-1}\) off center and \(q\ll T/\log^8 T\) at the center. The final density result only uses the fixed-conductor case.

## Explicit formula, support margin, and local repairs, §4

Choose \(\operatorname{supp}\widehat\phi\subset[-a,a]\) with \(a<\sigma/(\sigma+1)\). For \(x\) within \(T/2\) of \(t\), the gamma density in the explicit formula is \(\log T+O_q(1)\), uniformly for \(T\le t\le2T\). Outside this range the Schwartz tails after scaling control the integral of the logarithmic gamma growth. Dividing by \(\log T\) gives \(\widehat\phi(0)+O(1/\log T)\). The two possible pole terms for \(q=1\) are the test values at the translated arguments from \(s=0,1\); their imaginary scaled parts are \(\pm\log T/(4\pi)\) and their real parts have size \(\asymp T\log T\). Fourier integration by parts bounds them by \(O_M(T^{a/2}(1+T\log T)^{-M})\), with any fixed log losses absorbed by increasing \(M\). For primitive \(q>1\) these poles are absent. Prime powers of exponent at least three are absolutely summable in the explicit formula and cost \(O(1/\log T)\). Uniform errors can be integrated against the normalized positive weight directly.

For \(\sigma>1/2\), the prime main term is summable by \(\sum_p(\log p)p^{-1/2-\sigma}<\infty\). In (4.10)–(4.11), the three error exponents before fixed logarithms are \((a-2)(\sigma-1/2)\), \(3a/2-\sigma\), and \(a(\sigma+1)/2-\sigma/2\). All are strictly negative under the stated support, since \(1/(\sigma+1)\le2/3\). For squares put \(m=p^2\) and \(p\le T^{a/2}\). The square main term is summable, and the two power-growing errors give exponents \(a-\sigma\) and \((a-1)\sigma/2\), again negative.

The calculation just before (4.9) uses its estimate (4.8) with power \(2\sigma-3\le-1\), outside (4.8)'s hypothesis. The claimed decay of the prime sum is false: at fixed \(1/2<\sigma<1\) it increases to a positive finite limit, and at \(\sigma=1\) it grows logarithmically. Replace the sum by \(O_\sigma(1)\) for \(\sigma<1\), and \(O(\log T)\) at \(\sigma=1\). Its prefactor \(T^{1-2\sigma}\) leaves a bounded contribution, so (4.9) and the final \(O(1/\log T)\) conclusion are unchanged. At \(\sigma=1/2\), use the central normalized twist with \(m=p^2\) directly. The leading normalized square term is \(O(1/p)\), the error contributions have negative exponents \(a-1/2\) and \((a-1)/4\), and the secondary logarithmic term is summable. Thus the square estimate survives at the center as well.

In the central prime term the character product is \(\chi_1\overline{\chi_2}\). It is principal exactly when the two primitive characters agree, because equality of their induced characters forces equality of their primitive conductors and characters. If distinct, their product may be imprimitive, while Lemma 4.2 is stated for a primitive nonprincipal character. Pass to its inducing nonprincipal character and omit the finitely many additional modulus primes to obtain the same fixed-conductor \(O(1/\log T)\) bound. If equal, (4.12)–(4.13) give the integrals of \(\widehat\phi(v)\) and \(|v|\widehat\phi(v)\) by the two prime signs. The multiplier \((1-|v|)_+\) is the Fourier transform of \((\sin\pi x/(\pi x))^2\). The strict \(a<1/3\) absorbs the critical-line twisted errors. These checks give Theorems 4.1 and 4.3 with the scope stated in the introduction.

## Appendix, reference coverage, and transfer boundary

Appendix A proves the unweighted theorem for \(\operatorname{supp}\widehat\phi\subset[-2,2]\) by bounding each averaged prime phase. I checked the rendered p. 26. Its displayed exact value of \(I_{p^k}(T)=T^{-1}\int_T^{2T}p^{-kit}\,dt\) omits \(k\) in the denominator, which should be \(ikT\log p\). The argument uses only the weaker bound \(2/(T\log p)\), so the error estimate is unchanged. Under GRH, via a signed right-continuous zero count \(N(t,\chi)\) and the interval difference \(N(v+T,\chi)-N(v,\chi)\), the unweighted calculation instead permits any measurable test with \(O((1+|x|)^{-1-\epsilon})\) decay. Locally the count difference is \(T\log T/(2\pi)+O_q(T)\), including intervals through zero. Outside the local window the logarithmic count bound and test decay give an integrable tail. This appendix removes the support limitation only for the **unweighted** theorem under GRH. Its comment on Conrey's pole treatment is the author's contextual observation, without a fresh adjudication of Conrey.
