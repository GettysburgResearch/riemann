# Adversarial check of the compact-local smooth saddle

18 November 2026. The compact-local saddle comparison in analytic95 is valid. It uses a smooth average of contours and restores the target endpoints on the original derivative-weighted zero sum. The comparison reduces the long one-sided twist to the signed smoothly cut nonprincipal sum displayed in analytic95. The signed sum remains unresolved. [Adjudication](analytic95_pure_fourth_adjudication.md) · [Sharp comparison and pure-arm reduction](prime90_pure_fourth_bottleneck.md) · [HLZ primary source, §§4–6](../../sources/classical63_derivative_moments/hlz2022print.pdf)

## Averaged contours and sharp target heights

Put \(\theta=4b<2/3\), \(y=Y^\theta\asymp T^\theta\), \(H=T^\kappa\), and \(P=D^4\), with \(T\asymp Y\). The outer endpoint densities can be chosen supported in intervals of length \(O(H)\) with bound \(O(H^{-1})\). Their difference generates the desired compact weight \(w_T\) on the vertical integrals. These averaged contour identities are identities with the logarithmic derivative already inserted. The auxiliary shift is introduced later on the separate right integrals to evaluate that logarithmic derivative.

For \(R(t)=\int_{\sigma_L}^{\sigma_R}|\zeta'/\zeta(\sigma+it)|d\sigma\), the partial fraction remainder on the fixed strip is \(O(\log T)\), and integrating each pole in the real direction costs \(O(\log(2+1/|t-\gamma|))\) for \(|t-\gamma|\le1\). On each unit interval the number of relevant ordinates, counted with multiplicity, is \(O(\log T)\). Cauchy–Schwarz over these finitely many poles and the bounded integral of the square of the logarithmic kernel give \(\int_I R^2\ll(H+1)\log^2T\). This is uniform in the transition position. The Dirichlet mean-value bound for \(P\) on a fixed real strip, also with \(\sigma\) derivatives, supplies the required strip supremum bound by a one-dimensional Sobolev inequality in \(\sigma\). For \(\sigma\ge-C/\log T\), the coefficients have squared sum \(O(y)\), and \(\partial_\sigma P\) costs at most a fixed logarithm. Since \(H>y\), \(\int_I\sup_\sigma|P(\sigma+it)|^2dt\ll Hy\log^{O(1)}T\). Convexity of the two shifted zeta factors costs at most \(T^{1/2+\epsilon}\) across these strips. Cauchy–Schwarz after averaging gives \(O(T^{1/2+\epsilon}\sqrt y)\) for the initial horizontals. Moving the continuous gamma-log-derivative integral to the critical line uses the same estimate at the connectors, with the simpler logarithmic gamma-factor bound.

For the sharp replacement, write \(A=D^2\). Its coefficients satisfy \(\sum_n|[n^{-s}]A|^2/n=O(1)\), by its at-most-two ordered prime factorizations and Mertens' bounded harmonic sum. Cover an \(O(H)\)-length transition by unit intervals with boundedly overlapping enlargements. The \(O(\log T)\) upper bound for the zero count per interval, the real-variable Sobolev bound for \(A(1/2+it)\), and the Dirichlet mean value for \(A,\partial_t A\) give
\[
\sum_{|\gamma-U|\ll H}|P(\tfrac12+i\gamma)|
\ll (H+Y^{2b})\log^{O(1)}T
\]
uniformly for endpoints \(U\asymp T\). RH Lindelöf, the functional equation and a Cauchy circle of radius \(\asymp1/\log T\) give the uniform additional derivative-square factor \(T^\epsilon\). This controls either side of the strict endpoint, including its atoms. Thus no sharp saddle estimate is inferred from horizontal bounds.

## Saddle, normalization and complex shifts

Write \(s=\sigma_R+it\), choose \(\sigma_R=1+C/\log T\) with \(C\) larger than the sum of the fixed shift-circle radii, and use the standard convention \(\zeta(s)=\chi(s)\zeta(1-s)\). Reflection of \(\zeta(1-s+\beta)\) produces \(\chi(1-s+\beta)\zeta(s-\beta)\). The three zeta factors and reciprocal in the right integral have absolutely convergent Dirichlet series at \(\sigma_R\), uniformly on the microscopic shift circles. Shifting the integration variable \(s\) to \(s-\beta\) here is a reparametrization of the whole vertical integration with compact support. If \(\beta=\beta_R+i\beta_I\), it leaves real part \(c'=\sigma_R-\beta_R\) and time argument \(w_T(t+\beta_I)\). It introduces \(x^{-\beta}\) for \(x=m/k\), giving \(k^{-1+\beta}m^{-\beta}\) in the coefficient. This reparametrization introduces no connection segment.

Stirling gives the amplitude \(x^{-c'}(t/2\pi)^{c'-1/2}\), phase \(t\log(t/(2\pi x))-t-\pi/4\), and a symbol factor \(1+O(T^{-1})\) with derivatives of order \(j\) bounded by \(O_j(T^{-1-j})\) for the error. These bounds are uniform on the real part range of \(c'\). At the stationary point \(t_0=2\pi x\) the phase contributes \(e(-x)e^{-i\pi/4}\). Its second derivative is \(1/t_0\), so the Gaussian phase cancels \(e^{-i\pi/4}\) and the normalization is precisely
\[
(2\pi)^{-1}\sqrt{2\pi t_0}\,x^{-c'}x^{c'-1/2}=1.
\]

For \(x/T\) in a fixed positive compact range, write \(t=Tu\) and use the signed square root \(v=\operatorname{sgn}(u-u_0)\sqrt{2(\phi(u)-\phi(u_0))}\) with \(\phi'(u)=\log(u/u_0)\), \(\phi''(u)=1/u\), \(u_0=t_0/T\). On a fixed positive compact interval containing the weight support and all comparable saddles, this is a smooth diffeomorphism, with uniformly bounded derivatives of it and its inverse. The extension of the transformed amplitude by zero is smooth. This includes saddles outside the support, when the leading amplitude is zero. After the amplitude factor \(T^{-1/2}\) and the Jacobian \(dt=T\,du\), the oscillatory integral is a normalized Fresnel transform with prefactor proportional to \(\sqrt T\). Its error relative to evaluation at \(v=0\) is bounded by
\[
C T^{-1}\int_{\mathbb R}|\xi|^2|\widehat A(\xi)|\,d\xi.
\]
Indeed the normalized Fresnel multiplier is an exponential of a purely imaginary constant times \(\xi^2/T\), whose difference from 1 is \(O(|\xi|^2/T)\). Put \(\delta=H/T\). Using \(\|A\|_1\ll1\) and \(\|A^{(4)}\|_1\ll\delta^{-4}\), and splitting at \(\delta^{-1}\), gives \(O(T^{-1}\delta^{-3})=O(T^2/H^3)\) as claimed. The leading Stirling replacement costs \(O(T^{-1})\) per term, and \(|w_T(t_0+\beta_I)-w_T(t_0)|\ll H^{-1}/\log T\). Dropping that shift before forming the principal and nonprincipal sums leaves those sums holomorphic in the shift variables, suitable for Cauchy differentiation.

The bound can be improved for the specified two transitions. Choose the transitions by smooth monotone bump densities with transition support of length \(O(H)\). The transformed weight has two derivative supports of length \(O(\delta)\). The remaining amplitude factors are smooth at scale one, so \(\|A'\|_1\ll1\) and \(\|A^{(4)}\|_1\ll\delta^{-3}\). The estimates \(|\widehat A(\xi)|\ll\min(1,|\xi|^{-1})\) below \(\delta^{-1}\) and \(\ll\delta^{-3}|\xi|^{-4}\) above it give \(\int |\xi|^2|\widehat A(\xi)|d\xi\ll\delta^{-2}\). This yields \(O(T/H^2)\) per comparable term and permits \(\kappa>(1+\theta)/2\), which also ensures \(\kappa>\theta\). The exponents originally proposed in analytic95 already suffice.

## Summing errors and averaging principal residues

For \(m/k\) outside a larger fixed comparable interval, \(|\partial_t[t\log(t/(2\pi x))-t]|\gg1\) over the support. Derivatives of the inverse phase derivative cost successive factors \(T^{-1}\) uniformly, even when \(|\log(t/(2\pi x))|\) grows. Repeated integration by parts gives per term \(O_j(T^{c'+1/2}x^{-c'}H^{-j})\). In summing this bound, the factor \(x^{-c'}\) and the shift prefactors combine back to the original exponents:
\[
\sum_{k\le y}|a_k|k^{\sigma_R-1}
   \sum_{m\ge1}|c_{\alpha,\beta,\eta}(m)|m^{-\sigma_R}
 \ll y^{\sigma_R}\log^{O(1)}T.
\]
The right-hand Dirichlet series consists of majorants for four factors with real part \(>1+c/\log T\). This controls the full tails, including arbitrarily large \(m\). In the comparable range \(m\asymp Tk\) there are \(O(Tk)\) terms per \(k\), and multiplying the uniform divisor bound by \(k^{-1+O(1/\log T)}m^{O(1/\log T)}\) gives total coefficient mass \(O(T^{1+\epsilon}y)\). On the unreflected side \(k=1\) and convolution with the polynomial increases the fixed divisor exponent by at most one. With the prescribed \(\kappa>(2+\theta)/3\), all errors combined are \(O(T^{1-\eta_0})\) for a suitably small fixed \(\eta_0>0\), uniformly on the shift disks after fixing the right contour. The original shift derivatives then cost fixed log powers by Cauchy.

For the principal part, apply the sharp one-sided Perron residue identity before endpoint averaging. It is uniform for the averaged endpoints since all cutoffs \(Uk/(2\pi h)\) at \(U\asymp T\) lie between constant multiples of \(T/y\) and \(Ty\). This keeps their logarithms comparable to \(\log T\) and permits the fixed zero-free-region and short-interval estimates of HLZ §4.3. The endpoint average of its sharp principal sums is the smoothly cut principal sum by linearity. Its residue time factors are averaged by the same \(w_T\). Relative to sharp endpoints their change is \(O(H\log^{O(1)}T)\) uniformly on the shift circles, since the arithmetic outer residue factors have bounded harmonic or fixed divisor harmonic sums. Isolated poles in the separated factors are handled on separated circles and cancel in the full shifted expression.

The remaining nonprincipal sum is exactly the signed sum in analytic95 with weight \(w_T(2\pi m/k)\), retaining the uniform auxiliary-shift dependence or the corresponding differentiated bound. The contour average contributes no additional arithmetic main term. For strict weighted tails, truncate at \(RY\), use the compact-local conclusion and smooth restoration first, and then let \(R\) tend to infinity. The known positive \(D^2\overline{D}^2\) bound controls the remote part by Stieltjes integration uniformly in \(Y\).
