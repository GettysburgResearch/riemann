## Mathematical and normalization review

- Theorem 4.4 gives the **unnormalized** tilted minor as \(G^m Z\det\Gamma\,L_{\rm obl}\). In the CUE calculation \(G=1\), the divided Gram factor comes from dividing the minor by \(\det\Gamma(0)\), and the deterministic root separately supplies \(e^{-H_Nz}\). Ordinary BOGC on \(n=N+J-1\) modes uses the same \(Z=e^{H_Nz^2/4}\). Finite Jacobi gives the director’s quotient with no remaining root factor.
- The exponential symbol and polynomial tilts satisfy their regularity assumptions for all fixed complex \(z\). The Gram hypothesis is verified on the real axis and locally near complex zero. On the real axis \(R=C^*\) and \(C\) is injective by triangular invertibility and injectivity of the polynomial column map. Then \(I-K=T(b)T(b)^*\) is bounded below and the tilted projection is orthogonal, which gives strict positivity of both exact factors. Proposition 4.6 has the independent additional hypothesis on \(B=RP_m\); none of this establishes that hypothesis globally.
- The ambient tail support exclusion, the tilted quadratic trace, its strict sign from the constant deleted mode, and its limiting Riemann integral all check. For the comparison with the polynomial, the same \(e^{JH_Nz}\) is removed from the Gram and complementary determinant. Their constant and linear coefficients agree, so this does not change their quadratic discrepancy. The mass-normalized complementary determinant agrees with the extrapolating polynomial in the supported coefficient window. The entire polynomial may have higher-degree terms beyond the window.
