## Coefficients and aggregate error

Put \(L=\log T\), \(T\ge Y\). Let \(A,B,C\) be the shifts in units of \(1/L\), with \(C\) auxiliary. HLZ weights \(m=gh,n=gk\) by \(1/(ghk)=1/\operatorname{lcm}(m,n)\). The leading zeta factor of (24) is \(Lv/((u+v)(v+w))\). The three tuples \((u,v,w,d)\), with \(d\) the scaled time exponent, are \((A,B,C,0)\), \((-B,-A,C,-A-B)\), \((A,-C,-B,-B-C)\). The reflection exchanges \(A,B\). The normalized time integral is \((T/2\pi)^{d/L}/(1+d/L)=e^d+O(1/L)\), which accounts for the lower endpoint. Differentiation supplies the additional time logarithm in (5).

At \(x=\log p/L\) the local generating function from (6) is \((1-t)/[(1-e^{-ux}t)(1-e^{-wx}t)]\), evaluated at \(t=p^{-1-v/L}\). For a prime in \(k\), the shifted-series ratio has constant term \(e^{-ux}+e^{-wx}-1\) at exponent 1 and a uniform \(O(1/p)\) error. For bounded exponent the full ratio is uniformly bounded on the shift circles. A prime in \(h\) contributes \(e^{-vx}\). In (5) for allocations with totals \(H_h,H_k\) the two exponents are \(-BH_h-AH_k\) and \(-A-B+AH_h+BH_k\), with multipliers \(1/(A+B)\) and its negative. Choose distinct small shift-circle radii and the smallest for \(C\). Rotating all circles simultaneously retains only total degree two in (5) and three in (24) before differentiation. Thus take the cubic and quartic exponential terms respectively. For fixed \(A,B\) on their circles the auxiliary kernel is analytic through its disk. Add the differentiated and reflected terms before extracting the mixed coefficient, avoiding a separate multivariable holomorphy assumption on summands with polar divisors.

## Coincidences, strict tail and cumulant

For \(X_p=p^{-1/2}\cos(\gamma\log p)\), the all-equal part is bounded pointwise by \(\sum_{p>Y^a}p^{-3/2}=o(1)\). The exactly-two-equal part \(3\sum_{p\ne q}X_p^2X_q\) uses \(X_p^2=(2p)^{-1}+\tfrac12\Re(p^{-2\rho})\). Its constant part is \((3/2)H_Y E_T W+o(1)\), where \(H_Y=\sum_{\rm band}1/p\), including an \(o(1)\) correction from adding \(p=q\). For the oscillating part use \(D_2=\sum p^{-2s}\) and polarize \(D_2D\) against 1 and \(D_2\) against \(D\). The lcm weights are \(1/(p^2q)\) for distinct primes and at most \(p^{-2}\) for the same prime. The aggregate remainder and the bounded local factors make this part \(o(1)\). In the raw second moment \(|D|^2/2\) has exact diagonal \(H_Y/2\), while the diagonal from \(\Re D^2/2\) costs \(o(1)\). Consequently the surviving coincidence term cancels after subtracting \(3EW^2\,EW\), where \(EW^2\) is the raw second moment.

Let \(A(t)=\sum_{0<\gamma\le t}|\zeta'(\rho)|^2\), let \(B_j(t)\) be this sum with multiplier \(W_Y^j\), and use \(g(t)=(t^2+1/4)^{-1}\). The strict tail numerator is \(-g(Y)B_j(Y)+\int_Y^\infty-g'(t)B_j(t)\,dt\). The inclusive prefix at \(Y\) makes the tail strict. The denominator uses \(A\) and is asymptotic to \((\log Y)^4/(24\pi Y)\) on the positive side. Normalized boundary and integral magnitudes for a prefix error bounded by \(A(t)o(1)\) are uniformly bounded. Replacing \(\log t\) by \(\log Y\) in the bounded band polynomials costs \(O(1/\log Y)\), as the derivative errors involve one extra integrable \(\log(t/Y)\) against the scale \((t/Y)^{-2}(1+\log(t/Y))^4\). The factor two from reflection cancels. Multiple zeros give zero weight and simplicity is unnecessary.

## Fourth moment and no Gaussian subsequence

The bound \(|W|^4\le |D^2|^2\) uses one quadratic evaluation of length \(Y^{2b}\). Each pair's main term is bounded by a constant times its lcm weight, even for repeated primes. The sum with the nonnegative \(D^2\) coefficients equals the model \(|D|^4\) moment for independent circle Poisson phases of radius \(p^{-1/2}\). Indeed their Fourier coefficients give \((mn)^{-1/2}\prod_p p^{-|v_p(m)-v_p(n)|/2}=1/\operatorname{lcm}(m,n)\). In the model the mean is \(H_Y\), each centered term has size at most \(2/\sqrt p\), and total variance is bounded. Expanding the real and imaginary centered fourth moments gives the bounded sum of individual fourth moments plus the bounded square of the variances. This positive comparison does not assume arithmetic independence. The aggregate remainder and strict-tail transfer yield a uniform centered fourth moment. Thus the first and third moments pass to any weak subsequential limit by truncation, and its mean-zero third moment is the negative integral. A Gaussian, including a degenerate one, cannot have that third moment. The variance limit is positive since its covariance integrand is at least \(-4-4b^4>-5\) and \(\frac12\log(b/a)\ge (b-a)/(2b)\). The obstruction holds with or without centering, and after division by the limiting standard deviation.

## Separate CUE calculation

The HKO Haar derivative-square law uses joint weighting of the matrix and label, with (2.2), (2.3), (2.8) giving the extra \(|1-z|^4\) factor at the root. The projection DPP is the degree-\(0,\ldots,N\) projection minus the rank-two span of evaluation and derivative. For the cubic trace words the full projection is zero off resonance. On resonance the largest step equals the span of the three-step closed path and half the sum of its absolute steps. This cancels the full-projection third cumulant. The deterministic root has no centered term. The independent script sums all nonempty rank-two subsets in the six trace words using the rational Fourier basis \(1,s-1/2\), with Gram matrix \(\operatorname{diag}(1,1/12)\). For each shift segment with visited offsets \(t_i\), use \([-\min t_i,1-\max t_i]\) and integrate the shifted and unshifted basis products. The shifts are read from right to left. On \(x\le y\le z\) only \(z\lessgtr x+y\) further splits the chambers. Both give equality to the arithmetic centered polynomial for the negative cosine statistic, with boundaries by continuity. Uniform polynomial Riemann sums justify the discrete limit.

For CUE the trace-log expansion through order four cancels the word with projection \(P\) between all multipliers. Any remaining cyclic word has at least two crossings between \(P\) and \(I-P\), giving \(O(\|f\|_\infty^2\|Pf(I-P)\|_{\rm HS}^2)\). The squared Hilbert–Schmidt norm is the variance, and the band test has bounded supremum and variance. This yields uniform fourth moments separately on the CUE side.
