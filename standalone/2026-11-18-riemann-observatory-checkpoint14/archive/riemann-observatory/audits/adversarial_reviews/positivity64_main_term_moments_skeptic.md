## Phase and Euler remainder on fixed disks

For each $T\ge400$, use one integer $M=\lceil T/2\rceil$ throughout the block. On the strip $0<\operatorname{Re}s<1$, the principal analytic $\log\Gamma$ on the right half-plane defines
$$
A(s)=\exp\!\left[-\tfrac12(s-1/2)\log\pi
     +\tfrac12\log\Gamma(s/2)-\tfrac12\log\Gamma((1-s)/2)\right].
$$
The gamma arguments have positive real parts, so these logarithms have single holomorphic branches there. At $s=1/2+it$, conjugation gives modulus one and the continuous standard Hardy phase. The functional equation makes $A(s)\zeta(s)$ real on this line. In fact $A^2=\chi(s)^{-1}$ in the standard zeta functional equation and conjugation makes $\overline{A(s)\zeta(s)}=A(s)\zeta(s)$. No principal argument of $\zeta$ or change of phase branch is needed.

For $z=x+iy$, $x>0$, compare the left-endpoint sum of $1/(z+u)$ with its integral on consecutive unit intervals. The sum–integral discrepancy in modulus is bounded by $\int_0^\infty |z+u|^{-2}du\le\pi/(2|y|)$. The digamma limit then gives $|\operatorname{Re}\psi(z)-\log|z||\le\pi/(2|y|)$. Also $0\le\log|z|-\log|y|\le x^2/(2y^2)$. Thus the $\sigma$-derivative of $\log|A(\sigma+it)|^2$, obtained by averaging the real parts of the two digamma values and subtracting $\log\pi$, differs from $\log(t/(2\pi))$ by at most $\pi/t+9/(32t^2)$ on $1/4\le\sigma\le3/4$. For $0.89T\le t\le2.11T$ this differs from $\log T$ by less than two in total. Integrating from the critical line gives the bound $(5/3)T^{\sigma-1/2}$. On the real block $0.9T\le t\le2.1T$, the same estimate at $x=1/4$ in $\theta'=(\operatorname{Re}\psi(1/4+it/2)-\log\pi)/2$ gives $|\theta'-(\log M)/2|<0.67$. The maximum possible absolute logarithmic offset occurs at the smaller ratio $t/(2\pi M)$, since this ratio stays below one throughout the block. In the trigamma series, comparison with the decreasing integral gives $\sum_{n\ge0}|n+1/4+it/2|^{-2}\le4/t^2+\pi/t$ and hence $|\theta''|\le1/t^2+\pi/(4t)\le1/T$.

The fixed Euler summation identity for $\zeta-P$ holds first for $\operatorname{Re}s>1$ and continues to the working strip, since the bounded sawtooth integral converges there. The sign of its $-\tfrac12 M^{-s}$ endpoint is fixed by including the term at the integer $M$ in $P$. For each Abel-damped sawtooth mode, set $q(x)=x^{-\sigma-1}$ and $\phi(x)=\pm2\pi nx-t\log x$. Then $|\phi'|\ge2n$ follows from $t/M\le4.22$ and $2\pi-4.22>2$. One integration by parts gives
$$
\left|\int_M^\infty q e^{i\phi}\,dx\right|
\le M^{-\sigma-1}\left[\frac1n+
      \frac{t/M}{4n^2(\sigma+2)}\right]
\le\frac{3}{2n}M^{-\sigma-1}.
$$
The first term includes both the endpoint and the integral of $|q'|$. The second comes from $|\phi''|=t/x^2$. Opposite signs have coefficients of modulus $1/(2\pi n)$ each. Thus the integrated Fourier series is absolutely summable, with total bound $3M^{-\sigma-1}/\pi$. The Poisson-damped sawtooth is uniformly bounded by the original essential supremum and approaches the sawtooth almost everywhere, so this majorant passes to its actual integral. Adding the other Euler terms uses $|s-1|\ge t$, $M/t<0.57$ and $|s|/M<4.23$, giving $|\zeta-P|\le6M^{-\sigma}$.

A $w$-disk of radius $1/4$ about any $t$ in the real block maps under $s=1/2+iw$ into the enlarged rectangle above. It lies in a holomorphic domain for $A(\zeta-P)$. The squared bound is $60M^{-1}(T/M^2)^{\sigma-1/2}$, and $1/T\le T/M^2\le1$. This gives $120T^{-3/4}$ uniformly on the disk. By Cauchy's formula and the real interval length $1.2T$, its derivative norms are at most $12 j!4^jT^{1/8}$. This use of a fixed cutoff is essential to the verified remainder. The real-axis Gabcke bound is for $R_0$ after inclusion of $C_0$. The explicit AFE in Dhiman–Kadiri–Quesada-Herrera has height-dependent endpoints, and its second formula with half-integral cutoffs and $2\pi xy=|t|$ does not cover a complex neighborhood. I also checked that Dona–Helfgott–Zuniga Alterman Theorem 4.3 has the stated $5.45623/(2\sigma-1)$ upper error term away from the critical line and a different formula at $\sigma=1/2$. These do not supply a uniform shrinking-strip replacement with the desired numerical constants. ## Variable gaps and centered moments

I inspected Montgomery–Vaughan, *Multiplicative Number Theory II*, printed pp. 426–427. Theorem G.16 (G.27) is the bilinear inequality for arbitrary complex coefficients and local gaps, with $3\pi/2$ times the product of two weighted norms. Its (G.28) uses conjugate coefficients. For $\lambda_n=\log n$, the next-neighbor gap is $\int_n^{n+1}x^{-1}dx\ge1/(n+1)$. The preceding gap, when present, is at least $1/n$. As the frequencies are strictly increasing these are sufficient for the minimum gap bound including endpoints. Integrating the off-diagonal exponential terms gives the difference of two Hilbert forms, each with $3\pi/2$ and endpoint phases of modulus one. The coefficient $3\pi$ in the integrated bound is correct. [Rendered primary author text, p. 427](https://personal.science.psu.edu/rcv4/571s25/montgomery-vaughanII.pdf#page=439)

For $b_M=(\log M)/2$, the diagonal function $x^{-1}(\log x-b_M)^{2j}$ integrates exactly to $b_M^{2j}\log M/(2j+1)$ on $[1,M]$. Left of $\sqrt M$ it decreases. To the right it increases until $\log x-b_M=2j$ and then decreases, with the right side wholly decreasing for $j=0$. Compare the integer sums with unit integrals in the direction appropriate for each monotonic branch. The left side costs at most its first value. The right side costs at most two sampled terms at the boundary or peak, and each is at most $b_M^{2j}/\sqrt M$. This keeps the estimate valid for nonintegral $\sqrt M$. In the Hilbert correction, splitting $(n+1)/n=1+1/n$ gives $3\pi(M b_M^{2j}+S_j)$. The numerical constant $6.4$ follows after taking $M/T\le0.5025$, $\log M\le\log T$, $1+2/\sqrt M\le1.15$ and bounding $(\log T+1.15)/T$ by its value at 400.

On the real line $|A|=1$. Differentiating the product directly gives $iA(\beta Q_0-Q_1)$ for the first derivative and $A[-\beta^2Q_0+2\beta Q_1-Q_2+i\theta''Q_0]$ for the second, with $\beta=\theta'-b_M$. Combining the derivative remainder bounds and these three polynomial norm bounds by Minkowski gives the stated $a_0,a_1,a_2$ after normalizing by $\sqrt T(\log T)^{j+1/2}$. Every ingredient in the normalized constants decreases with the base height $V$, so using $K(V)$ for later blocks is justified.

## Zero sampling, the tail and reproducibility

The rescaled cubic cutoff is continuously differentiable at the four joins. Its first derivative is absolutely continuous and the second derivative is bounded almost everywhere by $600/T^2$. The cutoff and its first derivative vanish at both outer endpoints, and it equals one with zero derivative at both inner endpoints. For $f=\varphi Z$, choose one stationary point between successive distinct zeros in the block by Rolle, and use the outer endpoints as the first and last stationary points. Integrating the variation of $f'^2$ along each of the rises and falls gives at least $2\sum |Z'(\gamma)|^2$ even for a one-zero block. The total variation is $2\int |f'f''|$. At a critical-line zero, the phase-derivative term vanishes and $|Z'(\gamma)|=|\zeta'(1/2+i\gamma)|$. A multiple zero contributes zero, so using distinct roots loses no contribution. The product-rule norms and reflection supply the two factors in $K(V)$ and the overall $12=2\cdot6$, since $|2^{1-\rho}-1|^2\le3+2\sqrt2<6$.

Each disjoint block $(T,2T]$ contributes at most $K(V)\log^4 T/T$, using $|\rho|^2>T^2$. After eight direct blocks, the residual includes its initial block and uses $K(2^8Y)$ uniformly. Expanding the fourth power and summing $\sum_{\ell\ge0}2^{-\ell}\ell^j$ for $0\le j\le4$ gives $2,2,6,26,150$ and reproduces the exact polynomial $B$. The large-$Y$ coefficient is $2\cdot12\sqrt{0.1\cdot0.015}=28.8/\sqrt{960}$. This is an asymptotic for the bound, distinct from a proposed asymptotic for the series.
