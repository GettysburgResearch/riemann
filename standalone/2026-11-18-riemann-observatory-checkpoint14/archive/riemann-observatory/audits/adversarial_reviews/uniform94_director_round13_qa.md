## Tightness scope

1. The modulus constant is uniform in grids and laws. Tightness of the interpolation sequence also needs tight initial values for that sequence. A literal common tightness assertion over all permitted sequences would need a common bound on the initial laws. For example, every deterministic constant sequence \(Z_n\equiv c\) satisfies the increment assumptions and has a tight family for its own \(c\), but their union as \(c\) varies is not tight. Suggested wording is “The modulus estimate is uniform over the permitted grids and increment laws. With tight initial values, the parameter condition guarantees tightness for each such sequence.”
2. The continuous-clock composition clause should follow the step-to-interpolant transfer, unless the separately specified argument directly proves a composition discrepancy. Uniform convergence of clocks to a continuous clock alone does not control the discrepancy between steps and polygons. For example, take the fixed grid \(\{0,1\}\) with values 0 and 1 and identity clocks. The fine and coarse grid bounds hold for \(C=A_n=1\) and every positive \(\epsilon_n\to0\), but the identity clocks leave both the polygon \(Z(t)=t\) and the terminally jumping step unchanged. Suggested wording is “The step-to-interpolant transfer uses vanishing maximum mesh or maximum adjacent jump. A further deterministic clock composition uses the polygonal modulus at the clock discrepancy and continuity of the limiting clock.”
