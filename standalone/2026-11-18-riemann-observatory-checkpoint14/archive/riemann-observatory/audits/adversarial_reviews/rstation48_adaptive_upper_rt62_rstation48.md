## Exact claim and assumptions

The upper bound is for the infimum over actual complex Dirichlet polynomials \(A(s)=\sum_{n\le N}a_n n^{-s}\) in the norm \(\|1-\zeta A\|_*^2=(2\pi)^{-1}\int |1-\zeta A|^2\,dt/(1/4+t^2)\). The anchored variant uses \(A-A(1)\). The theorem assumes RH, allowing arbitrary positive finite multiplicities. Together with Burnol's lower bound it yields the limiting constant \(\sum_{\rho\ {\rm distinct}}m_\rho^2/|\rho|^2\). It changes the coefficients, and has no implication here for a prescribed shifted or Möbius array. Analytic inputs are the RH zeta and fixed-right-line reciprocal subpower bounds (with the usual functional-equation factor to the left), the Hadamard logarithmic derivative, the functional equation, and the RH fixed-interval zero-count estimate. I checked the latter's source locator, Balazard–de Roton, [arXiv:0812.1689](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0812.1689), Proposition 8. Its error is \(o(\log t)\) for each fixed interval size. Uniformity as the interval size tends to zero with height is unnecessary.

## Cluster terms without an inverse Gram matrix

Fix the source's \(D=e=10^{-4}\), \(\delta=10^{-6}\). Here \(e\) is a small exponent. For each fixed length interval, the nonnegative kernel \(\log(1+\eta^2/(t-\gamma)^2)\) has mass \(2\pi\eta\) in \(t\). By the unit local zero count the near contribution to its mean on an interval of length \(\ell\) is \(O((\eta/\ell)\log t)\), and the far contribution is \(O(\eta^2\log t)\). First choose fixed small \(\eta\), then choose a good height with this kernel sum small. The horizontal Hadamard identity (12) controls \(1/\zeta\) between the line and \(\Re s=1/2+\eta\), and the fixed-right-line RH estimate and functional equation handle the remaining bounded strips. This proves the claimed fixed-mesh good heights. Each cluster has width at most \(4D/3\), its center is uniformly separated from the other centers, and the cited zero-count bound gives \(k_C\le D\log T_C\) at large height, with \(k_C\) counting multiplicity. The mesh stays fixed throughout the limits. Finitely many low rectangles have their own fixed constants.

The coefficients of the grouped proper rational principal part \(R_C\) are controlled by (32)–(34). To check the orientation of (33), expand \(1/(s-z)\) successively using the ordered poles backwards, from \(\rho_k\) to \(\rho_1\); the coefficient of \(1/\prod_{i=j+1}^k(s-\rho_i)\) is \(\prod_{i=j+2}^k(z-\rho_i)\) under \((2\pi i)^{-1}\oint F(z)\,dz\). The remaining contour numerator contains the full product of the pole factors in \(z\) and integrates to zero for \(s\) outside the contour. This derivation also works with repeated poles. A term of denominator degree \(m=k-j\) then costs \(\ll T_C^e(2D)^m\) by contour length less than \(4D\) and maximum pole distance \(2D\). This does not divide by a separation or individual \(\zeta'(\rho)\).

The delayed profile \(w_k\) of (22)–(23) equals \(1-u\) for \(u\le b\), \(b=1/4\), and has a zero of order \(k\) at \(1\). Its piecewise polynomial coefficients have exponential bounds as in (27). For denominator factors \(P_j(s)=\prod_{r=1}^m(s-i\theta_r)\) translated to the critical line, the causal inverse transform is a convolution of exponential functions, with each subproduct inverse bounded by the simplex formula. Applying \(\prod_r(D-i\theta_r)\) to the tapered time kernel yields a delta at zero plus the terms indexed by differentiated subsets. No deltas occur at either nonzero join because the taper is \(C^{k-1}\) and \(m\le k\). The \(j\)-subset term is \(L^{-1} B_j(x/L)\mathbb E e^{ix\Theta_j}\), supported on \([0,L]\). Summing the coefficient and total variation bounds of \(B_j\) gives Lemma 3's \(L^2\) bound \(k^2B_b^{2k}/L\) and the far bound \(O(k^2B_b^k/(Ld))\), with \(B_b=200\). For the far bound the frequencies \(\Theta_j\) lie in the real hull of the poles, so integration by parts costs \(1/(Ld)\) uniformly at all collisions. The analytic quotient from \(1/P_j-T_{L,w_k}(1/P_j)\) to this normalized error contributes a further \(d^{-m}\) outside the hull.

The source's use of the zero-removed zeta factor in (35) is a Cauchy estimate, not an assumption on that factor. Dividing \(\zeta(s)\) by the full cluster product is the order-\(k\) Hermite divided difference at the \(k\) cluster nodes and \(s\), with multiplicity. The convex simplex arguments stay on the critical line, and Cauchy's formula on radius \(D\) bounds the \(k\)-th derivative by \(k!D^{-k}T_C^{D+e}\), uniformly for \(|t-\tau_C|\le5D\). The simplex volume cancels \(k!\). Restoring the unused \(k-m\) factors costs at most \((6D)^{k-m}\). Combining with the Newton coefficient bound cancels every inverse power of \(D\) from this near estimate and leaves at worst \(6^k\) before applying the profile bound. On the far region, \(d>13D/3\) and the Newton weights give a convergent sum in \(m\) with ratio \(2D/d<1/2\), producing square spatial decay for the whole cluster error. These are the near/far estimates (36)–(40) with the small powers recorded there.

The near windows overlap a bounded number of times, while \(\sum_C(1+|t-\tau_C|)^{-2}\) is bounded by uniform separation of the ordinates of the centers. The weighted convolution estimate uses the integrability of both factors and \(1<p<2\). It follows that the square of the sum of errors over every set of corrected clusters is bounded by
\[
 (C/L)\sum_C T_C^{-2+1/100}.
\]
Finite sets suffice first; the same estimate on differences gives Cauchy convergence and a uniform tail \(O(Y^{-99/100}/L)\) in squared norm. This establishes (41) without an arithmetic Gram inverse, private spacing conjecture, or uniform upper bound on \(k_C\).

## Complete high contours and arithmetic realization

For an independent check of the first-order decomposition, one can use the retained-edge variant of the same manuscript's §9 with \(W=1\). Fix \(a_0=1/2-\delta\) and use only the strip between \(a_0\) and a large right edge. The derivative Cauchy formula gives \(F'=\sum_C R_C'-J_0\), where \(J_0\) is the integral of \(F(z)/(z-s)^2\) over the upward left line. The sign follows because that boundary is traversed downwards when closing to the right. The grouped residues have absolutely summable tails on fixed compact \(s\)-sets. Perron shifted from \(2\) to \(a_0\) gives \(LV_{N,1}=LF+F'+\sum_C B_C+J_N\), where \(J_N\) contains the additional factor \(e^{L(z-s)}\). Substitution gives (45) with \(\zeta(J_0-J_N)/L\) in place of the trivial-pole contribution. On the critical line the multiplier \(e^{L(z-s)}\) has absolute value \(N^{-\delta}\). Convolution with \((\delta^2+(t-y)^2)^{-1}\) and the \(t^e\) growth bounds give the left term \(O(L^{-1})\) in the weighted norm, exactly as (97). Thus the main proof does not need an unproved entire remainder cancellation or a far-left interchange. The displayed §2.3 derivation instead subtracts the values at \(0,-1\), controls heights first, uses an odd negative left edge with factorial suppression, and removes the derivative's constant as \(\Re s\to+\infty\). Its sign and contour orders are consistent with the retained-edge check.

One may not bound the uncorrected high clusters by the delayed-profile tail estimate. The source instead sums the exact complete contour expression (46) over all exterior rectangles, including empty ones, before estimating. Shared edges cancel. The high remainder then consists of the true exterior indicator, the two vertical tails, and the horizontal edges at \(\pm h\), with \(h=H+O(D)\). The remote outer horizontal edges vanish at fixed \(N,s\) as the height goes to infinity. The vertical kernel is integrable with positive fixed \(\delta\), so applying Cauchy–Schwarz against that kernel and the weighted convolution estimate gives \(N^{2\delta}H^{-1+4e}/L^2\). On a horizontal edge the factor \(e^{L(z-s)}-1\) cancels a denominator factor, leaving the integrable squared logarithmic kernel \([2\operatorname{arsinh}(\delta/|t-h|)]^2\). The result is \(N^{2\delta}H^{-2+4e}\). The indicator contributes \(O(H^{-1})\). This verifies (49) with no evaluation-dependent contour moves, ungrouped near-edge singularities, or omission of the indicator.

For \(H=N^{1/20}\) and \(K=N^{3/20}\) the growing correction is first a continuous integral but is then realized by the discrete sum (54), not added as an arbitrary analytic function. All coefficients at \(n\le N^b\) vanish for this growing correction. The contour bound on the inverse Laplace kernel \(h_C\) gives a collective \(H^{1+e}y^{1/2+\delta}\) bound, and its logarithmic derivative costs one power of \(H\). The derivative bound on the delayed profiles costs less than that power. After multiplying by \(y^{-s-1}\), the quadrature derivative is \(O(H^{2+e}(1+|t|)y^{-2+\delta})\) on \(\Re s=1/2\). Its integral bounds the discrete error since the integrand extends continuously by zero at both ends. The low-frequency weighted error at \(|t|\le K\) and the absolute-value estimate above \(K\) give (59) and (60). The corresponding powers \(-0.1499595\) and \(-0.049958\) are negative, as is the largest exponent \(-0.049978\) in (49). Multiplication by \(L\) still sends all these squared errors to zero. The anchor change from the growing correction is \(O(N^{-0.07499475})\) by the same contour bound at \(s=1\). This is a genuine length-\(N\) construction with no coefficient-size restriction other than the displayed proved bounds and no reliance on the discrete phase-nonaliasing lemma for a different polynomial model.

## Fixed corrections and order of limits

For a fixed zero of multiplicity \(m\), the leading Laurent term cancels its own local analytic factor and yields the model (69). Lower Laurent orders carry an extra factor \((s-\rho)^\ell\), \(\ell\ge1\); their squared norms on a fixed neighborhood are \(O(L^{-2})\) by (71). Around other fixed zeros the corresponding pole term is outside its own neighborhood and gives \(O(L^{-1})\) in norm by the far profile bound. Distinct fixed zeros may be separated into disjoint neighborhoods with constants depending on the chosen set. The scaling \(v=L(t-\gamma)\), decay \(O((1+|v|)^{-1})\), and Plancherel give \(c_m(w)/|\rho|^2\) as the normalized leading contribution. For \(w=(1-u)^m\), integrating by parts against the first \(m\) shifted Legendre polynomials shows that the derivative in (70) is their degree-\((m-1)\) projection with squared norm \(m^2\). Thus the finite corrections only replace the leading Laurent profile at each fixed zero, leaving lower orders negligible.

The fixed corrections can start before the delayed prefix and require the separate quadrature lemma (63)–(68). For a fixed piecewise polynomial \(q\) with \(q(0)=q(1)=0\), on the first piece its monomial coefficients in \(x^{m-1}q(x/L)\) cost \(O(L^{-1})\) because \(q(0)=0\). On every later piece the regular-zeta Euler-summation term cancels between its two cutoffs. The noninteger-cutoff fractional-part term in (65) has the correct sign and same integrable remainder order. Derivatives are legitimate on \(\Re(s-\rho)=0\) by absolute convergence of the remainder integral. The residual regular terms have polylogarithmic growth and coefficient \(O(L^{-1})\). The remaining quadrature error is polynomially small after splitting the integral at \(N^{\alpha_1/2}\), with \(\alpha_1>0\) the first fixed positive knot or endpoint. At \(s=1\) the summability of \(n^{-3/2}(\log n)^m\) gives \(O(L^{-1})\) in the anchor. This step uses fixed Laurent coefficients, so arbitrarily bad individual derivatives at large heights do not enter any uniform constants.

For a fixed finite union \(\mathcal F\) of whole clusters, take \(N\to\infty\) with that set held fixed. The growing collection extends past it eventually. Given a larger fixed height \(Y\), local limits apply at the finitely many clusters below \(Y\), and (41) controls the normalized norm of the remaining delayed-profile errors uniformly in \(N\) by \(O(Y^{-99/200})\). This allows \(Y\to\infty\) after taking the \(N\)-limit and proves (75). Exhausting \(\mathcal F\) afterwards removes the summable surplus above \(m_\rho^2\). The argument neither assumes uniform control of the finite-profile repair over a growing set nor needs a single predetermined diagonal sequence to prove the infimum's upper limit. The limiting sum with \(m_\rho^2\) is finite using \(m_\rho\ll\log(2+|\gamma|)\) and the local count. The baseline value at \(1\) is \(O(L^{-1})\) by (76), or by Perron at \(1\) with \(F(1)=0,F'(1)=1\) in the retained-edge check. Thus subtracting \(A(1)\) changes the norm by \(O_{\mathcal F}(L^{-1})\), which is negligible against the established \(O_{\mathcal F}(L^{-1/2})\) norm. This proves the anchored upper bound before applying Burnol's lower bound.

## Comparison and audit disposition

The Observatory [fixed-size divided-difference theorem](../../proposals/independent_campaigns/multicluster_uniform.md) and [finite block extension](../../proposals/independent_campaigns/multicluster_blocks.md) concern exact interpolation optima in an ordinary polynomial model. They do not provide uniform large-multiplicity arithmetic coefficients, and global Riesz-basis or density assumptions cannot be imported for zeta zeros with increasing ordinate density. The manuscript bypasses this issue by constructing tapered rational errors rather than optimizing each growing cluster and inverting its Gram matrix. Its critical new arithmetic steps are the grouped Newton/Cauchy coefficient estimate, the separated-center *weighted* summation despite large within-cluster counts, the complete high strip, and delayed collective discretization. None requires separated individual zeros. The final fixed local \(m^2\) cost is consistent with the exact polynomial collision transition, but the latter alone supplies no arithmetic upper construction.

I found the stated Theorem 1 upper-bound argument reproducible from the specified classical inputs. The restrictive prefix lower bound in §8.1 additionally imports the compact zero-jet argument from rStation paper 47, with adjusted cutoff parameters. The finite-off-line extension in §9 additionally imports the discrete projection and lower bounds from rStation paper 1 v2. Those separate cited results were not independently reconstructed here. The finite numerical controls in §10 are not part of the infinite argument. This report is scoped evidence for director adjudication of the selected preprint and makes no publication, novelty, review, or unconditional RH claim.
