## Elementary bounds and the equivalence

For integers n≥1, use the finite integral. On [1,n], floor(t) is the count of 1≤r≤t, so
```text
integral_1^n floor(t)/t² dt = sum_(r=1)^n (1/r−1/n) = H_n−1.
H_n = log n + 1 − integral_1^n {t}/t² dt
    = log n + γ + integral_n^infinity {t}/t² dt.
```
The first integral on the printed v2 p. 6 has upper limit ∞ and the summand integral prints limits 1 to ∞. The left integral diverges. The corrected limits are 1 to n for the outer integral and r to n for the integral in each summand. The immediate (3.4) uses the correct outer limit and July p. 7 displays both corrected limits. The tail δ_n is strictly between 0 and 1/n. Its vanishing identifies γ with the standard limit. Since H_n≥log(n+1), log H_n≥log log n>0 at n≥3. Multiplying by exp(H_n)>e^γ n gives Lemma 3.1. The proof deliberately avoids log log 1 and does not multiply inequalities through a negative log log at n=2. [V2 pp. 6–7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0008177v2#page=6) · [July p. 7](https://websites.umich.edu/lagarias/doc/elementaryrh.pdf#page=7)

For the v2 upper bound write x=log n and d=log(1+1/n). From (3.4), H_n≤x+1. The potentially sensitive second inequality in (3.8) follows from
```text
log(1+1/x) ≤ 1/x − 1/[2x(x+1)] ≤ 1/(x+d).
```
For the first step, integrate 1/(1+u)≤1−u/(1+1/x) through 1/x. The second is equivalent to x(1−2d)≥d and follows for n≥3 from x>1, d<1/3. Also exp(δ_n)≤1+2/n, since exp(u)≤1+2u on [0,1] by the convex chord and e<3. The product thus exceeds the main term by at most e^γ n/log(n+1)+2e^γ(log log n+1). One may bound e^γ<2 using γ<H_5−log 5, H_5=137/60, and the seven-term positive atanh lower sum at 9/11 for log 10, which is rationally greater than 137/60. For x≥1, log x+1≤x and x²≤e^x by minimizing e^x/x². This gives at most 6n/log n for the product correction, in particular on the stated n≥10 range. Finally x+1≤e^x/x because e^x−x²−x≥1−x²/2+x³/6≥1/3 for x≥0. Thus H_n≤n/log n on the claimed n≥20 range. This establishes (3.7) with room in the intermediate bounds. [V2 Lemma 3.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0008177v2#page=7)

The July proof sharpens this to 4n/log n for n≥3. H_n−log(n+1)=integral_1^(n+1)(1/floor(t)−1/t)dt increases to γ. Therefore exp(H_n)≤e^γ(n+1). The simpler log bound is log H_n≤log x+1/x. To check its next step, e^x/2−x log x−1 is positive with positive derivative at x=log 3 and positive second derivative thereafter. For the initial signs, 1<log 3<1.1 and log log 3<0.1 suffice. The cross term is at most e^γ n/(2x). Adding H_n≤n/x and using 1+3e^γ/2<4 proves the sharper bound. [July Lemma 3.2](https://websites.umich.edu/lagarias/doc/elementaryrh.pdf#page=8)

The original Robin 1984 scan, printed p. 188 Theorem 1 and p. 204 §3 Proposition 3, gives strict σ(n)<e^γ n log log n for every integer n≥5041 under RH. V2 Proposition 3.1 only needs the weak version. Under ¬RH, Robin §4 pp. 204–205 gives σ(n)/(n log log n)=e^γ(1+Ω±((log n)^(-b))) on CA integers for each b in (1−Θ,1/2), where Θ is the supremum of real parts of nontrivial zeros. Choosing a zero ρ with Re ρ>1/2 and 1−Re ρ<b<1/2 meets those exact hypotheses. The positive side gives some fixed C>0 on an unbounded subsequence. Its excess over the baseline divided by 7n/log n grows as (C/7)(log n)^(1−b)log log n. The same domination works for the July constant 4. This establishes the converse without an effective first counterexample. The accepted Robin input includes the Nicolas/Landau ancestry as external. I checked the primary formulations and the normalization needed here. [Original Robin scan, pp. 188, 204–205](https://web.archive.org/web/20180205020128id_/http://zakuski.utsa.edu/~jagy/Robin_1984.pdf) · [Archive Robin scope](../robin_round2.md)

Under RH, Lemma 3.1 and positive H_n complete the proof for n≥5041. For n=1 direct evaluation gives 1 on both sides. I used independent divisor pairing and 192-bit Arb harmonic/log/exp enclosures through n=5041. Every n=2..5041 is strictly positive. The smallest gap is at n=2, greater than 0.3171685434. At n=5040 the gap is greater than 492.3187310. At n=5041 the gap is greater than 14727.4481969. This is the closed finite gap needed by the theorem, with an extra boundary check. It is distinct from the archive's later finite range through 720720. The July Kaneko note also follows: the exponential product alone is strictly above σ(n) for n=61..5041 by the replay and for n≥5041 under RH by Robin and Lemma 3.1. It fails at n=60 by a gap less than −1.70318. The reverse implication follows immediately from the same eventual oscillation comparison. ## Extremal results, table, and exact scope of the local issues

The mean follows from summing divisors over multiples. The resulting sum of floor(x/k)(floor(x/k)+1)/2 gives ζ(2)x²/2+O(x log x). In particular the average-order function is ζ(2)n, and the mean of σ up to n is ζ(2)n/2+O(log n). For Gronwall's upper bound, split the prime divisors at log n. The large-prime factor contributes exp(O(1/log log n)), and Mertens for the inverse factor over the small primes gives e^γ log log n. For the lower bound take the least common multiple of 1,...,floor x. Its log is ψ(x) and hence log log n=log x+O(1). Its finite local Euler factors differ from the full inverse Mertens product by 1+o(1), since the low p omissions are bounded by 1/x per p≤√x and the high p omission sum is bounded by the tail of sum 1/p². This gives the claimed limsup. [V2 p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0008177v2#page=3)

The v2 and July p. 3 Mertens display prints ∏_(p≤x)(1−1/p)∼e^γ/log x. The ordinary product has e^(−γ)/log x. Both p. 3 displays also print the strict unconditional (2.2) with 0.6482 for every n≥3. In the original Robin Theorem 2, the coefficient is the sharp k=0.6482… with equality at n=12. The abstract's 0.6483 is a rounded strict bound. At n=12, σ(12)=28, and the equality-forced coefficient (7/3−e^γ log log 12)log log 12 lies in the verified strict interval (0.64821364942179, 0.64821364942181). This is an explicit counterexample to the literal coefficient 0.6482. The p. 3 error envelopes compare in the asserted direction asymptotically, since n/log n=o(n/log log n). A uniform pointwise comparison of the two displayed upper envelopes on their starting range does not follow. Neither p. 3 assertion is used in §3. [V2 (2.2)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0008177v2#page=3) · [July (2.2)](https://websites.umich.edu/lagarias/doc/elementaryrh.pdf#page=3) · [Robin Theorem 2](https://web.archive.org/web/20180205020128id_/http://zakuski.utsa.edu/~jagy/Robin_1984.pdf#page=4)

For a fixed ε>0 the move from exponent a−1 to a of prime p improves the normalized multiplicative objective precisely below ε_(p,a)=log(1+1/(p+⋯+p^a))/log p. At equality both neighboring exponents maximize. Solving p^(a+1)≤(p^(1+ε)−1)/(p^ε−1) gives (2.3), which takes the larger tied exponent. The earlier strict-smaller-integer convention picks the smaller endpoint at a fixed tie. Under the later nonstrict convention the CA set includes all tied choices. The two endpoint sequences are unchanged for generic ε. Monotonicity in p and a gives initial prime support and nonincreasing exponents. Global maxima exist since σ(n)/n≤H_n, and H_n/n^ε→0. A CA maximizer n has σ(n)/n≥(n/k)^ε σ(k)/k>σ(k)/k for k<n. There are infinitely many maximizers as ε↓0, since σ(n)/n is unbounded by Gronwall and any fixed finite list can be beaten for sufficiently small ε.

**Fixed-parameter footnote boundary.** At ε=log(4/3)/log 3, the only nontrivial tied prime is 3. For p=2 the first threshold is strictly above ε and the second is strictly below it. Indeed log(7/6)/log 2 < 1/4 < ε < 1/3 < log(3/2)/log 2, by the rational inequalities (7/6)^4<2, (4/3)^4>3, (4/3)^3<3 and (3/2)^3>2. For p≥5 the first threshold is smaller by monotonicity. Thus the only maximizers are 2 and 6. Formula (2.3) gives exponent 1 at p=3 since its quotient is exactly (3·4/3−1)/(4/3−1)=9. It selects 6, while the strict condition relative to every smaller integer rejects 6 and selects 2. The trivial tie at ε=log(3/2)/log 2 gives the same distinction for 1 and 2. Accordingly the footnote is valid as a description of the aggregate set as ε varies. Each smaller endpoint occurs at a nearby larger generic ε, and each larger endpoint at a nearby smaller generic ε. It does not hold as an identification of the maximizer for each fixed parameter including ties. This is a local endpoint qualification and leaves the RH equivalence and generic table unchanged.

I ordered all relevant ε_(p,a) by disjoint Arb intervals. The computation includes primes through the first primorial above 10¹⁸ and prime exponents through the first p^a>10¹⁸. Any excluded increment is later than one already forcing a crossing of the cap, by monotonicity. It reproduces all 22 nontrivial CA integers and their factorizations in Table 1, omitting the trivial maximizer 1 as the table does. The displayed abundancy ratios agree with exact truncation to three decimals, rather than nearest rounding. The next crossing is 9,200,527,969,062,830,400. This needs no conjecture on all future ties. Erdős–Nicolas printed pp. 69–71 give the one/two/four possibilities, with four precisely when two distinct prime marginal thresholds coincide. Their reasoning permits this possibility, as does the paper under audit. [V2 Table 1 and (2.3)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0008177v2#page=4) · [Erdős–Nicolas primary text](https://www.numdam.org/item/BSMF_1975__103__65_0.pdf) · [Reproducible threshold check](../../scripts/release64_lagarias_whole_checks.py)

The four-exponentials discussion needs distinct primes p,q, intended in its use of independent log p,log q. The literal p=q case already fails with p=q=2 and exponent log 3/log 2. For distinct primes a rational noninteger exponent is excluded by valuations. For an irrational real exponent, the conjectural four-exponentials input is exactly the two rationally independent pairs (log p,log q) and (1,x). The discussion of prime ratios remains conditional on the stated noncoincidence conjecture. None of these conjectural statements enters Theorem 1.1. [V2 p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0008177v2#page=5)

The exceptional set in v2 p. 6 is of density zero even without RH. For n≥sqrt x, a counterexample requires σ(n)/n at least e^γ log log n, comparable to log log x. Yet sum_(n≤x) σ(n)/n≤ζ(2)x by switching divisors. Counting such n and adding the initial sqrt x terms gives O(x/log log x). If any Lagarias counterexample exists, the already established forward implication gives ¬RH, and the positive Robin oscillation on CA numbers supplies infinitely many CA counterexamples by the same quantitative comparison. This establishes the statement of existence of a CA exception. It supplies no first-counterexample CA restriction. [V2 pp. 4, 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0008177v2#page=4)
