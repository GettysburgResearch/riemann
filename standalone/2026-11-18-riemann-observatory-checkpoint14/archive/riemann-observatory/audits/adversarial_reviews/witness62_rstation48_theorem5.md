## Compact tests for an arbitrary bounded sequence

Use the physical norm \(L^2([1,\infty),dx/x^2)\) and \(u=\sum_{n=2}^N a_n\rho_n\), where \(\rho_n=\lfloor x/n\rfloor-\lfloor x\rfloor/n\). Including the effective \(n=1\) coefficient in the floor representation, jumps recover coefficients by finite Möbius inversion. Thus, as in RST047 Lemma 3, \(\sum_{n=2}^N|a_n|\le N\sqrt{4N+1}\|u\|_{[1,N+1)}\). If the anchored residual \(e_N=1-u\) has squared norm \(O(L^{-1})\), with \(L=\log N\), then this first-\(N\)-cell norm of \(u\) is bounded. The \(O(N^{3/2})\) coefficient control is therefore valid for any near-minimizing sequence, even without its own coefficient bounds or the minimizer's projection inequality.

Set \(k=\lceil L\rceil\), \(A=N\), \(R=\lceil 4NL\rceil\), \(B_k(z)=e^{-z}z^k/\Gamma(k)\) and \(W_{k,s}(z)=\chi_k(z)+B_k(z)/s\). The omitted kernel \(h=1-W_{k,s}\) has zero constant Fourier integral because \(h(z)z^{-s-1}=-(1/s)[(1-\chi_k(z))z^{-s}]'\) and both endpoints vanish in \(0<\Re s<1\). For nonzero angular frequency \(\omega\), first evaluate \(H(\omega)=\int_0^\infty x^{-s-1}h(x/N)e^{i\omega x}dx\) with positive damping \(\Re\lambda>0\), then take \(\lambda\to-i\omega\). Subtracting the first \(k\) terms of \((1-q)^s\), with \(q=(1+\lambda N)^{-1}\), yields RST047 (12), including the correction \(-(k/s)a_kq^k\), where \(a_k=(-s)_k/k!\). Uniform ratio asymptotics on a fixed compact \(s\)-set give
\[
 |H(\omega)|\ll_K k^{-\sigma}|\omega|^\sigma(1+N^2\omega^2)^{-k/2},\qquad \sigma=\Re s,\quad N|\omega|\ge1 .
\]
The Fourier coefficients of \(\rho_n\) at nonzero frequencies \(2\pi a/n\) are \(1/(2\pi ia)\) for \(n\nmid a\), and zero at the remaining integer frequencies. Fejér boundedness allows integration of the limiting periodic function against the absolutely integrable kernel. Its mean disappears, and the integrated nonzero series converges absolutely. For \(v=|a|N/n\ge1\),
\[
 1+4\pi^2v^2\ge (1+4\pi^2)v
\]
because the difference is \((v-1)(4\pi^2v-1)\). Thus the bound for one generator is \(O_K(k^{-\sigma}N^{-\sigma}(1+4\pi^2)^{-k/2})\), using the summable majorant \(a^{\sigma-k/2-1}\). Multiplying by the coefficient bound gives exponent \(3/2-\sigma-\tfrac12\log(1+4\pi^2)+o(1)\).

The remaining physical tail starts at \(z_0=(R+1)/N=4L+o(1)>2k\). There the truncated exponential inside \(\chi_k\) is at most a constant times the final gamma bump. The incomplete gamma bound \(\Gamma(k-\sigma,z_0)\le2e^{-z_0}z_0^{k-\sigma-1}\) and Stirling give exponent \(3/2-\sigma-(3-\log4)+o(1)\), again including the coefficient bound. At \(\sigma=1/2\) both exponents are strictly negative. On fixed sufficiently small compact disks around the chosen finite critical zeros these errors are analytic and tend to zero by a power, so Cauchy differentiation supplies every fixed \(j\) in (88). There is no uniformity in growing zero heights or derivative orders.

The full Mellin transform of \(u\) is \(\zeta(s)(A(s)-A(1))/s\). Thus all derivatives of order below \(m_\rho\) vanish at a fixed chosen \(\rho\), irrespective of the size of the admissible suffix. The compact test on \(1\) gives \(1/s\) plus an analytic negligible error by the same mean cancellation, the super-polynomial small-\(z\) bound on \(h\), and the physical tail without a coefficient factor. Hence \(T_{\rho,j,N}(e_N)\to\mathbf1_{j=0}/\rho\). This carries over the exact annihilation mechanism from RST047 §6 with the new parameters, rather than importing its old bounds unchanged. RST047 §5 concerns off-line zeros and is not needed.

## Arithmetic moments on the fixed coefficient prefix

For \(X=N^b\) and \(1\le x\le X\), only prescribed coefficients enter the floor sum. The identities \(\sum_{n\mid j}\mu(n)=\mathbf1_{j=1}\) and \(\sum_{n\mid j}\mu(n)\log n=-\Lambda(j)\) give \(\sum_{n\le N}a_n\lfloor x/n\rfloor=1+\psi(x)/L\). Thus the anchored residual is \(A(1)\lfloor x\rfloor-\psi(x)/L\) and obeys (85) exactly with \(d_N=LA(1)-1\) and \(R(x)=\lfloor x\rfloor-\psi(x)\). Also
\[
 \int_1^X R(x)\lfloor x\rfloor\,\frac{dx}{x^2}
 =\int_1^X(x-\psi(x))\,\frac{dx}{x}+O(\log X)=O(\sqrt X)
\]
under RH. The floor replacement outside the integral has bounded additional error using \(\psi(x)=x+O(x^{1/2}\log^2(2x))\). The integrated explicit formula makes the remaining zero sum absolutely convergent by the extra \(1/\rho\) factor. The squared norm of \(\lfloor x\rfloor\) on this interval is \(X+O(\log X)\). Pairing (85) with it proves \(|d_N|\sqrt X=O(\sqrt L)\). Complex coefficients pose no issue because the scalar coefficient can be bounded by triangle and Cauchy–Schwarz after taking the inner product.

For the weighted moment (87), one can first integrate the explicitly summed primitive of \(x-\psi(x)\) by parts. Its zero sum is absolutely and uniformly convergent on each fixed compact positive \(x\)-interval, so termwise pairing of the continuous primitive is valid. After recovering the individual weighted zero integrals, write \(v=\log x\), so that these integrals, apart from \(m_\lambda/\lambda\), are
\[
 \int_0^{bL} e^{(\lambda-\rho)v}(v/L)^j\,dv .
\]
The diagonal is \(Lb^{j+1}/(j+1)\). For \(\lambda\ne\rho\), integration by parts gives \(O_{j,b}(|\lambda-\rho|^{-1})\), using the total variation of \((v/L)^j\). At large ordinates the two denominators make the series absolutely convergent by the unit-window zero count \(O(\log(2+|\Im\lambda|))\), while finitely many nearby distinct zeros have fixed nonzero gaps. Floor errors, the constant and the logarithmic trivial-zero contribution near \(x=1\) are absolutely integrable against the weight. The extra drift term in the \(e_N\) moment costs \(O(|d_N|\sqrt X)=O(\sqrt L)\) before division by \(L\). This validates both (87) and the ensuing limiting head pairing.

## Split Gram bound and equivalence of the two norms

On \([1,X]\), the cutoff and its finitely many \(s\)-derivatives differ from the uncut constant kernel by a super-polynomially small amount: \(x/N\le N^{b-1}\) and the first omitted exponential term starts at order \(k\), while gamma derivatives in \(s\) only differentiate \(1/s\). This fact is where \(b<1\) fixed enters the lower proof. The full representer is the main \(x^{1-\bar\rho}(\log x/L)^j\chi_k(x/N)\) plus the differentiated gamma bump; (31) of RST047 explicitly includes the derivatives of \(1/s\). The bump has squared norm \(O(\sqrt L)\) by \(\int_0^\infty B_k(z)^2dz/z=\Gamma(2k)/(2^{2k}\Gamma(k)^2)\). The main term is unchanged to negligible error up through \(x=N\), and the remaining interval from \(N\) to \(R+1\) has logarithmic length \(O(\log L)\). Thus its same-zero Gram divided by \(L\) tends to the Hilbert block and the fixed distinct-zero terms are \(o(L)\) before normalization by one integration by parts and the \(O(\log L)\) tail. The bumps' mixed Gram costs \(O(L^{3/4})\). After removing the head, the Gram limit is the positive definite monomial block \(M_{m,b}\) and the limiting tail pairing is \(-v_{m,b}/\rho\).

On the head it suffices to project onto \(x^{1-\bar\rho}\) for the chosen finite set, with Gram divided by \(L\) tending to \(bI\) and the limiting functional \(m_\rho b/\rho\). The two projections have disjoint supports. The resulting lower bound sums \(m_\rho^2 b/|\rho|^2+v_{m,b}^{\mathsf T}M_{m,b}^{-1}v_{m,b}/|\rho|^2\). It holds for any fixed finite zero set. Exhaust the set after taking the lower limit in \(N\).

For the unrestricted physical residual, the same divisor identity gives \(A(1)x-\psi(x)/L\) on the head. Pair with \(x\) there to obtain \(A(1)=O(L^{-1})\) on every sequence of squared norm \(O(L^{-1})\). The difference from the anchored residual extended by zero is \(A(1)\{x\}\), whose norm is \(O(L^{-1})\) because \(\{x\}\in L^2((0,\infty),dx/x^2)\). The squared norm difference is \(O(L^{-3/2})\) for these sequences. The comparison keeps the same input polynomial and does not change its prescribed coefficient at \(n=1\).

## Upper bound and fixed parameters

The delayed polynomials of (22)–(23) work with any fixed \(b\). Their monomial coefficient bound in (27) gives \(B_b=72((1+b)/(1-b))^2\) in the rational estimates and derivative exponential base \(B_b/8\). The near exponent is \(2D(1+\log(6B_b))+5e\le0.0007\) with (82), and the far and derivative losses remain within their stated limits. Inserting \(H=N^{b/10}\), \(K=N^{3b/10}\), \(\delta\le b/1000\) in (49), (59), (60), (62) verifies the strict negative margins. The growing correction is supported only at \(n>N^b\) because the taper difference vanishes on \([0,b]\).

For a fixed zero of order \(m\), the endpoint jet constraints for \(f\) are equivalent to the moments in (83) for \(g=f^{(m)}\). The prefix profile gives \(g=-m\), and minimizing the remaining norm gives exactly (80) by projection onto monomials on \([b,1]\). Integrating the optimal \(g\) from the initial jets yields the fixed prefix and the vanishing endpoint jets at 1. This gives a piecewise polynomial profile, with all needed lower jets matching at \(b\). The fixed correction in (84) uses the ordinary Euler quadrature on \([N^b,N]\), since the profile difference has zero endpoint values and fixed polynomial pieces. Its error in the weighted zeta norm follows by splitting at \(N^{b/2}\); the lower and upper squared errors have the respective powers \(-5b/4+\varepsilon\) and \(-b/4+\varepsilon\) up to fixed log factors. At \(s=1\) its direct coefficient sum is \(O(L^{m-1}N^{-b/2})\). The fixed-Laurent-term localization used in the main theorem still applies to these profiles. The pointwise and local \(L^2\) estimates involve only fixed polynomial variation, since only finitely many top Laurent terms are replaced.

Each unoptimized delayed profile is feasible in the local minimum. The summable cluster tail thus bounds the remaining costs and justifies exhaustion only after \(N\to\infty\). The anchored upper comparison uses \(A(1)=O(L^{-1})\) from the base and the two corrections. The explicit \(m=1,2\) cases follow from the moment minimization; for \(m\ge2\) the unconstrained minimizer is the negative Legendre kernel at zero, a nonconstant polynomial that cannot equal \(-m\) on \([0,b]\). Consequently the equality of the two finite constants under RH is equivalent to all zeros being simple.
