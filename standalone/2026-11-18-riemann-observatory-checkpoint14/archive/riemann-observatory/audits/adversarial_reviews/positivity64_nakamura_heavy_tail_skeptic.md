## Strict tails and Poisson mixing

I checked Hasanalizade–Shen–Wong v1, its inclusive count on p. 1 and Corollary 1.2, equation (1.5). Its coefficients are $0.1038$, $0.2573$, and $9.3675$, with range $T\ge e$. Put $a=0.3611$, $b=9.3675$, using $\log\log T\le\log T$. For an ordinate cutoff $Y$ the integral over $(Y,U]$ is
$$
\int_{(Y,U]} v^{-2}\,dN(v)
=U^{-2}N(U)-Y^{-2}N(Y)+2\int_Y^U v^{-3}N(v)\,dv .
$$
This proves the inclusive-count subtraction even if $Y$ is an atom. Integrating the main derivative gives the stated main term, and integrating $4(a\log v+b)v^{-3}$ gives $(2a\log Y+2b+a)/Y^2$. Adding the endpoint error gives $(4a\log Y+4b+a)/Y^2$. [Primary zero-count bound](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.06506v1)

For the jump law normalized by intensity, the main term and the two endpoint errors give $|F(x\pm v)-F(x)|\ll(v+1)\log x/x^2$ for $0\le v\le x/2$. This estimate is valid for atomic $F$. The bound $F(x/m)\ll m\log x/x$ uses $F\le1$ when $x/m<e$ and therefore remains uniform for large jump counts. For $n\ge2$ put $u=x/(2n-2)$. If all jump magnitudes are at most $u$, the absolute sum is at most $nu\le x$, including equality when $n=2$. Thus a strict exceedance involves a large jump. The event of two or more jumps above $u$ is bounded by $\binom n2 F(u)^2$. In the lower sandwich, the singled-out jump exceeds $x+|R_i|\ge x\ge u$, whereas every other jump is at most $u$. This proves disjointness across the indices. In the upper sandwich, on the one-large-jump event, the reverse triangle bound requires $|J_i|>x-|R_i|$. All inequalities keep the strict cutoff.

Replacing the shifted tails inside the sandwich uses $|R_i|\le x/2$ on $G_i$ and $\mathbb E[|R_i|;G_i]\le(n-1)\mathbb E[|J|;|J|\le u]$, with the latter moment $O(\log^2 x)$ since $u\le x$. Removing the event indicator costs at most $nF(x)(n-1)F(u)$. These bounds give a uniform polynomial error of degree at most four in $n$, times $\log^3 x/x^2$. It is summable against the Poisson law. Thus both the stated leading and linear-in-$1/Y$ terms transfer from the Lévy tail, and the moment threshold and Pareto exceedance limit follow.

## Unconditional cusp and conditional Cauchy limit

In the paired sum, the strip bound and real-axis nonvanishing of $\xi$ permit selecting precisely the zeros with positive real coordinate. Replacing each by its real part costs $O(t^2\log(1/|t|))$ by the two derivative bounds on $k(z)=(1-\cos z)/z^2$ stated in the note. Integration by parts against the RH-independent count error is justified by $k'(w)=O(w)$ at zero and $k'(w)=O(w^{-2})$ at infinity. The Mellin signs agree with the alternative parameter $J(w)=\int_0^\infty(1-\cos v)v^{w-2}\,dv$, where $J'(0)=(\pi/2)(1-\gamma_{\rm E})$. The prime-free interval gives an independent exact differentiation check. For $0<t<\log2$, put $x=e^{-t/2}$. The derivative of the Lerch term is $-2\sum_{n\ge0}x^{4n+1}/(4n+1)=-\operatorname{arctanh}x-\arctan x$, by the identity $2/(1-x^4)=1/(1-x^2)+1/(1+x^2)$. Using $\operatorname{arctanh}x=\frac12\log\coth(t/4)$ and $\frac d{dt}\arctan(e^{-t/2})=-1/(4\cosh(t/2))$ yields the displayed quadratic coefficient $-7/8$ after integrating $g_\zeta'$. It also gives the next term $-|t|^3/288+O(t^4)$ after the quadratic term, since $\frac12\log\coth(t/4)=\frac12\log(4/t)+t^2/96+O(t^4)$ and the arctangent and elementary exponential derivative contribute no $t^2$ term.

At $a_n=n\log n/2$ the linear-logarithmic term tends to $-|t|$ after multiplying $g_\zeta(t/a_n)$ by $n$. Under RH this is convergence of characteristic functions to the standard Cauchy law. The same local scaling holds for the synthetic quartet model, since its perturbation is $O(t^2)$ and so vanishes under this scaling. The modulus bound for characteristic functions then independently fails at large real times in that model. On the torus, pushing forward the real-line law preserves a positive atom at the identity and the characteristic lower bound $e^{-2S}$, whereas the derivative-weighted product law has atomless circle marginals and coefficients tending to zero along powers of a single prime.
