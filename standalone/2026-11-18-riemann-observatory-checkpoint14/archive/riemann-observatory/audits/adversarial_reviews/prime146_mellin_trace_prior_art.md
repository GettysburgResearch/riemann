# Bounded prior-art comparison for absolute Mellin zero sampling

18 November 2026. The closest checked sampling predecessor is Luecking's Bergman embedding theorem. A direct conformal change of variables recovers the exact \(p>3\) logarithmic threshold on the **ambient weighted holomorphic strip space**. The classical explicit-formula sources checked below use different regularity conditions and specify a symmetric limit. None of the checked statements gives the archive's smooth real test with its stipulated physical decay and divergent absolute sampling at actual zeta zeros. This bounded comparison establishes no priority. ## Exact ambient embedding from Luecking

Daniel H. Luecking, *Multipliers of Bergman spaces into Lebesgue spaces*, Proc. Edinburgh Math. Soc. 29 (1986), 125–131, gives the following theorem on journal p. 126 (PDF p. 2). Let \(\mathbb D\) be the unit disk, \(m\) normalized area, and \(\mu\) a positive finite Borel measure on \(\mathbb D\). For \(0<q<p\), the embedding \(A^p(\mathbb D)\to L^q(\mu)\) is bounded exactly when \(\mu(D_\epsilon(z))/m(D_\epsilon(z))\in L^{p/(p-q)}(m)\), where \(D_\epsilon\) is a disk of fixed pseudohyperbolic radius \(0<\epsilon<1\). The lemma on p. 127 shows independence of that radius. The case needed here is \(p=2,q=1\), with a squared local-density integral. The pinned publisher PDF SHA-256 is e5d194ad05a844e376a65b18bc7082864baa38b01ddb1d4f217f67c28cb0d301. [Publisher PDF](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/B94CA41BD4DAC720691F9B48D56E29BE/S001309150001748Xa.pdf/multipliers_of_bergman_spaces_into_lebesgue_spaces.pdf) · [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S001309150001748X) · [Local copy](../../sources/prime146_mellin_provenance/luecking1986.pdf)

Here is the deduction, which is not stated for zeta zeros in that paper. Take \(S=\{a<\Re s<b\}\) with \(a<0<1<b\). Let \(w(t)\) be either \((1+t^2)^\sigma\) or \(\sqrt{1+t^2}\log^p(e+|t|)\), with fixed real exponents. There is a nonvanishing holomorphic \(G\) on \(S\) with \(|G(s)|^2\asymp w(\Im s)\). For the first weight take \(G(s)=(C+s)^\sigma\), and for the second take \(G(s)=(C+s)^{1/2}[\operatorname{Log}(C+s)]^{p/2}\), with \(C\) large enough that \(\Re(C+s)>e\). All logarithms have a holomorphic principal branch on the image, and the comparability constants are uniform in the strip.

For a conformal map \(\Phi:\mathbb D\to S\) and \(z_\rho=\Phi^{-1}(\rho)\), define
\[
 F(z)=h(\Phi(z))G(\Phi(z))\Phi'(z),\qquad
 d\mu=\sum_\rho \frac{\delta_{z_\rho}}{|G(\rho)\Phi'(z_\rho)|},
\]
counting multiplicities. The weighted strip norm of \(h\) is equivalent to \(\|F\|_{A^2}\) by the area Jacobian. The zero sampling sum is exactly \(\|F\|_{L^1(\mu)}\), and every \(F\in A^2\) arises this way because \(G\) and \(\Phi'\) do not vanish. The measure is finite. For example, map the strip first by \(s\mapsto\exp(i\pi(s-a)/(b-a))\) to the upper half-plane and then to the disk by Cayley's map. For \(\Re\rho\in[0,1]\) this gives \(1-|z_\rho|^2\ll e^{-\pi|\Im\rho|/(b-a)}\). Also \(|\Phi'(z_\rho)|(1-|z_\rho|^2)\) is uniformly bounded above and below on this interior substrip, by the explicit conformal radius of the strip. The polynomial or logarithmic atom factors together with the zero-count bound then give finite total mass.

For a fixed pseudohyperbolic radius the area \(m(D_\epsilon(z))\) is comparable to \((1-|z|^2)^2\). In strip coordinates such a disk is a ball of fixed hyperbolic radius. Wherever it contains or approaches a sampled zero, its center lies in a fixed interior substrip and its height differs from the sampled height by at most a fixed constant. The interior property follows from the explicit positive distance of \([0,1]\) to the strip boundaries and the fixed hyperbolic radius. On each such disk \(1-|z_\rho|^2\asymp1-|z|^2\), and \(w(\Im\rho)\asymp w(\Im\Phi(z))\) when \(\rho\) lies inside that disk. Thus the local density is comparable to the number of sampled zeros in the hyperbolic ball, times \(w(\Im\Phi(z))^{-1/2}/(1-|z|^2)\). On squaring and changing variables, the factor \((1-|z|^2)^{-2}\) cancels the area Jacobian up to constants. The embedding criterion becomes square-integrability of that local zero count divided by \(w^{1/2}\) on the strip.

More explicitly, let \(N_j\) count zeros with \(j\le\Im\rho<j+1\). The criterion is equivalent to
\[
 \sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}\frac{N_j^2}{w(j)}<\infty.
\]
For the upper comparison, a fixed hyperbolic radius meets only a bounded number of neighboring height cells and remains in an interior strip wherever the local mass is positive. Square the local cell sum and use bounded overlap and weight comparability. For the lower comparison, take disjoint small Euclidean disks about \(1/2+i(j+1/2)\). A sufficiently large *fixed* hyperbolic radius covers every point of \([0,1]+i[j,j+1]\) from each center in the corresponding small disk, by horizontal compactness and vertical translation invariance. Luecking permits that radius. Integrating over these disjoint fixed-area disks gives the lower comparison.

The elementary bound \(N_j\ll\log(2+|j|)\) proves convergence for \(\sigma>1/2\) or \(p>3\). For the reverse implication on the ambient class, a dyadic positive-height shell \([T,2T)\) has \(\asymp T\log T\) zeros by Riemann–von Mangoldt. Cauchy–Schwarz over its \(O(T)\) cells yields \(\sum_{\text{shell}}N_j^2\gg T\log^2T\). Dividing by the maximum weight on that shell gives a divergent dyadic series for \(\sigma\le1/2\) or \(p\le3\), including the harmonic dyadic contribution at \(p=3\). When the embedding is unbounded, there must be a holomorphic \(h\) in the weighted strip space whose sample sequence is outside \(\ell^1\). Otherwise the sampling operator into \(\ell^1\) would be everywhere defined with closed graph, since its coordinate evaluations are continuous by local holomorphic mean value. This conclusion concerns the full analytic Hilbert space. It does not supply a Mellin inverse that is real smooth, vanishes near 0 and 1, satisfies the prescribed pointwise decay, and has absolutely finite arithmetic and gamma terms. Those extra requirements are addressed by the archive's endpoint construction. Luecking's theorem also gives no signed symmetric convergence assertion or numerical tail constant.

## Classical explicit formulas and the closest zeta-specific results

- **Weil (1952), original French article in transcription.** *Sur les “formules explicites” de la théorie des nombres premiers*, Comm. Lund, volume dedicated to Marcel Riesz, pp. 252–265. In the accessed French transcription PDF p. 6, conditions (A) and (B) require \(F\) to be \(C^1\) away from finitely many first-kind discontinuities of \(F,F'\), with midpoint values for \(F\), and **both** \(F\) and \(F'\) to be \(O(e^{-(1/2+\eta)|x|})\) for some \(\eta>0\). In the notation of the transcription the transform is \(\Phi(s)=\int_\mathbb R F(x)e^{(s-1/2)x}dx\), so \(F(x)=e^{x/2}f(e^x)\) in the archive's notation. The conclusion on transcription p. 8 is a limit in \(|\Im \rho|<T\). The derivative at infinity is an independent condition, not a consequence of the archive's value bounds. The transcription is a reproduction of the primary text, not a facsimile checked against the original pagination. SHA-256 1edd4a831d2fcc3a5d01d82d7bbb933b7916b84d4e87b60bbe144dd883c8ce26. [French text](https://denisevellachemla.eu/transc-Weil-1952.pdf) · [Local copy](../../sources/prime146_mellin_provenance/weil1952_reprint.pdf)
- **Barner (1981) and Büthe (2014 preprint).** Barner's *On A. Weil's explicit formula*, J. Reine Angew. Math. 323, 139–152, Theorem 1 on p. 141, uses exponentially weighted bounded variation, midpoint values and a local symmetric regularity condition at the origin. Pages 140–142 were read in the Göttingen volume's primary OCR manifestation, with formula typography checked against the unambiguous analogous class of Büthe's *A method for proving the completeness of a list of zeros of certain L-functions*, arXiv:1308.6704v4, §2, pp. 2–3. Büthe's (Ba1)–(Ba3) demand \(f(x)e^{c|x|}\in BV\cap L^1\) for \(c\) exceeding the half-strip width, midpoint values, and \(f(x)+f(-x)-2f(0)=O(|x|^\eta)\) for some \(\eta>0\). His \(w_s\) is a symmetric limit in height, with zeros and poles counted algebraically. These are physical-space weighted-variation conditions and do not state the archive's absolute trace theorem. [Barner theorem page](https://gdz.sub.uni-goettingen.de/fulltext/PPN243919689_0323/00000145?_format=xml) · [Büthe primary §2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1308.6704v4)
- **Burnol (1998 v2).** In *The explicit formula in simple terms*, arXiv:math/9810169v2, PDF pp. 7–8, conditions \((A_\epsilon),(B)\) impose that \(g(u)(u^{-\epsilon}+u^{1+\epsilon})\) is of bounded variation and that \(g\) has midpoint values. The explicit-formula theorem on pp. 11–12 equates existence of the symmetric zero limit with existence of the archimedean contour limit. The convolution class \((E)\) on pp. 13–14 adds continuity and integrability of \(|\widehat g(c+it)|\log |t|\) on every interior vertical line. Its theorem still displays a symmetric zero limit, and states absolute convergence for the local \(W_\nu\) sums. These conditions and conclusions should not be rewritten as a weighted strip-area criterion or as an absolute sampling theorem. [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9810169v2)
- **Bondarenko–Radchenko–Seip.** *Fourier Interpolation with Zeros of Zeta and L-Functions*, arXiv:2005.02996, §1, (1.1), definition of \(\mathcal H_1\) and Theorem 1.1, pp. 2–3. The Riemann–Weil formula is recalled under a uniform pointwise power-decay condition \(O((1+|z|)^{-1-\eta})\) on a wider horizontal strip. The main interpolation result for even \(f\in\mathcal H_1\) instead uses a uniform vertical-coordinate supremum of \(\int |f(x+iy)|(1+|x|)dx\). It reconstructs \(f\) from a weighted combination of Fourier samples and zero samples with derivatives at multiple zeros, the latter ordered along a height subsequence. This addresses zero interpolation on a different function space and does not give the unweighted absolute sampling statement at the critical strip-area norm. [Primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.02996) · [Journal version](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00365-022-09599-w)

Generalized weighted variation and logarithmic smoothness are neighboring mechanisms. The Bulgarian Academy PDF of Avdispahić–Smajlović, *\(\phi\)-Variation and Barner-Weil Formula* (2003), was located as an image scan. Its full theorem was not used here. Domínguez–Tikhonov, arXiv:1811.06399, was located for general logarithmic Besov/Sobolev embeddings. Neither discovery record alone establishes a zero-sampling endpoint or identifies the archive's particular proof. General Carleson embeddings, Rademacher lower moment bounds, Paley–Zygmund and the zero count are established methods. The precise simultaneous construction of real smooth \(f\) with the stated support and decay at \(p=3\), and the signed question at that endpoint, remain separate provenance questions.
