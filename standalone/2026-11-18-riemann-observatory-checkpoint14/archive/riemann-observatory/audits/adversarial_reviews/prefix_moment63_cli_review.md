# Independent review of the rational prefix-moment calculator

17 November 2026. I traced the calculator to the finite prefix-inverse formulas, exercised its public JSON entry points and compared the enclosures with an independently generated family of exact profiles. The calculator applies to limiting Taylor data realized by a nonnegative profile on the triangular lattice. Arithmetic provenance and full truncated feasibility are separate inputs. ## Bounds and indexing

Write \(x_m=m(m-1)\), \(t_m=x_m^2W_m\), \(a_k=[b^k]C(b)\) and \(L(p)=\sum_{m\ge2}t_mp(x_m)\). The implemented recurrence for \(r_j(x)\) multiplies by \((x-j(j+1))/(j(j+1))\) at step \(j\), so its denominator is \(j!(j+1)!\). Squaring the series in \(s=b/(1-b)\) and integrating divides the order-\(s^d\) coefficient by \(d+1\). Re-expansion in \(b\) multiplies by \(\binom{k-1}{\ell-1}\) for an integrated order \(\ell\). This gives exactly \(a_k=L(p_{k-1})\). The diagonals are positive, with first rows \(1\), \(x/2\) and \((5x^2+8x)/36\). Descending elimination expresses the polynomial itself in this basis. Its coefficient row therefore integrates against the original jet, and taking the correct endpoint for each row sign gives the exact projection of the coefficient box. The necessary rejection added in this review uses \(p_{k-1}(x_m)\ge1\) at every site, since \(r_j(x_m)\ge0\) and the \(\ell=1\) term is 1. If an entire higher-coefficient interval lies below the first interval, no realizing profile exists.

For \(M\) supplied coefficients, the tool uses \(P_M(x)=\prod_{m=2}^M(x-x_m)\) and \(x_*=M(M+1)\). The tail polynomial is zero on the recorded earlier nodes and at least \(D=P_M(x_*)>0\) on every later node. Thus \(T=\sum_{m>M}t_m\le L(P_M)/D\) carries no finite-tail truncation. The empty product at \(M=1\) gives \(D=1\) and \(T=a_1\). For \(M\ge2\), the polynomial strictly increases on the tail, so equality holds when the positive tail is supported at \(x_*\), or when it is empty. Zero polynomial integral gives finite support. Exact zero-tail data then give the unique lattice masses on the included sites, and \(C(0)\) supplies \(W_1\) if given.

For an included site \(x_j\), set \(d=P'_M(x_j)\) and \(\ell_j=P_M/((x-x_j)d)\). The code projects two functionals, \(\ell_j\) and \(\ell_j-P_M/(d(x_*-x_j))\). On the tail their signs are opposite or one is zero because \((x-x_j)^{-1}\le(x_*-x_j)^{-1}\). For positive \(d\) the corrected functional supplies the lower bound and the cardinal supplies the upper bound. Negative \(d\) reverses the order. Both equal the site indicator on included nodes. The correction combines the two rows before projection, retaining coefficient dependencies. The tail at \(x_*\) saturates the one-sided error in either sign. Intersecting these bounds with nonnegativity and mass conservation is valid for every realizing profile.

The conversions are \(W_m=t_m/x_m^2\) and \(m^2W_m=t_m/(m-1)^2\). The omitted contributions are at most \(T/x_*^2\) and \(T/M^2\), respectively. The code subtracts the included and omitted constant-term contributions using outward bounds to enclose \(W_1\), then imposes its nonnegativity. This gives an enclosure using the constant term and leaves joint moment feasibility open.

## Feasibility controls and interface changes

- The exact jet \((1,15/2,415/12)\) gives mass 1, mean 15 and second moment 225. Zero variance forces \(\delta_{15}\), but 15 lies outside the triangular lattice. The calculator returns `positive_tail` under `tail_status_if_feasible` and records `feasibility_checked: false`.
- The interval jet \(([1/2,2],[3/2,5/2],[0,2/3])\) also has no realizing measure. At \(M=3\), \(L(P_3)=12a_1-(96/5)a_2+(36/5)a_3\) has upper endpoint 0 and reaches it only at \((2,3/2,2/3)\). That candidate would require zero tail and \(t_3=(a_2-a_1)/2=-1/4\). The individual interval projections cannot enforce that common corner. The calculator returns the conditional `zero_tail` status and leaves feasibility unchecked. Tightening the first interval to \([1,2]\) triggers the new exact first-coefficient comparison.
- The exact pair \((a_1,a_2)=(1,3)\) with \(C(0)=1/8\) also passes the computed bounds. Its probability measure would have mean 6. The function \(g(x)=((\sqrt{1+4x}-1)/2)^{-2}\) is strictly convex for \(x>0\), so the multiple-weight constant term is at least \(g(6)=1/4\), exceeding the budget. This is a separate joint-constraint obstruction.

The original flat certificate flags could be read as a feasibility verdict for those inputs. The updated JSON exposes `feasibility_checked: false` and the conditional enum `tail_status_if_feasible`. Its possible values are `zero_tail`, `positive_tail` and `undetermined`. The help and output retain the requirement for genuine limiting coefficients, and the archived fixtures remain synthetic. The JSON decoder rejects duplicate keys at every object level. An escaped lone surrogate in the source description now gives a controlled argument error. Valid Unicode metadata round-trips through ASCII-safe JSON output.

## Executed checks

On Python 3.12.14 I generated weights independently using the ordinary Legendre recurrence at \(1+2s\), differentiated in \(s\), integrated the square and re-expanded at \(b=0\). All 540 feasible exact or interval inputs over \(1\le M\le9\) enclosed the independently computed moments, tails and weights. Another 18 fixtures checked exact vanishing and first-tail saturation, including the empty product and equality of the cardinal error in both signs. Sixteen malformed or contradictory CLI inputs produced controlled exit status 2 and no traceback. The three infeasible controls above retained their explicit conditional scope. The exact and interval fixtures reproduced their saved JSON byte-for-byte with both file and standard-input entry points.
