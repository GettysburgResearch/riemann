# Independent audit of an effective scalar phase CLT via one omitted prime

18 November 2026. Under RH, the scalar prime-phase central limit theorem admits an effective conditional onset, in the sense of a computable threshold for a fixed computable cutoff exponent \(D>0\) and a requested Kolmogorov tolerance. The proof removes the possible exceptional character in each finite-height estimate, estimates the full statistic at that height, and only then integrates. It does not require an effective uniform estimate of every individual character. I did not compile a numerical onset. ## 1. Which conductor is excluded

Fix a largest even moment degree \(r\) and fix \(Y\), \(P=(\log Y)^D\). At each prefix height \(T\ge Y\), let \(L=\log T\) and allow monomials \(m,n\le P^r\le L^{Dr}\). Take \(Q=\max(3,\lceil L^{Dr}\rceil)\), \(U=\exp(\sqrt L)\). The classical effective zero-free region for primitive nonprincipal Dirichlet characters has width \(c_1/\log(q(|t|+3))\), with the sole possible simple real zero for a real character. The separate effective Landau–Page theorem for primitive real characters across all conductors \(q\le Q\) allows at most one real zero in \([1-c_2/\log Q,1]\). These are separate inputs; per-modulus uniqueness alone would not suffice. For large effective \(T\), \(\log[Q(U+10)]\le2\sqrt L\). Choose a fixed small \(c>0\), for example \(\min(c_1,c_2,1)/100\), and put \(\eta=c/\sqrt L\). Every participating character except possibly one has no zero in \(\Re s\ge1-16\eta,\ |\Im s|\le U+10\). Indeed any real zero in that rectangle is inside the Page interval, while nonreal zeros there are excluded by the classical bound. If the Page interval contains a zero, it is harmless to delete its conductor even if its zero is outside this narrower rectangle. [Michel, Lecture 1, §1.2.2, pp. 20–21, effective Landau–Page theorem and proof](https://www.epfl.ch/labs/tan/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/Parkcitylectures.pdf)

If a bad character exists with conductor \(q_0\), select one prime \(\ell_T\mid q_0\). If \(q_0\) is not \(P\)-smooth, choose a divisor above \(P\), which means there is no deletion from the statistic. Otherwise delete its selected prime from the \(P\)-block, in every monomial of degrees at most \(r\). The padded coefficients
\[
a_{\sigma,\tau}(d)=8\mathbf1_{d\mid m\text{ or }d\mid n}
 +\sigma\mathbf1_{d=m}+\tau\mathbf1_{d=n}
\]
remain supported on indices avoiding \(\ell_T\), because divisor closure preserves this avoidance. The bounded majorants and exact submultiplicativity are uniform. In the actual published HLZ §4.4 p. 1615, the nonprincipal error is summed over primitive \(\psi\) modulo \(q\), with outer factor \(a(kq)/(kq)^{1-\beta}\). The coefficient is identically zero when \(q=q_0\). This includes all terms originating from characters induced by the exceptional primitive character, since the source formula has already decomposed them by the primitive conductor. Any nonzero term necessarily has \(kq\le P^r\). The exceptional contribution therefore vanishes before any bound on \(b(md)\) or a reciprocal \(L\)-factor is used. No decision procedure to identify the exceptional character or its prime divisor is used to set a threshold. All subsequent estimates are independent of the choice of divisor. [HLZ journal, §4.4 and Proposition 10](../../sources/classical63_derivative_moments/hlz2022print.pdf)

## 2. Effectivity of the surviving prefix moment calculation

For the retained primitive nonprincipal characters, reciprocal bounds in a narrower rectangle are effective even near height zero. Here is one direct route. Periodic partial sums are bounded in modulus by \(q\). Truncate \(L(s,\psi)\) at \(N_0=\lceil(q(|\Im s|+3))^2\rceil\) and use summation by parts on the tail. For \(\sigma\ge1-16\eta\) and \(|\Im s|\le U+5\), the initial sum has absolute value \(O(\log N_0)\) because \(N_0^{16\eta}=O(1)\), and the tail is \(O(q(1+|s|)N_0^{-\sigma})=O(1)\). All constants are effective. On a disk of radius \(8\eta\) centered at \(1+2\eta+it\), choose the holomorphic log starting with the Euler-product value at the center. There \(|\log L|\le\log\zeta(1+2\eta)\ll\log L\). Throughout the disk \(\Re\log L=\log|L|\ll\log L\) by the polynomial logarithmic upper bound just proved. Borel–Carathéodory on the half-radius disk gives \(|\log L|\le C\log L\), hence \(1/|L|\le L^C\) throughout a strip containing the displaced Perron contour and the \(O(1/L)\) shifts. This uses the rectangle out to \(U+10\) for the disks centered at every ordinate on the vertical and horizontal parts. No appeal to Siegel's lower bound on \(L(1,\psi)\) is made.

In the identity on HLZ p. 1617, the only denominator with zeros near the contour is \(L(s+\beta,\psi)\). The other finite Euler factors are bounded by powers of \(L\): their primes divide \(d\le P^r\), all are bounded away from the relevant poles, and even a fixed constant per factor costs a power of \(L\). The polynomials \(A(s+\beta,d_1)\) there have \(md_1\le P^r\). The number and size of all outer finite factors in §4.4 are bounded by powers of \(L\), using just the finite formula for \(\delta(q,kq,d,\psi)\) and the Gauss sum bound. This simplifies the use of Proposition 10. It suffices to estimate each inner partial sum by \(xL^C e^{-c'\sqrt{\log T}}\) for \(x=Tkq/(2\pi d)\), so \(x\gg T\) and \(\log x=L+O_{D,r}(\log L)\). Initial Dirichlet coefficients satisfy a majorant \(\tau_5(md)(md)^{O(1/L)}\), from (28) and \(|a|\le10\). Absolute Perron series on \(1+1/L\) cost a fixed log power by the small shift radii. Taking \(U=e^{\sqrt L}\) and a truncation short interval of radius \(x/\sqrt U\), the usual truncated Perron error and the contour at \(1-\eta\) give the claimed exponential saving. The contour has no exceptional residue by construction.

For effectivity of short-interval bounds one can avoid an opaque divisor-sum constant. For fixed \(k\ge2\) and \(1\le h\le x/2\),
\[
 \sum_{x-h<n\le x+h}\tau_k(n)
 \ll_k h(1+\log x)^{k-1}+x^{1-1/k}(1+\log x)^{k-2},
\]
with computable constants. In each ordered factorization of \(n\), mark a largest factor. The product \(d\) of the others is at most \((2x)^{1-1/k}\). Count the largest factor by \(2h/d+1\) and bound the resulting sums of \(\tau_{k-1}(d)/d\) and \(\tau_{k-1}(d)\) by iterated harmonic sums. The \(h=x/\sqrt U\) range absorbs the second term effectively for \(\log x\asymp L\). The same argument applies in the principal Perron part of §§4.3.1–4.3.2; there the zeta zero-free region is the effective \(q=1\) case and the isolated poles give the fixed algebraic residue expression (24). These principal sums, which can have \(x=T\) times a polylogarithmic ratio, still have \(\log x=L+O_{D,r}(\log L)\). The source's far-modulus part \(E_2\) is empty with cutoff \(\eta_{\rm cond}=2Q\). None of these steps asks for an ineffective estimate.

The continuous term \(J\) can be treated effectively in this sparse range without relying on a black-box implied constant in the general length theorem. Use the shifted product approximate functional equation in Pratt–Robles §2, (2.3)–(2.4), a Mellin weight with the polynomial annihilating the two shift poles, and the gamma-ratio formula there. On a dyadic interval \(t\asymp X\ge T^{1/8}/2\), the Mellin weights and \(t\) times their \(t\)-derivatives decay by every fixed power of \(1+ab/X\) in the product index \(ab\), with at most polynomial factors in \(L\). To see this, use the Mellin contour to the right for \(ab>X\), and to a small negative fixed real part otherwise, taking the \(w=0\) residue. On bounded vertical displacement use the gamma quotient Stirling bound and its differentiated version or the digamma estimate. On the remaining displacement the Gaussian \(e^{w^2}\) in the weight makes all polynomial and gamma bounds summable. The factor from the pole annihilator is \(O(L^2(1+|w|)^2)\) on the fixed shift circles, where \(|\alpha+\beta|\gg1/L\). This proof, as well as termwise differentiation at these large \(t\), uses only explicit gamma estimates. Truncate \(ab\) at \(X^{5/4}\), taking a fixed large decay power for the discarded tail. The phase of a twist with reduced pair \(h,j\) is \((bj/(ah))^{it}\). Off the diagonal \(ah=bj\), integration by parts costs an effective polylogarithmic multiple of \(1/|\log(ah/bj)|\), including the extra \(\log(t/(2\pi))\) weight. For comparable \(ah,bj\), the weight \((ab)^{-1/2}\) times this inverse logarithm is \(\ll(\log T)^{Dr}/|ah-bj|\). For fixed \(a\), summing over \(b\) costs \(O(\log T)\) before the polylogarithmic factors, since the nonzero integer gaps have spacing at least 1 and cannot repeat on each side. Also comparability and \(ab\le X^{5/4}\) give \(a\ll X^{5/8}L^{Dr}\). For noncomparable pairs the inverse logarithm is bounded and \(\sum_{ab\le X^{5/4}}(ab)^{-1/2}\ll X^{5/8}\log(2X)\). Both off-diagonal bounds save a power of \(X\). On the diagonal \(a=ju,\ b=hu\), the Mellin sum contains \(\zeta(1+\alpha+\beta+2w)\); shifting to \(\Re w=-1/8\) crosses only the \(w=0\) pole because the other pole was annihilated. The power-saving remainder and the dual term yield exactly (5) with its reduced-index factors. The bounded low range \(t<T^{1/8}\) costs a power saving by the effective fixed strip estimates and the polylogarithmic support, and the main kernel can be extended down to 0 by its integrable shift powers. All constants in this short twist argument are calculable from elementary summation and the standard gamma/functional-equation derivation of the product approximate functional equation. [Pratt–Robles, (2.3)–(2.4)](../../sources/finite_moment63_uniform/pratt_robles_1706.04593v3.pdf)

The rest of the earlier HLZ reconstruction carries over with effective inputs: bounded-coefficient good-height contour errors, the stationary-phase kernel of Ng Lemma 4.1, principal residues (24), the local ratio \(1+O(\log(hj)/L)\) on fixed separated circles, exact real polarization, and auxiliary and outer Cauchy derivatives. In the stationary-phase step the shift-dependent polynomial bound is retained in the absolute infinite coefficient series; no fixed global divisor bound has to be asserted for the shifted coefficients. Returning from the good ordinate to an arbitrary \(T\) costs a power by the effective unit-window count and fixed-strip Cauchy bound. For the normalizing untwisted prefix Gonek's Corollary 1 gives \(A(T)=T L^4/(24\pi)+O(T L^3)\) under RH. Its prime number theorem input is the effective ordinary \(q=1\) prime number theorem (source p. 135, §3); the good-height and other zeta estimates can use effective bounds. Consequently \(A(T)\asymp T L^4\) with effective constants and large-height threshold. [HLZ reconstruction](uniform64_hlz_third_review.md) · [Gonek source](../../sources/classical63_lfunctions/gonek1984.pdf) · [Effective zeta strip and unit-window estimates](prime_array63_effective_bound_skeptic.md)

Combining these steps gives, for all monomials in the retained prime block with exponents of total degree at most \(r\), a bound for the normalized positive-height **real-part** expectation of the form
\[
 \frac{(m,n)}{\sqrt{mn}}
 +O_{D,r,B}^{\rm eff}\!\left(
  \frac{(m,n)}{\sqrt{mn}}\frac{\log(mn/(m,n)^2)}{\log T}
  +(\log T)^{-B}\right)
\]
for any fixed \(B\). Powers lost from differentiation and \(\sqrt{mn}\) in normalization are absorbed by requesting a stronger log saving in the shifted estimate. Real parts suffice because each real cosine power pairs inverse Fourier words with equal coefficients. Equivalently one can perform the entire comparison using two-sided prefixes. The actual proof of the retained bound is a restricted effective redo of the source, rather than the use of the formerly ineffective statement with \(q_0\) crossed out.

## 3. Remove the deleted prime before passing from prefix to tail

Write \(H=\sum_{p\le P}1/p\), \(V=\frac12\sum_{p\le P}(1/p-1/p^2)\), and \(W=\sum_{p\le P}\cos(\gamma\log p)/\sqrt p\). These three use the fixed cutoff \(P=(\log Y)^D\) for every prefix. Let \(X=(W-H)/\sqrt V\) and \(X_{\ell}\) be the statistic with one centered summand removed (or none), still normalized by the full \(\sqrt V\). Pointwise \(|X-X_\ell|\le(1/\sqrt2+1/2)/\sqrt V=O(H^{-1/2})\). For the independent Poisson product this holds on the same probability space, and the centered variables have effectively bounded fixed moments. This follows, for instance, by the tilted-variance exponential bound in the earlier CLT audit, uniformly for any omitted coordinate.

At fixed \(T\), apply the retained character bounds to raw powers \(j\le r\) of the deleted statistic. Nonnegative coefficients and the exact model moments bound their weighted coefficient sum by \(O_j(H^j)\). The unweighted coefficient sum is at most \((2\sqrt P)^j\). After binomial centering at the retained mean and dividing by \(V^{j/2}\), the discrepancy is bounded by effective constants times
\[
 \frac{H^{j/2}\log(2P)}{\log T}
 +(\log T)^{-B}(1+H+2\sqrt P)^r
 \quad (1\le j\le r),
\]
for \(H\ge1\) and sufficiently large \(Y\), dropping a harmless denominator of at least a fixed positive constant. Choose \(B>Dr/2+2\). Both terms are bounded uniformly over \(T\ge Y\) by computable expressions tending to zero. The even \(r\)-th prefix moment bound gives an effective uniform bound for \(\mathbb E_T|X_{\ell_T}|^r\), with \(\mathbb E_T\) normalized by \(A(T)\) using nonnegative RH derivative weights. Use the same deleted prime across all \(j\le r\), selected with the largest conductor range. The estimate
\[
 |x^j-y^j|\le j|x-y|(|y|+|x-y|)^{j-1}
\]
and Hölder then compare the full and deleted moments by \(O_r(H^{-1/2})\), for the arithmetic prefixes and the model. No bound on the exceptional twisted moment itself is needed. In particular for each \(j\le r\), writing \(m_j^*\) for the full-model normalized moment, one gets
\[
 \left|\sum_{0<\gamma\le T}|\zeta'(\rho)|^2 X(\gamma)^j-A(T)m_j^*\right|
 \le A(T)\,\epsilon_{D,r}(Y)\qquad(T\ge Y)
\]
with effective \(\epsilon_{D,r}(Y)\to0\). Here the left side uses only the **full** statistic, even though the proof of its bound may choose a different \(\ell_T\) at every \(T\).

Set \(f(t)=(t^2+1/4)^{-1}\) and let \(B_Y^+=\int_{(Y,\infty)}f\,dA\). The signed prefix discrepancy is right-continuous and its tail discrepancy is \(-f(Y)R_j(Y)-\int_Y^\infty f'(t)R_j(t)\,dt\). There is no differentiating a height-dependent deletion, because \(R_j\) is defined entirely from the full fixed-cutoff statistic. Dividing the absolute bound by \(B_Y^+\) costs at most
\[
 \epsilon_{D,r}(Y)\left(1+\frac{2f(Y)A(Y)}{B_Y^+}\right)
 = O^{\rm eff}(\epsilon_{D,r}(Y)).
\]
Indeed the Stieltjes identity for \(A\) gives the displayed factor, and effective \(A(T)\asymp T(\log T)^4\) gives effective boundedness, for example by the effective Gonek asymptotic and its integrated remainder. This use of the inclusive prefix correctly subtracts the endpoint for strict tails. Reflection preserves the real statistic, so using both signs gives the same normalized moments. Nothing requires knowing, measuring or integrating the height-dependence of the bad-prime choice.

## 4. Constructive passage to a Gaussian tolerance

For the model, the centered summands are \(O(p^{-1/2})\), and order \(s\ge3\) cumulants are \(O_s(p^{-s/2})\), with explicit constants from the finite cumulant–moment polynomial. Summing all primes and normalizing by \(V^{s/2}\) gives \(O_s(V^{-s/2})\) for each fixed \(s\ge3\). The first two normalized cumulants are exactly 0 and 1. The inverse finite polynomial therefore bounds every fixed moment difference from the Gaussian effectively. An elementary effective lower bound on \(H\) suffices: the reciprocal Euler product over \(p\le P\) dominates \(\sum_{n\le P}1/n\), and \(-\log(1-1/p)\le 1/p+1/p^2\). Hence \(H\ge\log\log P-C\) and \(V=H/2+O(1)\). No prime-number-theorem rate is needed for the model or for its slowly diverging variance.

Abstract moment determinacy is not used to claim a computable rate. A finite-order Esseen smoothing argument gives one. For a Kolmogorov tolerance \(\varepsilon\), first choose an effective Fourier cutoff \(R\) so that the smoothing correction \(C\sup_x\phi_{\rm Gauss}(x)/R\) is below \(\varepsilon/3\). For the smoothed integral through \(R\), Taylor-expand both characteristic functions through degree \(2K-1\), with remainder bounded by \(|t|^{2K}\mathbb E|X|^{2K}/(2K)!\) for real \(t,X\). The absolute moment \(2K\) is controlled by the even moment already transferred. Once \(\mathbb E|X|^{2K}\le2\mathbb E|G|^{2K}\), the contribution of the two Taylor remainders to the smoothing integral is bounded, up to the absolute smoothing factor, by
\[
 \frac{3 R^{2K}}{2K\,2^K K!}.
\]
Choose \(K\) effectively so this is below \(\varepsilon/3\). The lower moments contribute at most a known linear combination of their errors, with coefficients \(R^j/(j\,j!)\) for \(1\le j<2K\). Take \(r=2K\) in the entire analysis and increase \(Y\) until every required accuracy and even-moment bound follows from the effective estimates. This procedure only uses known constants and elementary eventually monotone majorants. One may take positive rational lower and upper bounds for \(D\) to implement an onset on any fixed computable \(D>0\). It produces an effective conditional existence theorem for an onset, not a practically usable numerical threshold.

The essential quantifier order is the requested tolerance, then the finite moment degree, then the height. This does not repair general effective character information at the omitted prime. A single deleted bounded summand is negligible after division by the diverging scalar standard deviation; the argument would not promote arbitrary coordinate observables or growing moment degree. No archive records changed in this check.

An explicit smoothing alternative avoids any dependence on the numerical constant in Esseen's theorem. Let \(Z\) have density \((1-\cos x)/(\pi x^2)\), with its removable value at zero. Its transform is \(\max(1-|t|,0)\), from the convolution of two interval indicators. Thus \(\mathbb P(|Z|>hR)\le4/(\pi hR)\). For \(Z/R\) independent and a reference Gaussian of density at most \(M\), translating by \(\pm h\) and taking the complement of this tail event gives
\[
 d_K(X,G)\le d_K(X+Z/R,G+Z/R)+2hM+8/(\pi hR).
\]
The smoothed distribution difference is the inverse Fourier integral of \((\phi_X(t)-\phi_G(t))\max(1-|t|/R,0)/(-it)\). This quotient is integrable because both first absolute moments are finite in the tail model under consideration and the transform is compactly supported. Differentiating the inverse integral gives the density difference, and Riemann–Lebesgue gives zero at the left endpoint. Therefore the first term is at most \(\pi^{-1}\int_0^R|\phi_X(t)-\phi_G(t)|\,dt/t\). Choose \(h\) small and \(R\) large from the displayed elementary constants, then follow the same finite Taylor procedure. The first absolute moment used here is of the bounded finite-prime phase statistic at fixed \(Y\), not a moment of the ordinate or of the Nakamura–Suzuki jump law.

## Comparison with the mature independent candidate

I read the complete companion proof after deriving the estimates above. It uses the stronger, directly applicable effective formulation in Ford–Green–Konyagin–Maynard–Tao, arXiv:1412.5029v3, §7, Lemma 7.1 and Corollary 6, pp. 32–33. I checked the primary PDF. Its denominator is \(\log(Q(1+|t|))\) for moduli at most \(Q\) coprime to the selected prime, and the argument explicitly avoids Siegel's theorem. Choosing \(Q=\lceil e^{\sqrt L}\rceil\) covers all polylogarithmic conductors with an effective threshold, without altering the \(\sqrt L\) size of the logarithmic denominator. The upper edge \(U+2\) and one-unit cushion used in the candidate cover both the \(O(1/L)\) complex shifts and the logarithm disks. Corollary 6 also gives the optional improvement because its exceptional prime is \(\gg\log\log Q\) and one centered summand is \(O(1/\sqrt{\ell_T})\). This improvement is not needed for effectivity. [FGKMT v3, Lemma 7.1 and Corollary 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.5029v3)

The candidate's proof uses a common prime through degree \(2r\) to obtain its Hölder bound through degree \(r\), which is at least as much moment control as the fixed even maximum used above. Its Gaussian smoothing argument is another constructive route, with explicit Gaussian tails and the same Taylor bounds. It states no numerical threshold. The direct sparse approximate-functional-equation treatment of \(J\) and the explicit Fejér smoothing above give independent ways to avoid reliance on undocumented numerical constants in those steps. I found no failure in the height-dependent deletion, primitive conductor support, restoration, or smoothing. The effective statement still concerns the normalized scalar alone. Any broader uniform effectivity claim needs additional input.
