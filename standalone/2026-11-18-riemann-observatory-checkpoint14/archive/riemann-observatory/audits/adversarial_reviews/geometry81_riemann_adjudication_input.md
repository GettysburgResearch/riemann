# Director input on Riemann's 1859 memoir

PAPER-S001, *Ueber die Anzahl der Primzahlen unter einer gegebenen Grösse*, Berlin Academy report for 3 November 1859, printed pp. 671–680. The opening names Kummer as presenter and gives Riemann's 19 October letter to Encke. This review reconciles the two independent whole-work audits. It assesses the historical text and the reconstruction, without changing canonical status. [Advocate](geometry74_riemann_whole_advocate.md) · [Skeptic](release74_riemann_whole_skeptic.md)

## Classification of the original mathematical claims

| Original locus | Historical proof status and modern check |
|---|---|
| pp. 671–673, Euler and Hankel continuation | Valid original derivation. Start in \(\Re s>1\); put the cut on the positive ray for \(\log(-x)\), use Euler reflection, and remove apparent positive-integer poles by agreement with the Dirichlet series. A fixed detour makes the contour integral entire in \(s\). For the functional equation residues use outer rectangles with imaginary boundaries at odd multiples of \(\pi\), or the skeptic's separated circles. In \(\Re s<0\) the exterior bound is \(O_s(R^{\Re s})\) and the residue sum is absolutely convergent. The pole at 1 has residue 1. The negative even zeros are simple by the completed functional equation. |
| pp. 673–674, theta route and \(\Xi\) | Valid second derivation using the explicitly cited Jacobi transformation. The correct intermediate split has \(\psi(1/x)x^{(s-3)/2}\) on \((0,1)\). The resulting tail on \((1,\infty)\) is entire and yields the symmetric completion. The two cosine formulas agree by twice integrating by parts and using \(\psi'(1)=-(1+2\psi(1))/8\). |
| p. 674, strip and total count | The memoir's Euler/symmetry argument gives the closed strip. Boundary exclusion is a later 1896 result, or can be avoided for the coarse placement by using a wider rectangle. The count has correct main terms and a relative error scale. Its contour sketch leaves top-edge argument control and endpoints unproved. The modern Jensen and Stirling argument supplies these. |
| p. 674, real zeros and RH | The empirical assertion of approximately the same number of real \(t\)-zeros is separate from the count of all zeros. There is no proof of that real-zero assertion in the publication, and the claim that all \(t\)-zeros are real is explicitly a conjecture. Neither is licensed by the analytic reconstruction. |
| p. 675, entire product | Valid conclusion with a later factorization theorem. The printed qualitative comparison of log growth is incomplete. From theta decay, \(\log\max_{|t|\le R}|\Xi(t)|=O(R\log(2+R))\). Evenness makes \(\Xi(\sqrt w)\) entire of order at most \(1/2\). Hadamard factorization there supplies genus zero and the center normalization, with the product over one member of each opposite pair and multiplicity. This also explains why a real-axis comparison of logarithms by itself would not suffice. |
| pp. 675–677, inversion | Valid midpoint transform and inversion, with exact endpoint conventions and local text repairs listed below. For \(c>1\), the exponentially tilted step function in \(\log x\) is integrable with bounded variation. Fourier inversion in symmetric height gives the midpoint. Integration by parts before splitting has boundary \(O_{x,c}(1/T)\), using the absolutely convergent original Euler logarithm. |
| pp. 677–679, explicit formula | Correct structural formula after replacing the historical additive constant and justifying passage to the ordered zero sum. The source states the latter passage without the necessary grouped remainder estimates. Von Mangoldt's subsequent proof and the skeptic's complementary modern reconstruction supply this dependency. The claim about arbitrary rearrangements is not justified by either route and plays no role. |
| pp. 679–680, Möbius inversion, density, tables | Exact finite Möbius inversion preserves midpoint weights. The derivative of a fixed finite zero truncation is a smooth surrogate for the distributional derivative of the step function. The prime-square correction and Gauss–Goldschmidt data through three million are historical discussion, without a global estimate of that scale or a persistent sign theorem. |

For modern notation use \(\xi_*(s)=s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s)/2\) and \(\Xi(t)=\xi_*(1/2+it)\). The two normalized values \(\Xi(0)\) and \(\xi_*(0)\) are different. The positive theta kernel representation gives \(\Xi(iu)>0\) for real \(u\), so the choice of one member with \(\Re\alpha>0\) in every \(\pm\alpha\) pair is legitimate. The skeptic obtains the same absence of real \(s\)-zeros via the eta series. For \(\rho=1/2+i\alpha\), the opposite partner is \(1-\rho\), which need not equal \(\bar\rho\).

## Count, chronology and endpoint conventions

If the top boundary avoids zero ordinates, the argument principle with multiplicity gives \(N(T)=1+\vartheta(T)/\pi+S(T)\), where \(\vartheta(T)=\Im\log\Gamma(1/4+iT/2)-(T/2)\log\pi\) on its continuous branch and \(S(T)=\pi^{-1}\arg\zeta(1/2+iT)\) via \(2\to2+iT\to1/2+iT\). Stirling gives \(T\log(T/(2\pi))/(2\pi)-T/(2\pi)+7/8+S(T)+O(1/T)\). The horizontal argument bound counts zeros of the real-part continuation \(g_T(z)=(\zeta(z+iT)+\zeta(z-iT))/2\). Jensen gives \(O(\log T)\) real crossings on the fixed segment because \(g_T(2)\ge2-\zeta(2)>0\), and \(g_T\) has polynomial bounds on a slightly larger fixed disk for large \(T\). Between crossings the argument varies by at most \(\pi\). This also supplies \(O(\log(2+T))\) zeros in a bounded-width height window. At a zero ordinate one may specify either one-sided count or the midpoint convention. The distinction does not affect the main terms or these errors.

The original p. 674 “Bruchtheil” of order \(1/T\) is relative. The first rigorous historical count to distinguish here is von Mangoldt 1895, p. 266, with additive \(O(\log^2 T)\). His 1905 paper p. 1 expressly cites that earlier estimate, \(0.34(\log T)^2+1.35\log T+2.58\) for \(T>12\), and states the sharper additive \(O(\log T)\) bound for \(T>28.558\); the last printed page gives its final estimate. The modern Jensen argument is a reconstruction and is not the text of the 1859 proof. None of these total counts shows that asymptotically all of the zeros are on the critical line. ## Edition-specific corrections

- **First print p. 676.** The substitution display for \(\log\zeta\) omits the sum before the single shifted quadratic log. The product on p. 675 and later summed expressions identify the intended formula. The 1892 p. 150 text and both Wilkins PDFs supply the sum.
- **First print p. 677.** The simple contour integral with denominator \((\beta-s)\beta\) has residue \(-x^\beta/\beta\) when \(x>1\) and the vertical line is to the right of \(\beta\ne0\). Both later witnesses insert a leading minus before the integral. The adjacent derivative of \(s^{-1}\log(1-s/\beta)\) is correct. This sign slip is distinct from the constant.
- **First print pp. 678 and 680.** The additive constant \(+\log\Xi(0)\) is wrong. The linear zero factors come from
  \[
  1+(s-\tfrac12)^2/\alpha^2
   = (1+1/(4\alpha^2))(1-s/\rho)(1-s/(1-\rho)).
  \]
  Compatible paired logarithms give \(D=\sum_\alpha\log(1+1/(4\alpha^2))=\log\xi_*(0)-\log\Xi(0)\). The final constant is \(\log\xi_*(0)=-\log2\). The same \(D\) produces \(-D\log x\) in the p. 678 displayed centered-log contour comparison, if the comparison is read literally (the display has the opposite sign to the integrated-by-parts identity for \(J_0\)). Genocchi pp. 57–58 gives the correction in eq. (11), and 1892 p. 155 explicitly records it while retaining the old value in the body. The 1892 speculation about a writing or printing slip does not determine the origin. [Genocchi primary source](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF03198027) · [1892 collected witness](../../sources/history_round4/riemann1892_collected.pdf)
- **1892 explanatory notes.** Note (2) on pp. 154–155 separates the even and odd Fourier inverses. The two pieces individually involve \(H(y)\pm H(-y)\), whose sum gives the claimed general inversion. Note (3) explains the distinct \(\operatorname{Li}\) contours. The quoted draft letter flags two pending proofs, namely the number of real roots and the match between the ordered series and the finite-height contour limit. This differs from attributing an RH proof or a full count proof to the draft.
- **Wilkins-only defects.** Both standalone p. 3 texts use \(1\) to \(\infty\) for the intermediate \(\psi(1/x)\) term, which at \(s=2\) tends to \(1/2\) and is divergent. Original p. 673 and 1892 p. 147 have \(0\) to \(1\), and all later final theta displays are correct. Both standalone p. 5 versions use \(ds\) for the preliminary elementary integrals with respect to \(x\); original p. 675 gives \(dx\). On p. 9 the English “in the first hundred thousand” represents German “schon vom ersten Hunderttausend an,” i.e. from that range onward, not just within it. These do not belong to the first-print findings.

## Exact explicit formula and later dependency

Write \(\pi_0(x)=\#\{p<x\}+\tfrac12\mathbf1_{x\text{ prime}}\) and \(J_0(x)=\sum_{m\ge1}\pi_0(x^{1/m})/m\). Then \(J_0(p^m)\) includes an increment \(1/(2m)\) at the jump of size \(1/m\), and \(J_0(2)=1/2,\ J_0(4)=9/4\). For \(x>1\), use the real principal-value \(\operatorname{Li}(x)\) and, for nonreal \(\rho\), the branch-complete primitive
\[
E_\rho(x)=\int_{-\infty+i\Im\rho}^{\rho}\frac{e^{w\log x}}w\,dw
=\operatorname{Ei}(\rho\log x)-i\pi\operatorname{sgn}(\Im\rho).
\]
The equality fixes the \(\operatorname{Ei}\) branch by continuation from the positive real convention. A principal logarithm of the number \(x^\rho\) in an \(\operatorname{Li}\) composition would wrap its argument and change the terms. With multiplicities and the **symmetric height cutoff**,
\[
J_0(x)=\operatorname{Li}(x)-\lim_{T\to\infty}\sum_{|\Im\rho|\le T}E_\rho(x)
  +\int_x^\infty\frac{du}{u(u^2-1)\log u}-\log2 .
\]
Conjugation cancels the imaginary branch offsets exactly at each cutoff. The same value is obtained by pairing \(\rho,1-\rho\) and ordering by Riemann's \(|\alpha|\), since the strip bounds the cutoff displacement and the \(O(\log T)\) local count times individual \(O_x(T^{-1})\) terms tends to zero. No unrestricted reordering follows. The separately singular form is only for \(x>1\); \(J_0(x)=0\) for \(0<x<2\), and \(\pi_0(x)=\sum_{m\ge1}\mu(m)J_0(x^{1/m})/m\) is a finite inversion for fixed positive \(x\).

The named 1895 source is *Zu Riemanns Abhandlung…*, J. reine angew. Math. 114, 255–305. On pp. 283–285, eqs. (43)–(46), von Mangoldt evaluates each paired resolvent contribution and states the growing-limit question. The exact decomposition is p. 286, eq. (49), the finite growing sums are pp. 287–289, and the infinite tails are pp. 289–291, eqs. (51)–(54). At p. 292 eq. (55) he establishes the ordered limit by applying local count bounds to harmonic shell sums, not by summing pointwise error estimates without control. On pp. 294–295 his step-function definition specifies the midpoint at jumps; p. 299 eq. (64) specializes to \(r=0\), prints the corrected \(-\log 2\), and the comparison through the start of p. 302 identifies the principal value and the \(\operatorname{Li}\) branches. Retain this as the later primary theorem rather than crediting it to Riemann's printed argument. Von Mangoldt p. 302 also comments on the two claims in the draft quoted by the editors. His rigorous total count and explicit formula verify those aspects that his proof treats, while the total count cannot establish the separate number of real roots. [Source audit of the grouped remainder](../../papers/PAPER-H406.md) · [1895 original](https://doi.org/10.1515/crll.1895.114.255)

The skeptic's independent modern route is compatible with that dependency. Invert the normalized product after dividing its logarithm by \(s^3\), where the paired zero log sum is \(O((1+|t|)\log^2(2+|t|))\) on a fixed line \(c>1\), so the inversion is absolute. Differentiating twice gives a distributional identity in \(y=\log x>0\). For the ordinary representative instead differentiate the log product in \(s\), and invert \(-\zeta'/(\zeta s)\) in symmetric height. Partial fractions in each zero term isolate the truncated Perron kernel at \(\rho\) and at 0. The local count and bounds \(O(1/(1+||\Im\rho|-T|))\) on oscillatory tails give a grouped \(O_x(\log^2(2+T)/T)\) remainder. The resulting \(\sum x^\rho/\rho\) has the midpoint representative from the original von Mangoldt summatory step function. The difference \(E_\rho(x)-x^\rho/(\rho\log x)\) is \(O_x((1+|\Im\rho|)^{-2})\) uniformly on compact \(x>1\). Thus the explicit-formula zero sum has that same midpoint convention after adding continuous terms and dividing by \(\log x\). This bridges the distributional identity to all \(x>1\), including every prime power, and avoids relying on equality only almost everywhere at a jump.

**Adjudication recommendation.** The two complete readings support recording an independently reconstructed whole historical proof audit. Flag the known Genocchi constant correction and local text findings with their edition labels. Preserve the named later inputs for the zero-free boundary/result scale, factorization, and ordered explicit formula. Neither the claimed real-zero asymptotic nor RH was established in this memoir.
