# Third independent check of the single-Mangoldt strata

18 November 2026. The short-factor, shared-prime and genuine-power estimates pass. The square bounds are uniform on dyadic modulus blocks, with their thresholds applied to the prime \(p\), not to the square \(p^2\). The stated estimates leave coprime primes and a genuine square interval at the largest moduli. I found no counterexample or loss of uniformity in the removed strata. [Poisson and pointwise proof](prime101_coprime_prime_strata.md) · [Estermann proof and square ranges](weighted101_single_mangoldt_strata.md)

## Exact modulus and two distinct quantifiers

Fix \(0<a<b\), \(\theta=4b\in(1/2,2/3)\), \(T\asymp Y\), and the supported coefficients of \(D^4\). For a fixed prime power \(\nu=p^j\), put \(g=(k,\nu)\), \(q=k/g\), \(M=kT/\nu\) and \(\delta=H/T\). The unit projection modulo \(k\) reduces exactly to that modulo \(q\), by the equal fibers of reduction and the invertibility of \(\nu/g\). There is no coprimality condition on the divisor variable. The term for \(q=1\) vanishes. Nonempty support of the original sum implies \(\nu\le c_1kT\) for a fixed cutoff constant and \(M\ge 1/c_1\).

For the rapid region, split the divisor product smoothly so the designated factor \(d\ll\sqrt M\). In the other factor the Fourier scale is \(L_d=kH/(\nu d)\). The term with \(q\mid d\) cancels altogether, and otherwise the closest frequency from a unit numerator is at distance at least \((q,d)/q\). The product of these two quantities is \(H(k,\nu d)/(\nu d)\ge Hg/(\nu d)\), using \((k,\nu d)=g(q,d)\). Thus the lower scale is \(Hg/\sqrt{kT\nu}\). When this is large, \(L_d\gg1\) and the nonnearest lattice samples with unit spacing sum at the same or better decay. The trivial divisor bound covers the complementary bounded-scale regime. Harmonic summation over \(d\) yields
\[
 |S_{k,\nu}|\ll_B M\log(2+M)
 \left(1+\frac{Hg}{\sqrt{kT\nu}}\right)^{-B},
\]
for arbitrary fixed \(B>1\) and uniformly in the modulus and numerator. The pre-decay outer absolute total is at most \(yT\log^{O(1)} T\), since \(M=kT/\nu\), the weights are \(O(\log^3 T)\) throughout effective support, and the remaining \(1/\nu\) is harmonically summable up to \(O(yT)\). Thus for every fixed \(\kappa>(1+\theta)/2\), an extra power margin \(\nu/g^2\le(H^2/(kT))T^{-\delta_0}\) gives arbitrary rapid decay in the aggregate. The choice \(j\le2v_p(k)\) with \(p\mid k\) lies in this region if \(0<\delta_0<2\kappa-1-\theta\).

The separate global pointwise estimate \(|S_{k,\nu}|\ll_\eta qT^\eta\) uses a chosen \(\kappa\), not all fixed \(\kappa>(1+\theta)/2\). To see the exact quantifiers, let \(\eta>0\) be fixed first. Choose \(\kappa<1\) with \(2(1-\kappa)<\eta\), also within the required saddle range. Choose a fixed real \(B>0\) with \(2(1-\kappa)+2B(\theta+1-\kappa)<\eta\). On \(\Re s=-B\), each Hurwitz Fourier series is absolutely convergent, with Dirichlet exponent \(1+B\). Finite orthogonality gives a bilinear sum of size exactly \(q\) for each unit numerator, because one residue class of the second variable is uniquely selected. A pole-plus-regular expansion at 1 makes both polar coefficients independent of the numerator. Thus one can shift the centered Mellin integral without poles. The bound is \(\ll_B q\delta^{-2}(q^2/(M\delta^2))^B\). With \(q\ll T^\theta\) and \(M\gg1\) this gives the desired exponent. An arbitrarily small \(B\) affects the implied constants only, since it is fixed independently of \(T,k,\nu\). For the explicit dual length, \(\sum_{h>N}d_2(h)h^{-1-B}\ll_B N^{-B}\log(2N)\) by partial summation of the elementary divisor bound. This confirms the dual threshold \(U=q^2/(M\delta^2)\) and tail cutoff \(N\ge\max(1,U)T^\varepsilon\) with any fixed positive margin. The two cosine signs can be retained separately in the exact formula. Under the initial negative phase the equal cosine signs give \(e(\overline{\nu/g}\,h/q)\) with the cosine factor and opposite signs give \(e(-\overline{\nu/g}\,h/q)\). Averaging uses inversion on the unit group and gives the normalized Ramanujan term for each dual index, including noncoprime ones.

## Common factors, higher powers, and the square cutoff

On the actual supported moduli, every \(p\mid k\) lies above \(Y^a\), and there are at most four such primes. Nonempty support gives \(p^j\ll kT\ll Y^{1+\theta}\), hence \(j\le(1+\theta)/a+O_{a,b}(1)\). In particular, all values of \(j\), including those past \(2v_p(k)\), are covered by the single pointwise estimate. Since \(q/k\le1/p\), the entire shared-prime contribution is \(O(T^{\theta-a+\eta}\log^3 T)\). Ignoring the gain \(1/p\) still gives a valid weaker bound.

For \(j\ge3\), there are \(O(X^{1/3}\log(2X))\) prime powers up to \(X\). This follows by counting bases by integers for each exponent through \(\log_2 X\), which is enough here. On dyadic shells above \(T\) it gives \(\sum_{p^j>T,j\ge3}p^{-j}\ll T^{-2/3}\log(2T)\). For powers at most \(T\), the pointwise bound costs \(O(T^\eta)\) per power after dividing by \(k\). Above \(T\) the elementary bound is \(O(T\log T/p^j)\) after division. The outer count is \(O(y)\), yielding \(O(T^{\theta+1/3+\eta}\log^C T)\). The weight is still bounded by its support-dependent \(O(\log^3 T)\) when the positive reciprocal majorant is extended beyond support. Taking \(\eta<2/3-\theta\) leaves a power margin, which also suffices for the shared factors.

For genuine squares on a dyadic block \(k\asymp K\), the bounds per \(k\) and prime after dividing by \(k\), with fixed log factors omitted, are
\[
 \min(T^\eta,T/p^2),\qquad p\ll\sqrt{KT}.
\]
The support cap in the prime variable is \(\sqrt{KT}\), not \(KT\). The balance between the two pointwise bounds occurs at \(p=T^{(1-\eta)/2}\), not at \(p^2=\sqrt T\). Splitting at this prime cutoff gives \(\ll K T^{(1+\eta)/2}\) for the full block up to logs. Thus the bounds remove all squares when \(K\le T^{1/2-\omega}\) for fixed \(\omega>0\) and \(\eta<2\omega\). The simpler split at \(p=\sqrt T\) suffices with the stronger restriction \(\eta<\omega\).

For general \(K\), remove primes \(p\le T^{1-\upsilon}/K\) by counting bases. Their block cost is \(O(T^{1-\upsilon+\eta})\) up to logs. Remove \(p\ge KT^\upsilon\) by summing \(p^{-2}\), with cost \(O(T^{1-\upsilon})\). These assertions use a fixed \(\upsilon>\eta\), so summing over \(O(\log T)\) dyadic blocks preserves a power saving. In the coprime square case the rapid short-factor criterion is \(p\le (H/\sqrt{kT})T^{-\delta_0/2}\), up to fixed cutoff constants, and is applied independently. The middle square range is the intersection of the complementary conditions and the actual support. It can be nonempty when \(K\asymp T^\theta\). For instance sufficiently small fixed \(\upsilon<(1-\theta)/2\) puts the two displayed block thresholds on either side of the balancing scale for large moduli, while the upper threshold is still within the support scale.

The choice of \(\eta\) can satisfy all these fixed positive margins simultaneously, after which one fixed \(\kappa\) and one fixed small \(B\) suffice for the pointwise argument. A separate large \(B\) may be used for rapid tails without changing the cutoff. The square-root character calculation for the surviving primes establishes a limitation of termwise absolute summation. It supplies no lower bound for the signed arithmetic sum. Neither the remaining coprime prime/square estimate nor the derivative-weighted fourth moment is proved. The eliminated smooth strata are unconditional, while transfer to the zero statistic retains the earlier RH hypothesis. No canonical record was changed.
