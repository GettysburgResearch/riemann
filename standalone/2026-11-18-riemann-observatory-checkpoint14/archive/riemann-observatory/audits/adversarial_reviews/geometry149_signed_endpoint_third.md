# Third independent check of the signed Mellin boundary

2026-11-18. The conditional-convergence argument passes. For \(p>1\), finite area norm in the indicated enlarged strip, the original value envelopes, and local \(H^1\) at logarithmic coordinate zero imply the ordinary compensated formula with raw symmetric height summation. In fact each half-height sum converges. When the literal Bombieri–Lagarias condition (A) is used with the area norm, the local regularity follows from (A) and need not be separately added. This is a next-round result. It leaves the dated Round 15 snapshot, the absolute counterexample, and the unrestricted continuous-class endpoint question in place.

The Bombieri–Lagarias author manuscript p. 5 says both \(f\) and \(f'\) have at most first-kind discontinuities at the finitely many exceptional points, and gives \(f\) its midpoint there. ## Independent trace and cutoff estimates

Write \(w_p(t)=(1+t^2)^{1/2}\log^p(e+|t|)\), and let \(N_p\) be the square root of the area integral with \(-\epsilon\le\Re s\le1+\epsilon\), \(0<\epsilon<\delta\). Exponential envelopes imply holomorphy on the larger open strip. A small fixed disk about every point of \([0,1]+i[n,n+1]\) is contained in the area strip. Expand in a disk power series and integrate \(|h|^2\) to control both its constant and linear coefficients, bounded by fixed powers of the disk radius. The disks are covered by a fixed enlargement of the cell and the weight is comparable to \(w_p(n)\) there. Summing over the bounded overlaps yields
\[
\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}w_p(n)\sup_{\substack{0\le\beta\le1\\n\le t\le n+1}}
(|h(\beta+it)|+|h'(\beta+it)|)^2\ll_{\epsilon,p}N_p^2.
\]
Both the area-to-line estimate and derivative estimate are uniform in cell and horizontal position.

For \(a_\rho=|\Re\rho-1/2|\), reflect left zeros to right zeros at the same height, preserving multiplicity. A layer-cake integral from the cited density estimate gives, for \(T\ge3\),
\[
\sum_{T\le\gamma\le2T}a_\rho^2
\ll T\log T\int_0^{1/2}v e^{-cv\log T}\,dv \ll T/\log T.
\]
Using cumulative counts up to \(3T\) bypasses any strict upper endpoint convention in the density input. If \(A_n=\sum_{\gamma\in[n,n+1)}a_\rho\), the local count and cell Cauchy–Schwarz give \(\sum_{T\le n<2T}A_n^2\ll T\) on integer dyadic blocks. Therefore \(\sum A_n^2/w_p(n)\) has dyadic cost \(O((\log T)^{-p})\). The holomorphic derivative estimate controls the sum of the individual absolute differences \(|h(\rho)-H(\gamma)|\) by \(C N_p\). Exactly the same bound applies at negative heights, or to any subcollection of a dyadic block. No grouping of zero signs is needed for this step.

For the projected sum use \(E(t)=N(t)-M(t)\) and \(N(t)=\#\{0<\gamma\le t\}\). Stieltjes integration by parts in \((B,T]\) gives the smooth term \(\int_B^T HM'\), the difference of \(HE\) at the endpoints, and \(-\int_B^TH'E\). The uniform upper boundary is \(O(N_p\log T/\sqrt{w_p(T)})=o(1)\). On each dyadic block
\[
\int_T^{2T}|E(t)|^2/w_p(t)\,dt\ll (\log\log T)/(\log T)^p,
\]
so the \(H'E\) integral is absolutely convergent by weighted Cauchy–Schwarz. At an exact ordinate, the entire jump contributes at most the cell zero count times the supremum of \(h\), again \(o(1)\). Thus strict, inclusive and midpoint upper endpoints agree in the limit, for both axes. An arbitrary final subinterval of a dyadic block is bounded by the same nonnegative weighted mass, without assuming short-interval Selberg estimates.

## The main log density and the approximation step

The remaining smooth density integral on each axis needs an argument distinct from the \(H'E\) estimate. Cut \(F\) using a fixed compact smooth \(\chi=1\) near zero, supported inside the local \(H^1\) neighborhood. The transform of \(\chi F\) is absolutely integrable against \(\log(2+|t|)\) by Plancherel and Cauchy–Schwarz with \(1+t^2\). For the remaining part set \(m(u)=(1-\chi(u))/(iu)\), smoothly extended at zero, and \(G(t)=\int m(u)F(u)e^{itu}du\). Differentiation is justified by the exponential envelopes and gives \(G'=\widehat{(1-\chi)F}\) for the positive sign convention. All weak derivatives of \(m\), including order zero, are in \(L^2\). Cauchy–Schwarz with sufficiently many Fourier powers gives \(\widehat m\) in \(L^1\) with every fixed polynomial weight. Moderate-weight convolution then bounds \(G\) in \(L^2(w_p)\) by \(C N_p\). The same is true of \(G'\), using \(\chi\) and the identity. Local one-dimensional Sobolev on intervals of unit size gives \(|G(T)|\ll N_p w_p(T)^{-1/2}\). For \(M'(t)=\log(t/(2\pi))/(2\pi)\), integration by parts now makes \(G(T)M'(T)\) vanish and leaves an absolutely integrable remainder \(G(t)/(2\pi t)\). Negative heights use \(G(-t)\) with opposite derivative sign. This proves continuity of the zero limit in \(N_p+\|F\|_{H^1(U)}\) for a fixed small neighborhood \(U\). The dyadic argument does not prove validity or failure at \(p=1\).

For identification use \(\chi_*(u/R)F(u)\), then compact smooth convolution with small radius. At each \(\beta\) the cutoff corresponds to a uniformly bounded weighted approximate identity on the frequency line, with uniform bound because the kernels are scaled Schwartz functions and the weight is polynomially moderate. The convolution instead multiplies by the Fourier transform of the exponentially tilted mollifier, uniformly bounded on the closed strip and tending pointwise to one. Dominated convergence over \(\beta\) and a diagonal choice give norm convergence. On a smaller fixed neighborhood of zero the cutoff is eventually one, and the convolution converges in local \(H^1\) by a compact localization inside \(U\). Pointwise convergence and uniform exponential envelopes follow from original global continuity and bounded compact convolution radii. They control the arithmetic sum, real-axis Mellin terms and infinite archimedean region. Near zero in \(u\), the compensated numerator is \(O(|u|^{1/2})\) uniformly along the approximants by the local \(H^1\) bound, which controls the simple reciprocal singularity. The ordinary classical compact smooth formula therefore passes to the limit. No use of the manuscript's unrestricted auxiliary formula is required.

## Scope of the consequence

The prior \(p=3\) smooth witness still diverges absolutely. It vanishes near one and consequently has signed symmetric convergence by this theorem. The newly covered class includes every literal (A)(B) test with finite strip norm \(p>1\). It leaves open whether the value envelopes and \(p=3\) norm alone suffice for generic continuous tests without the first-kind derivative premise or another local regularity condition. A proof for that larger class, a boundary theorem at \(p=1\), and publication priority remain separate.
