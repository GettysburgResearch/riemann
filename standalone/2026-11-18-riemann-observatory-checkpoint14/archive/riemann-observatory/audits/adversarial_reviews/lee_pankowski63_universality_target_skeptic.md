# Target hypotheses in Lee–Pańkowski's short-interval universality statements

17 November 2026. Internal version comparison of Yoonbok Lee and Łukasz Pańkowski, *Universality of the zeta function in short intervals*. I checked the theorem statements, local definitions and their proof dependency on the target logarithm. The Rouché obstruction below separates the RH-conditional assertion from the unconditional local limsup. [ArXiv v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.15364v1) · [Journal version](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11139-025-01144-0)

## Literal text and intended target class

The journal text defines \(\mathcal U=\{s:1/2<\Re s<1\}\) and \(D_r(s_0)=\{s:|s-s_0|\le r\}\) in §1, p. 1, for use throughout the paper. It first describes Voronin's nonvanishing target class. Theorem A on p. 2, attributed to Laurinčikas, explicitly assumes a compact \(K\subset\mathcal U\) with connected complement and a nonvanishing continuous \(f\) on \(K\) analytic in its interior. The text then announces the goal of shortening \(H\) in Theorem A, which gives a natural explanation for the intended nonvanishing target class.

The printed Theorems 1.1 and 1.2, pp. 2–3, each restate the target hypothesis as “a continuous function on \(D\), analytic in the interior of \(D\).” Their local setup defines no \(H(K)\) or \(H^*(K)\) target class that imports nonvanishing. The domain is a closed disc contained in \(\mathcal U\), with no empty-interior or vertical-segment restriction. The connected-complement statement belongs to Theorem A and the later extension remark. Theorem 1.1 assumes RH and states a positive liminf of the good-shift proportion on \([T,T+(\log T)^B]\). Theorem 1.2 states a positive limsup on such windows without RH. The abstract's “positive upper density” refers to this local normalization. The publisher HTML and the arXiv v1 wording match the statements visually checked in the PDF. [Archived journal PDF, pp. 1–3](../../sources/classical63_derivative_moments/lp2025print.pdf) · [Archived arXiv v1, pp. 1–2](../../sources/classical63_derivative_moments/lee_pankowski2025.pdf) · [Springer full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11139-025-01144-0)

The proof explicitly requires a logarithm of the target. The line following Lemma 2.4 on p. 5 sets \(g=\log f\). Section 3, p. 7, applies that lemma and obtains (8) by approximating \(\log f\). The final estimate in §3 compares \(\log\zeta(s+i\tau)\) with \(\log f(s)\). Section 4 copies that argument on zero-free blocks provided by Lemma 2.3. A continuous, zero-free \(f\) on a closed disc has a continuous logarithm there by the covering property of the exponential map on a contractible domain. The logarithm is holomorphic inside because it agrees locally with analytic branches. This supplies the target hypothesis needed by the displayed argument. An interior zero prevents such a logarithm. [Journal §§2–4](../../sources/classical63_derivative_moments/lp2025print.pdf)

## Rouché obstruction and absolute density scope

Take \(s_0=3/4\), \(r=1/8\), \(D=D_r(s_0)\), \(f(s)=s-s_0\), and \(\epsilon=1/16\). These satisfy the literal domain and target assumptions in both theorems. For example \(B=20\) meets both window-exponent thresholds, which are 8 and 16 for this disc. The translated zeta function is holomorphic on a neighborhood of \(D\) for every real \(\tau\), since the pole has real part 1. If
\[
 \max_{s\in D}|\zeta(s+i\tau)-f(s)|<\epsilon,
\]
then on the boundary the error is smaller than \(|f(s)|=r\). Rouché's theorem forces exactly one zero of \(\zeta(s+i\tau)\) in the interior, counted with multiplicity. Its real part exceeds \(5/8\). Under RH there are no such shifted discs, so the good-shift set is empty for every window. This contradicts the literal positive conclusion of Theorem 1.1 under its stated hypothesis. It makes the unrestricted limsup in Theorem 1.2 zero under RH as well.

Unconditionally, let \(E\) be the set of good shifts for this example. Each \(\tau\in E\cap[0,X]\) is within \(r\) of the ordinate of a zero with real part greater than \(5/8\). With \(\sigma=9/16\), the unconditional density bound in paper equation (3) gives
\[
 \mu(E\cap[0,X])\le 2r N(9/16,X+r+1)+O(1)
       \ll X^{15/16}(\log X)^5=o(X).
\]
This refutes a positive global upper density for the example without RH. It also shows that the short-window good-shift proportion is exactly zero for all but \(o(X)\) start points \(T\in[X,2X]\). Indeed a window with any good shift requires a zero of the indicated real part with ordinate in \([T-r,T+(\log(2X))^B+r]\). Cover those starts by intervals of length \((\log(2X))^B+2r\) around the relevant ordinates. The covered measure is \(\ll X^{15/16}(\log X)^{B+5}=o(X)\).

This argument yields a local liminf of zero and leaves open the local limsup over exceptional starts. The distinction is necessary: placing \(O((\log T_j)^B)\) abstract ordinates one unit apart near very sparse \(T_j=\exp(2^j)\) is compatible with the global density bound, and can fill a positive fraction of the corresponding windows after thickening each ordinate by \(r\). These are hypothetical ordinates used to compare the counting hypotheses, with no assertion about actual zeros or approximations. Theorem 1.2's proof specifically uses the zero-free windows of Lemma 2.3, on which the Rouché witness is impossible regardless of RH. Thus the missing logarithm hypothesis is material to that proof. The global density input alone does not refute the printed unconditional short-window limsup.

The corrected closed-disc formulation for the logarithmic proof takes \(f\) nonvanishing everywhere on \(D\). If that formulation is granted, uniform approximation extends it to a target holomorphic and zero-free in the open disc, continuous on \(D\), by replacing \(f(s)\) with \(f(s_0+\delta(s-s_0))\) for \(\delta<1\) sufficiently close to 1. Such replacements are zero-free on the closed disc. The identically zero target is also a uniform limit of small nonzero constants. These observations leave the isolated interior zero in the witness excluded.
