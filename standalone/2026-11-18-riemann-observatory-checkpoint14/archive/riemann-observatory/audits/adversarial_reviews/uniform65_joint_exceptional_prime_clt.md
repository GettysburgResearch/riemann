# Joint prime-phase limits and the scope of exceptional-prime deletion

18 November 2026. Assume RH. The exceptional-prime argument extends to fixed-dimensional Gaussian limits for deterministic arrays of first-harmonic prime weights, including overlapping bands and simultaneous sine and cosine statistics. The decisive condition is smallness of each prime's contribution **after** the proposed normalization. Raw variance divergence is one way to obtain this condition. It is unnecessary for moving bands in which all contributing primes tend to infinity. This extension uses the effective restricted prefix estimate established in the scalar audit. No numerical threshold or publication priority claim is made. [Effective scalar audit](uniform64_exceptional_prime_clt_skeptic.md) · [Uniform HLZ source audit](uniform64_hlz_third_review.md)

## 1. Statement

Fix \(d\ge1\) and \(D>0\). Use the normalized strict two-sided derivative tail on \(|\gamma|>Y\), with masses proportional to \(|\zeta'(1/2+i\gamma)|^2/(1/4+\gamma^2)\). Write \(L=\log Y\), \(P=L^D\), and \(U_p=e^{-i\gamma\log p}\). Let \(a_{p,Y},b_{p,Y}\in\mathbb R^d\) be deterministic columns after any chosen normalization, with support \(p\le P\). Put
\[
 X_Y=\sum_{p\le P}\left[a_{p,Y}\bigl(\Re U_p-p^{-1/2}\bigr)+b_{p,Y}\Im U_p\right],
 \quad
 \Sigma_Y=\sum_{p\le P} v_p(a_{p,Y}a_{p,Y}^{\mathsf T}+b_{p,Y}b_{p,Y}^{\mathsf T}),
 \quad v_p=\frac{1-1/p}{2}.
\]
Suppose \(\Sigma_Y\to\Sigma\) is a finite matrix and
\[
 \lambda_Y=\max_{p\le P}(\|a_{p,Y}\|+\|b_{p,Y}\|)\to0 .
\]
Then \(X_Y\Rightarrow N_d(0,\Sigma)\), including singular \(\Sigma\). This has an effective conditional onset in the bounded-Lipschitz distance for fixed computable \(d,D,\Sigma\), a computable bound for \(\|\Sigma_Y\|\), and computable moduli of convergence for \(\lambda_Y\to0\) and \(\Sigma_Y\to\Sigma\). In the positive-definite standardized case \(\Sigma=I_d\), it also gives an effective lower-orthant Kolmogorov onset. The same orthant conclusion holds when every limiting marginal variance is positive.

For unnormalized columns \(A_p,B_p\) with covariance \(C_Y\) positive definite, take \(a_p=C_Y^{-1/2}A_p\) and \(b_p=C_Y^{-1/2}B_p\). The exact criterion is smallness of the maximal normalized column, with \(\Sigma_Y=I_d\). Uniformly bounded unnormalized columns and \(\lambda_{\min}(C_Y)\to\infty\) are sufficient. Individual coordinate variance divergence, even for all \(d\) coordinates, does not substitute for a condition on every normalized direction.

More generally, without the smallness needed for a Gaussian conclusion, the prefix-deletion argument still gives effective agreement of all fixed directional moments with the full product Poisson model if
\[
 \lambda_Y^{\rm exc}
 :=\sup_{c\log\log Y\le p\le P}(\|a_{p,Y}\|+\|b_{p,Y}\|)\ \longrightarrow 0
\]
effectively, with \(c>0\) a sufficiently small effective constant from the exceptional-prime theorem. This uses the largest prime factor in Ford–Green–Konyagin–Maynard–Tao, §7 Corollary 6 with conductor range \(\lceil e^{\sqrt{\log T}}\rceil\) at prefix height \(T\ge Y\). Thus large coefficients at a fixed finite initial set do not themselves obstruct effective comparison to the product model. They can obstruct Gaussianity. Without this refinement it suffices to use \(\lambda_Y\). A more precise sufficient condition for the restoration step is smallness of the coefficient at whichever prime can be omitted, uniformly over the required prefix heights. The displayed supremum gives that without identifying the prime. [FGKMT, Lemma 7.1 and Corollary 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.5029v3)

## 2. Fourier support and signed moment comparison

At each height \(T\ge Y\) use the effective prime-excluding HLZ estimate, with one prime \(\ell_T\) selected for the largest moment order under consideration. All nonprincipal terms for the exceptional conductor vanish through the factor \(a(kq)\) when the divisor-closed padded monomial support omits \(\ell_T\). The same proof works for symmetric prefixes with both ordinate signs, because a complex character expectation there equals the real positive-height expectation used in the scalar audit. This is important for sine observables. It does not require a one-sided imaginary-character estimate. In the model the coordinates are independent Poisson phases of radii \(r_p=p^{-1/2}\), and its character expectation at \(m/n\) is \(\kappa(m,n)=(m,n)/\sqrt{mn}\ge0\).

Fix a real direction \(u\) with \(\|u\|\le R\), a finite constant. Write \(\alpha_p=u^{\mathsf T}a_{p,Y}\), \(\beta_p=u^{\mathsf T}b_{p,Y}\), and \(d_p=(\alpha_p^2+\beta_p^2)^{1/2}\). Since \(v_p\ge1/4\), the trace bound on \(\Sigma_Y\) bounds \(\sum(\|a_p\|^2+\|b_p\|^2)\), and hence \(\sum d_p^2\le C R^2\). For the raw retained sum, the coefficients of \(U_p\) and \(U_p^{-1}\) are \((\alpha_p\mp i\beta_p)/2\); their absolute values are \(d_p/2\). Therefore the coefficientwise absolute value of its raw \(j\)-th Fourier power is bounded by the coefficients of
\[
 \left(\sum_{p\le P,\ p\ne\ell_T} d_p\,\Re U_p\right)^j.
\]
Grouping coincident characters or reducing the rational numerator and denominator only decreases the absolute majorant. Before reduction every word has \(mn\le P^j\). Thus one fixed analytic exponent \(A=Dr\) covers every word through any fixed maximum \(r\), at every height \(T\ge Y\). Signed and complex coefficients cause no loss of the crucial factor \(\kappa(m,n)\).

The unweighted total of the majorant coefficients is at most \((\sum d_p)^j\), with \(\sum d_p\le \sqrt{P}(\sum d_p^2)^{1/2}\). The \(\kappa\)-weighted total is the retained model raw moment of that majorant. Its mean has absolute value at most \(\sum d_p/\sqrt p\le(CR^2H_P)^{1/2}\), where \(H_P=\sum_{p\le P}1/p\). Its centered moments are uniformly bounded at every fixed order. For example, the log moment generating function for a centered bounded summand has second derivative equal to a tilted variance bounded by a quarter of its squared range, here at most \(d_p^2\). The resulting sub-Gaussian bound is uniform after summing \(\sum d_p^2\). The raw majorant moment is consequently \(O_{j,R,C}((1+\sqrt{H_P})^j)\), as is the same bound for mixed contributions after binomial centering. The actual projected centering constant has modulus at most \(\sum d_p/\sqrt p\). Its terms in the centered polynomial therefore incur the same majorant.

Apply the effective retained Fourier error to the raw expansion and only then center, using triangle inequalities. The retained centered moment discrepancy at degree \(j\le r\) is bounded by an effective multiple of
\[
 \frac{\log(2P)}{\log T}(1+\sqrt{H_P})^j+
 (\log T)^{-B}(1+\sqrt P+\sqrt{H_P})^r.
\]
Choosing \(B>Dr/2+2\) makes this error effectively small uniformly for \(T\ge Y\). The formula identifies the two relevant coefficient sums for more general Fourier test families: the \(\kappa\)-weighted absolute coefficient sum times the reduced frequency logarithm, and the ordinary absolute coefficient sum. Both must be controlled in addition to fitting the supported rational characters into the range of the prefix theorem. For the first-harmonic arrays above the \(\ell_2\) bound makes both automatic at a fixed polylogarithmic cutoff; there is no extra \(\ell_1\) restriction after normalization.

Choose a common omission prime for all degrees up to a sufficiently large **even** \(r\). That even moment gives effective uniform bounds on retained prefix absolute moments, since the derivative weights are nonnegative under RH. For every direction in the fixed ball, the full centered scalar differs pointwise from the retained one by at most \(2R\lambda_Y\), or \(2R\lambda_Y^{\rm exc}\) with the largest-prime version. From
\[
 |x^k-y^k|\le k|x-y|(|y|+|x-y|)^{k-1}
\]
and Hölder, replacing the retained scalar with the full one in its \(k\)-th prefix moment costs an effective \(O_{r,R,C}(\lambda_Y)\) for \(k\le r\), or the corresponding exceptional bound. The model has the identical restoration bound. At this point the left side is the full scalar, with no height-dependent omission.

Let \(A(T)=\sum_{0<\gamma\le T}|\zeta'(\rho)|^2\). Use symmetric numerator sums or normalize the sum of the two reflected evaluations by \(2A(T)\). The Stieltjes argument is the one in the scalar proof. For each full fixed-\(Y\) directional moment the unnormalized discrepancy \(R_k(T)\) is bounded in modulus by \(A(T)\epsilon(Y)\) for all \(T\ge Y\), with an effective vanishing error independent of the direction in the ball. Against \(f(t)=(1/4+t^2)^{-1}\), the strict tail discrepancy is \(-f(Y)R_k(Y)-\int_Y^\infty f'(t)R_k(t)\,dt\). Its normalized bound is at most \(\epsilon(Y)(1+2 f(Y)A(Y)/B_Y^+)\), effectively \(O(\epsilon(Y))\) by the ordinary effective untwisted derivative asymptotic. The omitted coordinate never appears in the object being integrated. It is permitted to change for every \(T\). [Scalar source and endpoint proof](uniform64_exceptional_prime_clt_skeptic.md)

## 3. Gaussian limit, necessity in this array class, and effectivity

For the model, \(E\Re U_p=r_p\), \(E\Im U_p=0\), and the centered real and imaginary parts have equal variance \(v_p\) and covariance zero. The moment identities follow from \(E U_p=r_p\) and \(E U_p^2=r_p^2\). Thus \(\Sigma_Y\) is the exact model covariance. The independence and smallness of each centered summand give the Gaussian limit with covariance \(\Sigma\) in every fixed direction. An effective moment check is stronger than a qualitative Lindeberg appeal. For fixed order \(j\ge3\), the absolute cumulant of one directional summand is \(O_j(d_p^j)\) from the finite moment formula for cumulants and \(|X_p|\le2d_p\). Summing cumulants costs at most \(O_{j,R,C}(\lambda_Y^{j-2})\). The first cumulant is zero and the second is \(u^{\mathsf T}\Sigma_Yu\). Comparing the finite moment–cumulant polynomials gives effective Gaussian moment convergence, uniformly in the direction ball, with a bound using \(\lambda_Y\) and \(\|\Sigma_Y-\Sigma\|\). This also handles a zero limiting variance.

For a purely qualitative necessity statement, use the earlier **unrestricted**, possibly ineffective uniform HLZ estimate in place of exclusion. The same Fourier and coefficient comparison gives convergence of the signed differences of every fixed directional moment without a small omitted-prime hypothesis. Both the model and arithmetic distributions then have bounded higher even moments in each direction. If the arithmetic vectors converge to a Gaussian, their directional moments converge to the Gaussian moments by uniform integrability. The model moments therefore do also. Tightness and Gaussian moment determinacy give the corresponding model Gaussian limit. If \(\lambda_Y\) failed to tend to zero, pass along a subsequence where a selected column pair \(a_{p,Y},b_{p,Y}\) has a nonzero limit and \(r_p\) also has a limit in \([0,1/\sqrt2]\). Choose a fixed direction that detects one of the limiting columns. The individual model contribution then converges in law to a bounded nondegenerate random variable, since its limiting variance is \(v(d_\infty^2)>0\). The other contributions remain tight by their variance bound. Passing to a further subsequence, independence persists and the Gaussian limit factors as that bounded nondegenerate variable plus an independent variable. Cramér's Gaussian decomposition theorem excludes this. Therefore, within the fixed-dimensional first-harmonic array class with bounded normalized covariance and a covariance limit, infinitesimal largest column is necessary for a **qualitative** arithmetic Gaussian limit under RH and sufficient. This does not turn model comparison into effective general individual-character convergence.

For constructiveness, use the bounded-Lipschitz test class of real functions with \(\|h\|_\infty\le1\) and Lipschitz constant at most 1. Take independent smoothing coordinates with density proportional to \((\sin(x/4)/(x/4))^4\), with the removable value at zero. The Fourier transform is supported on \([-1,1]\), by its four-fold indicator convolution, and the second absolute moment is finite with an elementary bound. Convolve both vector laws with this product noise at scale \(1/R\). The smoothing cost for each test is at most the noise's expected norm divided by \(R\). Choose \(R\) effectively from the requested tolerance. Both smoothed laws have effectively bounded second moments, so a sufficiently large fixed box \([-B,B]^d\) controls their test-integral tails by Chebyshev. On the box their density difference is bounded pointwise by \((2\pi)^{-d}\) times the integral of the absolute characteristic-function difference over \([-R,R]^d\), by Fourier inversion and the smoothing multiplier bounded by 1. This integral is bounded by its box volume times the uniform difference on that compact frequency range.

To make the latter difference small, Taylor-expand \(e^{it^{\mathsf T}X}\) through degree \(2m-1\). On the frequency box the variance of the reference projection is at most \(dR^2\|\Sigma\|\). The even \(2m\)-th directional moments are controlled uniformly by the already established moment bounds and their Gaussian limits. The Taylor remainder after division by \((2m)!\) is eventually bounded by an effective constant times \(C_R^m/(2^m m!)\) for a fixed \(C_R\ge1+dR^2\|\Sigma\|\). Choose \(m\) first, then \(Y\) to control the lower moment differences and even bound. Fourier inversion of the smoothed densities now gives the effective bounded-Lipschitz conclusion. If each Gaussian marginal has a positive variance, approximate a lower orthant by bounded Lipschitz ramps across coordinate strips of width \(\delta\); the Gaussian mass of the union of strips is at most a computable constant times \(\delta\). This gives effective orthant convergence too. No orthant claim is made at a degenerate marginal atom. All onset assertions here are conditional on effective convergence rates for deterministic array parameters, and on the effective HLZ restriction under RH.

## 4. Bands and counterexamples

- For cosine bands \(S_i(Y)\subseteq\{p:p\le P\}\), weights \(p^{-1/2}\), center \(H_i=\sum_{S_i}1/p\), and normalization \(\sqrt{V_i}\) with \(V_i=\frac12\sum_{S_i}(1/p-1/p^2)\), the limiting correlations are the limits of
\[
 \frac{\sum_{p\in S_i\cap S_j}(p^{-1}-p^{-2})}
 {\sqrt{\sum_{p\in S_i}(p^{-1}-p^{-2})\,\sum_{p\in S_j}(p^{-1}-p^{-2})}}.
\]
If all \(V_i\to\infty\), the columns are infinitesimal. This formula retains overlaps and gives the same component for identical bands. Nested cumulative cutoffs \(P_i=L^{D_i}\) for fixed \(D_i>0\), each independently scaled by its own standard deviation, have limiting correlation 1, since each prime reciprocal sum is \(\log\log L+\log D_i+O(1)\).
- For disjoint moving bands \(L^{D_{i-1}}<p\le L^{D_i}\) with \(0<D_0<\cdots<D_d\), use the centered sums **without a diverging normalization**. Their variances tend to \(\frac12\log(D_i/D_{i-1})>0\) and their maximal columns tend to zero by the lower cutoff. The limits are independent Gaussians with these variances. One may add the cumulative initial block \(p\le L^{D_0}\) centered and standardized as another independent Gaussian component. An effective estimate of the covariance limits follows from elementary Mertens summation with effective \(O(1/\log x)\) remainder. One proof is \(\sum_{n\le x}\Lambda(n)\lfloor x/n\rfloor=\log(\lfloor x\rfloor!)\), an effective Chebyshev bound for \(\sum_{n\le x}\Lambda(n)\), and partial summation after removing the absolutely summable higher prime-power terms.
- To see a family with nontrivial overlapping cumulative correlations, fix \(0<u_1<\cdots<u_d\le1\) and use \(P_i=\exp((\log L)^{u_i})\). Divide every centered sum by \(\sqrt{\frac12\log\log L}\). Mertens summation gives limiting covariance \(\min(u_i,u_j)\). This is a fixed-dimensional Brownian covariance statement; it asserts no process tightness.
- A lone prime with coefficient 1 gives a bounded nondegenerate Poisson-coordinate limit, so a positive variance by itself does not imply a Gaussian. More strongly, let \(B_Y=\sum_{3\le p\le P}p^{-1/2}(\Re U_p-p^{-1/2})\) and \(J=2^{-1/2}(\Re U_2-2^{-1/2})\). The two rows \(B_Y\) and \(B_Y+J\) each have variance tending to infinity, with bounded raw columns. Their covariance-whitened vector has a fixed-variance direction given by the raw difference \(J\), which has a bounded non-Gaussian limit. Because the direction in whitened coordinates may vary, pass to a subsequence of its unit direction; bounded second moments allow the difference from the limiting direction to be discarded. This disproves replacing the joint column condition by separate row variance divergence. Individually standardizing the rows instead gives a degenerate Gaussian \((G,G)\), in agreement with the overlap formula.
- The proof gives no growing-degree moment theorem. The analytic character range would grow like \(P^r\), and even in the model the Gaussian moment comparison is false in general at rapidly growing orders. For a standardized sum of \(N\) equal-weight bounded Poisson coordinates, the support is bounded by \(C\sqrt N\). At order \(2N^2\), the corresponding Gaussian absolute-moment root is asymptotic to a positive constant times \(N\), by factorial growth. Every fixed-degree moment may converge while this growing-degree comparison fails. This is a model obstruction to an unrestricted extension, not a counterexample to the fixed-dimensional CLT.
