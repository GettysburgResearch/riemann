# Imprimitive Dirichlet characters and the optimal boundary distance

Let \(\chi\) be a fixed Dirichlet character modulo \(q\), induced by the primitive character \(\chi_*\) of conductor \(q_*\mid q\). Write \(F=L(\cdot,\chi_*)\). Assume GRH for \(F\), with a pole at 1 permitted in the conductor-one case. Let
\[
 d_N(G)=\inf_{A(s)=\sum_{n\le N}a_n n^{-s}}\|1-GA\|_*,
 \qquad
 \|g\|_*^2=\frac1{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb R}
       \frac{|g(1/2+it)|^2}{1/4+t^2}\,dt.
\]
The infimum uses arbitrary complex coefficients on the ordinary integer grid. The fixed primitive optimal theorem in this archive is the analytic input. The transfer below is a finite multiplier argument. [Primitive theorem](director_selberg_transfer_round6.md)

## Exact length comparison

Let \(S=\{p:p\mid q,\ p\nmid q_*\}\), \(M=\prod_{p\in S}p\) and \(E(s)=\prod_{p\in S}(1-\chi_*(p)p^{-s})\), with empty products set to 1. Euler products in \(\Re s>1\), followed by meromorphic identity, give \(L(s,\chi)=F(s)E(s)\). Primes dividing both \(q\) and \(q_*\) contribute no factor. In particular a character can be imprimitive even when \(E=1\).

The polynomial \(E\) has ordinary integer length at most \(M\). For every \(A\) of length at most \(N\), the polynomial \(EA\) has length at most \(MN\). Therefore
\[
 d_{MN}(F)\le d_N(FE).
\]
This inequality uses the unanchored residual and requires no control of the individual coefficients of \(A\).

For the upper bound, expand \(E^{-1}(s)=\sum_{n\ge1}c_n n^{-s}\) by the finite product of geometric series on \(\Re s>0\). The nonzero coefficients lie on integers generated by \(S\), with \(|c_n|=1\) there. For any \(0<\eta<1/2\),
\[
 \sum_{n>X}|c_n|n^{-1/2}
 \le X^{-\eta}\prod_{p\in S}(1-p^{-1/2+\eta})^{-1}.
\]
Thus with \(Q_X(s)=\sum_{n\le X}c_n n^{-s}\) one has
\[
 \delta_X:=\sup_{\Re s=1/2}|1-E(s)Q_X(s)|\ll_{S,\eta} X^{-\eta};
\]
indeed \(E\) is bounded there and the geometric series converges absolutely uniformly. If \(S\) is empty, the error is identically zero.

Take integer \(X_N=\lfloor\exp(\sqrt{\log N})\rfloor\) and \(J_N=\lfloor N/X_N\rfloor\), for large integer \(N\). Then \(J_NX_N\le N\) and \(\log J_N=\log N-\sqrt{\log N}+o(1)\). For any length-\(J_N\) polynomial \(B\), choose \(A=BQ_{X_N}\), of length at most \(N\). The identity
\[
 1-FEA=(1-FB)+(FB)(1-EQ_{X_N})
\]
and \(\|1\|_*=1\) give
\[
 \|1-FEA\|_*\le\|1-FB\|_*+\delta_{X_N}(1+\|1-FB\|_*).
\]
Taking the infimum over \(B\) is legitimate even without a chosen minimizer, since the right side is increasing in \(\|1-FB\|_*\). In particular
\[
 d_{MN}(F)\le d_N(FE)\le
 d_{J_N}(F)+\delta_{X_N}(1+d_{J_N}(F)).
\]
The multiplier error is \(o((\log N)^{-1/2})\). It is paid for by boundedness of the product \(FB\) in the Cauchy norm on a near-minimizing sequence, without a coefficient majorant on \(B\). The floor functions preserve both length endpoints and logarithmic scale.

## Limit and finite Euler zeros

The primitive theorem gives \((\log n)d_n(F)^2\to C_F=\sum_{\rho\ {\rm distinct}}m_\rho^2/|\rho|^2\), where \(\rho\) ranges over nontrivial zeros of the primitive completion. Since \(\log(MN)/\log N\to1\) and \(\log J_N/\log N\to1\), apply the comparison to the nonnegative norms multiplied by \(\sqrt{\log N}\), then square. This gives
\[
 \lim_{N\to\infty}(\log N)d_N(L(\cdot,\chi))^2
 =\sum_{\rho\ {\rm distinct\ for}\ F}\frac{m_\rho^2}{|\rho|^2}.
\]
Each additional factor can vanish only at \(\Re s=0\), because \(|\chi_*(p)|=1\) on \(S\) and a zero requires \(|p^{-s}|=1\). On the critical line \(E\) is nonvanishing, so the primitive zero multiplicities there are unchanged. The lower comparison accounts for any approximation benefit from multiplication by \(E\), while the upper comparison proves that its reversible boundary action adds no leading cost. The transfer does not need a functional equation for the imprimitive series.

For a principal character of modulus \(q>1\), \(F=\zeta\), \(S=\{p:p\mid q\}\) and \(E(1)=\prod_{p\mid q}(1-p^{-1})>0\). Its pole at 1 persists but is outside the norm's line and causes no difficulty. Under RH the limit equals the primitive zeta multiplicity-squared sum. The argument is for fixed \(\chi\). It gives no uniformity with growing modulus and does not preserve prescribed initial coefficients or support on integers coprime to \(q\).
