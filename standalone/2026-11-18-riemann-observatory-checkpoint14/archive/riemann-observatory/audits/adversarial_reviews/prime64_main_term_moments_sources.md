# Source ledger for effective Hardy derivative moments

18 November 2026. The numerical theorem in the companion note uses a fixed Euler polynomial, the weighted Hilbert inequality, and a differentiated Hardy phase. The references below were checked for inputs that could retain leading constants. An asymptotic error with no numerical constant was used only for comparison.

| Source and inspected locator | What was checked | How it bears on the proof |
|---|---|---|
| Montgomery–Vaughan, author-hosted *Multiplicative Number Theory II*, §G.4 pp. 426–427, Theorem G.16 and equations (G.26)–(G.28) | Distinct real frequencies, the local minimum gap \(\delta_n\), arbitrary complex coefficients, and weighted constant \(3\pi/2\) in the single Hilbert form. | This is the quantitative external input. Applied twice to the integrated square, it gives \(3\pi\sum (n+1)|c_n|^2\) for \(\lambda_n=\log n\). The uniform-gap version with constant \(\pi\) is a different theorem. [Author text](https://personal.science.psu.edu/rcv4/571s25/montgomery-vaughanII.pdf) |
| Bui–Hall, arXiv:2304.05178v1, p. 2 equation (1) and reference [11] | For fixed \(k,\ell\) with \(k+\ell=2s\), \(|k-\ell|=2d\), the mean product is a monic polynomial of degree \(2s+1\) times \((-1)^dT/[4^s(2s+1)]\), with remainder \(O(T^{3/4}(\log T)^{2s+1/2})\). It is attributed to Hall's Theorem 3 and integration by parts. | For \(k=\ell=1,2\) the leading coefficients are \(1/12,1/80\). Equation (1) supplies no numerical remainder constant or threshold and was not put into the rigorous enclosure. [Bui–Hall full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.05178v1) |
| Dona–Helfgott–Zuniga Alterman, JTNB 34 (2022), 91–133, Theorems 1.2, 4.3 and 4.5 | At \(\sigma=1/2\), Theorem 4.3 bounds the integrated square by \(T\log T+2\sqrt{T\log T}+23.05779T\) for \(T\ge4\). At \(1/2<\sigma<1\) the upper error coefficient contains \(5.45623/(2\sigma-1)\), and Theorem 4.5 treats \(\sigma<1/2\) separately by the functional equation. | This is a genuine explicit strip estimate. Direct insertion on a Cauchy circle meets the singular constants as \(\sigma\to1/2\); even at \(\sigma-1/2=c/\log T\) its stated error may be of order \(T\log^2T\). An effective estimate uniform on that shrinking strip, or a different use of the pointwise-shifted estimates, is required to recover the leading Hardy derivative constants. [Journal PDF](https://www.numdam.org/item/JTNB_2022__34_1_91_0.pdf) |
| Simonič–Starichkova, arXiv:2105.06821v3, Theorem 2, Table 1 and Corollary 1 | Explicit Atkinson formula remainder with numerical parameters \(A'\le N/T\le A''\) and tabulated thresholds \(T_0\ge10^{20}\). Corollary 1 gives an explicit \(J(T_0)T^{1/3}\log^{5/3}T\) majorant for the usual \(E(T)\) at \(T\ge1.1T_0\) with its specified \(T_0\) values. | The data concern \(|\zeta(1/2+it)|^2\), with no small complex shift and no Hardy derivatives. Differentiation of the scalar error in its integration endpoint would not establish either derivative moment. [Versioned full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.06821v3) |
| Gabcke, deposited thesis, introduction equations (6)–(8) | For real \(t\ge200\), the stated \(0.127t^{-3/4}\) bound is \(R_0\), the remainder **after** the explicit \(C_0\) term. Additional \(R_K\) constants are supplied through \(K=10\). | A real-axis bound and a floor depending on \(t\) cannot be differentiated by a Cauchy circle without proving analytic error control on a fixed-cutoff patch and reconciling cutoff switches. The quoted error was not used as a remainder for just the cosine polynomial. [Deposited thesis](https://d-nb.info/1072293277/34) |
| Dhiman–Kadiri–Quesada-Herrera, arXiv:2609.00537v1, Corollaries 0.3–0.5 and §5 Theorem 10 | Corollary 0.3 is an explicit first AFE for \(0<\sigma\le1\), \(t\ge t_0\ge14\), with a real \(t\)-dependent truncation. The numerical example \(1.2552 t^{-\sigma}\) uses \(t_0=14.13472\). The second AFE requires \(x,y\in\mathbb Z+1/2\) and \(2\pi xy=|t|\), with \(1/2\le\sigma\le1\). | The first estimate does not by itself give an analytic remainder with one fixed cutoff on a disk. The exact product of half-integer cutoffs in Theorem 10 only permits a discrete set of heights and cannot be used unchanged on a continuum. [Versioned full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.00537v1) |
| Arias de Reyna, *Asymptotic Expansions of the auxiliary function*, Theorems 5 and 12 and Proposition 8 | Sectorial expansions for \(\mathcal R(s)\) and finite truncations, with asymptotic remainder and constants treated as \(O\)-bounds or existence constants in the inspected statements. | The analytic auxiliary-function route might be made effective for short polynomials on disks, but no numerical compact-strip remainder suitable for the present cutoff was extracted. [arXiv full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.04714) |
| Rodgers, arXiv:2608.12315v1, §1 equation (1) and Theorem 1.1 | Identifies the original \(3\pi/2\) constant, the smaller Preissmann upper bound and a strict lower bound above \(\pi\) for the weighted problem. | Corroborates the distinction between weighted and uniform-gap constants. The proof uses the Montgomery–Vaughan author text and does not need the recent optimal-constant result. [Versioned full text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.12315v1) |

The Hall (1999) DOI is verified, but the Cambridge and Wiley retrievals did not supply the primary PDF in this run. The explicit polynomial and error quoted above are from Bui–Hall's equation (1), which references the original Theorem 3. The Kobayashi publisher endpoint returned 403. His author publication list and a workshop scan located the research line, but they were insufficient to establish a numerical whole-range second-derivative moment error. The Fan URL found by search returned 404. None of these access outcomes was used as evidence that an explicit theorem does not exist. [Hall publication](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0025579300007762) · [Kobayashi publication](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022247X19310406)

The effective fallback proved here is a one-piece polynomial on each dyadic height block. Its centered coefficient integrals retain the leading \(1/12\) and \(1/80\) terms per unit interval length, with explicit lower-order allowances. A genuine effective continuously shifted Hardy mixed-moment theorem, or analytic errors for a Riemann–Siegel cutoff uniform over the required neighborhood and its switches, could lower the finite-height corrections. It would still need a positive inequality for transferring integrated derivative products to zero-sampled squares. No uninspected \(O\)-constant was set numerically.
