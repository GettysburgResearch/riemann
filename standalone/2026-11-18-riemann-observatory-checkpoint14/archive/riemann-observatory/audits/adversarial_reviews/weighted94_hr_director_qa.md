## Two scope edits

1. In the prime-sum calculation, retain the PNT limit \(\int_0^\infty v\widehat f(v)^2\,dv\) without qualification, but identify this with \(V/2\) only for the range relevant to variances, \(m\ge2\), or with an independent assumption \(\operatorname{supp}\widehat f\subset[-1,1]\). Journal p. 668 displays \(V=\int_{\mathbb R}\min(|v|,1)\widehat f(v)^2\,dv\). The print on p. 670 invokes support inside \((-1,1)\) after the even-moment calculation. In the range \(m=1\) the theorem only concerns the mean, and when \(a>1/2\) it permits tests with support outside \([-1,1]\). There is no extra factor of two in the whole-line integral. The factor of two on preprint p. 6 multiplies the positive-frequency sum, as the two opposite-sign orientations demand.

2. Add the inherited height domain to the discussion of journal Proposition 2.3. The phrase “if \(H\to\infty\)” appears on p. 669 under the setup \(H=T^a\), \(0<a\le1\), on p. 668. With the first logarithmic moment condition or \(I=0\), the mean conclusion also follows for \(H\to\infty\) with \(H\le T\), and the same estimates allow \(H=O(T)\). No Fourier support restriction tied to \(H\) is needed for this mean, since the prime mean vanishes once \(H\) exceeds a fixed constant, and the signed polar mean is \(O((H\log T)^{-1})\) by the source's Fourier calculation. Under an unrestricted literal reading of the clause on \(H\), take \(H=T^2\), \(I\neq0\), and an admissible Schwartz weight. Writing \(\log(1+|T+T^2u|)=2\log T+\log(|u+1/T|+T^{-2})\) gives mean \(2I+O(1/\log T)\). The second logarithm has uniformly bounded absolute average by the bounded weight near its moving singularity and the first log moment at infinity. This illustrates the reason to retain the contextual height domain. It need not be classified as another source error.

## Verified against the primary displays and repair

- The real even \(f\) has \(\widehat f\in C_c^\infty\). The entire representative by Fourier inversion decays rapidly on fixed horizontal strips. Since \(\gamma=(\rho-1/2)/i\) is potentially complex, the reflection \(\rho\mapsto1-\overline\rho\) pairs \(\gamma\) and \(\overline\gamma\) with multiplicity. The realness of the statistic follows without RH, using normal convergence on compact sets of real centers from the unit-height zero count. The indicator-count example explicitly supposes real ordinates.
- Both version definitions require only nonnegativity, mass one, and compact Fourier support for \(w\). A smooth nonnegative bandlimited probability bump \(b\) can be made as a normalized square. The weighted translates \(w=\sum_{j\ge1}2^{-j}b(\cdot-\exp(4^j))\) converge in \(L^1\) and preserve compact Fourier support. The sum and all derivatives converge uniformly by the bounded derivatives of \(b\), so smoothness alone would not repair the theorem. A fixed positive mass of each translated bump lies near its center, and Tonelli makes the first logarithmic moment infinite. This is one fixed weight independent of \(T,H\).
- Equations (2)–(4) give \(N_f(\tau)=I\log(1+|\tau|)/\log T+O_{f,T}(1)\) uniformly for real \(\tau\) at each fixed \(T>1\). The finite prime sum and both polar evaluations are bounded at fixed \(T\), and \(\Omega(r)=\log(1+|r|)+O(1)\) plus the logarithmic triangle inequality controls the gamma integral. Choose a bandlimited nonnegative test with \(I=\int f>0\) inside any desired strict support window. Then \(\langle N_f\rangle_{T,H}=+\infty\) for the witness at every fixed \(T>1,H>0\). When \(I\neq0\), the same growth gives the stated necessity and sufficiency for finiteness of the \(m\)-th absolute moment at fixed \(T,H\), including after a fixed centering. If \(I=0\), the statistic is bounded at fixed \(T\) and no extra weight tail is needed for the asymptotic theorem.
- For a signed word of length \(j\le m\), compact Fourier support of \(w\) and \(\min(M,N)\le T^{j\sigma/2}\) force equality of the two integer products eventually. Opposite signs on a shared ordinary prime give the leading paired terms, with two orientations. Higher prime powers, colliding pair labels and blocks of size at least three have factors dominated by a summable \(p^{-2}\) times fixed logarithmic powers. This arithmetic step works with the literal printed weight. For translated and contracted slab majorants it works uniformly down to width \(T^{-\epsilon}\), provided \(0<\epsilon<a-m\sigma/2\).
- Write the smooth gamma deviation as \(\Delta=G-I\). For \(I=0\) it is uniformly \(O(1/\log T)\). Otherwise the bound in the draft follows from \(\log(1+|T+Hu|)-\log T=\log(H/T)+\log(|u+T/H|+H^{-1})\). For a lower bound on this difference, \(|u|\le T/(2H)\) gives a constant, and outside that range \(\log(T/H)\le\log(2+|u|)+O(1)\). When a mixed term is \(|S_f|^r|\Delta|^q\) with \(q\ge1\), the needed even order \(2\lceil r/2\rceil\) is at most \(m\), including odd \(m\). In the far region, reproduce \(w=w*\eta\) with \(\eta\) Schwartz. The sum of unit-cell suprema weighted by \(1+\log^q(2+|\text{cell index}|)\) is bounded by convolution and the corresponding log moment of \(w\). In the local region, the available even prime moment gives \(O(\delta)\) for the unweighted integral of \(|S_f|^r\) on slabs of size \(\delta\). The geometric annuli give a summable log-weighted series. The innermost slab contributes \(O(T^{-\epsilon}\log^qT)\) before dividing by \((\log T)^q\). These bounds justify an additive \(O(1/\log T)\) mixed comparison. The draft properly avoids applying Hölder at an unavailable odd highest absolute moment.
- Fourier integration by parts for the polar terms costs powers of \(\log T\) from the imaginary shift, canceled by the oscillatory denominator \((|\tau|\log T)^A\). The bound is \(O_A(T^{\sigma/2}(1+|\tau|)^{-A})\). For an expanded term containing at least one polar factor, any remaining smooth gamma logarithms are absorbed by increasing the decay exponent \(A\), and \(\|w\|_\infty/H\) bounds its density in \(\tau\). The prime factors cost at most \(O(T^{\sigma/2})\) per slot. Thus the polar contribution is \(O(T^{m\sigma/2}/H)\), smaller than any fixed negative log power for the strict Fourier margin. This absolute bound for mixed terms is distinct from the stronger signed first-polar-mean calculation in the source.
- The \(7/8\) constant, the ordinary CUE comparison and the roles of the three references check against pp. 667–670. The ordinary CUE variance has the same clipped frequency kernel after scaling the trace covariance \(\min(|k|,n)\). Neither the arithmetic finite window nor the ordinary CUE comparison supplies a derivative-weighted arithmetic transfer.
