# A low-index integer purity certificate

18 November 2026. The director reconstructed both independent proofs, read the decisive original Heinig–Rost p.62 passage, and checked the exact cases. The result is a specialization of a documented classical power-sum Toeplitz criterion, not a new root-location theorem. It makes the earlier joint finite-spectrum test an efficient exact integer algorithm.

Let q>=1 be an integer and P in Z[X] monic of degree d>=1, with nonzero root multiset invariant under alpha->q/alpha. Put t_n=sum alpha^n with multiplicity and t_0=d. For indices0<=i,j<d set K_ij=q^min(i,j)*t_|i-j|. Then all roots have modulus sqrt(q) iff K is positive semidefinite. If c is the number of distinct circle roots and b the number of off-circle pairs under alpha->q/conjugate(alpha), its inertia is (c+b,b,d-c-2b). Repeated and reducible cases, nonsquare q and negative constant coefficients are allowed.

Here is a direct proof avoiding square roots. Let V_i,alpha=alpha^i on the DISTINCT roots, and A_alpha,beta=m_alpha times the indicator beta=q/conjugate(alpha). This is Hermitian. Then K=V A V*, since each summand for i>=j is m_alpha*alpha^i*(q/alpha)^j. The leading r-row Vandermonde is invertible for r distinct roots. For d rows it has full column rank, giving a congruence to A plus d-r zero dimensions. A fixed circle root gives a positive multiplicity block; an off-circle pair gives the block [[0,m],[m,0]], with one positive and one negative eigenvalue. This proves every assertion and shows why the trace form sees distinct roots but not Jordan chains.

Newton identities compute all needed integer t_n through d-1. Exact rational symmetric elimination handles positive pivots by Schur complements, zero diagonal/nonzero row by a negative two-by-two principal minor, and a zero row by deletion. To compute full inertia, a zero-diagonal nonzero two-by-two block has one sign of each kind. These rules must be applied to the current Schur complement; nonnegative leading determinants alone do not certify semidefinite matrices. Coefficient root bounds and the bordered-minor formula bound intermediate rational bit sizes polynomially. Thus dense arithmetic uses O(d^3) rational operations with polynomial bit complexity. The reciprocal identity X^dP(q/X)=P(0)P(X) checks the input symmetry exactly.

Corollary8.7 and Theorem10.10 give the relevant congruence and derivative-based Bezoutian. The ordinary Schur–Cohn form in Theorem10.9 vanishes on proportional self-inversive inputs and is not this test. The integer K is the q-reciprocal arithmetic congruence of their form. This finite algorithm requires one compatible spectrum with positive multiplicities. It supplies neither an infinite classical-zeta trace realization nor convergence of finite models.
