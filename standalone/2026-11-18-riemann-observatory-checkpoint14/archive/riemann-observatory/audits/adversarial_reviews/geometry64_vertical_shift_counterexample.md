# A degree-one central zero in the printed Selberg class

The five axioms in Nakamura–Suzuki v1, §2.2 pp. 2–3, admit a degree-one function with a central zero. The claim concerns the scope of the degree discussion on p. 5. The regularized-drift criterion in Theorems 2.1–2.2 applies to the example. [Primary PDF](../../sources/positivity64_operator_probability/suzuki_2307.pdf) · [Drift audit](geometry64_central_zero_scope.md)

Let \(\chi_4\) be the primitive odd character modulo four, and let \(\tau\) be an actual zero ordinate in \((6.02094890469,6.02094890471)\). The archived interval proof evaluates the completed beta function using Hurwitz zeta and encloses its values at these endpoints strictly on opposite sides of zero. The completed function is real on the line by its sign \(+1\) functional equation, so the sign change proves existence. Set \(F_\tau(s)=L(s+i\tau,\chi_4)\). [Interval proof](../chi4_effective_phase_lower_skeptic62.md) · [Certificate implementation](../../scripts/chi4_phase_certificate_skeptic62.py)

- **S1 and S4.** The coefficients \(\chi_4(n)n^{-i\tau}\) have modulus at most one, giving absolute convergence for \(\Re s>1\) and the Ramanujan bound.
- **S2.** The primitive nonprincipal Dirichlet L-function is entire of finite order. Translation preserves finite order, with \(m_F=0\).
- **S3.** Write \(X(s)=Q^s\Gamma((s+1)/2)L(s,\chi_4)\), \(Q=\sqrt{4/\pi}\). The translated completion in the paper's convention is \(X_\tau(s)=Q^{-i\tau}X(s+i\tau)\). From \(X(s)=\overline{X(1-\bar s)}\) it follows that \(X_\tau(s)=Q^{-2i\tau}\overline{X_\tau(1-\bar s)}\). Thus \(\lambda=1/2\), \(\mu=(1+i\tau)/2\) and \(\Re\mu=1/2\), as allowed.
- **S5.** The Euler logarithm has coefficients \(\chi_4(p)^r p^{-ir\tau}/r\) at \(p^r\), zero elsewhere. Their moduli are at most one, so \(\theta=0\) suffices.

The degree \(2\lambda=1\) is unchanged and \(X_\tau(1/2)=0\). The degree-one classification of Kaczorowski–Perelli includes \(L(s+iu,\chi)\) for primitive \(\chi\) of conductor at least two. In Perelli's survey cited by the paper this is Theorem 6.3, p. 94. The survey distinguishes primitive functions from primitive and normalized functions on p. 107. The five printed axioms have no normalization excluding the imaginary part of \(\mu\). Primitivity also cannot exclude this example, since degree is additive and the only class member of degree less than one is the unit. [Kaczorowski–Perelli, Theorem 3, p. 211](../../sources/release64_vertical_shift/kp1999.pdf) · [Perelli, Theorem 6.3 and p. 107](../../sources/release64_vertical_shift/perelli2004.pdf)

The classical unshifted central-value conjecture for Dirichlet L-functions does not address these translates. Indeed \(\beta(1/2)>0\) by the alternating series lower bound \(1-1/\sqrt3\). The positive central multiplicity of \(F_\tau\) is unconditional, and translation leaves the real parts of zeros and therefore the GRH assertion unchanged. Under that shared GRH hypothesis, the Gaussian variance changes from zero to the multiplicity of the zero originally at height \(\tau\). It records the zero multiplicity at the chosen center, rather than a translation-invariant quantity.
