## Formal moments and Hermite Gram check

The director's source window is \(rb<1/2\), with \(r\) fixed, and consequently exactly the orders \(0,\ldots,4\) at \([1/20,1/10]\). The quartic frequency degree is independent of the support length of the aggregate \(D^r\). Opposite-sign pairs contribute \(H/2\), including their sign probability, and the number with \(j\) pairs is \(r!/(2^j j!(r-2j)!)\). The single-, pair-, triple-, and quadruple-index integrals are \(-4d+q,\ 12d^2-2dq,\ -24d^3,\ 24d^4\); their EGF is \(e^{Hz}[(1-dz)^4+qz(1-dz)]\) and Wick pairs multiply by \(e^{Hz^2/4}\). The formal inverse and \(R(1/s+\sqrt3)\) check. If the whole sequence had a positive law, its even moments could be bounded by the absolute Gaussian-polynomial inverse. The resulting monotone cosh argument really does force all finite exponential moments, so vanishing at \(1/d\) is impossible. The degree-70 finite witness uses only formal orders through 140 and cannot contradict the genuine moment window. The separate positive-definiteness proof on the genuine window uses \(s^2\le b^2\), the Hermite multiplication bound \(\sqrt n+\sqrt{n+1}\), and \(b<1/(4n)\) for a degree-\(n\) Gram test. Its numerical inequalities hold beginning at \(n=2\), with the strictly positive variance treating \(n=1\).
