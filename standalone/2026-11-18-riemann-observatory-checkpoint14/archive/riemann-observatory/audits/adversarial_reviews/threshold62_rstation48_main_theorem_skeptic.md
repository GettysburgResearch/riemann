## Norm, Mellin model, and coefficient constraint

With s=1/2+it, ∥f∥_*²=(1/(2π))∫_R |f(s)|² dt/(1/4+t²). In Theorem 1, Δ_N²=inf_{A∈D_N}∥1−ζA∥_*² with D_N all ordinary Dirichlet polynomials Σ_{1≤n≤N}a_nn^(−s) and arbitrary complex a_n. This is length **at most N**, and Δ_N is the infimum of precisely our classical d₁(A). 

The anchored d_N uses 1−Σ_{2≤n≤N}a_n[floor(x/n)−floor(x)/n] on x≥1 in L²(x^(−2)dx). Under x=1/u, the Mellin transform M[f](s)=∫_1^∞ f(x)x^(−s−1)dx is the usual transform on 0<u≤1 and is isometric at Re s=1/2 for boundary dt/(2π). The floor difference is −{x/n}+{x}/n and is supported on x≥1. The full fractional-part transform in 0<Re s<1 evaluates its transform as (n^(−s)−n^(−1))ζ(s)/s. Thus the anchored residual transform is (1−ζ(s)(A(s)−A(1)))/s. Conversely every D∈D_N with D(1)=0 is exactly Σ_{n=2}^N a_n(n^(−s)−n^(−1)). Hence d_N is the classical norm infimum restricted to D(1)=0, and Δ_N≤d_N. The continuous Burnol lower bound applies to Δ_N by the full-space transform of the unconstrained residual and inclusion of these integer dilations in all scales [1/N,1].
