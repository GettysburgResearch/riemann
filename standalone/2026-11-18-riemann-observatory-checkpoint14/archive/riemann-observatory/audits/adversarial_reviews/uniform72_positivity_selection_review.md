## Historical sign claims

- **Odlyzko–te Riele, record 13.** Printed pp. 138–140 give the strict limsup \(>1.06\) and liminf \(<-1.009\) for \(M(x)/\sqrt{x}\). The authors explicitly describe the result as indirect and supply no particular violating integer. The bounds imply infinitely many strict violations in both directions, including upon restricting to integers, since \(M(x)\) is constant on \([n,n+1)\) and \(\sqrt{x}/\sqrt n\to1\). The fixed-constant \(O(\sqrt x)\) question and RH remain distinct from the unit bound. The historical account in record 14 distinguishes Stieltjes’ boundedness claim and Mertens’ specific numerical conjecture. [Disproof scan](../../sources/release70_positivity/mertens.pdf) · [Historical account](../../sources/release70_positivity/mertens-history.pdf)
- **Best–Trudgian and Hurst, records 11–12.** Best–Trudgian Theorem 1 has nonstrict \(\limsup\ge1.6383,\ \liminf\le-1.6383\), obtained using weakened finite-relation tests. Hurst Theorem 6.1 in §6.3 has strict \(\liminf<-1.837625,\ \limsup>1.826054\). Hurst’s computations cover integer arguments through \(10^{16}\) with extrema, zeros and selected regular values recorded. None of these displayed inequalities supplies the least violation. [Best–Trudgian](../../sources/release70_positivity/1209.3843.pdf) · [Hurst](../../sources/release70_positivity/1610.08551.pdf)
- **Kim–Nguyen, record 31.** The bounds in (1.1)–(1.3) concern setup and prior estimates. The new conclusion is explicitly (1.4), \(x<\exp(1.96\cdot10^{19})\) for the least argument failing the strict conjecture. Section 2.2 gives the Pintz transfer theorem and the refined \(h_{\rm StR}\) version. Section 4.1 and Table 2 give the chosen phase near \(1.957\cdot10^{19}\) and a smoothed value near \(-1.007\). The phase is input to an existential transfer. The certified numerical evaluation and transfer proof remain unassessed at this admission scope. [Kim–Nguyen](../../sources/release70_positivity/2502.21021.pdf)
- **Haselgrove, record 15.** The surviving Cambridge article extract has the Liouville parity definition and its zeta Dirichlet series, plus edition metadata and references. Citation metadata gives *Mathematika* 5(2), pp. 141–145, Dec. 1958, online 26 Feb. 2010. The pinned HTML contains no original proof, no cutoff of 1000, no phase 831.847, and no count of 649 used in the proof. The main record now distinguishes extract from later attribution. Borwein–Ferguson–Mossinghoff pp. 3–4, (3)–(7), report precisely those details for the positive smoothed unweighted sum. They separately give weighted phases 853.853 and 996.980 yielding a negative smoothed Turán sum. The derivation quoted there treats RH and simplicity for the residue expressions and invokes the alternative cases separately. Smoothed values are not evaluations of the step sums at a violating integer. [Publisher extract](../../sources/release70_positivity/haselgrove_publisher.html) · [Borwein–Ferguson–Mossinghoff](../../sources/release70_positivity/borwein.pdf)
- **Explicit crossings, records 16–17.** Borwein–Ferguson–Mossinghoff Theorem 1 gives the least negative Turán sum at \(72{,}185{,}376{,}951{,}205\), separately from the argument minimizing that weighted sum through \(7.5\cdot10^{13}\). Theorem 2 concerns \(L(n)>0.061867\sqrt n\) infinitely often. Mossinghoff–Trudgian §1 distinguishes \(L(x)=\sum_{n\le x}\lambda(n)\) from \(T(x)=\sum_{n\le x}\lambda(n)/n\), gives Turán's sufficient eventual lower bound \(T(n)>-c/\sqrt n\), and credits Lehman with \(L(906180359)=1\) and Tanaka with the least positive crossing after 1, at \(906105257\). It qualifies the popular conjecture labels relative to the printed historical formulations. Humphries record 30 Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.3 concern the RH-equivalent growth bound and eventual-sign implication for the unweighted sum. His limiting law results use other hypotheses. [Crossings and distinctions](../../sources/release70_positivity/polya-turan.pdf) · [Humphries](../../sources/release70_positivity/1108.1524.pdf)
- **Ingham, record 40.** Theorem A concerns both limits for both normalized sums, under the indicated ordinate relation hypothesis. The last paragraph of §3 explicitly applies Kronecker above the last ordinate in all the allowed initial relations. Read its finite-relations wording through that proof, since scalar multiples of a relation are themselves relations. The argument distinguishes off-line and multiple-zero cases before using phase alignment in the simple critical-line case. These are checks on the source summary, without a new proof audit. ## Positivity and operator boundaries

- **de Branges, record 34.** The 13 April 2017 title page, abstract and §1 frame the proposal via quaternionic arithmetic zeta functions and maximal accretivity of a Radon transformation in the nonsingular case, or of a parity restriction in the singular case. Conrey–Li §3.1 supplies concrete failures of two earlier shift conditions. Their (3.2) is the negative real-part calculation at the 34th zero for (3.1), on \(H(E)\) with \(E(z)=\xi(1-iz)\). Their (3.4) is a negative ratio real part at the shift determined by 282 for (3.3), on \(F(W)\) with \(W(z)=1/\xi(1-iz)\). These obstructions apply to the specific earlier conditions. No passage identified them with the exact hypotheses of the later quaternionic Radon argument. The 2017 manuscript is admitted as a proposal at opening scope. [2017 manuscript](../../sources/release70_positivity/branges-2017.pdf) · [Conrey–Li](../../sources/release70_positivity/math-9812166.pdf)
- **Landau/Pétrovitch, records 42–44.** Landau §I, p. 242, restates Pétrovitch's Taylor-coefficient least-zero bound for nonzero constant term. The footnote (3) explicitly replaces strict \(>\) by \(\ge\), because the proof only excludes the interior of the guaranteed radius. The Pétrovitch Taylor article p. 306 supplies the original estimate. His separate note on entire functions, pp. 65–67, inserts a Cauchy coefficient bound using the supplied weighted maximum modulus and finite genus, and applies the conclusion to \(F-C\). These are local estimates of the distance to zeros, distinct from real-root or total-positivity equivalences. [Landau](../../sources/release70_positivity/petrovitch.pdf) · [Taylor note](../../sources/release70_positivity/taylor.pdf) · [Entire-function note](../../sources/release70_positivity/entire.pdf)
- **Schoenberg and the finite-order \(\Xi\) source, records 24 and 29.** Schoenberg's 1947 note §3 Theorems 1–2 gives the bilateral Laplace reciprocal characterization with a strip of regularity and the condition that its convergence strip contains zero for the Pólya-frequency function. The new \(\Xi\) preprint cites the developed 1951 formulation and later \(\Xi\) specializations. Its Proposition A.1 states whole-order equivalences for each of the two specific densities, not for arbitrary positive kernels. Its §5.2 credits Gröchenig's specialization and notes Roynette–Yor's earlier bilateral \(\Lambda\) equivalence. The finite-order statement is \(k_*=1{,}810{,}540{,}931{,}075\) from verification through \(3{,}000{,}175{,}332{,}800\). The separate generalized Laguerre statement takes \(T>1/2\) and gives all orders for \(|x|\le T-1/2\). The numerical certificate and finite-order derivation remain unextracted. Pickrell's abstract and §0 distinguish the narrower Gaussian-weighted class from Newman's and pose a direct spectral interpretation question. [Schoenberg 1947](../../sources/release65_positivity/schoenberg.pdf) · [Finite-order preprint](../../sources/release70_positivity/2611.02944.pdf) · [Pickrell](../../sources/release70_positivity/2104.05143.pdf)
- **Finite coefficient and strip results, records 2, 3, 6, 19, 22, 23.** Kostov–Shapiro's positive constant-term normalization uses initial sections; the equivalent monic formulation uses reversal and leading sections. The minimizer’s uniqueness in Theorem 2 is in degree \(i+1\), up to scaling the variable. Kostov's small spectral disk Theorem 1 includes Laurent radii tending to 1 and finite multiple-zero pairs in a compact \(q\)-range. The later separation source gives the annuli via Definition 4 and Theorem 5(1) with \(\alpha_0=\sqrt3/(2\pi)\). Brändén–Ottergren’s generalized Laguerre-basis test is for their nontrivial sequences. The Gaussian strip source has its own input class. Humphries' generalized Laguerre consequence fixes the order before taking large shifts; its converse is expressly conjectural. [Kostov–Shapiro](../../sources/release70_positivity/1106.6262.pdf) · [Small spectral disk](../../sources/release70_positivity/1605.05469.pdf) · [Separation](../../sources/release70_positivity/1704.01901.pdf) · [Generalized Laguerre bases](../../sources/release70_positivity/1210.7151.pdf) · [Strip article](../../sources/release70_positivity/1401.0237.pdf) · [Shifted class](../../sources/release70_positivity/2108.01827.pdf)
- **Jensen, record 18.** Printed p. 190 states the real axis and conjugate-pair diameter discs for zeros of \(F'\) and \(aF+F'\), with \(a\) real and \(F\) real entire of genus 0 or 1. A distinct result on a polynomial differential operator uses ellipses. The scope in the record is the former statement only. [Jensen scan](../../sources/release70_positivity/jensen.pdf)

## Remaining opening or focused scopes

Record 1's Theorem 1 fixes nonzero \(|q|<1\), gives distant zeros near \(-q^{-k}\), the normalized canonical product, and the positive spectral interval \((\widetilde q_j,\widetilde q_{j+1}]\) with degree \(2j\) nonreal factor. The negative-\(q\) case uses LP rather than LP-I for the real factor. Record 5 is an admissible-kernel and real generalized Laguerre reading, with main theorem conditions unextracted. The opening records 4, 7–10, 20–21, 25–28, 32–33, 35–39, 41, 45–46 describe, respectively, named special-function families, generalized Laguerre multipliers and finite free convolutions, partial-theta parameter loci, polynomial interlacing and diagonal operators, surveys, partial-theta branches/formal roots and TP/TN transformations, and preservation frameworks. Their abstract-level claims were compared with the cited openings. They now explicitly leave precise theorem conditions and proofs unextracted. No transfer to \(\zeta\) or \(\xi\) is made on the basis of those openings.

The source material for Haselgrove remains publisher-extract-only, and verifying the numerical and analytic arguments behind the recent large finite-order \(\Xi\) and Mertens claims remains outside this selection review.
