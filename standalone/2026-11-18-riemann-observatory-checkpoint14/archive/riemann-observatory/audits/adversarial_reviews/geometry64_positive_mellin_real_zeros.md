# The real zeros of three completed cusp-form L-functions

The three level-one forms listed in §3 of Nakamura–Suzuki v1 each have exactly one real zero in their **completed** L-function, at the center, and that zero is simple. The split Mellin integral proves this for all real arguments and gives zero regularized drift. [Primary §3, p. 5](../../sources/positivity64_operator_probability/suzuki_2307.pdf) · [Exact version receipts](../../sources/geometry64_central_zero_scope/receipts.jsonl)

Let \(f\) be a full-level holomorphic cusp form of weight \(k\equiv2\pmod4\) with real Fourier coefficients. Suppose \(f(iy)>0\) for \(y>1\). For its classical Mellin completion \(\Lambda_f(u)=(2\pi)^{-u}\Gamma(u)L(u,f)\), inversion gives \(f(i/y)=i^k y^k f(iy)=-y^k f(iy)\). Split the integral at 1 and substitute \(y\mapsto 1/y\) in the lower portion. This yields the identity
\[
\Lambda_f(k/2+h)=\int_1^\infty f(iy)\left(y^{k/2+h}-y^{k/2-h}\right)\frac{dy}{y}.
\]
Exponential decay of the Fourier series on \([1,\infty)\) gives convergence and holomorphy locally uniformly in every complex \(h\). The split expression represents the entire completion, including at negative real \(u\). For every real nonzero \(h\), its integrand has the strict sign of \(h\), as does the value. The central derivative is \(2\int_1^\infty f(iy)y^{k/2}\log y\,dy/y>0\), proving simplicity. The split expression is odd in \(h\), so division by \(h\) leaves an even function and the logarithmic derivative of that quotient vanishes at \(h=0\). The formula in §3 is the usual Mellin proof of the functional equation. This argument uses its positivity in this specific setting and makes no priority claim. [Primary text and Miyake references, §3](../../sources/positivity64_operator_probability/suzuki_2307.pdf)

For \(f=\Delta E_6,\Delta E_4E_6,\Delta E_4^2E_6\), the weights are \(18,22,26\). Multiplication by \(\Delta\) identifies \(M_{k-12}\) with \(S_k\). The level-one graded ring is generated by \(E_4,E_6\), and the only monomials of respective weights \(6,10,14\) are \(E_6,E_4E_6,E_4^2E_6\). Each cusp space is therefore one-dimensional and its normalized generator is a Hecke eigenform. For \(y>1\), the product for \(\Delta(iy)\) is positive, the coefficients of \(E_4\) after its constant term are positive, and the expansion \(E_6(iy)=1-504\sum_{n\ge1}\sigma_5(n)e^{-2\pi n y}\) is strictly increasing as a function of \(y>0\). Inversion gives \(E_6(i)=0\). All three forms meet the positivity hypothesis. [Source examples and dimension references, §3](../../sources/positivity64_operator_probability/suzuki_2307.pdf)

In the unitary normalization \(F(s)=L(s+(k-1)/2,f)\), the unique real zero of its completion is \(s=1/2\). The uncompleted \(L(u,f)\) also has trivial zeros at nonpositive integers, where the gamma factor has poles and the completed value from the split integral is finite and nonzero. The criterion counts completed zeros. For Selberg-class membership the needed coefficient bound is \( |a_f(n)|\le d(n)n^{(k-1)/2}\) from unit-modulus normalized Satake parameters, which gives (S4) and suffices without any stronger coefficient estimate.
