# Independent check of the Petrov positive tail reduction

I checked the claimed reductions in analytic102 against the exact matched Hardy factors and the source formulas. With \(C=T(\phi_-)X\), upper triangularity preserves the first \(n=m+J\) modes, so \(V=Q_nH\oplus W\) with \(\dim W=J\). The quotient of Fredholm determinants is the determinant of the \(J\)-dimensional Schur complement. The block elimination uses unit triangular identity-plus-finite-rank factors, and the infimum characterization preserves the direct lower bound for \(I-K\). The calculation does not require the separate Proposition 4.6 chart. [Source comparison](geometry97_petrov_gram_match.md) · [Proof reviewed](analytic102_petrov_positive_tail_reduction.md)

The finite lower triangular factor \(L=P_nT(\phi_+)|_{P_n}\) has determinant one. On the real axis \(C=L^*X\), so the complementary frame is \(L^{-1}U\). Applying Jacobi to \(LL^*\) gives the normalized Gram determinant \(\det(U^*L^{-*}L^{-1}U)\). The triangular operators in the Wiener–Hopf identity give precisely the finite ambient inverse compression \(L^{-1}A_nL^{-*}\). This checks the order of the factors in both identities.

In the normalized continuum Gram, a shift by \(p\) in one factor and \(q\) in the other is supported from \(\max(p,q)\) through 1. Changing variable to \(s=t-p\) gives the range \([\max(0,q-p),1-p]\). With the transpose of the oriented formal entry, the difference is the ordinary lower interval of length \(\min(p,q)\), including a reversed formal interval. This confirms consistency with the strictly positive quadratic discrepancy established independently. The Gram convergence needs the true cutoff and the interval shrinks to zero at its boundary.

The determinant comparisons follow from positive compact compression min–max for \(K\), with the full Hardy determinant \(e^{-H_Nz^2/4}\). The Hankel cutoff error starts with Fourier index \(M+1\), which requires at least \(\lfloor M/k_*\rfloor+1\) convolution factors before the derivative-count bound is applied. This gives the stated factorial tail. The log determinant difference is bounded by the trace-norm error times the reciprocal common spectral lower bound, using interpolation. An empty finite band is trivial, with \(K=0\) and factors one, and the bandwidth parameter \(k_*\) is needed only for a nonempty band. I found no change to the real-parameter claims or the separate arithmetic scope.
