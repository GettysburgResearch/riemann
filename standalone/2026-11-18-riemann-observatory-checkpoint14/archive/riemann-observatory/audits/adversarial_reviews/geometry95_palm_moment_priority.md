## The measure and the one-point calculation

- **Hughes–Keating–O’Connell, §2(a), (2.2)–(2.9).** The root derivative is a product of eigenvalue distances, and (2.8) gives precisely the post-tilt exponent \(2k+2\). At \(k=J-1\) this yields \(N-1\) free particles with weight \(|1-w|^{2J}\). Equation (2.9) relates the normalization of the derivative tilt to a size-\(N-1\) characteristic-polynomial power moment. The band statistic is an additional observable. [Primary PDF](../../sources/classical63_lfunctions/hko2000.pdf)
- **Bourgade–Nikeghbali–Rouault, (1.6), and Bufetov, §2.6, finite OPE formulas (6)–(7), Theorem 1 and its remark.** The circular Jacobi density identifies the finite ensemble at \(\beta=2,\delta=J\). Bufetov gives the Palm projection formula, multiplication of the finite OPE weight by \(|1-w|^2\) when taking the Palm measure at 1, and the confluent-hypergeometric limiting hierarchy. The limiting theorem is stated at infinity in the original coordinates and at zero after inversion. It identifies the fixed-rank Palm family without computing the band multiplier's Fourier-source coefficients. [Circular Jacobi primary PDF](../../sources/prime77_rmt/bourgade_nikeghbali_rouault_2009.pdf) · [Bufetov primary PDF](../../sources/prime77_rmt/bufetov_2401.00518.pdf)
- **Forrester–Shen, §2.2 (2.7)–(2.12), Proposition 2.6 and Remark 2.7.** At \(\beta=2,q=0,p=J\), (2.7)–(2.8) give the limiting kernel and intensity in Bessel form, (2.10)–(2.11) give the \(J=1,2\) intensities explicitly, and (2.12) gives their Fourier-transform normalization. Proposition 2.6 and Remark 2.7 obtain a small-frequency series for the one-point transform, including the screening charge. In the frequency coordinates of the current proof, \(1-\rho_1\) is the sum of the \(J\) squared removed wavefunctions. By Fourier transformation its ordinary overlap trace is \(\sum_{j<J}\int_0^{1-|t|}u_j(s+|t|)u_j(s)\,ds\) for \(|t|\le1\) and zero outside. The band mean with the deterministic root is therefore available from the known intensity. The first beyond-window moment at \(r\ge2\) involves the oriented polynomial continuation of that trace outside its physical support and a higher-order coefficient identity. ## Exact determinant and frequency antecedents

- **Diaconis–Evans, Theorem 2.1(a), and Hughes–Rudnick, Theorems 1, 2, 7 and remarks.** Diaconis–Evans' exact Haar trace rule requires \(n\) at least the larger signed weighted total and applies even when frequencies grow with \(n\). It gives the full auxiliary unweighted determinant coefficient. Hughes–Rudnick identify the pure Haar restricted window with unitary Fourier support \([-2/m,2/m]\), and discuss failure beyond Gaussian windows. They obtain support thresholds at \(1/m\) in other classical compact groups, where their matched moments are Gaussian. These are genuine antecedents for frequency-restricted moment identities and departures, with different processes and coefficients from the derivative-tilted circular Jacobi statistic. [Diaconis–Evans primary PDF](../../sources/prime84_cue/diaconis_evans.pdf) · [Hughes–Rudnick primary PDF](../../sources/analytic79_prior_art/hughes_rudnick_mock_v1.pdf)
- **Bump–Diaconis, (2.1), (2.4), Theorem 3, and Borodin–Okounkov, §2 Theorem 1 (2.3)–(2.4).** Bump–Diaconis' finite character integrals are exact for their shifted minors. The asymptotic theorem involves a specified symbol and partition data and does not directly control a band of frequencies \(k\asymp N\) in an \(N\)-dependent symbol. Borodin–Okounkov's Szegő factor and tail identity is exact and includes a graded formal setup. Basor–Widom's second proof uses triangular factors and compression. These supply exact base machinery. The next operation in the current proof is the oriented finite-section inverse compression onto missing evaluation jets. - **Petrov, arXiv:2605.24976v1, Theorem 4.4 and Propositions 4.6–4.7.** This is a closer exact antecedent than a fixed-symbol minor asymptotic. Use his size \(N-1\), symbol \(\phi(w)=e^{z f_N(w)}\), and identical column and exterior row tilts \(\xi_j=(1-w)^J\), \(\theta_i=(1-w^{-1})^J\). Their product is \(|1-w|^{2J}\), so the theorem's left side is the exact finite free-circle Laplace numerator. Divide by its value at \(z=0\) and insert the marked-root factor. The smooth nonvanishing symbol is admissible at each fixed \(N\). The chart Gram is the ordinary free-basis Gram at zero, while the additional map in Proposition 4.6 is upper triangular with unit diagonal there. Both remain invertible locally at fixed \(N\). Proposition 4.7 handles the bounded-degree polynomial tilts. This alternate exact Fredholm identity applies to the finite moving-symbol problem itself. I found no specialization to the separate signed totals, the oriented Legendre compression, its first crossing or positive-real-zero obstruction in the theorem, its remarks, §5 or Appendix A. ## Search limits and arithmetic scope

Discovery queries combined circular Jacobi or confluent-hypergeometric Palm processes with microscopic Fourier-band linear statistics, cumulants and mock Gaussian windows. Additional queries combined Toeplitz, Christoffel, finite Fourier support, two-boundary inversion, and real Laplace zeros. I read the primary theorem passages cited above. Several other matches concerned powers of characteristic polynomials or their derivatives at fixed or microscopically shifted points, whose observables differ from a truncated Fourier band. Coverage of confluent Christoffel–Uvarov transforms, Toeplitz finite sections and determinantal cumulants remains incomplete.

The positivity of a real Laplace transform where finite is classical. The scoped obstruction here is the specific positive real zero of the formal rooted-cosine determinant extrapolation, alongside the finite moment match and explicit first crossing. The rank-two comparison also includes the sine term of the joint quartic. The arithmetic application uses \(|\zeta'(\rho)|^2\) weighting and Heap–Li–Zhao's Theorem 5 with a Dirichlet-polynomial length exponent strictly below \(1/2\), yielding the RH-conditional fixed-moment range \(rb<1/2\) in that application. The intrinsic matrix window \(rb\le1\), all-rank barrier and first correction leave the arithmetic window unchanged. Each window is finite at a fixed positive band and gives no unrestricted all-moment identification.
