# Effective scalar prime-phase CLT by excluding one prime

18 November 2026. Candidate proof for independent adjudication. Assume RH. In the strict two-sided tail with masses \(|\zeta'(\rho)|^2/|\rho|^2\), put \(P=(\log Y)^D\) for fixed \(D>0\), and use the full prime sum \(W_P\) and the exact \(H_P,V_P\) from the prior CLT. The argument below gives effective convergence of all fixed moments of \((W_P-H_P)/\sqrt{V_P}\) and an effective Kolmogorov CLT. No explicit numerical onset is calculated. The proof changes the treatment of the possible exceptional character for the scalar statistic. It still permits a dependence on that character for estimates about prescribed individual prime coordinates.

## 1. Effective shifted prefix estimate for prime-excluding support

Write \(L=\log T\) and let \(A>0\) be fixed. We use HLZ journal Theorem 5, proof §§4.1–4.4, and its pp. 1616–1618 character Dirichlet-series identity. In §4.4, p. 1615, the asterisk sums over **primitive** characters modulo \(q\). Each complete summand of \(E\) has the explicit multiplier \(a(kq)\); its other factors include the Gauss sum, \(\delta(q,kq,d,\psi)\) and the inner sum with argument \(x=Tkq/(2\pi d)\), \(d\mid kq\). Thus a term with \(q>\max\operatorname{supp}(a)\) vanishes. A term for a conductor \(q\) divisible by \(\ell\) vanishes if the support consists of \(\ell\)-free integers. This observation happens before any Perron or character estimate. It also applies to the reflected shifted expression, where conjugating real arrays leaves their support unchanged. [HLZ original pp. 1615–1619](../../sources/classical63_derivative_moments/hlz2022print.pdf)

Take \(Q=\lceil\exp(\sqrt L)\rceil\) and \(U=\exp(\sqrt L)\), so eventually \(L^A\le Q\) with an effective threshold. Let \(h=\log(Q(U+3))\asymp\sqrt L\). The effective Landau–Page formulation of Ford–Green–Konyagin–Maynard–Tao, Lemma 7.1 and Corollary 6, supplies a prime \(\ell_T\) or the value \(1\) if none is needed. For all nonprincipal primitive characters of conductor at most \(Q\) whose conductor is coprime to \(\ell_T\), the rectangle \(\Re s\ge 1-c/h\), \(|\Im s|\le U+2\) is zero-free for a fixed effective \(c>0\). In this notation \(\ell_T=1\) means impose no support restriction. The corollary takes the largest prime of the exceptional conductor. Its proof uses the effective classical real-zero separation \(1-\beta\gg q^{-1/2}(\log q)^{-2}\) and a prime-product bound to obtain \(\ell_T\gg\log\log Q\). The argument below requires only that at most one prime be omitted. Lemma 7.2 and (7.2) explicitly apply the same deletion with effective constants in the prime-gap setting. [Pinned primary source, arXiv:1412.5029v3 §7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.5029v3)

The four arrays \(8\,1_{d\mid m\ \mathrm{or}\ d\mid n}+\sigma\,1_{d=m}+\tau\,1_{d=n}\) used in the prior uniform estimate have support exactly the union of the divisor sets of \(m,n\). For \(\ell_T\nmid mn\) they therefore satisfy the support restriction above. On this support the coefficients are in \([6,10]\) and obey exact submultiplicativity. Use the source’s declared length \(y=T^{1/4}\). The true support ends at \(L^A\), so the large-modulus piece is absent on taking \(\eta=L^{A+1}\). If the exceptional conductor lies above \(L^A\), its multiplier already vanishes. Deleting its largest prime even in that case retains the bound and costs at most one summand. [Uniform padding and polarized kernel](finite_moment63_uniform_prime_blocks.md)

For the reciprocal bound, use disks centered at \(1+\delta+iu\) with outer radius \(4\delta\) and inner radius \(3\delta\), \(\delta=c/(10h)\), \(|u|\le U+1\). The outer disk is inside the zero-free rectangle. At height \(u\), the inner interval contains \([1-2\delta,1+4\delta]\). Throughout the outer disk, the periodic partial sums of a nonprincipal primitive character have magnitude at most \(q\). Truncate its Dirichlet series at \(N=\lceil (Q(U+3))^2\rceil\) and use partial summation. In the disk, \(\sum_{n\le N}n^{-\Re s}\ll h\) because \((1-\Re s)_+\log N\) is bounded. The tail is \(\ll q(1+|s|)N^{-\Re s}\), bounded effectively. Hence \(\log|L(s,\psi)|\le \log(Ch)\). At the center the Euler-series branch has \(|\log L|\le\log\zeta(1+\delta)\ll\log h\). Apply Borel–Carathéodory to the holomorphic logarithm with that branch. It gives \(|\log L(s,\psi)|\le C'\log h\) on the inner disk, uniformly in \(u,q\), so \(|1/L|\le h^{C'}\) there. On the Perron rectangle take the left edge at \(1-\delta\) and the right edge at \(1+C_1/L\), where \(C_1\) makes the initial Dirichlet series absolutely convergent. All HLZ shifts have size at most \(C_2/L\) on the fixed Cauchy circles and auxiliary disk. For effectively large \(T\), their real parts are at most \(\delta/2\) in magnitude and their imaginary parts are at most 1. Thus every \(s+\beta\) used in the denominator is covered, including after interchanging and conjugating the shifts. The same \(|L|\ll h\) bound applies to the numerator factors. This removes the need for an estimate of an exceptional residue in (39)–(41).

In the HLZ identity immediately before (38) the only reciprocal \(L\)-function is \(1/L(s+\beta,\psi)\). The other possible denominators are finite Euler factors of the form \((1-\psi(p)p^{-s-\beta})^{-1}\), regular and bounded by fixed powers of \(d\) in \(\Re(s+\beta)\ge3/4\). The polynomial \(A(s+\beta,d_1)\) has support \(u d_1\le L^A\). All finite coefficient, divisor and Gauss-sum factors can be bounded by \(L^{C_A}\), since \(d,kq\le L^A\) and their number is polynomial in \(L\). The same is true of the modified absolute divisor sums used at the initial Perron line. At \(x=Tkq/(2\pi d)\), \(\log x=L+O_A(\log L)\), so the left vertical integral is \(O_A(xL^{C_A}x^{-\delta})\), and the horizontal ones \(O_A(xL^{C_A}/U)\). The truncation error in the source, before (38), is of order \(xL^{C_A}/\sqrt U\). In particular its short divisor sum requires no ineffective input. For a fixed integer \(r\) in a short interval of radius \(v=x/\sqrt U\), inspect a largest factor of each \(r\)-tuple counted by \(\tau_r(n)\). The product of the other factors is at most \((2x)^{1-1/r}\). Summing \(O(v/d+1)\) over those products with multiplicity \(\tau_{r-1}(d)\) gives \(O_r(v\log^{r-1}(2x)+x^{1-1/r}\log^{r-2}(2x))\), and the second term is bounded by the first once \(T\) is effectively large. The complex-shift factor \(n^{O(1/L)}\) is bounded for \(n\asymp x\) and is carried in absolute-series powers off that range. Summing these estimates gives \(T L^{C_A}e^{-c_A\sqrt L}\) for \(E\), with all constants effective.

The earlier source steps do not invoke a Dirichlet exceptional-zero estimate. The good-height contour and its restoration to the endpoint use unit-window zero counts, bounds for \(\zeta'/\zeta\) at a distance from the zeros, Stirling and convexity bounds, and fixed divisor envelopes. The initial contour, the continuous shifted integral of Pratt–Robles Theorem 1.1 used by HLZ in §4.1, and Ng's pointwise oscillatory estimate used in §4.2 contribute power savings with \(y=T^{1/4}\) and a fixed small loss exponent. The principal-character Perron shift in §4.3 uses only the effective zeta zero-free region, with finite Euler factors and the same effective divisor estimates. In §4.2 the minor repair to the complex convolution bound is to retain \(n^{O(1/L)}\), as in the prior third reconstruction. That argument uses absolute Dirichlet series and \(n^\varepsilon\) bounds for fixed divisor functions on the central dyadic interval. The latter have computable constants by bounding prime factors with \(p^\varepsilon\) above a finite prime threshold. The local main-term comparison and the Cauchy constants are elementary and effective. These observations prove effectivity in principle, without tabulating the numerical constants in those original contour and mean-value bounds. Let \(M(T)=\sum_{0<\gamma\le T}|\zeta'(\rho)|^2\), and write
\[
C_T(m,n)=\frac{1}{M(T)}\sum_{0<\gamma\le T}|\zeta'(\rho)|^2\cos(\gamma\log(m/n)).
\]
The untwisted case with support \(\{1\}\) has no nonprincipal contribution, and its effective main term is \(M(T)=TL^4/(24\pi)+O(TL^3)\) by the same residue and differentiation argument. Apply the established local-factor comparison after polarization, which carries the \(1/\operatorname{lcm}(m,n)\) weight, now with the effective shifted error just proved. In the same notation \(g=(m,n)\), for any fixed \(A,B>0\),
\[
 C_T(m,n)=\frac{g}{\sqrt{mn}}+
 O^{\rm eff}_{A,B}\left(
 \frac{g}{\sqrt{mn}}\frac{\log(mn/g^2)}{L}
 +\frac{L^{-B}}{\sqrt{mn}}\right),
 \quad m,n\le L^A,\quad \ell_T\nmid mn,
 \tag{1}
\]
where the final restriction is vacuous if \(\ell_T=1\). The first term of the error is the difference from the **exact** untwisted kernel, after outer Cauchy, and does not accrue an independent common \(O(1/L)\) correction. The second term comes from shifted errors of arbitrarily large log saving, multiplied by \(\sqrt{mn}\) when converting the Dirichlet monomials to characters. The resulting factor \(mn\le L^{2A}\) relative to (1) is absorbed by choosing the pre-Cauchy saving larger by \(2A+O(1)\). Positive-ordinate real polarization suffices for \(C_T\) and for all real statistics below.

## 2. Restore the excluded summand before tail integration

Fix a finite maximal moment degree \(r\), a real \(D>0\), and \(P=(\log Y)^D\). Use the same \(P,H_P,V_P\) at every prefix height \(T\ge Y\). Take \(A\ge 2Dr+1\), so \(\ell_T\) from §1 works for every word through degree \(2r\), and take \(B>2Dr+10\). Define \(W'_P,H'_P\) by omitting \(\ell_T\) if it is at most \(P\), and let \(S'_P=W'_P-H'_P\). The omitted prime may change with \(T\). The product model is the Poisson-circle law with radii \(p^{-1/2}\). Model expectation is denoted by \(\mathbb E_\star\); actual prefix expectation by \(\mathbb E_T\).

Expand \((W'_P)^j\) as a Laurent polynomial with nonnegative symmetric coefficients \(b_j(m,n)\), where \(m,n\le P^j\), \(\ell_T\nmid mn\), and \(\sum b_j\le(2\sqrt P)^j\). The cosine form and symmetry justify the use of (1). The model coefficient at \(m/n\) equals \(g/\sqrt{mn}\ge0\). Since model coordinates are independent, their centered increments \(X_p=p^{-1/2}\cos\theta_p-1/p\) have mean zero, variance \(\tfrac12(p^{-1}-p^{-2})\) and magnitude at most \(2/\sqrt p\). Expanding an even moment of the centered sum into blocks containing at least two repeated indices gives \(\mathbb E_\star|S'_P|^{2j}\ll_j (1+H_P)^j\): for each multiplicity \(a\ge2\), \(\sum_p\mathbb E|X_p|^a\ll_a H_P\), and there are at most \(j\) blocks. It follows by binomial expansion and Hölder that \(\mathbb E_\star (W'_P)^j\ll_j (1+H_P)^j\) for each \(j\); the left side is nonnegative by the Fourier expansion, including for odd \(j\).

In the relative term of (1) use \(\log(mn/g^2)\le 2j\log P\) and sum against the model raw moment. Binomial centering then gives, uniformly for \(0\le k\le 2r\), \(T\ge Y\),
\[
\left|\mathbb E_T(S'_P)^k-\mathbb E_\star(S'_P)^k\right|
\ll^{\rm eff}_{D,r,B}
(1+H_P)^k\left(\frac{1+\log P}{\log T}+(\log T)^{-B}(2\sqrt P)^k\right).
\tag{2}
\]
For \(H_P\ge 1\), \(V_P\ge H_P/4\). Equations (2) and the model moment bounds give \(\mathbb E_T|S'_P/\sqrt{V_P}|^{2r}\le C_r\) for effectively large \(Y\), uniformly in \(T\) and in the omitted prime. Let \(S_P=W_P-H_P\) be the same full statistic at every \(T\). For every ordinate \(|S_P-S'_P|\le 2\). For \(1\le k\le r\), use
\[
|(x+z)^k-x^k|\le k|z|(|x|+|z|)^{k-1}
\]
with \(z=(S_P-S'_P)/\sqrt{V_P}\). Hölder and the \(2r\)-th moment bound make this restoration cost \(O_r(H_P^{-1/2})\) in each normalized moment. The identical argument applies to the full product model. Thus for \(k\le r\)
\[
\left|\mathbb E_T(S_P/\sqrt{V_P})^k-\mathbb E_\star(S_P/\sqrt{V_P})^k\right|
\ll^{\rm eff}_{D,r}
H_P^{-1/2}+(1+H_P)^r\frac{1+\log P}{\log Y}+(\log Y)^{-2}.
\tag{3}
\]
The last term absorbs the negligible error in (2), using \(H_P\le 1+\log P\) and the fixed choice of \(B\). Picking the largest prime as in Corollary 6 would lower the restoration term to \(O_r((H_P\log\log Y)^{-1/2})\) by \(|X_{\ell_T}|\le 2/\sqrt{\ell_T}\), but this improvement is unnecessary.

For the full model, cumulants of order \(j\ge3\) of \(S_P\) are bounded in magnitude by an effective \(C_j\sum_{n\ge2}n^{-j/2}\). This follows directly by the finite cumulant polynomial and \(|X_p|\le 2/\sqrt p\). The standardized first and second cumulants are 0 and 1 exactly. The moment–cumulant formula therefore gives an effective error \(O_r(H_P^{-3/2})\) for all fixed moments through \(r\) compared with the normal law, with any odd Gaussian moment zero. This uses only a finite number of elementary estimates at each fixed order.

For \(f(\gamma)=(S_P(\gamma)/\sqrt{V_P})^k\), let \(A_f(T)=\sum_{0<\gamma\le T}|\zeta'(\rho)|^2 f(\gamma)\). Formula (3) controls \(A_f(T)-\mu_k M(T)\) by \(M(T)\) times a single effective error \(e_{D,r}(Y)\to0\) for every \(T\ge Y\). At this step \(f\) is the full fixed statistic, so there is no height-dependent integrand from the omitted prime. With \(K(T)=(1/4+T^2)^{-1}\), the strict one-sided tail is \(-K(Y)A_f(Y)-\int_Y^\infty K'(t)A_f(t)\,dt\). The boundary at infinity vanishes by \(|A_f(T)|\le \|f\|_\infty M(T)\) for fixed \(P\). Since \(K(Y)M(Y)+\int_Y^\infty |K'(t)|M(t)\,dt\ll Y^{-1}\log^4Y\) effectively and the strict-tail mass is effectively asymptotic to \(Y^{-1}\log^4Y/(24\pi)\), the tail error is \(O^{\rm eff}(e_{D,r}(Y))\) in normalized expectation. Reflection gives the same law for the two-sided tail. This transfer requires no eventually fixed exceptional conductor.

## 3. Effective passage from moments to a distribution

Here is a finite procedure for every \(D>0,\varepsilon>0\) with those parameters specified effectively. Let \(Z_Y=(W_P-H_P)/\sqrt{V_P}\) on the strict tail and \(G\sim N(0,1)\). Add an independent \(N(0,\tau^2)\) noise to each law, with \(\tau>0\) chosen below. The smoothed distribution functions differ by at most
\[
\frac{1}{\pi}\int_0^\infty
   |\phi_{Z_Y}(u)-\phi_G(u)|e^{-\tau^2u^2/2}\frac{du}{u}.
\tag{4}
\]
Indeed the signed smoothed density has a Fourier transform integrable after division by \(u\), since both distributions have finite first moment and the signed measure has mass zero. Invert after dividing by the Fourier differentiation factor. The primitive tends to zero at infinity by the Riemann–Lebesgue lemma, fixing the integration constant. This gives (4) without any asymptotic choice of test functions.

For any \(\eta>0\), the Kolmogorov distance for the unsmoothed laws is at most the right-hand side of (4), plus \(2\Pr(|N(0,\tau^2)|>\eta)+2\eta/\sqrt{2\pi}\). To see this, compare the unsmoothed CDFs at points shifted by \(\pm\eta\) with the corresponding smoothed ones, then use the bounded normal density for the shift by \(2\eta\). Choose a small rational \(\eta\) and then a small rational \(\tau\) by Chebyshev's bound so these two errors total at most \(\varepsilon/3\). Choose a finite \(R>0\) so the interval \([R,\infty)\) in (4) contributes at most \(\varepsilon/3\), using \(|\phi_{Z_Y}-\phi_G|\le2\) and the Gaussian factor.

For the integral through \(R\), Taylor-expand both characteristic functions through degree \(2m-1\). The moment error contribution before the factor \(1/\pi\) is bounded by \(\sum_{j=1}^{2m-1}|\mathbb E Z_Y^j-\mu_j|R^j/(j\,j!)\). The remainders together contribute at most
\[
\frac{(\mathbb E Z_Y^{2m}+\mu_{2m})R^{2m}}{(2m)(2m)!}.
\]
Choose \(m\) effectively so that the latter, with \(\mathbb E Z_Y^{2m}\) replaced by \(2\mu_{2m}\), is less than the allotted bound for remainders. This is possible since \(\mu_{2m}=(2m)!/(2^m m!)\). Now set \(r=2m\) in the preceding uniform estimates. Take \(Y\) beyond the effective threshold giving \(\mathbb E Z_Y^{2m}\le 2\mu_{2m}\) and the required at-most-\((2m-1)\)-th moment errors. Such a computable threshold follows from (3) and effective growth of \(H_P\), for instance the elementary lower bound \(\sum_{p\le P}p^{-1}\ge\log\log P-O(1)\) from the finite Euler product majorizing \(\sum_{n\le P}1/n\). The upper bound \(H_P\le1+\log P\) gives elementary effective decay of the two \(L\)-dependent terms in (3). This proves the claimed distributional effectivity for fixed parameters and leaves the statistic exactly the original sum over all primes up to \(P\).

## Scope of the new step

The deletion is the classical Landau–Page device as used by Ford–Green–Konyagin–Maynard–Tao. The application here uses the factor \(a(kq)\) in the discrete derivative-moment proof and restores the omitted prime at the level of the full normalized scalar before partial summation. No location or identification of the exceptional prime is needed for the bound. A numerical value of \(Y_0(D,\varepsilon)\), an effective all-prime covariance theorem, and priority of this particular application remain outside the result.
