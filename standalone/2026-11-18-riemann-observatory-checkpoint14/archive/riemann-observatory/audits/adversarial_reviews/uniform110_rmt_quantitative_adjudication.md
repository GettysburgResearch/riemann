## Finite witness and its circle bound

Write \(M(z)=\sum_k\mu_k z^k/k!\), with \(M(0)=1\), and choose a real zero \(0<t_*\le1/d\). Cauchy's estimate on \(R=2/d\) controls each \(|\mu_k|/k!\) by \(BR^{-k}\). For products of the two degree-\(L\) Taylor polynomials of \(e^{\pm t_*x/2}\), omitted index pairs have total degree \(m>L\). The unrestricted sum over pairs of that degree uses \(\sum_{i+j=m}\binom mi=2^m\), canceling the factor \(2^{-m}\). The tail is at most \(B\sum_{m>L}(t_*/R)^m\le B2^{-L}\), including equality of the scale ratio at \(t_*=1/d\). With the proposed ceiling for \(L\), the error \(e\) is at most \(1/[8(B+1)]\). The finite diagonal product has absolute value at most \(e(B+e)\le1/8\), while the off-diagonal square is at least \((1-e)^2\ge(7/8)^2\). This gives a negative determinant even if the negative-real evaluation of the entire function is negative. The specified three cases for the first diagonal entry produce valid negative squares. The Hankel matrix is \((L+1)\)-by-\((L+1)\) and uses moments through raw degree \(2L\).

The oriented change of variables sends the profile arguments to \(p+\ell t,q+\ell t\), where \(\ell=1-p-q\), including when \(\ell<0\). Coefficientwise absolute majorization by \(s=1+p+q\) bounds each profile argument by \(2s\). The shifted Legendre coefficient sum is at most \(\sqrt{2i+1}\,8^i\), since \(\binom{i+k}{k}\le4^i\) for \(k\le i\) and \(\sum_k\binom ik=2^i\). Hence the proposed entry bound is \(\sqrt{(2i+1)(2j+1)}16^{i+j}s^{i+j+1}\). All formal monomial expectations have nonnegative coefficients in the cosine parameter because their moment polynomials are those of independent compound Poisson sums with positive jump moments. Evaluation at \(R>0\) therefore bounds evaluation on the complex disk. The common entry constant follows from \(s^{2J-1}\le(2J-1)!e^s\) and
\[
 E_R e^s=\exp\!left(1+R\int_a^b(e^x-1)\frac{dx}{x}\right)
 \le \exp(1+Rd e^b).
\]
The two intensities are each \(R/2\), giving \(R\) in this display. Leibniz's \(J!\) factor and the absolute bound on the exponential in \(M\) give precisely the stated circular majorant, also at \(J=1\). Positive semidefiniteness is protected through every order with \(2L\le\lfloor1/b\rfloor\). This argument supplies no least-order formula for general \(J\).

At rank one, \(\sigma^2=H/2\), so the signed Gaussian density modifier is \(1-dx/\sigma^2\). The finite moment form in normalized probabilists' Hermite polynomials is \(I-(d/\sigma^2)T_L\). The edge between indices \(k-1,k\) has weight \(\sigma\sqrt k\). The determinant recurrence for \(T_L\) therefore gives the order-\(L+1\) polynomial \(\sigma^{L+1}\operatorname{He}_{L+1}(x/\sigma)\). The largest zero of \(\operatorname{He}_{L+1}\) must strictly exceed \(\sigma/d\) to obtain a negative square. Equality is singular positive semidefiniteness. The two-vertex lower bound and truncated-shift upper bound give the stated strict lower condition \(L>\sigma^2/(4d^2)\) and sufficient upper integer \(\lfloor\sigma^2/d^2\rfloor+1\). The last-vertices sine-vector bound correctly proves the leading asymptotic \(L_*\sim\sigma^2/(4d^2)\). As an optional refinement of that same comparison, taking \(q=\lfloor L^{1/3}\rfloor\) balances the errors \(O(q/\sqrt L+\sqrt L/q^2)\), giving \(\lambda_{\max}(T_L)/\sigma\ge2\sqrt L-O(L^{-1/6})\). For \(v=\sigma/d\to\infty\), this also yields \(L_*=v^2/4+O(v^{2/3})\) by the strict root criterion. The fixed-ratio example \(a=b/2\) gives its printed leading \((\log 2)/(2b^2)\), distinct from the first raw crossing \(\lfloor1/b\rfloor+1\).

## Petrov finite chart and continuum cutoff

The local Petrov v1 PDF matches the stated SHA-256. Equations (3.5)–(3.6), Corollary 3.3 and Theorem 4.4 use the stated triangular orders and determinant normalization. For the real parameter, \(b_0\) is unimodular. Thus \(I-K=T(b_0)T(b_0)^*\) with \(K=H(b_0)H(b_0)^*\). The inverse triangular factors each have norm at most \(e^{|z|H_N/2}\). This gives the common lower bound \(e^{-2|z|H_N}\) for \(I-K\). The range of \(X\) has highest degree \(m-1+J=n-1\), and \(T(\phi_-)\) sends these first \(n\) modes into themselves. Therefore the orthogonal complement of \(C=T(\phi_-)X\) really contains the whole tail \(Q_nH\), with a \(J\)-dimensional residual space \(W\). This uses the actual dimension difference and requires no invertible graph chart over the smaller tail. [Pinned Petrov v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.24976v1)

On \(W\oplus Q_nH\), block elimination by unit triangular identity-plus-finite-rank operators gives the Schur complement quotient. The infimum form of the complement proves its lower bound because the two summands in the decomposed vector are orthogonal. Substituting zero for its tail gives the upper bound. With \(L=P_nT(\phi_+)|_{P_n}\), the exact relations are \(C=L^*X\), \(W=\operatorname{Ran}(L^{-1}U)\), and \(M=U^*L^{-*}L^{-1}U\). Substituting Petrov's Wiener–Hopf identity gives \(P_nA^{-1}|_{P_n}=L^{-1}A_nL^{-*}\) in that order. Thus \(S=M^{-1/2}(U^*A_n^{-1}U)M^{-1/2}\). For the Gram identity, first orthonormalize the columns of \(X\); the corresponding basis-change determinant cancels between \(\Gamma(z)\) and \(\Gamma(0)\). Jacobi applied to \(LL^*\), whose determinant is one, then gives exactly \(\det\Gamma(z)/\det\Gamma(0)=\det M\).

For \(z\ge0\), each convolution factor from \(L^{-1}\) is, after scalar division by \(e^{H_Nz/2}\), a compound Poisson shift law. The two independent shifts give the scalar divisor \(e^{H_Nz}\) for \(M\). Their scaled laws converge since \(n/N\to1\) at fixed \(J\) and the jump measure converges by Riemann sums. A vector evaluated at mode \(t\) shifted by \(p\) vanishes when \(t<p\). Thus the common true range is \([\max(p,q),1]\) and vanishes for a shift above 1. The limiting integral shrinks continuously at either boundary. Uniform Riemann approximation on the admissible grid and boundedness of the zero-extended kernel justify passing to weak limits. Changing coordinates to \(s=t-p\) gives the transposed formal integrand, with true lower endpoint \(\max(0,q-p)\) and formal lower endpoint \(q\). Subtraction is the ordinary lower interval of length \(\min(p,q)\) for \(p,q\le1\), even if the formal range is reversed. The statement about pure signs concerns that coefficient window. Beyond it an individual shift can exceed one and must be cut off.

For the quantitative lower bound, the sum in Petrov (3.6) is \(\sum_{k\in\mathcal B_N} k(z/(2k))^2=H_Nz^2/4\). Positive compact compression min–max bounds both tail determinants below by the full Hardy determinant and above by one. Combining \(L_n\le1\) with \(S\le I\) proves the quoted two-sided quotient bounds. The finite symbol compression has spectrum between \(e^{-|z|H_N}\) and \(e^{|z|H_N}\); the stated lower bound after normalization at the positive formal zero follows. These bounds are real-axis statements and do not require a continuum identification of the normalized complementary determinant.

## Exact bandwidth indexing in the truncation

For a nonempty band, the two signed Laurent coefficients for a frequency \(k\) contribute \(2k/(2k)^2\) to the weighted square norm, giving \(\|g\|_*^2=H_N/2\) and \(\|g\|_1=H_N\). The absolute convolution inequality for \(\sqrt{|j|}\) and Young's inequality yield the claimed factor \(r H_N^{r-1}\) for the \(r\)-fold convolution. A matrix entry removed by cutting at the first \(M\) modes has at least one index \(\ge M\), so its Hankel coefficient index \(i+j+1\) is \(\ge M+1\). Among all entries carrying coefficient index \(k\) there are precisely \(k\) choices, so the Hilbert–Schmidt estimate from the positive-frequency tail is valid. A power with \(rk_*\le M\) contributes nothing to that tail. The first possible power satisfies \(r\ge\lfloor M/k_*\rfloor+1\). Reindexing \(r/r!\) by \(s=r-1\) gives exactly the stated factorial-tail lower index \(\lfloor M/k_*\rfloor\), including equality when \(M=Lk_*\).

The trace-norm difference follows from the two Hilbert–Schmidt factors. Both real compressions along the determinant interpolation remain bounded below by the same \(\delta=e^{-2|z|H_N}\), since truncating the Hankel operator cannot increase its norm. Its inverse bound controls the log derivative, so the difference of the two logs for a quotient costs at most twice the individual uniform constant. With \(M\ge n\), each relevant subspace contains \(Q_MH\) and the truncated factor is the identity there. In an empty finite band one instead takes \(K=0\) and obtains exact identity factors, with no bandwidth needed. These checks concern the finite rooted CUE model and its formal extrapolation.
