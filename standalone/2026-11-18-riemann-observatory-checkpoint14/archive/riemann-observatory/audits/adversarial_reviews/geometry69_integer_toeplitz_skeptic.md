# Independent review of the integer reciprocal trace form

18 November 2026. The finite purity and inertia formulas hold for the stated positive-integer-\(q\) input, including repeated roots, reducible polynomials, and nonsquare \(q\). The leading block indexed by the number of distinct roots suffices. The direct congruence below also works without computing or representing \(\sqrt q\). The unscaled Toeplitz result has a primary antecedent. [Director finite trace review](director_finite_trace_round10.md) · [Parallel derivation](uniform69_integer_toeplitz_purity.md)

## Direct congruence in the original radius

Let \(P\) be monic real with nonzero roots and multiplicity-preserving symmetry \(\alpha\mapsto q/\alpha\), \(q>0\). Write \(D_P\) for the set of its distinct roots, \(m_\alpha\) for multiplicities, and \(r=|D_P|\). Conjugation and reciprocity give an involution \(\iota(\alpha)=q/\overline\alpha\) of \(D_P\) with \(m_{\iota(\alpha)}=m_\alpha\). Let \(V_{i,\alpha}=\alpha^i\) for \(0\le i<k\), and \(A_{\alpha,\beta}=m_\alpha\mathbf{1}_{\{\beta=\iota(\alpha)\}}\). For \(i\ge j\),
\[
(VAV^*)_{ij}
=\sum_{\alpha\in D_P}m_\alpha\,\alpha^i\overline{\iota(\alpha)}^{\,j}
=q^j\sum_{\alpha\in D_P}m_\alpha\alpha^{i-j}
=q^{\min(i,j)}t_{|i-j|}.
\]
Both sides are Hermitian, and each \(t_n\) is real by conjugate symmetry, so this proves the other half of the matrix too.

For \(k=r\) the leading \(r\)-row Vandermonde matrix \(V\) is invertible. For \(k\ge r\), \(V^*\) is surjective. Take a right inverse of \(V^*\) and a basis of its kernel. Using these columns as a basis exhibits a congruence \(VAV^*\sim A\oplus 0_{k-r}\), including repeated roots since \(V\) was formed only on the distinct ones. A fixed point of \(\iota\) contributes the positive block \((m_\alpha)\), and an off-circle orbit contributes the symmetric block \(\begin{pmatrix}0&m_\alpha\\ m_\alpha&0\end{pmatrix}\), of inertia \((1,1,0)\). Consequently the dimension-\(k\) matrix has inertia \((c+b,b,k-r)\) for \(k\ge r=c+2b\). The integer matrix at \(k=d\) is PSD exactly when \(b=0\). The full \(d\times d\) matrix is positive *definite* exactly when all roots lie on the circle and are distinct.

The interpolation argument on real polynomials also follows: if a complex coefficient vector yields a negative value, its real and imaginary parts have real quadratic forms whose sum is that value. At least one is negative. A zero \(1\times1\) pivot after elimination is a feature of the coefficient form, independent of whether the original \(t_0=d>0\).

## Exact test, edge cases and reduced size

For monic integral \(P\), verify \(P(0)\ne0\) and \(X^dP(q/X)=P(0)P(X)\) coefficientwise. The left leading coefficient is \(P(0)\). Both sides have the same roots precisely under \(q\)-reciprocity, with multiplicities, and the comparison automatically allows a negative constant coefficient. Use Newton's identities to compute the integer \(t_k\) for \(0\le k\le d-1\). If \(r\) is desired, it is \(d-\deg\gcd(P,P')\) over \(\mathbb Q\). One can also apply the entire test with unit multiplicities to the monic squarefree part. Its roots are the same distinct \(q\)-reciprocal set, so the size-\(r\) version then has the same inertia even though its matrix entries may differ. The squarefree part has integer coefficients by Gauss's lemma. Either procedure uses exact arithmetic on coefficients.

For PSD, check zero diagonals with nonzero off-diagonal in the same row before discarding any row with zero diagonal. Such a \(2\times2\) principal minor has determinant \(-b^2<0\). If every surviving diagonal is positive, eliminate one by the exact rational Schur complement and repeat. To compute *full inertia*, use any nonzero diagonal as a signed \(1\times1\) pivot. If the remaining diagonals all vanish but some off-diagonal \(b\ne0\), its \(2\times2\) block \(\begin{pmatrix}0&b\\b&0\end{pmatrix}\) is invertible with one sign of each kind, and the same congruence operation gives its Schur complement. The entirely zero residual contributes only zero eigenvalues. In both algorithms positive, negative and rank-deficient cases are decisive without numerical tolerances. The formula for intermediate Schur complements as bordered minors divided by the pivot principal-submatrix determinant gives polynomial bit bounds for reduced rationals. The operations are cubic in the matrix size in the usual dense arithmetic count. Sylvester's *leading principal minor* positivity test addresses positive definiteness. Its nonnegative variant alone cannot certify PSD for arbitrary singular real symmetric matrices.

The independent replay verifies degree one with \(P(0)<0\), purity at nonsquare \(q=2\), a singular repeated pure root, two complex off-circle pairs, an off-circle pair both repeated, and a zero-diagonal nonzero-row Schur complement in a bona fide reciprocal polynomial. For the last, \(q=1\) and \(P=(X^2-3X+1)(X^2-X+1)\) have \(t_0=t_1=4\), \(t_2=6\). Eliminating the first diagonal \(4\) leaves second diagonal \(4-4^2/4=0\) and the next entry in that row \(4-4\cdot6/4=-2\). The matrix correctly has one negative eigenvalue. For \(q=100\), the companion example \(P=(X^2-21X+100)(X^2+100)\) gives exact traces \(4,21,41,2961\) and a positive definite leading \(3\times3\) block. All three two-term bounds at these indices pass but the size-four matrix has inertia \((3,1,0)\). Thus the uniform claim cannot generally drop the last row and column, and individual short two-term tests are weaker. [Exact replay](../../scripts/geometry69_integer_toeplitz_checks.py)

## Primary comparison

I checked Heinig–Rost, *Bezoutians*, TU Chemnitz preprint 9/2008, printed pp. 47–50 and 62. The 82-page PDF has SHA-256 f86348b398730c09eccaaebdfd9a367fec5d606ca48de4b2151df2dc8e883cd6. Example 8.2 gives the ordinary power-sum Toeplitz matrix as a rational generating function with simple partial fractions counted with multiplicity. Proposition 8.6, (8.10), and Corollary 8.7 give the Vandermonde congruence with swapped-pair blocks for the simple-pole, nonsingular core. In the repeated-root case, identical terms in the rational generating function merge to one simple pole with multiplicity weight. The direct congruence above identifies the nullity added by the rectangular Vandermonde. On p. 62, §10 subsection 7, the authors use the logarithmic-derivative reversal quotient \((w')^\#/w^\#=\sum_j(1-\bar z_jt)^{-1}\) and explicitly state that the Toeplitz matrix has signature equal to the number of distinct roots on the circle. Theorem 10.10 phrases this in terms of the associated Toeplitz Bézoutian. The monic normalized polynomial \(w\) in the present test is self-inversive. If its reversal equals \(-w\), multiplying by \(i\) gives a conjugate-symmetric coefficient vector, and the common scalar cancels from the reversed quotient. Thus the source's normalization covers both signs. The source’s different open-disk Schur–Cohn Bézoutian \(\mathrm{Bez}_T(w^\#,w)\) in Theorem 10.9 vanishes when the arguments are proportional. Using that test instead would lose the present information. The primary antecedent establishes that the signed Toeplitz criterion already exists. The integer scaling is a specialization to arithmetic reciprocity. [Primary preprint](https://www.tu-chemnitz.de/mathematik/preprint/2008/PREPRINT_09.pdf) · [Cached copy](../../sources/geometry69/heinig-rost.pdf)

As \(k\) grows across a family of unrelated polynomials, a sequence of positive trace forms is meaningful for a limiting spectrum only with an independently justified common spectral realization, trace convergence on a suitable domain, and control of any signed or divergent terms. The finite congruence concerns positive multiplicities of one fixed finite spectrum. It identifies no Jordan blocks and supplies no such arithmetic or limiting realization for classical \(\zeta\).
