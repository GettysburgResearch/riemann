# Primary antecedents for the Manzur–Noor logarithmic group

17 November 2026. Sklyar–Marchenko's logarithmic group is exactly the time-reversed adjoint of the Manzur–Noor group after a unitary difference map. Their preprint proves strong continuity, the generator on the difference space and the nonbasis eigenvector geometry. Their follow-up gives the spectrum and resolvent, and Sklyar–Marchenko–Polak give linear norm growth. The step-coordinate expressions, the pointwise sharp norm and the complete invariant-metric condition below are deductions from these and other identified inputs. This bounded search does not assign publication priority to those deductions. [Sklyar–Marchenko, arXiv:1405.2731v4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.2731v4) · [Manzur–Noor, arXiv:2610.03568v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.03568v1)

## Exact identification and spectral consequences

Index the original disk coordinates from zero, so \(S e_j=e_{j+1}\), \(D_t e_j=(j+1)^{it}e_j\), and \(F=I-S\). Sklyar–Marchenko v4 §2.1 and equations (6)–(8) use the completion of the canonical first-difference space. In indices starting at one, its vector \(c=(c_n)_{n\ge1}\) has norm \(\|(c_n-c_{n-1})_{n\ge1}\|_{\ell^2}\), with \(c_0=0\), and the group \(U_t\) of Theorem 6 multiplies its coefficients by \(n^{it}\). The difference map \(L:c\mapsto Fc\) extends unitarily onto \(\ell^2\), since \(\ker F^*=0\) and its finite-sequence range is dense. Equation (15) of Manzur–Noor is \(T_{-t}^*F=FD_t\). Consequently

\[
 L U_t L^{-1}=T_{-t}^*,\qquad A_T=-\bigl(L A_U L^{-1}\bigr)^*.
\]

The identity first holds on finitely supported \(c\) and extends by boundedness. The same multiplier appears in both sources with the index shift shown above. No assertion about how the authors found their operators follows from this identification. [Manzur–Noor (13)–(15)](../../sources/prefix_skeptic62_criteria2/manzur_noor.pdf) · [Sklyar–Marchenko v4, Theorem 6](../../sources/finite_moment63_operator_antecedents/sklyar_marchenko_eigenvectors_v4.pdf)

For this exponent, Sklyar–Marchenko 2021 Theorem 3.1 gives spectrum \(\{i\log n:n\ge1\}\) and coefficient resolvent \(r_n=(i\log n-\lambda)^{-1}\) in the convention \((A_U-\lambda I)^{-1}\). The adjoint and time signs preserve that spectrum. Compactness follows directly from their printed resolvent formula. Under \(L\), its matrix is a diagonal with entries \(r_n\to0\) plus the strict Hardy mean preceded by a diagonal with entries \((n-1)(r_n-r_{n-1})\to0\). The Hardy mean is bounded and both displayed diagonals are compact. This verifies compactness without treating the biorthogonal vectors as a Schauder basis. The same representation with \(r_n\) replaced by \(i\log n\) shows that the transformed generator is diagonal log multiplication plus a bounded Hardy term, with the maximal logarithmic weighted domain. [Sklyar–Marchenko 2021, Theorem 3.1 and proof of \(k=1\)](../../sources/finite_moment63_operator_antecedents/sklyar_marchenko_resolvent.pdf)

Sklyar–Marchenko v2 Proposition 6(1)–(3) gives, in disk indices for \(k=1\), the complete biorthogonal pair. The same formulas follow from v4 Proposition 3(1)–(4) under the isometry \(L\).

\[
 \phi_n=e_{n-1}-e_n,\qquad \psi_n=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}e_j.
\]

Here \(\psi_n\) is the eigenvector of \(T_t\) with character \(n^{it}\) and \(\phi_n\) is its adjoint eigenvector. The coefficient resolvent has a simple pole at each distinct \(i\log n\). The Riesz projection of \(A_T\) is therefore \(a\mapsto\langle a,\phi_n\rangle\psi_n\) and its norm is \(\sqrt{2n}\) from the two elementary vector norms. Any Schauder enumeration would make the norm of the individual coefficient projections bounded by twice its partial-sum bound. Thus every enumeration fails, with Riesz bases excluded as well. [Sklyar–Marchenko v2, Proposition 6](../../sources/finite_moment63_operator_antecedents/sklyar_marchenko_eigenvectors.pdf) · [v4, Proposition 3](../../sources/finite_moment63_operator_antecedents/sklyar_marchenko_eigenvectors_v4.pdf)

The step map \(B\) already appears, with opposite overall sign, from Noor's (1.3)–(1.4). Indeed, the coefficient of \(z^{n-1}\) in \(((1-z)g)'/(1-z)\) equals \(na_n-\sum_{j<n}a_j\). Thus \(B\psi_n=n u_n\), where \(u_n=\mathbf1_{[n,\infty)}\), and \(\langle b,B\phi_n\rangle=(b_n-b_{n-1})/n\) in the step norm. The imported spectral projection is exactly \(b\mapsto(b_n-b_{n-1})u_n\). In these coordinates put \(b_0=0\), \((Db)_n=(\log n)b_n\), and \((Hb)_n=\sum_{k<n}\log(1+1/k)b_k\). The discrete product rule gives \(\Delta(D-H)b=(\log n)\Delta b_n\), where \(\Delta b_n=b_n-b_{n-1}\). For the norm \(\sum |b_n|^2/[n(n+1)]\), \(H\) is bounded by the Hardy inequality after dividing coordinates by \(\sqrt{n(n+1)}\). In fact \(\log(1+1/k)\sqrt{k(k+1)}\le1\), from \(2\sinh(x/2)\ge x\) with \(x=\log(1+1/k)\). Finite cell sequences form a core for \(D\) by truncation, and their finite jump expansion differentiates exactly. Conversely, existence of the generator limit in step norm implies coordinatewise differentiation and hence \(Db\) belongs to the step space, since \(Hb\) already does. This recovers \(A=i(D-H)\) on the stated log-weighted domain. [Noor, arXiv:1809.09577v1, §1.2](../../sources/finite_moment63_operator_antecedents/noor_step.pdf) · [Archived step bridge](director_manzur_noor_round8.md)

## Sharp norm and invariant metrics

Sklyar–Marchenko–Polak 2021 §1, (1.2) and Theorem 2.1 give linear norm growth for the same logarithmic choice, with bounds \(c|t|\le\|U_t\|\le p(|t|)\) and a linear \(p\). They credit their prior JFA theorem for that case. Theorem 4.2 gives no maximally growing orbit for their class. The 2025 paper §1 and Theorem 2.1 revisit the same family and give sublinear orbit estimates on dense subsets. These are exact-family antecedents for growth order. The bounds inspected there leave the pointwise norm formula unspecified. [One Class of Linearly Growing \(C_0\)-Groups](../../sources/finite_moment63_operator_antecedents/sklyar_marchenko_polak_linear.pdf) · [Sub-Linear Growth of a Special Class of \(C_0\)-Groups on Dense Subsets](../../sources/finite_moment63_operator_antecedents/sklyar_marchenko_polak_dense.pdf)

The classical discrete Cesàro operator gives a short independent path to the exact lower bound. Let \(C_0\) be the standard averaging operator in zero indices, so the Manzur–Noor strict Copson operator is \(C=C_0^*S^*\). The matrix difference \(C-C_0^*\) is compact. For the adjoints this follows by writing the difference between \(j^{-1}\sum_{k<j}b_k\) and \((j+1)^{-1}\sum_{k\le j}b_k\) as the standard mean multiplied by a diagonal tending to zero plus a diagonal tending to zero. The entry at zero is finite-rank. Equation (7) gives

\[
 T_tD_{-t}=I+it C_0^*\pmod{\mathcal K},
 \qquad q_j(t)(j+1)^{-it}=j\bigl(1-(j/(j+1))^{it}\bigr)\longrightarrow it.
\]

Brown–Halmos–Shields Theorem 2 identifies \(\sigma_p(C_0^*)\) as the open disk \(|z-1|<1\). Let interior eigenvalues approach a boundary point \(z_*\) maximizing \(|1+it z_*|\). Corresponding normalized eigenvectors are weakly null, since every weak cluster vector would be an eigenvector at the boundary point, where there is no point spectrum. The compact term tends to zero on these vectors and the norm is at least \(|1+it|+|t|\). On the other side, the step Mellin representation is \(f(s)\mapsto (s-it)f(s-it)/s\), first on the dense span of \(n^{-s}/s\). Ambient vertical translation is unitary on the boundary line and the multiplier norm is the supremum of \(|(1/2+iy-it)/(1/2+iy)|\), equal to \(\sqrt{1+t^2}+|t|\). This proves the sharp equality using an antecedent Cesàro spectral theorem and the step model. [Brown–Halmos–Shields, Theorem 2, printed p. 130](../../sources/finite_moment63_operator_antecedents/brown_halmos_shields.pdf) · [Manzur–Noor (7), (9)–(11)](../../sources/prefix_skeptic62_criteria2/manzur_noor.pdf)

Every bounded positive metric \(M\) invariant under all \(J_t=B T_t B^{-1}\) is a weighted jump form. Distinct characters force \(\langle Mu_n,u_m\rangle=0\) for \(n\ne m\). Set \(w_n=\langle Mu_n,u_n\rangle\ge0\). On the dense finite jump span, the form is \(\sum w_n|\Delta b_n|^2\). Testing a vector supported on cell \(n\) gives \(n(n+1)(w_n+w_{n+1})\le\|M\|\), since that vector has only two jumps. Conversely this boundedness condition, equivalently \(\sup_n n^2w_n<\infty\), bounds the proposed quadratic form on the step space by \(|\Delta b_n|^2\le2(|b_n|^2+|b_{n-1}|^2)\). Density now identifies the form on the full space. It is injective exactly when every \(w_n>0\). It cannot be coercive because \(\|u_n\|^2=1/n\) but \(w_n=O(n^{-2})\). Equivalently, coercivity plus invariance would imply uniform boundedness of \(J_t\), contrary to the known linear growth. In the completion under any such injective metric, the jump map with weights \(\sqrt{w_n}\) is unitary onto \(\ell^2\) and the group becomes the diagonal unitary group. This is a concrete instance of the metric completion in Antoine–Trapani §1 and Propositions 3.12–3.14. [Antoine–Trapani, arXiv:1307.5644v2](../../sources/finite_moment63_operator_antecedents/antoine_trapani.pdf)
