# Prior art for band moment windows and Gaussian–polynomial positivity

18 November 2026 UTC. The bounded search found a precise classical antecedent for the **finite order Fourier-support window**, a precise primary antecedent for the **derivative-square rooted CUE law**, and standard **polynomial–normal transform theory** for the positivity test. The strongest earlier arithmetic matches use the same derivative-square prefix weight with particular Dirichlet-polynomial tests. I did not locate a source computing the proposed polynomial-band coefficient family with that weighting, or the particular quartic prefactor in the project's formal transform. This is a comparison of inspected statements, not a publication-priority claim.

## A scope issue in the 2002 continuous-height source

The introductory definition of \(w\) in the four-page journal and arXiv v1 lists only \(w\ge0\), mass one and compact Fourier support. Read literally this permits weights with infinite logarithmic moment, so the first moment statement needs a tail qualification. To witness this, take a nonnegative Schwartz \(w_0\) of mass one with compact Fourier support, for example a normalized square of a real Schwartz bandlimited function. The weight
\[
 w(u)=\sum_{n\ge1}2^{-n}w_0(u-e^{4^n})
\]
is smooth, nonnegative, integrable with mass one, and has the same compact Fourier support. The \(L^1\) convergence of the sum ensures the support statement. A fixed bounded interval on which \(w_0\) has positive mass, translated by \(e^{4^n}\), gives \(\int w(u)\log(2+|u|)\,du=\infty\). A symmetric mixture of positive and negative translations works too.

Choose an even real Schwartz \(f\) of positive integral and smooth Fourier support strictly inside the theorem's range. At fixed \(T\) the oscillatory term (4) is a bounded finite sum. The two polar evaluations of \(f\) remain bounded as \(\tau\) varies. In (3), use \(\Omega(r)=\log(1+|r|)+O(1)\) and substitute \(r=\tau+2\pi x/\log T\). The logarithm differs from \(\log(1+|\tau|)\) by at most \(\log(1+2\pi|x|/\log T)\), using the triangle inequality in both directions. Thus \(N_f(\tau)=(\int f)\log(1+|\tau|)/\log T+O_T(1)\), and averaging with this \(w\) diverges. Even \(w\) of class \(C^\infty\) is insufficient. The flaw is in the averaging weight's tail, independently of the arithmetic Fourier separation calculation.

Taking \(w\) Schwartz with the stated Fourier support repairs this issue. For example put \(K=T/H\ge1\). Outside \(|u+K|<K/2\), the negative part of \(\log(1+|T+Hu|)-\log T\) is bounded. Inside, use \(w(u)\ll_A K^{-A}\) and the integrable powers of \(\log(K/|u+K|)\); the positive part is bounded by a multiple of \(\log(2+|u|)\). Thus the logarithmic term differs from its main value by \(O(1/\log T)\) in each fixed \(L^m(w\,du)\). The polar evaluations have \(m\)-th absolute integrated mass \(O(T^{m\sigma/2}H^{-1}(\log T)^{O_m(1)})=o(1)\) if \(\widehat f\) is supported in \([-\sigma,\sigma]\) with \(\sigma<2\eta/m\), by Fourier integration by parts at the fixed imaginary shifts and boundedness of \(w\). The prime-product window is unchanged. This is a source-scope qualification for the comparison, not a canonical error adjudication.

## Standard positivity mechanism and application to the project's extrapolation

Take the formal expression as supplied, with \(H=\log(b/a)>0,\ d=b-a>0,\ d_3=b^3-a^3>0\),
\[
 M(z)=e^{Hz+Hz^2/4}P(z),\qquad
 P(z)=(1-dz)^4+\frac23 d_3z(1-dz).
\]
This is a formal extrapolation of the candidate low-support coefficients. The following is a test of that extrapolation at **all orders**, separate from any assertion about how many coefficients agree with the arithmetic model.

Let \(g\) be the normal density of mean \(H\), variance \(H/2\), and write \(P(z)=\sum_{k=0}^4 c_kz^k\). By repeated integration by parts, the finite signed density \(q(x)=\sum_{k=0}^4c_k(-1)^k g^{(k)}(x)\) has bilateral Laplace transform \(M\) for every complex \(z\). Equivalently it is a Gaussian times a finite combination of the corresponding Hermite polynomials. Its integral is one. The transform and positivity relation is the standard one in the Tokyo paper, with a signed density here before testing its sign.

There is an immediate obstruction for every nonempty band. \(P(1/d)=0\), whereas a finite real Laplace transform of a probability measure is strictly positive. Moreover \(P'(1/d)=-(2/3)d_3<0\), so \(M\) is strictly negative at some real \(z_*>1/d\). For an explicit choice take \(z_*=(1+\delta)/d\) with \(0<\delta\le\min(1/2,(d_3/(3d))^{1/3})\); expanding gives \(P(z_*)=\delta^4-(2/3)(d_3/d)\delta(1+\delta)<0\). This forces the signed density to change sign, independently of computing any individual Hermite polynomial's sign.

The same observation disproves realizability of the full formal **moment sequence**, without assuming an analytic mgf for a hypothetical probability. The polynomial
\[
 R_n(x)=\sum_{k=0}^n\frac{(z_*x/2)^k}{k!}
\]
satisfies \(R_n(x)^2\to e^{z_*x}\), bounded by \(e^{z_*|x|}\). That majorant is integrable against \(|q(x)|\,dx\). Thus \(\int R_n^2q\to M(z_*)<0\). For sufficiently large finite \(n\) the associated polynomial-square expectation is negative, which is impossible for a positive realization of all moments through \(2n\). This gives a finite Hankel-matrix obstruction for the extrapolation. It does not locate the witness within \(2nb<1/2\), nor does it invalidate or identify any moment inside an independently proved support window. No assumption that the eventual arithmetic limit is Gaussian or uniquely determined is used.

The named Marcinkiewicz theorem concerns an **exponential of a polynomial** (polynomial log characteristic function). The above transform is a Gaussian exponential **multiplied by** a polynomial. Many nonconstant factors do produce positive normal-polynomial distributions. The diagnostic here is positivity of the actual density, or the real Laplace-root and polynomial-square arguments, not a general claim that nonconstant Gaussian multipliers are impossible.
