## Two-term expansion and exchange

Write b_k=binom(m,k), with zero entries outside 0≤k≤m, and e_k for the elementary symmetric functions. For M=2 put a=2^(−m). The coefficient of QA at index n is (−1)^n(e_n−ae_1 e_{n−1}). Its constant coefficient is one. Expanding and using Vandermonde gives the stated energy and coalescence value. The diagonal a²-term has nonnegative coalescence deficit, since |e_k|≤b_k and |e_1|≤m. With D_k=b_k²−|e_k|² and cross deficit C_k=mb_{k−1}b_k−Re(e_1e_{k−1}conj(e_k)), this gives the lower bound D−2aC for the energy gap.

All subset choices involve distinct labels within each subset. The phase values attached to different labels may coincide. Let d be the mean of ρ(q_i conj(q_j)) over distinct ordered labels, with ρ(u)=1−Re(u). For independent uniform singleton I and (k−1)-subset S, the event I∈S has probability (k−1)/m. Conditional on I∉S, S∪{I} is uniform because the law is invariant under all label permutations and the action on k-subsets is transitive. Conditional on I∈S, replacing the extra copy of I by a label J uniformly sampled outside S gives U=S∪{J}, again uniform by the same transitivity. The pair (I,J) is a uniform ordered distinct label pair by simultaneous permutation symmetry. The independent k-subset T keeps its law under the conditioning and is independent of U. The factors q_I conj(q_J) and q_U conj(q_T) in the duplicated case may depend on each other. The pointwise inequality ρ(uv)≤2ρ(u)+2ρ(v) requires only their marginal means. These checks give equation (1), including k=1 where the duplicated branch has probability zero.

For equation (2), condition on a uniform (k−1)-subset H. Given H, let X and Y be independent products of H extended by a uniform label outside H. Then E[ρ(X conj(Y))|H]=1−|E[X|H]|²=Var(X|H). Within the conditional experiment with replacement, the same label is sampled twice with probability 1/(m−k+1), which contributes zero deficit. Conditional on sampling distinct labels, their unconditional pair law is uniform by simultaneous relabeling and the factor contributed by H cancels from the quotient. This is exactly the factor (m−k)/(m−k+1) multiplying d. Total complex variance is valid by the squared-modulus Pythagorean identity for conditional expectation. The unconditional extended set is uniform, so Var(X)=D_k/b_k². Applying the bound to m−k and using e_{m−k}=e_m conj(e_k) gives the stronger of the two constants in equation (2). The argument is valid for all 1≤k<m, with the trivial endpoint deficits D_0=D_m=0 excluded from division by the complement size.

## Summation and equality

The same complement identity gives C_{m+1−k}=C_k, since e_{m−k}conj(e_{m+1−k})=e_{k−1}conj(e_k). On the lower half of k, b_{k−1}/b_k≤1 and p_k≤1/2. Including the reflection weights bounded by two, the contribution from D_k in (1) is at most 3mD. The reflected products b_{k−1}b_k and Vandermonde bound the coefficient on d by m binom(2m,m−1)≤mB. Sum (2) after multiplication by b_k² to obtain d(B−2)≤(3/2)D for m≥6. Since B≥8, this gives Bd≤2D. Therefore C≤5mD, yielding a positive coefficient of D for m≥6 by 10m<2^m. This strict inequality starts at 6 and persists because doubling grows faster than multiplying by (m+1)/m.

For m=5, the lower-half rows k=1,2,3 have weights 2,2,1. The resulting D_k coefficients in (1) are respectively 2, 6, 7. Their d coefficients are 0, 200, 400. Here max(k,5−k)≥3 for every interior k and B−2=250, giving d≤(4/3)D/250. Thus C≤7D+600(4/3)D/250=(51/5)D<16D when D>0. For either range the gap vanishes only when D=0. As D_k≥0, this implies |e_1|=m, whose triangle equality forces all phases to coincide. Common rotation multiplies each nth coefficient by the nth power of the common rotation, preserving its modulus.

I checked the small-degree imports in the same taper convention. Degree one is direct, giving E=1/2 for M=2, with coalescence automatic. The two-root report, equations (10)–(11), uses M=2 within its all-M power-two result, giving a strict first relative-phase coefficient and maximum 5/2. The cubic report uses A=1+Sz/8 and gives a gap whose term 31(9−|S|²)/32 is strictly positive away from coalescence, plus the nonnegative cubic discriminant. This gives maximum 169/16. The independently reconstructed quartic gap uses A=1+Sz/16 and has a first positive coefficient (131−7|S|²)/128 multiplying 16−|S|² over its entire range. Its maximum is 363/8. All allow repeated labels. The three maxima match the uniform formula. The quartic also follows from the new subset bound alone. In m=4 the reflected bound is C≤2D_1+(20/3)D_2+96d, while d=D_1/12 exactly. With D=2D_1+D_2 this gives C≤(20/3)D<8D for D>0. ## Larger windows

For every fixed n, each term indexed by j in the nth coefficient of QA is the elementary subset sum at size n−j times the complete homogeneous sum at degree j, with phase-independent coefficient (−1)^{n−j}w_j. Normalizing by its count at coalescence indeed uses a product of a uniform subset and a uniform weak composition. Their product may give the same monomial in several ways, and the resulting sampling weights correctly preserve its algebraic multiplicity. Label-permutation invariance holds before and after ordering the n positions uniformly. Conditional on different labels appearing at a coordinate of the compared terms, the ordered pair is uniform over distinct pairs. If the event has zero probability, the coordinate deficit vanishes. Conditioning on different phase values would be invalid with repeats. The proof conditions on labels instead. Distinct labels with coincident phases merely yield zero for those pairs.

The inequality ρ(∏u_l)≤nΣρ(u_l) follows by expanding 1−∏u_l as a telescoping sum with unit-modulus multipliers, then squaring and using Cauchy–Schwarz. The normalized cross deficit bound n²d is valid even when there are repeated labels or dependence across positions. In the energy expansion an ordered pair contributes with sign (−1)^{j+k}. Summing over ordered pairs accounts for the customary factor two on each unordered off-diagonal pair. The diagonal j=k=0 over n≥1 contributes D. Other even-parity deficits are nonnegative by the triangle bound on normalized monomials. For odd parity use n≤L and Cauchy–Schwarz on the shifted binomial sequences to obtain the proposed bound L²dBc_jc_k per ordered pair. The total products of odd pairs are at most H²−1 since c_0=1 and all c_j≥0. Equation (2) gives d≤2D/(B−2), with B≥6 and dB≤4D. This gives the claimed strict sufficient inequality. For fixed M, the binomial coefficient has degree j in m, while each w_j for j≥1 is at most (1−1/M)^m. Thus L²(H²−1)→0, and the bound covers every fixed M at sufficiently large m.

## Curvature

For q_i=exp(itx_i) with Σx_i=0, the normalized phase factor in e_kh_j has the law of exp(it⟨x,1_S+α⟩) for independent uniform k-subset S and weak j-composition α. Each dot product has mean zero. One and two inclusion probabilities give the subset variance k(m−k)Σx_i²/[m(m−1)]. Marking one, or two different, composition coordinates in (1−z)^{−m} gives factorial moments 2j(j−1)/[m(m+1)] and j(j−1)/[m(m+1)], respectively. Combining these with Eα_i=j/m and Σ_{i≠j}x_i x_j=−Σx_i² gives the composition variance j(j+m)Σx_i²/[m(m+1)]. Independence removes the mixed term. Taylor expanding each normalized character gives the claimed v_{k,j}/[m(m²−1)] variance coefficient after factoring Σx_i². In the squared moduli the factor 1/2 cancels through the two equal real cross terms of order two, and invariance under conjugation makes the energy even in t. The residual constant does not contribute. This reproduces the exact numerator and the O(t⁴) remainder for fixed parameters and direction.
