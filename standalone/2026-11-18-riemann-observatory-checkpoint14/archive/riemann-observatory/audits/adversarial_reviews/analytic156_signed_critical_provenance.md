# Bounded primary-source comparison for the signed critical Mellin formula

18 November 2026. Scope is the symmetric inclusive sharp-height theorem for a continuous physical test with exponential value decay in log coordinates, finite Mellin-strip area norm with weight \(\sqrt{1+t^2}\), and local \(H^1\) at the origin. I compared the [director statement](director_signed_mellin_round16.md), [weighted152 construction](weighted152_signed_p1_campaign.md) and [uniform153 reconstruction](uniform153_contour_signed_critical_review.md) with the source passages below. This review addresses cited hypotheses and proof mechanisms. It supplies no field-priority conclusion.

## Fourier inversion is a direct Cesàro Tauberian argument

For \(g\in C_0(\mathbb R)\cap H^{1/2}(\mathbb R)\), compare the sharp inverse multiplier \({\bf1}_{[-T,T]}\) with the triangular multiplier \((1-|t|/T)_+\). The latter gives convolution with the nonnegative Fejér approximate identity and hence uniform convergence. Their difference equals \((|t|/T){\bf1}_{[-T,T]}\) off the endpoints. Its squared dual critical norm is at most
\[
\frac{2}{T^2}\int_0^T\frac{t^2}{\sqrt{1+t^2}}\,dt\le1.
\]
On a fixed bounded frequency interval the dual norm tends to zero. On its complement apply Cauchy–Schwarz with the small \(H^{1/2}\) tail. The argument is uniform over an equicontinuous bounded family with uniformly vanishing weighted Fourier tails, as furnished by the physical tilts and strip submean. It also holds at a single point of continuity of a bounded \(H^{1/2}\) representative. This is the continuous-frequency form of comparing a partial sum with its first Cesàro mean, for which the discrete difference coefficients are \(O(|n|/N)\) on \(|n|\le N\). The Gaussian version in the director note is equivalent in this respect. The source search did not establish an exact earlier citation for the stated \(C_0\cap H^{1/2}\) real-line lemma. Its short derivation uses the classical approximate identity and a standard Tauberian comparison.

One can separate this condition from sufficient conditions phrased in terms of a Dini modulus. Let \(k\) be positive even, integrable, and equal to \(1/(|t|\log^2|t|)\) for large \(|t|\), and let \(K\) be its inverse transform. Then \(K\in C_0\cap H^{1/2}\). At small \(u\), the portion of \(K(0)-K(u)\) with \(|t|\ge 1/|u|\) is bounded below by \(c/\log(1/|u|)\). Indeed the constant tail has that order and integration by parts bounds the cosine tail by \(O(1/\log^2(1/|u|))\); the remaining integrand \(k(t)(1-\cos(tu))\) is nonnegative. Thus the pointwise symmetric Dini integral diverges at zero and the uniform modulus is not \(o(1/\log(1/|u|))\). Localize smoothly about that point and translate the result away from physical zero to put it in the signed theorem's class including local \(H^1\) at the origin. This comparison does not test all classical Fourier convergence criteria.

## Exact arithmetic antecedents and margins

**Weil (1952).** The reprint's printed pp. 4–5, (7)–(10), already give the folded, truncated contour identity under an exponential \(L^1\) moment and \(\Phi(\sigma+it)=o((\log|t|)^{-2})\) uniformly on a narrower strip. Weil moves from good heights to arbitrary cutoffs using \(O(\log|t|)\) zeros in a bounded interval and the same transform estimate. He also moves the gamma terms to the central line, puts the arithmetic factor on the outer line, and writes it as a truncated Fourier integral of prime translates. The initial hypotheses and this truncation step are met by the critical strip theorem's trace bound \(O(|t|^{-1/2})\). This is an exact predecessor for the contour and endpoint mechanism. The later evaluation of both limits on pp. 6–8 supplements it with conditions (A) and (B), including piecewise \(C^1\) with finite first-kind discontinuities and exponential bounds for both the test and its derivative. The new hypotheses ensure uniform sharp prime inversion and convergence of the gamma pairing by separate estimates. Those hypotheses need not ensure Weil's stronger final derivative conditions. On pp. 7–8 Weil already isolates the \(\log |t|\) gamma multiplier, subtracts a model at the origin, and uses an integration by parts and a sine-integral limit. Thus compensation at the origin and the logarithmic integration by parts have clear antecedents as well. [Pinned Weil reprint, pp. 4–8](../../sources/prime146_mellin_provenance/weil1952_reprint.pdf)

**Guinand.** Guinand's *Concordance and the harmonic analysis of sequences* (Acta Math. 101, 1959), §10(2), p. 263, (10.5), gives a prime/zero summation formula with Fourier cosine transforms as in (10.1), under RH in the cited display, and with a smooth logarithmic-density subtraction on the zero side. This is a genuine predecessor for pairing the zero sum with a smooth log-density term. It does not state the unconditional unsmoothed critical-strip criterion. The passage references his 1948 paper directly. Publisher retrieval did not yield a readable copy of the 1948 article, so the comparison here does not assign its exact test class or infer that each of its formulations needs RH. **Barner (1981) and Büthe (v4).** Barner's Theorem 1, p. 141, requires normalized midpoint values, exponentially weighted bounded variation, and a symmetric local condition on the place-twisted test. The pinned primary page is OCR with damaged formula typography. Büthe's primary §2, p. 3 makes an explicit Barner class with \(g(t)e^{c|t|}\in BV\cap L^1\) for \(c>\sigma_1-\sigma_0/2\), midpoint values, and \(g(t)+g(-t)-2g(0)=O(|t|^\eta)\) for some \(\eta>0\). Its spectral functional uses the ordinary symmetric height limit with multiplicities and signed poles. Consequently an ordinary sharp-height conditional formula and local symmetry compensation are classical. The compact continuous critical example below falls outside even unweighted \(BV\), so neither weighted-variation hypothesis is automatic. Conversely a normalized compact step away from zero meets these kinds of variation and local conditions while its genuine jump rules out \(H^{1/2}\). **Burnol (1998, v2).** *The explicit formula in simple terms*, pp. 7–8, requires bounded total variation of \(g(u)(u^{-\epsilon}+u^{1+\epsilon})\) and midpoint normalization. From the variation it obtains the uniform \(O(1/|t|)\) estimate and uses Weil's Fourier inversion of the summed prime translates. Its finite archimedean-integral theorem on p. 11 imposes a continuous locally BV derivative and a joint decay bound on \(g\) and \(ug'\). The local-variation premise in the classical approach does not follow from critical Sobolev regularity, as the example below also shows after change of variables. **Avdispahić–Smajlović (2003).** *\(\phi\)-Variation and Barner–Weil Formula*, Mathematica Balkanica 17, pp. 267–289, uses another genuine antecedent of uniform sharp inversion and local gamma compensation. I checked the actual scan, pp. 271–274, 280, 283–285 and 287. Theorem 3.1 on p. 272 gives sharp inverse truncation convergence uniformly on compact sets of continuity for \(HBV(\mathbb R)\cap L^1(\mathbb R)\), using Waterman's Fourier-series theorem. The \(\phi\) condition at p. 273 is \(g\in\phi BV(\mathbb R)\cap L^1(\mathbb R)\) with \(\sum_n\phi^{-1}(1/n)n^{-1/p}<\infty\) for some \(p>1\). The separate \(\alpha\) condition is reciprocal-logarithmic growth at zero. Theorem 5.1 controls the sharp truncation modulus at zero under both conditions. The generalized Parseval theorem 6.1 requires the \(\phi\) condition on \(g^{(M)}\), harmonic bounded variation and \(L^1\) for lower derivatives, and the \(\alpha\) condition for \(g^{(M)}-g^{(M)}(0)\) with \(\alpha>M+2\). Its conclusion is an ordinary symmetric truncated integral against the transform of a special-pair distribution. The paper explicitly uses that the \(\phi\) assumption implies harmonic bounded variation. The capacitary example below separates even that implied condition from the director's hypotheses. Hence this generalization cannot simply be invoked for every critical test. I did not rely on an identification of its special-pair theorem with an unconditional sharp zeta zero sum. **Modern strip-decay formulations.** Carneiro–Milinovich, *On Littlewood's estimate for the modulus of the zeta function on the critical line*, §2.2 Lemma 4, requires analyticity in the strip \(|\Im z|\le1/2+\epsilon\) and uniform \(O((1+|z|)^{-1-\delta})\) decay, \(\delta>0\). Bondarenko–Radchenko–Seip, p. 2, use the same kind of decay in (1.1). Such decay gives absolute spectral convergence from the unit count. The critical theorem includes a smooth absolute-divergence witness, so these decay statements cannot cover its entire class. The latter paper's distinct interpolation theorem 1.1 uses a strip-supremum weighted \(L^1((1+|x|)dx)\) hypothesis and an even test. It is a different assertion. ## A compact critical test outside harmonic bounded variation

Choose disjoint intervals \(J_j\subset(2,3)\) with the unique accumulation point of their closures at \(2\). If \(R>2\), take \(P_R\) to be the inverse transform of a positive even density proportional to \({\bf1}_{1<|t|<R}/|t|\), normalized so \(P_R(0)=1\). Then \(|P_R|\le1\), \(P_R\) is smooth, and \(\|P_R\|_{H^{1/2}}^2=O(1/\log R)\). For each \(j\), multiply the translate of \(P_R\) at the midpoint of \(J_j\) by a smooth bump in \(J_j\) between 0 and 1 and equal to 1 at the midpoint. For each fixed bump the multiplication operator is bounded on \(H^{1/2}\) by the weighted Fourier convolution estimate. Choose \(R_j\) large enough that the resulting \(P_j\) has norm at most \(2^{-j}\). Thus with \(a_j=1/\log(j+2)\), \(F=\sum_j a_jP_j\) is continuous and compactly supported and belongs to \(H^{1/2}\). It vanishes near zero. One interval joining a zero value to the midpoint in each \(J_j\) gives a harmonic variation subtotal \(\sum_{j\le n}a_j/j\to\infty\). Every fixed exponential tilt on this common compact support can be implemented by smooth multiplication bounded on \(H^{1/2}\), uniformly for the bounded tilt parameter. Hence \(f(x)=x^{-1/2}F(\log x)\) satisfies the critical strip theorem's area norm and value conditions. If a weighted \(BV\) condition held for it, dividing out the smooth positive weight on the compact support would give ordinary \(BV\), contradicting the same variation test.

The search covered combinations of uniform Fourier convergence, critical \(H^{1/2}\), Dirichlet/Fejér, Hardy–Littlewood Tauberian criteria, Guinand's summation formula, and modern Guinand–Weil strip hypotheses. The audited comparisons identify standard Fourier and contour mechanisms and distinguish the stated critical test class from the evaluated source theorems. The 1948 Guinand text and any exact earlier formulation of the same combined class remain unverified.
