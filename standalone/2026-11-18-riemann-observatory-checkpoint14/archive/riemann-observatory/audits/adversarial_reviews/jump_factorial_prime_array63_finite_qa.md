17 November 2026. I checked the target and prime coefficients, the streamed cell calculation, the infinite-tail inequality, and the exact endpoint representation. ## Streamed cells and the full norm

For the anchored (k=2) array, (v_N(n)=sum_d a_d(lfloor n/dfloor-n/d)). On each cell ([n,n+1)), this is the value of the step function formed from the integer floor generators. Their Mellin transforms are (zeta(s)(d^{-s}-1/d)/s), by termwise integration on (Re s>1) and analytic continuation for the bounded differences. Mellin–Plancherel converts the full critical-line squared error to (sum_{nge1}(R_n-v_N(n))^2/[n(n+1)]), with boundary measure (dt/(2pi)) and physical measure (dx/x^2). The weight (1/[n(n+1)]) is the exact integral over a unit cell.

The jump at (n) is (sum_{dmid n}a_d-A_N(1)). Each block contains all (n) from its inclusive start to its exclusive stop. Its divisor offsets ((-mathrm{start})mod d) mark exactly the multiples of (d) in that block. The running approximation is carried across blocks, and the anchor is subtracted on every cell. Likewise the running target starts at (R_0=0), adds (log n) for even (n), and subtracts it for odd (n). This gives (R_n=log(4^q(q!)^2/n!)) with (q=lfloor n/2floor). The coefficient sieve implements (2mathbf1_{2mid d}Lambda(d/2)-Lambda(d)+2log 2,mathbf1_{d=2}) before tapering. The special (d=2) term survives the sieve because 2 is prime. The coefficient at (d=N) vanishes exactly, including when (N=2).

Independently, I recovered (Lambda) by trial division for all 140 coefficient positions at (N=10,30,100), including the zero endpoints. I checked 90 further block-local cells using the residue formula (-sum_d a_d(nmod d)/d) and a separate target evaluation. The small cells used an exact central binomial coefficient. For large cells the gamma duplication formula gives (R_{2q}=	frac12logpi+logGamma(q+1)-logGamma(q+	frac12)), with the next odd cell subtracting (log(2q+1)). These checks included cases across the original block locations and immediately beyond the physical cutoff. I also compared three whole small finite sums with direct evaluation, at ((N,X)=(2,1),(11,32),(20,17)). They agreed as intervals, with exact zero in the first case.

## Infinite tail and directed endpoints

For the even cells, (R_{2m}) is a sum of nonnegative terms (log(2j/(2j-1))) and (R_{2m}lelog(2m)) by induction, starting from (R_2=log2). Odd cells follow by subtracting (log(2m+1)), giving (|R_n|lelog n) for all (nge1). The certificate uses the weaker bound (2log n). The fractional-part factors in (v_N(n)) have magnitude at most 1, so (|v_N(n)|le S_N=sum_d|a_d|).

The run sums exactly cells 1 through (X). The omitted cells start at (Y=X+1), and their squared absolute upper envelope ((2log x+S_N)^2) is increasing on (xge1). Integrating this envelope from (Y) with measure (dx/x^2) dominates the entire omitted sum, using the exact cell weights. With (u=2log Y+S_N), the substitution (x=Ye^z) gives ((u^2+4u+8)/Y). The result therefore takes the outward lower endpoint of the finite sum and the outward upper endpoint of the finite sum plus that tail majorant. The scaled error uses the same operations after multiplication by the enclosed positive (log N). Ball arithmetic accounts for both coefficient and summation errors.

I decoded the exact mantissa-times-power-of-two endpoints as rational numbers. The two original results contain 228 interval pairs, all with correctly directed 18-place decimal endpoints, including negative endpoint controls. All 192 original saved direct-cell difference intervals enclose zero. For the three common cases the 224-bit finite-sum intervals are contained in their 160-bit counterparts. Anchor, coefficient sum, tail and full-error enclosures overlap across both original runs. The raw full-error intervals are disjoint and decreasing, their widths are each below 0.18% of the corresponding lower bound, and the scaled (N=30) interval lies strictly above the scaled (N=100) interval.

## Replays and calculator hardening

The original result pins use Python 3.12.14, python-flint 0.9.0, FLINT 3.6.0, and one Arb thread. The main run uses 160 bits and block size 32,768, and the second uses 224 bits and block size 8,191. I checked the recorded source and result SHA-256 values. At the first configuration, my (N=10) replay finished in 7.18 seconds and reproduced all metadata and the full archived case exactly, including the 16 direct-cell check records and their dyadic bounds.

I found a separate low-precision failure in the original CLI. General Arb exponentiation can return an indeterminate ball for an inexact base centered at zero. The original program demonstrated this with `--case 5:36 --precision 2 --block-cells 7`. Its finite-endpoint check stopped the run before it could publish a result. The byte-preserved original source retains its matching pins. Version 2 uses explicit ball multiplication for the squares and reports nonfinite endpoints with case context. The two original result files retain their original source hashes. The full (N=10) version 2 replay took 6.80 seconds, uses the same 160-bit parameters, overlaps the original dyadic bounds and gives the same 18-place endpoints. Its separate result pins the version 2 source. The (N=30) and (N=100) certificate evidence remains the two original executions.
