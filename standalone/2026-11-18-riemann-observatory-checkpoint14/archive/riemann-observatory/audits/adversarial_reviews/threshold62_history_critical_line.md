# Primary-source checkpoint on critical-line zero counts

17 November 2026. Existing corpus identity PAPER-S007 is retained for Hardy. New selected records below distinguish papers from announcements and record actual access and reading. No current-record assertion.

## Hardy, 1914

I reread all three original French pages of *Sur les zéros de la fonction ζ(s) de Riemann*, Comptes rendus 158, pp. 1012–1014, séance du 6 avril 1914, transmitted by Hadamard. The footnote on p. 1012 records communication to the London Mathematical Society on 12 March. The source conclusion is infinitude of critical-line zeros, hence distinct zeros at arbitrarily large heights. Its eventual-sign contradiction in fact forces infinitely many sign changes of the real completed function. It does not identify these as simple zeros or bound a proportion. [Existing PAPER-S007](../../papers/PAPER-S007.md) [Original leaves](https://archive.org/details/comptesrendusheb158acad/page/n1019/mode/2up)

Hardy begins with inverse Mellin for exp(−y), sums at n²y, and shifts the contour across the ζ(2u) pole to Re u=1/4 in equation (1). Substituting y=πexp(iα), he differentiates the resulting theta identity 2p times. As α tends to π/2 along the indicated path, the theta contribution and its needed derivatives vanish by his argument citing Bohr and Marcel Riesz. Under an eventual-sign hypothesis, monotone convergence on the tail gives moments proportional to (−1)^p16^(−p). The moment on [2T,2T+1] grows like (2T)^(2p) and contradicts the upper bound K T^(2p) along the parity forcing the wrong sign. The historical summability references were outside this rereading. ## Selberg, 1942

The official NTNU/DKNVS retrospective reproduces the first page of Selberg's *On the zeros of the Zeta-function of Riemann*, Forhandlinger XV no. 16, pp. 59–62, submitted 23 May 1942 through Brun. I visually checked the original page, reproduced as PDF leaf 1. Theorem 1 and (2) state existence of A>0,T₀ with N₀(T)>AT log T for T>T₀, where N₀ is the critical-line zero count at 0<t<T. The source display gives no numerical A and imposes no simplicity condition. It sets up a real X(t) from the completed zeta function and a Dirichlet polynomial η(t) with coefficients of ζ^(−1/2), cutoff T^(1/100), and linear logarithmic taper. The remainder of this official file is the accompanying retrospective, which quotes Selberg's account of the nonnegative weight |η(t)|² and its use to regularize the size of the real function for sign detection. The archived facsimile establishes the original theorem and setup. It does not establish a whole reading of the four-page announcement or of the distinct 59-page Oslo memoir *On the zeros of Riemann's zeta-function*, Skrifter Oslo I no. 10 (1942), pp. 1–59. A full original of that memoir was not located at the checkpoint. ## Levinson, 1974

I read all three pages of the English PNAS announcement *At Least One-Third of Zeros of Riemann's Zeta-Function are on σ = 1/2*, 71(4), 1013–1015, April 1974. It is distinct from the 54-page *More than One Third of Zeros of Riemann's Zeta-Function are on σ = 1/2*, Advances in Mathematics 13, 383–436. That longer original was acquired from NTNU and read only at the opening theorem, multiplicity corollary, and main-proof setup, pp. 383–386. The announcement gives a critical-line count exceeding one third in the late interval T<t<T+U with U=T/log^10 T, and reports a separate byproduct about the proportion of zeros that can be multiple critical-line zeros. The primary theorem is a critical-line count. The later simple-critical interpretation is separately attributed to Heath-Brown and Selberg in Conrey's introduction, and is not the PNAS headline theorem. The detailed paper's theorem uses L=log(T/(2π)) and U=T/L^10, with a multiplicity-excess corollary. [PNAS primary PDF](../../sources/threshold62_history/levinson_cdn.pdf) [Advances primary PDF](../../sources/threshold62_history/levinson_long.pdf)

The method differentiates the functional equation, then counts phase crossings of hG on the line for G=ζ+ζ′/[f′(s)+f′(1−s)] with f=log h. The argument principle bounds loss of phase by zeros of G in a rectangle. An additional mollifier ψ has cutoff y=T^(1/2)/log^40 T in the PNAS source. Littlewood's lemma and the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality reduce the zero loss to the mollified mean square left of the line. In the announcement the evaluation combines several pieces from the Riemann–Siegel decomposition, using stationary phase for the mixed terms and cancellation of asymmetric terms. Its quantitative end uses R=1.3 and obtains a lower bound about 0.34 for the interval proportion. A full proof audit of those displayed asymptotics was outside scope.

## Conrey, 1989

I read the published English Crelle original *More than two fifths of the zeros of the Riemann zeta function are on the critical line*, vol. 399, 1–26, 1989, from the Durham University copy, all eight sections and references. The extraction drops some displays, so I verified key statements and the added note visually. This is substantive architecture-level reading, with scope-limited mathematical verification. Section 2, (8)–(12), defines κ as liminf N₀(T)/N(T), with N₀ the critical-line zero count and N(T) the total nontrivial zero count with multiplicity, and κ* as liminf N₀*(T)/N(T), with N₀* requiring ζ′(ρ)≠0 on the critical line. Neither denominator is a count of distinct zeros. Theorem 1, p. 4, gives κ≥0.4077 and κ*≥0.401. The p. 25 note added in proof raises the all-critical κ bound to 0.4088. Its improved polynomial concerns κ and does not replace the distinct simple-critical statement. The proof is unconditional. [Published primary PDF](../../sources/threshold62_history/conrey.pdf) [Publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1515/crll.1989.399.1)

Sections 3–4 start with a fixed linear combination of ξ and its derivatives whose real part on the line is a nonzero multiple of ξ. Changing argument gives critical-line crossings. The proof of the simple count uses a degree-one auxiliary polynomial so that η≠0 at a crossing certifies ξ′≠0. A mollifier with coefficients μ(n)P(log(y/n)/log y), P(0)=0, P(1)=1, and y=T^θ with fixed θ<4/7 is used. Theorem 2 evaluates the mollified mean square, and §4 optimizes the resulting functional, including an Euler–Lagrange problem for P. Sections 5–7 use a Gaussian-smoothed shifted moment and a two-variable analytic differentiation argument, then obtain the main term by the Estermann functional equation and contour residues. Section 8 bounds off-diagonal oscillations through dyadic decomposition, Vaughan's identity, Deshouillers–Iwaniec averages of incomplete Kloosterman sums, and an auxiliary Weil-bound case. The end of §8 exhibits errors with factors T^(−1)y^(7/4) and T^(−1/2)y^(7/8), which are small for fixed θ<4/7 and explain the range pressure. This is the concrete analytic barrier, separate from optimizing P or the ξ-derivative combination.

The transition is from Hardy's unlocalized transform moments to Selberg's mollified sign detection, then to Levinson and Conrey's argument-principle and mollified mean-square approach. A modern example targeting the same off-diagonal issue is Pratt–Robles–Zaharescu–Zeindler's development of long-mollifier means and Kloosterman-sum tools for combined mollifier pieces. This comparison is a targeted source example and does not rank the literature. [PRZZ institutional manuscript](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/139205/1/PRZZ_RMS_Post_Referee_swap_ab.pdf)
