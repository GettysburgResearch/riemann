# Independent audit of the finite Frobenius trace criterion

17 November 2026. The finite-spectrum equivalences hold with multiset invariance, including multiplicities. The prime-degree example survives an integral alternating similitude and all-degree positive formal closed-point counts. The rank-size Toeplitz certificate is sharp for the stated class over the reals. The observations below concern finite spectra and carry no publication priority claim. ## Reciprocity, trace positivity and the density argument

For the normalized set the two-term calculation gives $d(a^2+b^2)+ab(s_n+s_{-n})$. Invariance as a multiset under inversion makes $s_{-n}=s_n$. Invariance under conjugation makes this common trace real. These are separate conditions. For instance $\{2i,-i/2\}$ is reciprocal but gives $s_1=3i/2$. Multiplicity also matters. The underlying set of $\{2,1/2,1/2\}$ is reciprocal, while $s_1=3$ and $s_{-1}=9/2$. In the operator setting, an invertible matrix $F$ with $F^{\mathsf T}JF=qJ$ for nonsingular real $J$ is similar to $qF^{-\mathsf T}$, so reciprocity includes algebraic multiplicities. A real matrix also has conjugate-stable spectrum. The evaluation ideal is preserved by inversion under the stated assumptions, and the trace annihilates it. On the circle one has $p(z)p(z^{-1})=|p(z)|^2$ for real coefficients. The calculation on $a+bX^n$ then gives precisely $|s_n|\le d$, with no assertion about Jordan blocks.

Here is an independent quantitative substitute for the bounded-gaps proof. Write the $m$ maximal-modulus phases as $e^{2\pi i\theta_j}$, with multiplicity, and place the $N+1$ vectors $(k\theta_1,\ldots,k\theta_m)\bmod1$, $0\le k\le N$, into $6^m$ equal half-open boxes. One box contains at least $(N+1)/6^m$ vectors. Differences from its earliest time supply that many minus one distinct positive integers $n\le N$, all with phase real parts greater than $1/2$. Thus the good return times have lower density at least $6^{-m}$. Along these times $s_n\ge R^n(m/2-o(1))$ since the maximum modulus $R$ dominates the remaining finitely many terms. In particular the proof works for any $S$ of upper density greater than $1-6^{-m}$, using a sequence of endpoints where the upper density is approached. The top root multiplicities must be positive for this argument. For comparison of two spectra with signed grouped coefficients, the reviewed note correctly changes to a mean-square argument. At maximal modulus the normalized sequence is $u_n+o(1)$, where $u_n=\sum c_j e^{in\theta_j}$ with distinct phases and nonzero grouped coefficients. The mean square of $u_n$ tends to $\sum|c_j|^2>0$ by geometric sums. Boundedness lets one remove a density-zero set, contradicting equality on the remaining powers.

The sign bound alone gives containment in the closed disk. Inversion with multiplicities then excludes interior roots. It also follows from the same recurrence proof that along a set of density one the positive-part $n$th-root limit superior equals $R$ even when $R\le1$. An arbitrary infinite set has no such consequence. The explicit example below passes the one-sided upper bound on a set of density $7/8$ and the absolute-value bound on a set of density $3/4$.

## An integral alternating similitude with positive formal counts

For $q=4$, let $A$ send the successive standard basis vectors to $2e_2,2e_3,2e_4,-4e_1$. It has characteristic polynomial $X^4+32$. Set
$$
 D=4A^{-\mathsf T},\qquad F=\operatorname{diag}(A,D),\qquad
 J=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_4\\-I_4&0\end{pmatrix}.
$$
Since the nonzero weights of $A$ are $2,2,2,-4$, their reciprocals times four are integers. Therefore $D$ and $F$ are integral, and direct multiplication gives $F^{\mathsf T}JF=4J$. The spectrum of $D$ is obtained from that of $A$ by $\alpha\mapsto4/\alpha$, so its polynomial is $X^4+8$. This gives the reviewed polynomial together with an explicit integral alternating duality. It also works as an unramified representation for any odd $\ell$, because $F$ is invertible over $\mathbb Z_\ell$ and the powers have compact closure there. The reviewed obstruction already exists at the level of one integral operator with all its iterated traces.

The unnormalized trace is zero for $4\nmid n$, and is $4((-32)^k+(-8)^k)$ for $n=4k$. After dividing by $2^n$ this gives $s_4=-10$ and $s_8=17$. Every prime **extension degree** passes because no prime is a multiple of four. Composite prime-power degrees such as $4$ fail. The absolute bound passes exactly when $4\nmid n$. The upper bound $s_n\le e^{\epsilon n}$ for every positive $\epsilon$ passes along $8\nmid n$, with exponential violations at multiples of eight. In particular a Frobenius-degree sample can be extensive while missing the positive returns.

This same example has positive formal closed-point exponents at **every** degree. Its rational series is
$$
 Z(t)=\frac{1+40t^4+256t^8}{(1-t)(1-4t)},\qquad
 N_n=1+4^n-\operatorname{Tr}(F^n).
$$
The coefficients of $Z$ are integral and its constant term is one. Successively canceling its lowest-degree nonzero coefficient using an integral power of $1-t^n$ proves integral Euler exponents $b_n$. Taking logarithmic derivatives gives $nb_n=\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(n/d)N_d$. For all $d$ one has $N_d\le1+2\cdot4^d$, using zero trace when $4\nmid d$ and the bound $|\operatorname{Tr}(F^d)|\le8\cdot 32^{d/4}\le4^d$ for $d\ge4$. For $n\ge8$ the stronger lower bound $N_n\ge(7/8)4^n$ follows from $8\cdot32^{n/4}/4^n\le1/8$. Every proper divisor is at most $m=\lfloor n/2\rfloor$, and
$$
 \sum_{d=1}^m N_d\le m+\frac83(4^m-1)\le 3\cdot4^m.
$$
Hence $nb_n\ge N_n-\sum_{d\mid n,d<n}N_d>0$ for $n\ge8$. The initial $N_n$ for $1\le n\le7$ are $5,17,65,417,1025,4097,16385$. They give $b_n=5,6,20,100,204,670,2340$. The abstract example thus combines positive formal counts, alternating duality and prime-degree two-term positivity. The earlier $q=5$ model already establishes the obstruction using positive Euler exponents and duality. The extra feature here is prime-degree agreement with the two-term bound. ## Sharp size and the scope of finite searches

The interpolation witness of degree at most $r-1$ follows by prescribing real values $+1$ on the conjugacy class of an off-circle root and $-1$ on its inverse class, with zero elsewhere. Conjugate class and inverse class are disjoint whenever the modulus is different from one. The negative value of $\tau(pp^*)$ is the sum of the multiplicities on those classes with a minus sign. This proves sufficiency of a positive semidefinite Toeplitz matrix of size $r$ using moments through $s_{r-1}$.

In this class no smaller universal size works. For any $r\ge2$ start from $1$ with multiplicity two and $r-2$ other distinct unit roots different from $1$, stable under conjugation. Use pairs off the real line and include $-1$ if $r-2$ is odd. There are $r-1$ distinct evaluation points. The Toeplitz form on degree at most $r-2$ is strictly positive by the vanishing bound for polynomials, since a nonzero polynomial of degree at most $r-2$ cannot vanish at all $r-1$ points. Replacing the two copies of $1$ by $R,R^{-1}$ with $R>1$ sufficiently close to one preserves positive definiteness by continuity. This gives exactly $r$ distinct roots with two off-circle roots and a positive definite Toeplitz matrix of size $r-1$.

The reviewed finite-list two-term obstruction deliberately uses real coefficients. If the ambient class is restricted to monic **integral** polynomials of a fixed degree $d$ with a fixed normalization $\rho$, a finite cutoff does exist. The first $d$ tests bound each unnormalized trace by $d\rho^n$, $1\le n\le d$. Newton identities bound each integral coefficient in terms of these finitely many bounded traces. There are finitely many candidate integral polynomials. Each nonpure reciprocal candidate has a violating positive index by the all-index implication, so the maximum of their first failures supplies a finite cutoff after the first $d$ tests. This compactness argument gives no practical cutoff and does not justify using prime indices alone.

## Direct check of Weil’s two-term specialization

On p. 346 Weil’s quotient removes principal correspondences and the horizontal and vertical fiber classes. He gives $\sigma(\gamma')=\sigma(\gamma)$ there. The generic complementary correspondence yields $2m=\sigma(\gamma\gamma')\ge0$. For Frobenius $\iota$ the same page has $\iota\iota'=q$ and $\#X(\mathbb F_{q^n})=1+q^n-\sigma(\iota^n)$. The top of p. 347 identifies these traces with the independent power sums of the same reciprocal roots. Specializing his displayed quadratic form to exponents $0,n$ gives $2g(a^2+q^n b^2)+2ab\sigma(\iota^n)$. The rational-coefficient and real-coefficient extensions follow by homogeneity and continuity, as Weil states immediately below the display. The discriminant is nonpositive exactly when $|\sigma(\iota^n)|\le2gq^{n/2}$. Positivity of the geometric form supplies this inequality. The integral similitude in the countermodel supplies the adjoint identity but does not supply that geometric sign. ## Katz–Messing and what is inherited

I independently checked the five original printed pages 73–77. Section I p. 73 distinguishes D1, the integral pure degree factors, from D2, reconstruction of $P^{n-1}$ through divisibility across smooth sections in a Lefschetz pencil. Here $g^{(r)}$ means raising each reciprocal root to the $r$th power. The proof on pp. 74–75 allows finite base-field extension to obtain the pencil and a smooth section. Descent uses the powers to obtain radii and integrality first. Once the radii are distinct, the alternating zeta function recovers each exact unpowered degree factor. The proof uses weak Lefschetz in both theories and the printed Poincaré pairing with Frobenius acting by $q^n$ on the top degree. Induction first gives equality outside degrees $n-1,n,n+1$. D2 yields divisibility at $n-1$, duality at $n+1$, and the alternating identity then gives divisibility at $n$ and the exact equation $R^{n-1}R^{n+1}=R^n$. The three disjoint Deligne radii force all three residual polynomials to be $1$. The source does not assume already matched per-degree traces in the new theory.

For § III Theorem 2(1), the printed extra premise is a Weil cohomology in the cited sense or the specified crystalline theory after tensoring with the Witt-vector fraction field of the algebraic closure. The construction uses smooth Frobenius graphs. Its rational Chinese-remainder polynomial is $1$ modulo the target degree characteristic polynomial and $0$ modulo all others. Cayley–Hamilton supplies the projectors even if a degree has Jordan blocks. Thus the implication here is disjoint weights to algebraic projectors, consistent with the earlier corpus notes. The finite trace reduction supplies neither Deligne's input nor D2.
