# Whole-work advocate review of PAPER-E014

Read all seven article pages of Luis Báez-Duarte, *A strengthening of the Nyman–Beurling criterion for the Riemann hypothesis*, Rendiconti Lincei, series 9, 14(1), 2003, pp. 5–11. Read all seven pages of arXiv:math/0202141v2 and compared the complete v1 extraction. The later preprint math/0205003 is PAPER-E001, a separately cataloged sequel containing substantially the journal’s quantitative section. The source receipts and reading checklist are in [analytic74 source receipt](../../sources/analytic74_baez/receipt.md). This is the advocate review. An independent skeptical review and root adjudication remain separate.

**Disposition.** Theorem 1 and its corollary are sound after local repairs. The journal’s RH-conditional Theorem 2 is supported with an explicit constant-tracking bridge for its shrinking-gap Möbius estimate. The journal states that bridge in §3, but the cited fixed-gap Lemma 1 alone does not supply it. The limiting transform is bounded and convergent independently of RH. Identifying it as the transform of the actual pointwise Möbius-damped function uses RH. Reading the whole paper does not change the conditional status or establish a priority claim.

## Introduction and exact spaces

Write χ=1_(0,1], H=L²((0,∞),dx), ρ_a(x)={1/(ax)}. The spaces B and B_nat are **finite** complex linear spans with a≥1 real and a≥1 integral, respectively. The criterion uses their closures in H. The real span gives the same criterion by taking real parts. Each basis function is bounded by 1 on (0,1] and by 1/(ax) for x>1, and the single endpoint 1 is null for H. The original interval-space formulation cited in the introduction has a different ambient space and the classical constraint, so direct identification of those bare spans would be incorrect.

One direct check of the claimed strict difference of closures uses the restriction to (1/2,∞). All integral dilations except a=1 restrict there to scalar multiples of 1/x. The restriction of a=1 adds only the step supported on (1/2,1]. This is a closed two-dimensional space. The restriction for a=3/2 has an additional breakpoint at 2/3 and is outside that space. Thus even the H-closure inclusion is strict, including under RH.

The old raw Möbius sum F_n is distinguished from its damped variants. Its local a.e. and L¹ behavior and H divergence are cited historical results, while the lower bound (1.2) is a restriction on finite-cutoff approximants with max a=N, asymptotically for large N. Neither the proof of raw divergence nor an improved distance lower bound is needed to establish the new criterion.

For ε>0 the defining damped series f_ε converges absolutely pointwise since |ρ_a(x)|≤1/(ax). Expanding the floor gives (2.3) and (2.4), as the truncated reciprocal-zeta sum at 1+ε is absolutely convergent. Under v2’s broader complex notation the original series is defined by absolute convergence for Re ε>0, with the displayed right side giving its meromorphic continuation outside this region. At x>1 the floor vanishes. As ε↓0 the reciprocal ζ(1+ε) tends to zero and the finite floor sum tends to Σ_{a≤1/x} μ(a) floor(1/(ax)). Counting products gives Σ_{m≤1/x}Σ_{a|m}μ(a)=χ(x), including x=1. These are pointwise statements. The proof separately establishes both norm limits.

## Lemmas and qualitative criterion

Balazard–Saias §3, Lemme 2, as reproduced in journal Lemma 1 and preprint Lemma 2.1, gives the error O_(α,δ,η)(n^(−δ/3)(1+|t|)^η), for n≥2, 1/2≤α<1, δ,η>0, α+δ≤Re s≤1 and ζ zero-free in Re s>α. At s=1, 1/ζ means its analytic extension. Under RH the estimate itself gives fixed positive-gap convergence to 1/ζ, so the parallel Littlewood citation is dispensable for this invocation. The gap remains fixed throughout this first use.

For ζ(s_−)/ζ(s_+), s_±=1/2±ε+it, conjugation and the functional equation give the exact modulus
`π^(−ε)|Γ(1/4+ε/2+it/2)/Γ(1/4−ε/2+it/2)|`.
This holds after the removable extension in t if the denominator ζ is zero. The same relation as a complex product has a conjugation phase, which the modulus alone removes. Stirling on a fixed strip and boundedness on the compact part give C_(ε0)(1+|t|)^ε for 0≤ε≤ε0<1/2. The v2 denominator appears with a plus sign in both gamma arguments. The published proof has the correct minus sign. At ε=0 the zeta ratio extends to 1.

The actual Mellin convention in (2.6) is ℳf(t)=∫₀∞x^(−1/2+it) f(x) dx. Put x=e^u to obtain the ordinary Fourier integral of e^(u/2)f(e^u). Plancherel gives norm dt/(2π) on the transform line. Both versions print (2π)^(−1/2)dt, which differs by a harmless fixed factor for convergence but fails the printed isometry and conflicts with the correct journal p. 10 norm identity. The integral is initially on absolutely convergent inputs and extends in L². In the strip 0<Re s<1 the Mellin integral of ρ_a is −a^(−s)ζ(s)/s. For a=1 substitute y=1/x and use the standard floor integral for ζ, separating at y=1 and continuing from Re s>1. The elementary integral for χ gives 1/s. The weight X_ε=x^(−ε) shifts s to s−ε. In f_(2ε,n) the factor a^(−2ε) therefore sends the Dirichlet argument to s+ε, giving (2.7).

For fixed 0<ε<1/4 assume RH. Apply Lemma 1 with α=1/2 and δ=η=ε. In (2.7), the main quotient is O_ε((1+|t|)^(−1+ε)). For the error, the functional equation and Lindelöf on the reflected right-hand abscissa give |ζ(1/2−ε+it)|≪_(ε,η)(1+|t|)^(ε+η) for every η>0. This gives an L² majorant O_ε((1+|t|)^(−1+3ε)) if both subpower exponents are chosen as ε. To obtain the paper’s O((1+|t|)^(−1+2ε)) form, choose instead the two subpower exponents ε/2. Constants can depend on ε. The resulting square is integrable for ε<1/4. The journal’s printed range ε0<1/2 for this displayed majorant is too broad. Only sufficiently small ε is needed. Choosing the two subpower exponents with combined sum below 1/2−ε also gives convergence for any fixed ε<1/2, independently of how the printed bound is named.

By dominated convergence and Mellin inversion the transforms converge in L², and the inverse limits agree a.e. with X_ε f_(2ε) by taking an a.e.-convergent subsequence of the norm-convergent sequence and using pointwise (2.4). On (0,1), multiplication by x^ε is contractive. On (1,∞), (2.9) must read x^(−1)Σ_{a≤n}μ(a)/a^(1+2ε) and converges in H there because x^(−1) is square-integrable and the coefficient converges absolutely. Thus f_(2ε,n)→f_(2ε) in H, placing it in the closed natural span under RH.

Now let ε↓0 in the limiting transform −ζ(s−ε)/(s−ε)ζ(s+ε)^(-1) at Re s=1/2. The quotient converges to 1 a.e. and has a uniform majorant O((1+|t|)^ε0), so the transforms tend in L² to −1/s for any fixed ε0<1/2. This analytic limit is unconditional for the abstract ratios. The preceding identification with ℳ(X_ε f_(2ε)) was conditional. Under RH the inverse transform converges to −χ. On (0,1), x^ε(X_ε f_(2ε)+χ) has norm bounded by the latter’s norm and χ(1−x^ε) tends to zero in L². On (1,∞) use x^(−1)/ζ(1+2ε). Together these give f_(2ε)→−χ in H. Burnol §4, particularly Theorem 4.2, explains why unconditional square-integrability of actual f_ε along a null sequence cannot be claimed without RH. This distinction also sharpens the reading of the unconditional language in the introduction and end of §2.

For the converse the original cited criterion suffices by inclusion. One direct reconstruction avoids appealing to its deep direction. On the closure of B, the tail over x>1 lies in the closed span of x^(−1) there. For 1/2<Re s<1 the Mellin evaluation on (0,1) is continuous in H by Cauchy–Schwarz, and the tail evaluation is the continuous extraction of the tail coefficient times 1/(1−s). Thus if ζ(s)=0 in that strip, the bounded evaluation vanishes on the closure of the dilation span but is 1/s on χ. Functional-equation symmetry then gives RH. For the corollary it is enough to have H convergence of f_(ε_j,n) as n→∞ for each member of a sequence ε_j↓0. Its limit for fixed ε_j agrees with pointwise f_(ε_j). For a hypothetical off-line zero ρ on the right, bounded evaluation at ρ applies also to f_(ε_j) from the convergence. Direct evaluation at ρ is not needed to identify the subsequent ε_j limit. Instead use the conditional proof forward, and for the converse use the following analytic obstruction. For Re s>1 and the part k_ε=f_ε·1_(0,1], integrate the absolutely convergent formula with the finite floor sum at each x to obtain
`∫₀¹ k_ε(x)x^(s−1)dx = 1/[(s−1)ζ(1+ε)] − ζ(s)/[sζ(s+ε)]`.
This follows by termwise absolute integration there, since floor(1/(ax)) vanishes for x>1/a and the scaled floor integral gives a^(−s)ζ(s)/s. If f_ε∈H, the left side is holomorphic on Re s>1/2 by Cauchy–Schwarz locally uniformly. Hence if Re ρ−ε>1/2, the pole from ζ(s+ε)^(-1) at s=ρ−ε forces ζ(ρ−ε)=0, with sufficient multiplicity. Discreteness prohibits this along a null sequence. This proves the corollary and the preprint’s subsequence variant with unbounded cutoffs for each damping. It avoids a circular assertion that the second norm limit for the actual functions has already been established unconditionally.

## Journal §3 quantitative approximation

Theorem 2 is conditional on RH, with d_n=c/loglog n and approximant −Σ_{a≤n}μ(a)a^(−d_n)ρ_a. The printed order is O((loglog n)^(−1/3)) in H. The paper attributes the quantitative theorem to Balazard, mentions Bombieri’s parallel suggestion and attributes its symmetric-line ratio technical input to Burnol. The sketch uses real ε, n large enough for loglog n>0 and eventually ε≤1/2. Equation (2.6) and correct Plancherel bound the squared norm by a constant times the sum of
`I = ∫ |ζ(s)|² |M_n(s+ε)−1/ζ(s+ε)|² dt/|s|²`
and
`J = ∫ |ζ(s)/ζ(s+ε)−1|² dt/|s|²`, with s=1/2+it.

For J choose δ=ε^(2/3), with ε sufficiently small. On the δ-neighborhood of zero ordinates, Burnol’s (3.2), proved in his Lemma 3.1 by pole removal, the functional equation and Phragmén–Lindelöf, bounds the integrand by C(1+|t|)^(−2+ε). For each interval [j,j+1] the exceptional set has length O(δ log(|j|+3)) by the classical local zero count, and summing the weighted measures gives O(δ), without independence or simplicity. Elsewhere choose the logarithm by integrating ζ'/ζ horizontally from s to s+ε. Under RH all nearby zero terms have denominator modulus ≥δ and their number is O(log(2+|t|)). The classical local partial-fractions formula on this strip gives |w|≤Cε δ^(-1)log(2+|t|) for the logarithmic difference w. At bounded heights the strip up to 3/4 avoids the pole. Use |e^w−1|≤|w|e^|w|. For ε/δ small enough, absorb the logarithmic factor and the exponential loss into (1+|t|)^(1/4), obtaining |ζ(s)/ζ(s+ε)−1|≪ εδ^(-1)(1+|t|)^(1/4). The square divided by |s|² is integrable. Therefore J≪δ+ε²δ^(−2)≪ε^(2/3). The boundary conventions at the split do not matter. No zero separation estimate is needed.

For I the special form printed on p. 10 has constants uniform for c/loglog n≤ε≤1/2 and t real. The cited fixed-gap lemma cannot simply be evaluated with δ=ε_n. I rechecked the following Perron reconstruction of the repository’s earlier dependency audit. Let L(T)=log(T+3)/loglog(T+3). Titchmarsh–Heath-Brown §14.14, pp. 354–355, gives on RH a critical-line upper bound exp(C L(|t|)) for ζ and, after its adjoining regions are combined, exp(C L(|v|)) for the reciprocal at distance ≥a/loglog |v| to the right of 1/2, for fixed a>0 and large |v|. The equality-line formula alone is insufficient. For the right part of the strip one can use the bound at 3/4 and the absolutely convergent Euler logarithm at 2, interpolated by Phragmén–Lindelöf for the branch of log ζ at large heights. Polynomial growth of log ζ in this fixed strip follows from the pole-removed Borel–Carathéodory argument below.

For finite height V, use the preceding inverse bound between exp(sqrt(log V)) and V, since 1/loglog V≥1/(2loglog |v|) there. At lower heights put H(s)=(s−1)ζ(s). Its logarithm on Re s>1/2 is fixed by the positive value at 2, and at 2+iv it is the sum of the Euler logarithm and Log(1+iv). Use disks centered at 2+iv with radii 3/2−Δ/2 and 3/2−Δ where Δ=1/loglog V. Polynomial growth of H on the outer disk and Borel–Carathéodory bound log H on the inner disk by O(Δ^(-1)log(|v|+3)). This gives O(loglog V sqrt(log V)) in the low-height range and handles the removable zero of the reciprocal at 1. It follows that |1/ζ|≤exp(C log V/loglog V) uniformly over |v|≤V and 1/2+Δ≤Re s≤2.

For the truncated Möbius sum use Perron at κ=1−Re(s+ε)+1/log n and height U=n⁴, then shift to −ε/2. The endpoint convention changes the sum by O(n^(−Re(s+ε))) at integer n. To see the remaining truncation error directly, the standard Perron kernel contributes ≪Σ_{m≠n}m^(−σ)(n/m)^κ min(1,1/(U|log(n/m)|)), σ=Re(s+ε). For m∈[n/2,2n], use |log(n/m)|≫|m−n|/n and the harmonic sum to obtain O(n^(1−σ)log n/U). Outside this range use the absolutely convergent series at σ+κ=1+1/log n to get the same bound. Thus total error is O(n^(−1/2)). The rectangle crosses only the kernel pole, with residue 1/ζ(s+ε). Choose V=|t|+U+3. For c large enough ε/2≥1/loglog V. The finite-height bound on the rectangle is ≤exp(C₁ log n/loglog n+C₂L(|t|)), by comparison to the larger of n⁴ and |t|+3. The shifted vertical integral contributes this factor times O(n^(−ε/2)log n), and the horizontals contribute the same factor times O(n^(−7/2)). Enlarging c absorbs the n-dependent exponential and logarithm into n^(ε/6). This yields uniform O(n^(−ε/3)exp(C₂L(|t|))). With the RH upper bound for ζ on the critical line and L(|t|)=o(log|t|), this yields I≪n^(−2ε/3) with an absolute constant for the fixed admissible c. See the earlier [dependency reconstruction](../baez_uniform_dependency_round2.md).

Consequently the squared approximation error is O(n^(−2ε/3)+ε^(2/3)). Taking ε=c/loglog n makes the exponential term smaller than the power of ε. Taking square roots gives the journal theorem. The argument does not estimate practical n thresholds or claim an optimal rate.
