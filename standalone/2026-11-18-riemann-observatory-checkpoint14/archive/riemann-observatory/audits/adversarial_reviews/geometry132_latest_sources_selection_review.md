## Fiori–Jaskari v2

I reread Theorem 1.1 on PDF p. 4, the \(x_0=e^3\) table row, Theorem 1.4 on p. 5, Theorems 5.2–5.3 on p. 44, and the assembly on p. 45. The prose introduction defines signed \(E_\psi\), and Theorem 1.1 states its result in that notation. The absolute-value bound is explicit in Theorem 5.2 and the conclusion of §5.1. The latter assembles the initial interval, finite-height estimates, existing large-\(x\) bound and new explicit tail. This supports the stated unconditional use of the \(x_0=e^3\) bound in the Abel prime-to-integral substep for Gonek Lemma 8, subject to the ordinary status of that cited preprint theorem. I did not audit the numerical interval tables or its code. Theorem 1.4 and §5.2 are structurally separate, with an explicit premise on every positive-real-part zero. The \(7/8\) specialization is only a conditional transfer in this decision. No half-plane establishment, other effective Gonek constants, or derivative-tail constant follows. ## Inumaru–Sugiyama v1

The authors are **Naoto Inumaru and Shingo Sugiyama**. Sugiyama–Suriajaya authored the separate comparison paper arXiv:2610.05227, which is already canonically recorded. I checked the new PDF's front matter, Theorems 1.3–1.4, and its appendix boundary against the two complete reading records. Theorem 1.3 fixes a primitive character and a real \(\sigma\in[1/2,1]\), and evaluates a continuous \(|L(\sigma+it,\chi)|^2\)-weighted one-level statistic at strict support inside \((-\sigma/(\sigma+1),\sigma/(\sigma+1))\), unconditionally. For \(q=1\) the principal case is classical zeta. Theorem 1.4 permits distinct fixed primitive characters in the statistic and its weight. These are precise comparison results for the continuous statistic. The longer discrete additive prime sum and the derivative-rooted law require different inputs. Appendix A's GRH support-removal statement concerns the unweighted result.
