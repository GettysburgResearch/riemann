# Scoped independent audit of Rodgers–Tao's local energy estimates

18 November 2026. The endpoint cancellation and the unsigned first-distance bound have literal failures with local repairs. The previously proposed \(X_3\) and signed \(X_4\) estimates survive independent reconstruction. The journal's original boundary argument is superseded by the authors' amended arXiv version. The amended argument's window estimate can be made precise by retaining the harmless classical baseline and centering the controlled windows at the reference endpoints. This note is confined to the displayed local estimates and their stated uses. It changes no registry counts and makes no new classification of the main theorem.

## Basic estimates and endpoint witness

Here \(L=\log T\), \(N=TL\), \(w_j=(1+|j|/N)^{-100}\), and \(M=NL^2+\widetilde E_T\), with the source's finite-energy times and uniform displacement \(x_j-\xi_j=O(\log(2+|j|))\). Indices are in \(\mathbb Z^*\), the points are strictly ordered and odd, and \(i\sim j\) means \(0<|i-j|<(T^2+|i|+|j|)^{1/10}\). Use the classical gap bounds (43)–(45), the displacement (50), and the renormalization (62)–(67). The positive potential gives \(|x_i-x_j|^{-2}\ll|\xi_i-\xi_j|^{-2}+\widetilde E_{ij}\) by splitting the gap ratio at \(1/2\). Thus the weighted inverse-square sum is \(O(M)\) for actual and classical points.

The Lemma 16 intermediate odd cubic sum on \(I=[J,2J]\) with \(j=J\) is at least 1 for \(J>2\). The displayed maximum endpoint distance gives order \(J^{-2}\). Pair opposite increments before bounding what remains. The unmatched sum is bounded by \(2/(1+d_j)^2\) with \(d_j\) the minimum endpoint distance, and its sum over \(j\) is \(O(1)\). The spacing expansion (45) contributes a polylogarithmic factor times \(1/j\) to each row after summing the local quadratic-denominator error. The range \(|i-j|\ge j/2\) has row sum \(\ll \log^C(2+\max|I|)/j^2\) without cancellation. Splitting an interval across zero leaves a cross gap \(\gg (i+|j|)/\log(2+i+|j|)\) and displacements of logarithmic size. At each sum \(r=i+|j|\) there are at most \(r\) choices, so the cubic cross correction is \(\ll\sum_{r\ge2}\log^4(2+r)/r^2\). Reflection handles negative intervals. This proves the aggregate polylogarithmic correction used later.

The global first-distance sum has the valid upper bound
\[
 \sum_{i\ne j} w_iw_j |x_i-x_j|^{-1}\ll M.
\]
For \(h=|i-j|\) above a sufficiently large constant times \(\log^2(2+|i|+|j|)\), actual gaps are comparable to classical gaps. Summing \(\log(2+|i|+|j|)/h\) on \(|i|\le2|j|\), then summing \(w_i\log(2+|i|)/|i|\) outside that range, gives a bound \(\ll\log^2(2+|j|)+L^2\) for each row before the outer \(w_j\). In the complementary range there are \(O(\log^2(2+|j|))\) indices with weights comparable to \(w_j\), and Cauchy–Schwarz against the inverse-square sum applies. The same proof restricts to both indices below \(2T^{7/10}\) with pair weight \(O(T^{7/10}L^2)\), or both above \(T^{11/10}/2\) with pair weight \(O(T^{11/10}(N/T^{11/10})^{200}L^2)\) in the complementary range. In the comparable-gap range, retain \(w_j\) and its row bound; restricting the outer \(j\) gives polynomial savings at either endpoint. Both restricted estimates are \(o(M)\) even with a fixed additional power of \(\log(2+T+|j|)\). These restrictions apply to the actual pairs used below, since near indices lie within a comparable endpoint threshold.

The prefactor \(L\) in the printed Lemma 21(ii) cannot hold. In the explicitly permitted classical case let \(n=\lfloor N\rfloor\) and take \(n\le j\le2n,\ i=j+h,\ 1\le h\le n\). The weighted sum is \(\gg nL\sum_{h\le n}h^{-1}\gg NL^2\) by (44), with a fixed positive lower bound on the weights. Yet \(\widetilde E_T=0\). Restricting to \(h\le T^{1/5}/2\) retains the same logarithmic order. This is a finite block witness for every sufficiently large \(T\), in the exact specialization stated by the lemma.

## The two affected terms of Proposition 22

For \(X_3-X'_3\) retain the full domain \(i,j\sim k,\ i\not\sim j\). Write \(D=(T^2+|k|)^{1/10}\) and \(\epsilon=T^{-9/5}/10\). The threshold function changes by at most \(\epsilon\) times a change of one index. Thus \(D\le|j-k|+(1+\epsilon)|i-k|\), so one near distance is at least \(D/3\). All near distances are \(O(D)\) and near weights are comparable. In the outer low and high ranges of \(k\), summing over the \(O(D)\) choices for the larger distance costs \(O(\log(2+T+|k|))\) by the reciprocal-distance bound. The remaining inverse-distance sums are the two restricted cases above. For \(T^{7/10}\le|k|\le T^{11/10}\), if both index distances exceed \(T^{1/10}\), relative reciprocal errors are \(O(L^2T^{-1/10})\); the product of the two classical harmonic rows is \(O(L^4)\). Summing \(w_k\) then gives \(o(M)\).

If the shorter distance is \(h<T^{1/10}\), on either ray from \(k\) the function \(F(r)=r-(T^2+|k|+|k\pm r|)^{1/10}\) is increasing with difference quotients at least \(1-\epsilon\). The near and not-near requirements place it in \([-(1+\epsilon)h,0)\). Therefore there are \(O(h+1)\) slots and their long reciprocal sum is \(O(LT^{-1/10})\). Transfer the weight to the short index when needed and apply the valid \(O(M)\) first-distance bound. This costs \(o(M)\) in either orientation. Using a closed left edge respects equality in the complement of the strict near cutoff. For example \(i=99,k=100,j=110,T^2=11^{10}-209\) meets \(i,j\sim k\) and equality in the not-near threshold for \(i,j\); any written strict inequality in that expansion excludes this witness. The local range bound includes it.

For \(X_4\), define \(A_k\) to be the inner signed symmetric principal value. Outside the near range the difference between the actual and classical reciprocals is
\[
 O\!\left(\frac{\log^3(2+|i|+|k|)}{|i-k|^2}\right).
\]
This sum is absolutely convergent at fixed \(k,T\) and is \(O(L^3T^{-1/5})\) in the bulk by summing up to \(2|k|\) and beyond. Outside the bulk a uniform crude \(A_k\ll\log^2(2+T+|k|)\) follows by harmonic summation out to \(C(|k|+D)\), then by pairing \(i,-i\) and bounding the resulting summable \(O(|x_k|\log^2|i|/i^2)\) tail. Thus the end ranges already cost \(o(M)\) by the restricted pair estimates, respecting both indices.

For bulk positive \(k\), the exact near cutoff differs by at most \(O(1)\) slots from \(|i-k|\le R=\lfloor T^{1/5}\rfloor\), for error \(O(L/R)\). Write \(x=\xi(k)\) and \(p(u)=(x-\xi(u))^{-1}+(x+\xi(u))^{-1}\) on \(u\ge0\), with the increasing inverse branch of (43). It is monotone on either side of \(k\). Sum–integral comparison on the two outside ranges costs \(O(L/R+1/x)\); retaining the negative partners of the deleted positive slots costs that same order in comparison. One obtains the full principal-value integral of \(p\) minus the principal value of \((x-\xi(u))^{-1}\) over \([k-R,k+R]\). The latter is \(O(R/k)\) because its leading Taylor pole is odd and the residual is \(O(1/k)\). The transformed measure in the full integral is \(\log(y/(4\pi))\,dy/(4\pi)\), \(y=\xi(u)\); the ratios of the small one-sided gaps at the pole tend to 1, preserving the principal value. Extending to zero costs \(O(1/x)\). With \(y=xv\) the constant-numerator integral of \(1/(1-v^2)\) vanishes and the log-numerator integral is \(-\pi^2/4\), by substituting \(1/v\) above 1 and using the series below 1. Including \(1/(2\pi)\) gives \(-\pi/8\). Hence
\[
 A_k=-(\pi/8)\operatorname{sgn}k+O(L^3T^{-1/5}).
\]
In the symmetric bulk pair domain every near pair has the same sign. Its leading term cancels by exchanging the two indices. The remaining sum is \(O(L^3T^{-1/5}M)=o(M)\) using the corrected first-distance bound. The same argument applies to classical points. This explicitly replaces the invalid step from \(o(L)\) times the printed \(O(M/L)\). ## Boundary propagation and the reference windows

For the old journal argument, small boundary gaps cannot be controlled just by (50), (91) and (92) as cited there. Even a very large *internal* inverse-square energy can occur with the two *boundary* gaps fixed. For an exact finite toy witness, an exterior point at \(-1\) and two interior points at \(0,\delta\) contribute \(8/(\delta^2(1+\delta))\) to the ordered environmental sum in the propagation inequality, while the left-boundary gap remains 1. To realize the same issue with (50), keep classical points throughout the bulk except for moving one interior point \(x_{j_-+2}\) to \(\xi_{j_-+1}+T^{-10}\), and reflect the modification on the negative side. Both boundary gaps are unchanged, the displacement is \(O(1/L)\), and the environmental product from the exterior neighbor and this close pair is \(\gg T^{20}L^2\). This targets only the cited static inference. The configuration is not assumed to be a solution of the source dynamics.

I checked the amended arXiv v5 p. 58 display directly in the PDF image. For close spatial pairs the penultimate line transfers the distance weight to \(I_\pm^0\), the fixed reference anchors. The final dyadic window display instead prints \(I_\pm\), and has no additive classical baseline. With the printed convention that the softened inequality is multiplicative, taking all \(x=\xi\) gives positive close-pair energy on the left and all renormalized terms zero on the right. This does not obstruct the propagation proposition: the missing term is polylogarithmic and the bound can use the fixed reference windows throughout.

Here are details. Put \(J=j_-\) or \(j_+\), and let the exterior index on that side be \(\ell\). From the separated boundary gap and (43)–(50), uniformly for \(k\in K\) and exterior \(\ell\) of size at most \(C N\), one has
\[
 |x_k-x_\ell|^{-1}\ll \frac{L^C}{1+|k-J|+|\ell-J|}.
\]
Indeed large index distances \(\gg L^2\) give the classical lower bound, and smaller ones use the preserved boundary gap \(\gg 1/L\). For \(|\ell|>C N\) use macroscopic separation \(|x_k-x_\ell|\gg|\ell|/\log(2+|\ell|)\), choosing \(C\) large, so the inverse-square tail is \(O(L^2/N)\). Consequently the sum of exterior inverse squares at fixed \(k\) on this side is \(\ll L^C/(1+|k-J|)\). The product bound follows by Cauchy–Schwarz and arithmetic–geometric mean, giving the sum of the two interior index weights. For close spatial pairs the exterior distances are comparable up to \(O(L)\) because the boundary gap is \(\gg1/L\) and the pair displacement is less than 1. Therefore use just the weight for \(k\) at polylogarithmic cost. For spatial gaps \(\ge1\), the row sum of \(|x_k-x_{k'}|^{-2}\) over \(k'\in K\) is polylogarithmic uniformly, by counting \(O(L^2)\) nearby indices and then summing the classical inverse-square upper bound. Summing the endpoint weights is \(O(\log N)\). This establishes the claimed softened bound for the large-gap part.

In the close part put \(\Delta=1+|I_--I_-^0|+|I_+-I_+^0|\). Transferring \((1+|k-J|)^{-1}\) to \((1+|k-I_\pm^0|)^{-1}\) costs at most \(O(L^C\Delta)\), since \(|J-I_\pm|\le L^3+1\). Under \(a^{-2}\ll b^{-2}+\widetilde E_{kk'}\), the classical \(b^{-2}\) part sums to another polylogarithm using the full uniform classical row sum and the harmonic endpoint weights. For the renormalized part note that \(|x_k-x_{k'}|<1\) implies \(|k-k'|\ll L^2\), from the same displacement and bulk spacing. Thus dyadic weights \(h^{-1}\) for windows of radii \(h\ge1\) centered at \(I_\pm^0\) dominate \((1+|k-I_\pm^0|)^{-1}\) up to a factor \(O(L^C)\): choose a dyadic radius comparable to \(\max(1,|k-I_\pm^0|,|k'-I_\pm^0|)\). If that radius exceeds \(0.1N\), the two distances differ by \(O(L^2)\), so the weight is \(O(1/N)\). Charge it to the full bulk interval with the weaker coefficient \(T^{-1}\). This yields a bound with exactly the reference-window functional from Proposition 27 plus \(T^{-1}\widetilde E^{[0.5N,3N]}\) and an additive 1, all multiplied by \(O(L^C\Delta)\). Integrating applies Proposition 27 and Theorem 17 and costs \(O(L^C\Delta)\) over the short step.

For completeness, the reference selection is valid with integer endpoints: average each endpoint over its indicated \(0.1N\) band, and count at most \(2h+1\) anchor choices for any pair to lie in a window of radius \(h\). Divide by the number of eligible anchors, multiply by \(1/h\), sum \(O(L)\) dyadic scales, and apply Theorem 17. This gives a polylogarithmic average and allows fixed anchors independent of time. The boundary gap selected at \(t_2\) stays \(\gg L^{-1}\) by Lemma 12(iv), since its squared decrease going backward is at most \(8/(100L^2)\). Lemma 16's repaired endpoint estimate and the \(O(L^3)\) deleted indices convert the unrenormalized inequality to the claimed renormalized one, with a further polylogarithmic classical row cost. Over \(O(L^2)\) steps the total anchor drift is \(O(L^5)\). The reference-center repair and added classical baseline fit the same error allowance in Proposition 28.

Thus the literal local comparisons require care, while the relevant implications admit the reconstructions above under the stated inputs. The old journal boundary inference is a documented historical issue. No conclusion is drawn here about portions of the paper outside these local arguments.
