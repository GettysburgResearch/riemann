## Section 1, pp. 1589–1593

Write \(k=a/b>0\) for fixed positive integers \(a,b\), \(N=a+b\), and \(x=T^{\theta/N}\) for fixed \(0<\theta<1/2\). On RH, \(overline{P(\rho)}=P(1-\rho)\) for real coefficients and similarly for the zeta derivative. Hölder is applied to
\[
|P(\rho)|^{2a}
=(|\zeta'(\rho)|^2|P(\rho)|^{2(a+b)})^{a/(a+b)}
(|\zeta'(\rho)|^{-2k})^{b/(a+b)}.
\]
Simplicity ensures division is legal for every zero. The case \(k=0\) follows from the ordinary zero count and need not use a choice of \(a=0\) in the convolutions for positive \(k\). From \(S_1\asymp T L^{k^2+1}\), \(S_2\ll T L^{k^2+3}\), one obtains \(J_{-k}\ge S_1^{k+1}/S_2^k\gg T L^{(k-1)^2}\). The heuristic replacement of \(\zeta'\) by shifted \(\zeta\) motivates \(P\), and is not used as a mathematical premise. The range of fixed rationals and constants depending on their height are explicit. The suggestion to treat every real \(k\) remains a future question.

## Section 2, pp. 1594–1600

For integral \(B\ge1\), \(\psi(n)=\mathbf1_{n\le x}(\log(x/n)/\log x)^B\) follows from the simple inverse Mellin kernel in (8); at \(n=x\) the value is zero. Local fractional coefficients are defined by \(\sum_{j\ge0}\tau_\alpha(p^j)z^j=(1-z)^{-\alpha}\), with the analytic germ at zero. For \(b\ge1\), \(|\tau_{-1/b}(p^j)|\le\tau_{1/b}(p^j)\), via comparing each factor of the rising factorial. The multivariable series can be opened on positive lines by the analogous absolute coefficient series. The external discrete mean input is Ng [2008a], Proposition 4(i), the Landau–Gonek mean-value formula with a diagonal and the \(\Lambda\)-weighted off-diagonal. The support length here is \(x^a=T^{\theta a/(a+b)}<\sqrt T\), and the coefficients are bounded by a fixed divisor function.

In (10), balancing total left/right prime degree forces a first-degree prime term to come from a single variable on each side. Its coefficient is \((-1/b)^2\). Removing those terms leaves an absolutely convergent Euler product in a neighborhood of the origin. With \(L_x=\log x\), shift variables only by scaling \(s_j\mapsto s_j/L_x\). The pre-expansion bounds follow from absolute zeta series on \(\Re s_j=1/L_x\), and any tail beyond \(\sqrt{L_x}\) in scaled height costs \(O(L_x^{-B/2})\). On the central box the Laurent expansion of each fractional factor is uniform on its Euler-series branch, since the real part of each sum is positive. This gives (13); no implicit complex root choices across a left contour are needed.

For (14), insert \(\Lambda(\ell)=\partial_\gamma\sum_{uv=\ell}\mu(u)v^\gamma|_0\). The first-degree prime imbalance from \(u,v\) and one right polynomial variable has coefficient \((-1/b)(-1+p^\gamma)\), giving the displayed ratio \(\zeta(1+s_R)^{1/b}/\zeta(1+s_R-\gamma)^{1/b}\). At \(\gamma=0\) the inserted convolution is the unit, so the arithmetic factor is \(A\). Its derivative costs only the original order, and the derivative of the singular ratio adds \((1/b)\zeta'/\zeta\) with leading negative sign. This produces \(S_{12}=-k A(0)\gamma(a,b)L_x^{k^2+1}+O(L_x^{k^2+1/2})\). Rendered (9) supplies \(T/\pi\) on the off-diagonal, and \(N(T)\sim TL/(2\pi)\). The displayed recombination near the top of §2.3, p. 1599, drops the common \(1/\pi\) from both leading terms. The exact leading sum reconstructed from (9), (13), (14) is
\[
\frac{T}{\pi}A(0)\left[\tfrac12\beta(a,b)L L_x^{k^2}
+k\gamma(a,b)L_x^{k^2+1}\right].
\]
The positive-constant conclusion is unchanged.

At each prime the local factor of \(A(0)\) is \((1-1/p)^{k^2}\sum_j\tau_{-k}(p^j)^2p^{-j}>0\), with Euler remainders absolutely summable. The Brevig–Heap construction in §2.3 is direct via Laplace inversion of \((s_i+s_{a+j})^{-1/b^2}\). Introduce nonnegative \(u_{ij}\) with density \(u_{ij}^{1/b^2-1}/\Gamma(1/b^2)\); the other inversions impose row and column sums at most one with polynomial gap weights. The integrals converge near the coordinate hyperplanes because \(1/b^2>0\), and the strict interior has positive measure. Replacing one gap power by \(B+1\) and dividing by \(B+1\) gives the constant \(\gamma(a,b)>0\). The positive moment is not inferred from positivity of every original zero summand off RH.

## Section 3 and the smoothed diagonal reduction in §4, pp. 1600–1606

Let \(a(n)\) denote the actual coefficients of \(P^N\), as opposed to the count numerator \(a\). They satisfy \(|a(n)|\le\tau_{N/b}(n)\le\tau_R(n)\) for a fixed integer \(R\ge N/b\). The displayed application of the shifted theorem additionally requires a uniform submultiplicative bound in the *actual coefficient magnitudes*. That bound does not follow merely from a divisor-type majorant of a signed convolution. One can close the application with the following finite polarization. On all \(n\le y\) let \(B_0(n)=K\tau_R(n)\) with \(K\ge4\), and apply the theorem to \(B_0\), \(B_0+a\), and \(B_0-a\), all extended by zero. On support the moduli lie between \((K-1)\tau_R(n)\) and \((K+1)\tau_R(n)\). For \(mn\le y\), \(\tau_R(mn)\le\tau_R(m)\tau_R(n)\) and \((K-1)^2\ge K+1\) prove the required bound. Outside support the output is zero. Each array meets the fixed divisor/log envelope, interpreting \(\log n\) at 1 in the bounded-initial-value sense. Their parallelogram combination returns the desired entire sesquilinear expression and remainder. This variant extends the earlier archive's bounded-coefficient padding to the full divisor majorant. It does not require an example violating the inequality for the original signed array. None of this invokes RH.

In §3.1, the two residues in (16) regularize the opposed zeta poles. Expanding \(F\) as in the paper, a singleton in the left group has Euler factor \(\zeta(1+s_j-z_2)^{-1/b}\); a singleton in the right group has \(\zeta(1+s_{N+j}+z_1)^{-1/b}\). Rendered (17), p. 1602, uses \(+z_2\) in the latter slot, whereas the immediately preceding Dirichlet series has \(+z_1\). The paired first-degree terms cancel the gcd's shifts and give \(\zeta(1+s_i+s_{N+j})^{1/b^2}\). The corrected slot yields the same bound on the full small-shift box. Scaling and localization as in §2, the exponent is \(N^2/b^2-2N/b=k^2-1\). The two contour lengths and squared difference factor save four log powers against the pole factors, logarithm, and derivatives. The resulting factor \(T L^4\) gives \(S_{21}\ll T L^{k^2+3}\). Below \(t=1\), keep the integrable \(t^{-O(1/L)}(1+|\log t|)^j\) explicitly. Choose integral \(B\ge\lceil14+12k\rceil\); the displayed equality for \(B\) p. 1594 need not give an integer for every rational \(k\).

In §3.2, the three copies come from choosing the distinguished middle variable of the ordered triple, with the first and third slots symmetric. The squared Vandermonde gives six negative log powers, three contour lengths give three more, and nine simple denominators and three differentiations cost twelve. The prefactor is \(T L^3\). Equation (22) is obtained by balancing the two sides of \(h_1\cdots h_Nm_1m_2m_3=k_1\cdots k_Nn\). Single left factors can pair with a right polynomial or with \(n\), and single right factors can pair with left polynomials or the three \(m\) slots. The signs of \(\mu\) and \(\tau_{-1/b}\) explain the reciprocal and numerator factors. At the localized \(w=0\) residue, the net exponent is \(N^2/b^2-2N/b+1=k^2\). The rendered closing sentence p. 1605 omits the overall \(T\) in saying \(S_{22}\ll L^{k^2+3}\); the preceding bound and Proposition 3 have the correct factor.

There is a real contour issue in the stated smoothing. Lemma 6 p. 1605 requires \(H(0)=1\) and zeros at \(2w=\beta-\alpha,\beta-\gamma\), but its p. 1606 Euler numerator has poles at \(2w=-\alpha-\beta,-\beta-\gamma\). Even the corrected zero locations demand a separate convention if a pole collides with zero, when one cannot impose both \(H(0)=1\) and vanishing. The \(\log_3T\) in the rendered shift is the iterated logarithm, and a small constant in the width should be chosen from the actual zero-free bound. Ordinary source use of the explicit unsmoothed Theorem 5 avoids all these smoothing issues.

For the specific Proposition 3 estimate, use Theorem 5 before applying the smoothed replacement. Form the symmetric three-choice Vandermonde contour for the summed **unsmoothed** \(Z\) kernel (24). Its candidate pairwise poles come from the two zeta numerator factors and are simple along each respective collision hyperplane. The squared Vandermonde clears them, and repeated selections still vanish because at least one collision factor remains. This permits nested outer contours separated by \(\gg L^{-1}\). On those contours alone, replace each unsmoothed value by its diagonal-smoothed version with a pointwise kernel
\[
H_{\boldsymbol z}(w)=e^{w^2}\prod_{i<j}\left(1-\frac{4w^2}{(z_i-z_j)^2}\right).
\]
Its zeros are at the correct poles when \((\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=(z_1,-z_2,z_3)\). It is symmetric and normalized, with only a fixed \(L^6\) polynomial cost on separated circles. Since smoothing comes after the contour representation, holomorphy of this chosen kernel at collisions is not required. In the diagonal Mellin argument shift \(w\) by a small fixed multiple of \(1/\log\log T\), keeping all fractional factors with unbounded imaginary Mellin shifts in the absolute half-plane, while the factors depending on \(w\) alone use the zero-free strip up to \(O(\sqrt L)\). The suppression \(T^{-(4/3-2\theta)/\log\log T}\) in the paper's choice, or a similar positive amount after compatible rescaling of the initial Mellin real parts, beats all fixed log costs. At \(w=0\) the kernel is one, so the localized estimates (22)–(23) then give the same Proposition 3 exponent. This is a repair of the particular estimate, not a claim that arbitrary kernels in the printed Theorem 4 satisfy its collision formulation.

## Theorem 5, source §§4.1–4.4, pp. 1607–1615

The theorem evaluates the bilinear zero sum with \(Q(\rho)\overline Q(1-\rho)\), where the bar indicates coefficient conjugation. The reflected term is \(\overline{I(\bar\beta,\bar\alpha,T)}\), with exchange of the coefficient slots on conjugating. This is holomorphic in the original shifts. The right/left contour transformation uses residue multiplicities, independent of simplicity or RH. With \(a=1+1/L\), sufficiently small shifts, and the lower edge at height 1, zeta poles of the shifted factors are below the rectangle. At a good top height within bounded distance of \(T\), the logarithmic derivative costs \(O(L^2)\) on the horizontal segment. The pointwise products of zeta factors and both polynomial factors are \(O(yT^{1/2+\varepsilon})\) uniformly there by convexity and divisor bounds, with the same order for the bounded-height restoration using \(O(L)\) nearby zeros. The Stirling error in the \(\chi'/\chi\) integral costs at most that order. All save a power at the fixed length \(y=T^\theta\), \(\theta<1/2\).

### Sharp continuous term

Theorem 1.1 in the cached Pratt–Robles arXiv:1706.04593v3 evaluates the integral (1.5) with a compactly supported smooth \(\Phi(t/T)\); it does not state the sharp \([1,T]\) integral printed in HLZ §4.1. Simply substituting the characteristic function in the quoted theorem leaves a proof interface. The following separate argument closes it in the required \(\theta<1/2\) range, including the divisor-bounded coefficient family.

Keep \(\alpha,\beta\) initially on distinct fixed-radius-over-\(L\) circles, so \(|\alpha+\beta|\gg L^{-1}\). On a dyadic interval \(t\asymp X\), use the product approximate functional equation from integrating the product of completed zetas against
\[
\frac{e^{w^2}}{w}\left(1-\frac{4w^2}{(\alpha+\beta)^2}\right).
\]
Moving the completed-product contour from right to left crosses the central residue, with the distant shifted completed poles suppressed by the Gaussian for \(X\ge T^\nu\), any fixed \(\nu>0\). The reflected integral is the dual product with shifts \((-\beta,-\alpha)\), times the usual \(\chi\)-factor. Stirling on the central vertical portions gives weights supported to negligible error on \(mn\le X^{1+\varepsilon}\), with \(j\)-th \(t\) derivative \(\ll X^{-j}T^{O(\varepsilon)}\) for \(j=0,1\). The Gaussian polynomial normalization costs only \(L^{O(1)}\). On a fixed finite strip, the tails at vertical distance a fixed fraction of \(X\) are exponentially small. This proves the stated derivative and truncation properties directly from the standard product contour, without smoothing in the external variable \(t\).

For the off-diagonal term, combine all factorizations with the same frequency pair \(r=dm,\ s=en\), where \(d,e\le y\) are polynomial indices. The full coefficient envelope at each pair, including the factorizations and the integral estimate by parts, is bounded by \(T^{O(\varepsilon)}(rs)^{-1/2}\min(X,|\log(r/s)|^{-1})\). This uses \(a(d)\ll_\varepsilon d^\varepsilon\) uniformly, the divisor count, and the bound for the first derivative of the actual weight on the interval. For comparable unequal \(r,s\), both are \(\ll yX^{1/2+\varepsilon}\), and the summand is \(\ll T^{O(\varepsilon)}/|r-s|\). Summing reciprocal differences costs \(O(yX^{1/2}T^{O(\varepsilon)})\). For noncomparable \(r,s\) the frequency bound is \(O(1)\), and absolute sums in \((d,e,m,n)\) give the same bound from \(\sum_{mn\le X^{1+\varepsilon}}(mn)^{-1/2}\) and \((\sum_{d\le y}d^{-1/2+\varepsilon})^2\).

On the diagonal \(d=gh,e=gk,m=k\ell,n=h\ell\). The \(\ell\)-sum produces \(\zeta(1+\alpha+\beta+2w)\). Shift its weight contour to \(\Re w=-1/4\); the pole at \(-\tfrac12(\alpha+\beta)\) is canceled by the explicitly chosen kernel. The main residue at 0 is precisely the first summand of (5) without the extra \(\log(t/2\pi)\). The dual gives the other summand, using the Stirling \(\chi\)-factor \((t/2\pi)^{-\alpha-\beta}(1+O(X^{-1}))\). The diagonal contour remainder per reduced pair has factor \(X^{-1/4}(hk)^{1/4}\) times \(T^{O(\varepsilon)}\). On integrating over the dyadic interval and summing \(g,h,k\),
\[
X^{3/4}T^{O(\varepsilon)}\sum_{gh,gk\le y}\frac{|a(gh)a(gk)|}{g(hk)^{3/4}}
\ll X^{3/4} y^{1/2}T^{O(\varepsilon)}.
\]
The same bound suffices for the gamma-ratio replacement error. These estimates hold for sharp partial dyadic subintervals, since integration by parts only adds the two end values of the weight. Summing dyadically to \(T\) gives power savings at fixed \(\theta<1/2\). For \(t\le T^\nu\) take \(\nu>0\) sufficiently small and use the contour's pointwise convexity/product bound, which integrates to \(O(y T^{3\nu/2+\varepsilon})\). The diagonal main term on that initial range is bounded by harmonic divisor sums and pole factors, and is also negligible after integration. This recovers the sharp formula uniformly in the two shifts. The summed diagonal has removable apparent poles, and separate shift circles suffice for Cauchy differentiation and continuation to their interior. Abel partial summation inserts \(\log(t/2\pi)\); all saved powers absorb the logarithms. This direct argument uses the standard functional equation, Stirling and polynomial zeta bounds on fixed vertical strips. It does not purport to recover Pratt–Robles beyond half-length.

### Right-edge saddle and principal characters

In §4.2 introduce \(\zeta(s+\gamma)/\zeta(s)\), differentiate in \(\gamma\) at the end, and expand the shifted factors on a right line. For the paper's functional-equation convention \(\zeta(s)=\chi(s)\zeta(1-s)\), the conversion of \(\zeta(1-s+\beta)\) is \(\chi(1-s+\beta)\zeta(s-\beta)\), with \(\chi(1-s)\) after translation. The three preceding §4.2 displays use the other \(\chi\) arguments. Lemma 7 itself has the correct \(\chi(1-s)\); its displayed \(m\le nT/(2\pi)\) uses \(n\) where the underlying ratio requires \(k\). Ng 2007 Lemma 4.1 states the exact pointwise kernel for \(r=m/k>0\), including the indicator \(r\le T/(2\pi)\), phase \(e(-r)\), and errors \(r^{-c}[T^{c-1/2}+T^{c+1/2}/(|T-2\pi r|+\sqrt T)]\). In particular the sign has a saddle at positive \(2\pi m/k\) in the integrand actually needed.

The shifted convolution (28) is bounded by \(m^{O(1/L)}(\tau_4*|a|)(m)\). The factor \(m^{O(1/L)}\) is bounded in the finite saddle range \(m\ll yT\), and remains in the absolute infinite series. Fixed small shift radii leave a positive margin \(\gg L^{-1}\) on the right contour. For the first pointwise error, summing \((m/k)^{-c}\) costs \(k^cL^{O(1)}\), canceled up to bounded factors by the outside \(k^{-1+\Re\beta}\). In the central range \(m/k\asymp T\), the second kernel has numerator \(O(\sqrt T)\) and reciprocal distances to \(Tk/(2\pi)\) sum to \(O(k\log(Tk)+T^{-1/2})\). The complement is bounded by the first error method. Bridges restoring the complex-shifted endpoints to heights 1 and \(T\) on the translated vertical line have length \(O(L^{-1})\) and are bounded by Stirling and absolute convergence. The total \(O(yT^{1/2+\varepsilon})\) saves a power. This is the same mechanism previously audited in the cutoffs, retained here to verify the whole paper.

The additive-to-multiplicative expansion follows from Gauss-sum orthogonality after dividing by \((m,k)\), with principal coefficient \(\mu(k')/\varphi(k')\). The formula for the nonprincipal term uses primitive conductors and Möbius inversion for this gcd split. The combinatorial convolution identity of Lemma 8 can be seen prime by prime by greedily allocating the factorization of \(D\) to the convolution slots. Until the prescribed exponent of a prime in \(D\) is allocated, a slot's residual exponent must vanish whenever a later slot receives a positive part. This is precisely the displayed extra coprimality test and gives a reversible map between the terms.

For the principal term, use the coefficient series after grouping \(g=(h,k)\), reduced \(h,k\), and \((n,k)\) in (31)–(35). Finite Euler factors at primes dividing \(kd\) obey a divisor-weighted polylogarithmic bound. The Liu–Ye bounded-truncation Perron formula uses height \(U=\exp(c\sqrt L)\) and **window parameter** \(H=\sqrt U\) to match the actual \(x/\sqrt U\) displayed on p. 1612; the preceding sentence saying \(H=U\) does not give that displayed bound. The short interval is far longer than a fixed power of \(x\) below one because \(x=Tk/(2\pi h)\) has \(\log x\asymp L\). Apply the short-divisor-sum estimate to \(\tau_4(dn)\), using its submultiplicativity at the fixed \(d\) and the factor \((dn)^{O(1/L)}\) in the supported interval. The global absolute series on the right grows only as a fixed power of \(L\). In the outside sums, the sharp cutoff factor \(x\) restores harmonic weights (including \(k/\varphi(k)\)); the divisor envelopes save these sums as fixed log powers. The shifted zeta zero-free line at \(1-c'/\log U\) and the horizontal estimates then save \(\exp(-c''\sqrt L)\).

One can verify (36) without dividing separately by \(p^{-\alpha}-p^{-\gamma}\). At \(p^e\Vert k\) the Ramanujan principal weight equals zero at valuation below \(e-1\), \(-1/(p-1)\) at \(e-1\), and 1 at valuation at least \(e\). Put \(v=1/p\), \(z=p^{-\beta}v\), and \(f_i=f_{\alpha,\gamma}(p^i)\). The local coefficients before applying the weight are \(d_j=\sum_{i\le j}f_i p^{-\beta i}\). The weighted sum at the pole \(s=1\) is
\[
\sum_{j\ge e}d_jv^j-\frac{d_{e-1}v^{e-1}}{p-1}
=\frac{1}{1-v}\sum_{i\ge e}f_i z^i.
\]
Dividing by the untwisted Euler factor gives \(z^e(\sum_{m\ge0}f_{m+e}z^m)/(\sum_{m\ge0}f_mz^m)\). The outside \(k\) and \(h\) factors yield exactly the local quotient and \(h^{-\beta}\) in (24). At the other two poles, reorder the convolution slots so the pole factor is the newly unshifted zeta; this gives \((\alpha,\beta)\mapsto(-\beta,-\alpha)\) and \((\gamma,\beta)\mapsto(-\beta,-\gamma)\), with the printed transforms of the \(t/2\pi\) powers. For colliding poles keep the **sum** of residues, holomorphic by a common surrounding contour. Separated radii are enough for the proof's auxiliary derivative, and avoid any illegitimate division by a vanishing shift difference.

## Section 6, pp. 1619–1624

For the larger conductors, first write \(k=k'k_q\) with \((k',q)=1\) and \(k_q\) supported on primes dividing \(q\). If the Gauss-sum factor in \(\delta\) is nonzero, its character forces the whole \(q\)-primary part of \(k\) into the inner divisor \(e\), hence also into \(d\). It can therefore be removed from the congruence condition. This is the mechanism replacing squarefree coefficient support. The remaining elementary bound \(\delta\ll d(kq)^{-1}(\log\log T)^2\), followed by grouping \(d\) with \(k_q\), gives the displayed dyadic problem with \(KQD\ll y\), \(x\ll KQT\). The product variable is \(md\ll KQDT\ll yT\). The source's abbreviated intermediate \(md\ll KQT\) does not hold with an independent \(d\), but the final \(yT\) bound suffices.

Apply the finite Heath–Brown inverse-zeta identity to \(\mu\) through \(T^{3/2}\), using \(yT=o(T^{3/2})\) at fixed \(\theta<1/2\). The identity follows by expanding \(1-(1-M\zeta)^J\), since the first possible coefficient of the remainder is beyond the chosen range. There are at most \(2J+3\) active convolution slots, with trivial delta slots inserted to unify their count. Dyadic splitting costs log powers; greedy allocation of \(d\) again gives the conditions \((m_i,D_i)=1\). Intervals with product upper cutoff below the requested \(x\) may replace \(x\) by the actual support cutoff. Sizes \(x\ll(yT)^{1/2}\) are trivial as in (45). For the other sizes, take \(W\asymp(KQT)^{2/3}\). If \(W\ge x\) both grouped supports already admit this length, with an empty group interpreted as the constant polynomial. Otherwise a slot of length at least a fixed multiple of \(W\) gives Type I, or a greedy product of small slots yields a subset with product between \(x/W\) and \(W\), up to fixed factors. The last conclusion uses \(W\gg x^{2/3}\).

A Type I large slot cannot be a \(\mu\) truncation or the polynomial coefficient slot, since their lengths are \(\ll T^{3/(2J)}\) and \(y\). When \(x\gg(yT)^{1/2}\), these are below the working threshold on choosing fixed \(J\) large. The remaining slots have weights \(n^c\), \(c=O(1/L)\), with possibly complex shifts. Pólya–Vinogradov after Möbius removal of the finitely many prime divisors of \(D_i\), and partial summation with derivative \(c n^{c-1}\), give the stated \(Q^{1/2}T^\varepsilon\) factor per long character sum. The remaining convolution has length \(\ll x/W\). With the outside dyadic weights and character count the contribution is \(\ll T^{1+\varepsilon}Q^2/W\), at most \(y^{4/3}T^{1/3+\varepsilon}\).

For Type II, use finite Perron at \(\kappa\asymp1/\log(KQT)\), height \(T^{20}\); near an integer the boundary coefficient costs \(T^\varepsilon\), within the claimed error, and away from it the kernel tails are negligible. Separate two grouped polynomials of lengths \(A,B\ll W\), \(AB\ll KQT\). The fixed divisor envelope on their coefficients, including factors from \(d\), yields squared coefficient sums bounded by corresponding lengths times divisor and log factors. Apply Cauchy–Schwarz to \(V^{-1}\sum_{q\asymp Q,\psi}^*\int_{-V}^V|A(\psi,t)B(\psi,t)|dt\), then the hybrid large sieve with coefficient factor \(Q^2V+\mathrm{length}\) (Iwaniec–Kowalski, Theorem 7.17, as used in the source). After the harmonic \(d\) and dyadic normalization this gives
\[
