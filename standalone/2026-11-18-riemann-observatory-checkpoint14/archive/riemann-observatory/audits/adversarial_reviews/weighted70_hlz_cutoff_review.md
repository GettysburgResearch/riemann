# Independent review of increasing scalar prime cutoffs in HLZ

Assume RH. The scalar statistic \((W_P-H_P)/\sqrt{V_P}\) under the two-sided strict derivative-weighted tail has all Gaussian moments for every subpower cutoff \(P(Y)\to\infty\) with \(\log P=o(\log Y)\), and converges weakly for \(P=Y^\theta\) with \(0<\theta<1/2\). More precisely, for fixed integer \(k\ge1\) with \(k\theta<1/2\) the polynomial cutoff has Gaussian moments through \(2k\). The argument uses aggregate HLZ polarization, the source's full declared-length range, and an upper-band bound using \(|D|^{2k}=|D^k|^2\). Additional Dirichlet GRH is unnecessary. These assertions concern the scalar natural weights \(p^{-1/2}\) for cosines. [HLZ journal version, Theorems 4–5 and Propositions 10–11](../../sources/classical63_derivative_moments/hlz2022print.pdf)

## Coefficient hypotheses, effective conductor split, and uniformity

Use \(L_T=\log T\) and declare source length \(y=T^\delta\) with a fixed \(0<\delta<1/2\). Journal Theorem 5, p. 1605, evaluates the shifted product against a quadratic Dirichlet polynomial. Let \(D(s)=\sum_{p\in\mathcal P}p^{-s}\) for a prescribed subset of primes. Every coefficient of \(D^u\) is at most \(u!\), since the coefficient counts ordered permutations of the prime factorization. For fixed \(u,v\), with supports inside \([1,y]\), choose the union of their divisor closures, pad it with a fixed baseline \(A\ge 4(1+u!+v!)\), and use the four arrays \(A{\bf1}_{\rm union}+\sigma[D^u]+\tau[D^v]\). On their common support all coefficients lie between \(3A/4\) and \(5A/4\). Thus \(|a(mn)|\le |a(m)a(n)|\) because the factors of a supported product belong to the support and \((3A/4)^2\ge5A/4\). A uniform divisor/log envelope holds, interpreting the coefficient bound at 1 separately as the source already does in its applications. No count of the support is inserted into the implied constant. Four-sign polarization with factor \(1/8\) cancels the baseline and returns the average of the two ordered bilinear kernels. Under RH and reflection this suffices for the real moments. For an even moment of \(D\), one may instead extract the quadratic kernel of \(D^k\) from the two signed arrays around a padded baseline and the baseline alone.

The ordinary use of Theorem 5 allows the fixed bounds to depend on \(\delta,u,v\) and the chosen saving. Its coefficient estimates depend on the common envelopes, as can be checked in the source's principal Perron estimate (31)–(35) on pp. 1611–1613 and nonprincipal Propositions 10–11 on pp. 1615–1624. In the initial contour and continuous moment use the same first coefficient case of Pratt–Robles Theorem 1.1 as HLZ, p. 1608, uniformly under the fixed coefficient bound. The errors \(T^{3/20}y^{33/20}\), \(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}y^{1/2}\), and the horizontal contour cost \(yT^{1/2+\varepsilon}\) save a fixed power for \(\delta<1/2\). The pointwise oscillatory calculation on pp. 1608–1609 also saves a power. The \((r/k)^{-c}\) summation in its first error contributes \(k^c\) before multiplication by \(k^{-1+\Re\beta}\), leaving a logarithmic factor per supported \(k\). In its central range the reciprocal-distance sum contributes \(O(k\log(Tk)+T^{-1/2})\), leaving \(O(T^{1/2+\varepsilon} y L_T^C)\) after the outer weights. Shift-dependent factors \(n^{O(1/L_T)}\) can be kept in the absolute series, choosing the Cauchy radii small. In the central coefficient estimate the argument is at most \(T^{O(1)}\), so these factors are bounded. Positive and negative reflections and restoration to the exact prefix endpoint use the same coefficient envelope. For aggregate evaluation at a good height, one can bound each \(D^u(\rho)\) by \(O_u(y^{1/2})\), giving \(O(T^{1-\varepsilon})\) for the endpoint by RH and the convexity bound for \(\zeta'\) with real-part radius chosen sufficiently small relative to \(1/2-\delta\). An arbitrary ordinate is within bounded distance of a good height and contains \(O(\log T)\) zeros in that interval. [Pratt–Robles, Theorem 1.1](../../sources/finite_moment63_uniform/pratt_robles_1706.04593v3.pdf) · [Earlier uniform source audit](uniform64_hlz_third_review.md)

The effective small-modulus repair also works at the full declared-length cutoff. Split at \(\eta=(\log T)^K\), with \(K\) fixed after the desired saving. Keep HLZ's Proposition 11 for \(E_2\), without changing its summands. Its bound on p. 1615 is
\[
 O\!\left(L_T^C T\eta^{-1/2+\varepsilon}
       +yT^{1/2+\varepsilon}+y^{4/3}T^{1/3+\varepsilon}
       +y^{1/3+\varepsilon}T^{5/6+\varepsilon}\right).
\]
Its proof on pp. 1619–1624 uses the uniform submultiplicative and divisor envelopes, Heath-Brown's convolution, Pólya–Vinogradov and the hybrid large sieve. The shifted monomials in the convolution have size \(n^{O(1/L_T)}=O(1)\) on the relevant \(n\ll yT\). In Type I, complex shifting is harmless by partial summation, because the interval sits inside a dyadic scale and \(d(n^c)/dn=cn^{c-1}\) for the complex exponents \(c\ll1/L_T\). All terms save any requested power of \(L_T\) after choosing \(\varepsilon>0\) small enough for \(\delta\) and taking \(K\) large. The printed coefficient format is important here. It controls the whole Dirichlet polynomial, and no unbounded arbitrary normalized array is admitted by fiat.

For \(E_1\), use the effective Landau–Page form of FGKMT, Lemma 7.1 and Corollary 6, choosing \(Q=\lceil e^{\sqrt{L_T}}\rceil\) and Perron height \(U=e^{\sqrt{L_T}}\). Since \(\eta\le Q\) eventually, one prime \(\ell_T\), or none, ensures zero-freeness on \(\Re s\ge1-c/\sqrt{L_T}\), \(|\Im s|\le U+2\) for primitive conductors \(q\le\eta\) coprime to \(\ell_T\). The summands on HLZ p. 1615 contain \(a(kq)\). Deleting \(\ell_T\) from the prime band before powering and padding makes each summand for a conductor divisible by \(\ell_T\) vanish exactly. Every denominator \(L(s+\beta,\psi)\) in the character identity on p. 1617 is in the translated zero-free region when the contour is at \(1-\Delta\), \(\Delta=c'/\sqrt{L_T}\) with \(c'\) small. The prior holomorphic-logarithm disk argument gives \(|L|+|1/L|\ll L_T^{C}\) there and on both horizontal edges through \(|\Im|=U\), including all shift permutations. [FGKMT §7, pp. 32–33](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.5029v3) · [Prior disk argument](analytic64_exceptional_prime_clt.md)

The essential support estimate is not a count of the supported integers. In the identity on p. 1617 the finite Dirichlet polynomial \(A(s+\beta,d_1)\) satisfies, by the common bounded coefficients,
\[
 |A(s+\beta,d_1)|\ll y^{(1-\sigma)_+}\log(2y)
\]
throughout the Perron rectangle. Indeed \(m d_1\le y\), \(m^{-\Re\beta}\ll1\), and \(m^{1-\sigma}\le \max(1,y^{1-\sigma})\). All other finite factors of that identity are bounded by \(\tau_R(d)\) with fixed \(R\). Each shifted \(d_i\) factor is bounded because \(d\le y\), the ordered factor count is \(\tau_5(d)\), and the finite Euler products grow at most a constant per prime dividing \(d\) on \(\sigma\ge3/4\). Enlarging \(R\) absorbs that constant prime by prime. At \(x=Tkq/(2\pi d)\gg T\), the left vertical integral therefore costs \(O(x\tau_R(d)L_T^C(y/x)^\Delta)\); since \(y=T^\delta\), the ratio saves \(e^{-c''\sqrt{L_T}}\). The horizontal ones cost \(O(x\tau_R(d)L_T^C/U)\) since \(x^\sigma y^{(1-\sigma)_+}\ll x\). The absolute coefficient bound for the original Perron line is \(O(\tau_5(d)L_T^C)\) by (28), retaining \(n^{C'/L_T}\) in the infinite series and choosing \(1+C_1/L_T\) with \(C_1>C'\). The short sum of the resulting divisor function on radius \(x/\sqrt U\) has the same \(x/\sqrt U\) log cost as the source. One may use the elementary largest-factor argument in the previous scalar audit. These truncation costs also save \(e^{-c''\sqrt{L_T}}\) after possibly reducing \(c''\).

The outer summation remains polylogarithmic even though \(y\) is a power of \(T\). By the Gauss sum bound and HLZ (42), its cost after extracting \(Te^{-c''\sqrt{L_T}}L_T^C\) is bounded by
\[
 \sum_{q\le\eta}\sqrt q\sum_{kq\le y}
 \frac1{\varphi(k)}\sum_{d\mid kq}\frac{(d,k)\tau_R(d)}d
 \ll \eta^{3/2} L_T^{C'} .
\]
For the last inequality expand \((d,k)=\sum_{e\mid(d,k)}\varphi(e)\le\sum_{e\mid(d,k)}e\). Then put \(d=eu\), use \(\tau_R(eu)\le\tau_R(e)\tau_R(u)\), and enlarge the \(u\)-sum to \(u\le y\). This gives \(\tau_{R+1}(k)L_T^R\). The remaining \(k\)-sum is polylogarithmic since \(k/\varphi(k)\le\tau_2(k)\) and the product of the two fixed divisor functions is bounded by a fixed divisor function. This is an independent derivation of the summation mechanism on HLZ p. 1619. The principal Perron part needs no Dirichlet exceptional control and uses the effective zeta strip, retaining the same fixed coefficient envelopes. Thus the aggregate \(O_A(TL_T^{-A})\) shifted remainder is effective after deletion, for an arbitrarily prescribed \(A\), at the original full declared length.

Journal p. 1593 also says that under Dirichlet GRH the small-modulus error can be replaced by \(O(y^{3/2+\varepsilon}T^{1/2+\varepsilon})\) for \(a(n)\ll_\varepsilon n^\varepsilon\). This is a remark about the small-modulus term, so using it alone for the complete error or for arbitrary \(\delta<1/2\) would be an extra inference. The unconditional large-sieve branch and the bounded aggregate coefficients already handle the range used here.

## Relative local quotient and aggregate moments

Let \(h=m/(m,n)\), \(j=n/(m,n)\), and \(\kappa=(m,n)/\sqrt{mn}\). On fixed separated Cauchy circles scaled by \(1/L_T\), the exact local factor ratio for \(p^e\Vert j\) is
\[
 V^e+(U-1)\Bigl(\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}U^i V^{e-1-i}\Bigr)
                 \frac{1-Vz}{1-z},
 \qquad U=p^{-u},\ V=p^{-v},\ z=p^{-1-b}.
\]
Here \(|u|,|v|,|b|\ll1/L_T\). For \(h,j\le y\), the bound \(p^{eO(1/L_T)}=O_\delta(1)\) gives a change from one of size \(O(e\log p/L_T)\). Summing and multiplying the factors gives \(O(\log(hj)/L_T)\), since the exponential of that total error is bounded throughout the declared length. Each main term retains the weight \(1/\operatorname{lcm}(m,n)\). This also holds for the elementary explicit factors in (5), and for both orders in (24). Comparing against the exact untwisted kernel before the auxiliary and outer differentiations gives a model-relative error at most \(C\kappa\log(hj)/L_T\) per character after normalization. It does not improve to a uniformly vanishing relative error at a fixed positive power cutoff.

For the full aggregate of \(D^u\overline D^v\), the coefficient of the model character is a product of bounded Dirichlet coefficients and \((mn)^{-1/2}\). The sum of these weights times \(\kappa\) is the corresponding model moment, since Poisson prime phases of radius \(p^{-1/2}\) have nonnegative character expectation \(\kappa\). Thus the normalized difference from the model has two terms: a relative-error sum bounded by \(C (u+v)(\log P)/L_T\) times the positive raw moment, and a single \(O_A(L_T^{-A})\) after enlarging \(A\) before Cauchy. There is no additional multiplication by the number of characters or by \(\sqrt{mn}\), since the source remainder is used on the full quadratic form before expanding its main term. In the centered cosine power the binomial centering costs powers of \(H_P\) only. The positive model raw moments are \(O_j((1+H_P)^j)\) by independence and bounded centered coordinates. The product model has all Gaussian moments as \(P\to\infty\), because its standardized third and higher cumulants tend to zero, with the first two cumulants exactly 0 and 1.

For an effective estimate, at each \(T\ge Y\) remove the one prime chosen at \(T\), using the same \(\ell_T\) through a prescribed finite maximal degree. Restore it at the level of the centered normalized variable before partial summation. The omitted centered summand has magnitude at most \(2/\sqrt{\ell_T}\), and the requisite even bounds for the remaining statistic and Hölder's inequality give restoration cost \(O_k(V_P^{-1/2})\) in the \(k\)-th moment when the corresponding higher even moment is available. In the upper-band argument below one can restore it directly in the \(L^{2k}\) bound using the triangle inequality, with cost \(O(V_P^{-1/2})\) in the norm. The final full statistic is fixed across prefix heights \(T\ge Y\) even if the \(\ell_T\) vary. Partial summation against \((t^2+1/4)^{-1}\) with Gonek's untwisted prefix asymptotic therefore transfers uniform normalized prefix errors and moment upper bounds to the strict two-sided tail. ## Near-power truncation and weak polynomial cutoff

Put \(L=\log Y\) and \(P_*=\exp(L\exp(-(\log L)^{1/3}))\). For any fixed degree, the moment support below \(P_*\) has length \(Y^{o(1)}\) and the main comparison error \((1+H)^k(\log P_*)/L\) tends to zero. This proves the all-moment result for \(P\le P_*\), assuming \(P\to\infty\) there. In particular it includes \(\exp(L^a)\) for every fixed \(a<1\).

For \(P>P_*\) and \(P\le Y^{1/2-\varepsilon}\), the discarded band has \(H_{\rm band}=\sum_{P_*<p\le P}p^{-1}=O(1+(\log L)^{1/3})\) by Mertens, while \(V_P\asymp\log L\) and \(V_{P_*}/V_P\to1\). If \(D_{\rm band}=\sum_{P_*<p\le P}p^{-s}\), then
\[
 |W_P-W_{P_*}-H_{\rm band}|^{2k}
 \le 2^{2k-1}\bigl(|D_{\rm band}|^{2k}+H_{\rm band}^{2k}\bigr).
\]
Apply the aggregate quadratic formula to \(D_{\rm band}^k\), of length \(P^k\). The exact local ratio is bounded uniformly since the declared length is at most \(T^\delta\); the model raw moment \(E_\star|D_{\rm band}|^{2k}\) is \(O_k((1+H_{\rm band})^{2k})\), by expanding the centered independent real and imaginary components. Consequently the normalized band cost in \(L^{2k}\) tends to zero whenever \(P^k\le Y^\delta\) for fixed \(\delta<1/2\). The estimate already yields weak convergence for every fixed \(\theta<1/2\) by taking \(k=1\) and Slutsky. It yields moment convergence through \(2k\) for each permitted \(k\) by Hölder and the moment bounds below \(P_*\). For subpower \(P\), every fixed \(k\) is permitted, giving the claimed all-moment extension. The moments available for a fixed positive \(\theta\) form a finite list by this argument. No endpoint claim at \(\theta=1/2\) is made.

## Obstacles to other substitutions

An error described only by \(O_A(L^{-A})\) for every fixed \(A\) need not absorb \(P^{k/2}\) from an individual-character expansion. For example \(E(L)=\exp(-(\log L)^2)\) satisfies every such bound, but \(P^{k/2}E(L)\to\infty\) at \(P=\exp(L^a)\) for every \(a>0\). Tracking the effective individual-character Perron bound \(e^{-c\sqrt L}\) and coarse support powers gives the range \(a<1/2\) by direct multiplication. The boundary \(a=1/2\) depends on actual competing constants and the degree, and that argument gives no all-moment boundary conclusion. The aggregate quadratic formula avoids this multiplication for the scalar weights. For arbitrary normalized first-harmonic arrays, the corresponding Dirichlet coefficients are the prime weights times \(\sqrt p\), and need not have a bounded envelope. For example the normalized coordinate with complex weight 1 at a single increasing prime has Dirichlet coefficient \(\sqrt p\). Even the distributed band array with weights \(1/\sqrt N\) at the \(N\) primes in \([P/2,P]\) has coefficients of size \(\sqrt{\log P}\), not fixed bounded size. The bounded-envelope argument must be reconsidered for such families.

## Leading prime correction at a power scale

The ordered local ratios at \((h,j)=(p,1),(1,p)\) are \(e^{-vx}\) and
\[
 e^{-wx}+(e^{-ux}-1)\frac{1-e^{-wx}z}{1-z},
 \qquad x=\frac{\log p}{L_T},
\]
on the scaled \((u,v,w)\) circles. Their average differs from \((e^{-vx}+e^{-wx}+e^{-ux}-1)/2\) by \(O(x^2/p)\), using \((e^{-ux}-1)(1-e^{-wx})z/(1-z)\). This uniform sharpening matters if these errors are summed over all primes down to 2. The leading residue multiplier in HLZ (24) is \(L_T v/((u+v)(v+w))\); Laurent expansion of the zeta factors is uniform on the separated circles. The three tuples \((u,v,w,\lambda)\), with \(\lambda\) the scaled time exponent, are \((a,b,c,0),(-b,-a,c,-a-b),(a,-c,-b,-b-c)\). Each time integral, after division by \(T\), replaces its time power by \(e^\lambda+O(1/L_T)\). The extra \(\log(t/(2\pi))\) in (5) similarly contributes \(L_T(1+O(1/L_T))\).

Under simultaneous rotation of the three Cauchy variables, only homogeneous degree three in (24) contributes to the mixed outer and auxiliary coefficient. Thus the degree-four exponential terms suffice before division by the linear denominator degree remaining after the numerator \(v\). In (5) the corresponding degree-two mixed coefficient uses the cubic exponential term. The symbolic check yields the \(\alpha\beta\) coefficients \((x-1)^2(x+2)/6\) from (5) and \((2x-3)/12\) from (24) with its reflection. The common untwisted coefficient is \(1/12\), giving \(F(x)=1-4x+2x^3\). This checks directly that the \(\kappa\)-relative main term need not approach one for an individual logarithmic prime scale of fixed positive ratio \(x\).

For example when \(P=Y^\theta\), \(\theta<1/2\), aggregate first-moment polarization at each \(T\ge Y\) gives
\[
 E_T W_P-H_P=\sum_{p\le P}\frac{F(\log p/L_T)-1}{p}+o(1)
\]
with uniform \(o(1)\). The Laurent remainder is \(O(H_P/L_T)\) and the local approximation error is \(O(\sum_{p\le P}(\log p)^2/(p^2 L_T^2))\), both \(o(1)\). Arbitrary source log savings still apply to the aggregate remainder. The prime number theorem and partial summation give \(\sum_{p\le P}(\log p)^j/p \sim (\log P)^j/j\) for \(j=1,3\). Across \(Y\le T\le Y e^{\sqrt{\log Y}}\) this yields the constant \(-4\theta+(2/3)\theta^3\) uniformly. For larger \(T\) the displayed sum remains bounded, and its contribution to strict-tail integration is \(o(1)\) compared with the total tail mass, by the untwisted prefix asymptotic. The strict-tail mean therefore has the same limiting drift, which disappears after division by \(\sqrt{V_P}\). This calculation uses the ordinary possibly ineffective version of HLZ. Applying exceptional-prime deletion to the unnormalized first moment would require tracking the size of the omitted term, rather than using a normalized restoration argument.
