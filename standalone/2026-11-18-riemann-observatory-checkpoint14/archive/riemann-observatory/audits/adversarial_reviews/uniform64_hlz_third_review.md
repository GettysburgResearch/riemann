# Third audit of the HLZ growing-block specialization

17 November 2026. Under RH, the proposed two-sided normalized phase error \(O_D(1/\log Y)\) and prime covariance operator error \(O_D((\log\log Y)^2/\log Y)\) hold throughout \(m,n\le(\log Y)^D\) for each fixed \(D>0\). The proof uses the exact untwisted tail as its denominator and retains the reduced-rational logarithm \(\log(mn/(m,n)^2)\) in the error. The cutoff from the source proof is ineffective.

I checked the journal rendering of Theorem 5 and its coefficient conjugations, as well as the arXiv version. Journal Theorem 5 and (24), p. 1605, correspond to arXiv Theorem 2 and (5)–(8), pp. 3–4. Journal Proposition 10 and Lemma 12, pp. 1615–1619, correspond to arXiv Proposition 7 and Lemma 9 in §§4.4–5. The argument below uses the explicit principal-character residues (24), without a passage through the smoothed diagonal formula or Lemma 6. ## Uniform shifted remainder

Set \(L=\log T\) and declare length \(y=T^{1/4}\), with all array entries beyond \(L^D\) zero. For each \(m,n\le L^D\), use \(a_{\sigma,\tau}(d)=8\mathbf1_{d\mid m\text{ or }d\mid n}+\sigma\mathbf1_{d=m}+\tau\mathbf1_{d=n}\) for \(\sigma,\tau=\pm1\). The divisor-closed support and \(6\le a(d)\le10\) on it give exact \(|a(uv)|\le |a(u)a(v)|\), including when \(m=n\) or one index equals 1. Use the simultaneous envelopes \(|a(d)|\le10\) and \(|a(d)|\le \tau_{16}(d)\log d\) for \(d\ge2\), with \(|a(1)|\le10\) separately. The convention at 1 is necessary in applying the printed bound, since the source's own polynomials have nonzero initial coefficients. Any divisor/log estimate used in the proof works with \(\log(2d)\).

- On pp. 1607–1608, the horizontal edges are \(O(yT^{1/2+\varepsilon})\) from the uniform coefficient envelope and standard good-height bounds. For the continuous part, §4.1 gives the sharp-interval version of Pratt–Robles, Theorem 1.1, first coefficient case. Its errors \(T^{3/20}y^{33/20}\) and \(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}y^{1/2}\) have exponents \(9/16\) and \(5/8+\varepsilon\) here. The general-case coefficient input \(a(d)\ll_\varepsilon d^\varepsilon\) has one constant in this family. Integration by parts and the Stirling correction on pp. 1607–1608 cost at most logarithmic factors. [Pratt–Robles v3, §1.2](../../sources/finite_moment63_uniform/pratt_robles_1706.04593v3.pdf)
- In §4.2, pp. 1608–1609, the complex \(b\) from (28) satisfies \(|b(r)r^{-\beta}|\le10\tau_5(r)r^{C_0/L}\), where \(C_0\) can be as small as needed by choosing fixed small shift radii. The divisor-only bound in §4.2 is immediate on the range \(r\le T^{O(1)}\); absolute series on the contour can use the global bound with \(r^{C_0/L}\) retained. Explicitly, apply the pointwise oscillatory integral estimate reproduced in Ng, Lemma 4.1, to each \(r/k\) in (27) on the line \(\Re s=c=1+1/L-\Re\beta\). Moving the upper and lower endpoints vertically by \(\Im\beta\) costs \(O(T^{1/2}L^{C_1})\) after summation by absolute convergence and the gamma-factor bound. For the pointwise error term \(T^{c-1/2}(r/k)^{-c}\), the \(r\)-sum is \(O(k^c T^{c-1/2}\zeta(c-C_0/L)^5)\). For the second term \(T^{c+1/2}(r/k)^{-c}/(|T-2\pi r/k|+\sqrt T)\), the same argument covers \(r/k\) outside \([T/(4\pi),T/\pi]\). Inside that interval, \(T^{c+1/2}(r/k)^{-c}\ll \sqrt T\), the coefficients are \(O_\varepsilon(T^\varepsilon)\) for \(k\le L^D\), and the denominator reciprocals sum to \(O(k\log(Tk)+T^{-1/2})\) by grouping successive distances to \(Tk/(2\pi)\). The outer factors \(|a(k)|k^{-1+\Re\beta}\) and the polylogarithmic support give \(O_{D,\varepsilon}(T^{1/2+\varepsilon}L^{C_2})\) in total. This supplies the uniform Lemma 7 step without requiring a global shift-independent divisor bound. [Ng 2007, Lemma 4.1](../../sources/classical63_derivative_moments/ng2007.pdf)
- The principal-character Dirichlet series and residue algebra are in §4.3, pp. 1610–1615. In the original sums all indices of \(a\) are \(\le L^D\). The Perron parameter \(x=Tk/(2\pi h)\) therefore has \(\log x=L+O_D(\log L)\). On the initial Perron line the coefficient sum is bounded by a fixed power of \(L\), times \(\tau_4(d)\) in the notation of pp. 1612–1613, by the absolute series of \(\tau_4\) with exponent \(1+\Omega(1/L)\). With \(U=\exp(c_1\sqrt L)\) and short interval radius \(x/\sqrt U\), Lemma 9 gives \(O(x L^{C_3}\tau_4(d)/\sqrt U)\) by the short divisor-sum bound on p. 1612. The contour at \(1-c_2/\sqrt L\) lies in the shifted zeta zero-free region. The vertical bound from (32) and horizontal \(1/U\), with the same fixed coefficient and divisor envelopes, give \(T L^{C_4}\exp(-c_3\sqrt L)\) after summation. There are only polylogarithmically many outer indices and their size factors are polynomial in \(L\). The three crossed numerator poles on these circles are \(1,1-\alpha-\beta,1-\beta-\lambda\), with \(\lambda\) the auxiliary shift. They are distinct for the radii below. Their sum is (24) before auxiliary differentiation.
- In §4.4, p. 1615, each summand of \(E\) contains \(a(kq)\), so every contributing primitive conductor \(q\) is at most \(L^D\), and \(E_2\) is empty at \(\eta=L^{D+1}\). Proposition 10, pp. 1615–1619, is uniform here. To see the complex-shift and exceptional-zero issue without estimating an exceptional residue, take \(U=\exp(c_5\sqrt L)\). The usual Dirichlet zero-free region, with Siegel's bound \(1-\beta_{\mathrm{exc}}\gg_\varepsilon q^{-\varepsilon}\) for \(\varepsilon D<1/2\), gives a zero-free rectangle \(\sigma\ge1-c_6/\sqrt L,\ |t|\le U+1\) for these \(q\), including after the \(O(1/L)\) shifts. Shrinking \(c_6\) gives room for the usual polylogarithmic bounds on \(L(s,\psi)\) and its reciprocal throughout the displaced rectangle. The possible exceptional zero lies farther left than the line of integration, so its location and residue after a complex shift are immaterial. The identity on p. 1617 and (38) bound the integrand using \(A(s+\beta,d_1)\), a polynomial supported on \(m d_1\le L^D\), and the divisor factors from \(d\mid kq\). Those factors, the polynomial, and the number of outer indices and characters are all bounded by fixed powers of \(L\). At \(x=Tkq/(2\pi d)\) one has \(x\gg T\) and \(\log x=L+O_D(\log L)\). The same Perron error estimate, using \(\tau_5(md)\le \tau_5(m)\tau_5(d)\) and the short divisor-sum bound used after Lemma 12, gives \(xL^{C_7}\exp(-c_7\sqrt L)\). The shifted vertical and horizontal integrals obey this order as well upon changing constants. Even bounding the Gauss sums and finite coefficients in \(E_1\) by fixed powers of \(L\) gives \(TL^{C_8}\exp(-c_8\sqrt L)\). This yields every fixed log saving. Siegel's theorem makes this argument ineffective.

Thus for each fixed \(M,D\) the theorem remainder is \(O_{M,D}(TL^{-M})\) on the stated complex shift region for all four arrays. The auxiliary derivative in (24) costs a further \(O(L)\) on the corresponding circles, which can be absorbed by increasing the log saving before differentiating. In the use of the finished Theorem 5, its remainder already includes that differentiation.

## Coefficient cancellation and which phase is recovered

In the rendered journal Theorem 5, the third term is \(\overline{I(\bar\beta,\bar\alpha,T)}\) and \(I\) uses \(a(gh)\overline{a(gk)}\). For real coefficients the explicit kernels respect complex conjugation, so the reflected term reduces to \(I(\beta,\alpha,T)\). Multiplying the four evaluations by \(\sigma\tau/8\) and adding cancels every contribution involving the padding and leaves the *average* of the two ordered monomial terms. This applies to the entire source main term, including the reflected term. The sum of four remainders has the same uniform order. Under RH the two zeta derivatives give \(|\zeta'(\rho)|^2\), and the coefficient after differentiation is \(\Re(m^{-\rho}n^{-\bar\rho})\). Reflection of the ordinates conjugates the phase, so the two-sided numerator is twice this real positive-height numerator. Multiple zeros have derivative zero.

The real-array derivation by itself gives only this real part. The adjacent review's optional one-sided complex variant can use the actual journal formula because complex conjugation of the reflected \(I\) exchanges the coefficient slots, so the result is still the diagonal of a sesquilinear form. Reflected arguments and outer conjugation also preserve holomorphy in the shifts. In addition to the real cross term, polarizing with \(e_m\pm i e_n\) recovers \((H_{mn}-H_{nm})/(2i)\) for the ordered shifted bilinear values \(H_{mn}\). The same bounds apply to either ordered kernel, including the exchanged slots. No version of the two-sided result needs this additional complex-coefficient argument.

## Arithmetic on the differentiation contours

Put \(g=(m,n)\), \(h=m/g\), \(j=n/g\), and \(w=1/(ghj)\). The real-polarized kernel is the average of the two ordered kernels \((h,j)\) and \((j,h)\), each with scalar weight \(w\). Compare with \(w\) times the untwisted kernel, using \(|\alpha|=2c/L,\ |\beta|=3c/L,\ |\lambda|=c/L\) for a constant \(c>0\) small enough. In (24), each numerator zeta argument differs from 1 by at least \(c/L\) and at most \(O(c/L)\) throughout the auxiliary disk, for both orders of the outer shifts. The reciprocal zeta factor is holomorphic there, including when its argument is 1. The bare zeta quotient is \(O(L)\). The terms in (5) also have bounded denominators in this sense.

For an ordered local ratio use \(U=p^{-u}, V=p^{-v}, z=p^{-1-b}\) for shifts \(u,v,b\) of modulus at most \(\delta=O(1/L)\). The coefficients of \(F(z)=(1-z)/[(1-Uz)(1-Vz)]\) are \(f_r=V^r+(U-1)\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}U^iV^{r-1-i}\), by (6). For \(e\ge1\), splitting the sum in \(f_{r+e}\) at \(r\) gives the identity
\[
f_{r+e}=V^e f_r+(U-1)U^r \sum_{i=0}^{e-1}U^iV^{e-1-i}.
\]
Dividing the resulting generating series by \(F(z)\) proves that the local ratio in (24) is \(V^e+(U-1)(\sum_{i=0}^{e-1}U^iV^{e-1-i})(1-Vz)/(1-z)\). Here \(|z|\le p^{-1+\delta}\), so the quotient is bounded uniformly. Also \(|V^e-1|\le \delta e\log p\, p^{e\delta}\) and \(|U-1|\le\delta\log p\,p^\delta\). Since \(p^e\le j\le L^D\), the local ratio differs from 1 by \(O(\delta e\log p)\), uniformly even at \(p=2\). Summing over \(p^e\Vert j\) and using the product perturbation bound \(\exp(\sum_p|r_p|)-1\) gives \(O(\delta\log j)\), and \(h^{-b}\) adds \(O(\delta\log h)\). The total arithmetic multiplier differs from 1 by \(O(\log(hj)/L)\); for \(h=j=1\) it equals 1 exactly.

The small powers of \(t/(2\pi)\) integrate in absolute value to \(O(T)\), or \(O(TL)\) with the factor \(\log(t/(2\pi))\), by direct integration on \((0,1]\) and \([1,T]\). Thus the change of \(J/w\) in (5) is \(O(TL\log(hj))\), since each zeta pole factor is \(O(L)\) and each monomial factor differs from 1 by \(O(\log(hj)/L)\). The change of the prederivative integrand in (24), divided by \(w\), integrates to \(O(T\log(hj))\). Auxiliary Cauchy differentiation makes the change in \(I/w\) at most \(O(TL\log(hj))\).

Apply outer Cauchy directly to the difference of finite shifted zero sums. This function is holomorphic in the product disk, irrespective of poles in individual main-term pieces. On the product circles the bounds just established apply, and the outer derivatives cost \(O(L^2)\). Writing \(A_{mn}(T)\) for the positive-height real derivative sum, the outcome is
\[
A_{mn}(T)-w A_{11}(T)
 \ll_{M,D} wTL^3\log(hj)+TL^{2-M}
\]
in absolute value. A good contour height within bounded distance of an arbitrary endpoint changes each derivative sum by \(O(T^{7/8}\log T)\) under RH, from the \(O(\log T)\) unit-window zero count, \(|\zeta'(\rho)|\ll T^{7/16}\) by convexity and Cauchy, and monomial modulus at most 1. This is within the displayed remainder for every fixed \(M\), including when the strict-tail boundary is at a zero.

## Strict tails and dimension control

For every \(t\ge Y\) the pair remains admissible at \(t\). With \(f(t)=(t^2+1/4)^{-1}\) and \(C(t)=A_{mn}(t)-wA_{11}(t)\), the strict-tail difference is \(-f(Y)C(Y)-\int_Y^\infty f'(t)C(t)\,dt\) on the positive half-axis. The corresponding two-sided quantity is twice this. Since \(\int_Y^\infty t^{-2}(\log t)^b\,dt\ll_b Y^{-1}(\log Y)^b\) for every fixed real \(b\), its two component errors are \(O(w(\log Y)^3\log(hj)/Y)\) and \(O((\log Y)^{2-M}/Y)\). The exact untwisted tail \(B_Y\) has asymptotic \((\log Y)^4/(12\pi Y)\) by the untwisted Gonek moment. Multiplying by \(mn/B_Y\) gives
\[
\frac{mn\,\mathcal T_Y(e_m,e_n)}{B_Y}
=g+O_{B,D}\!\left(\frac{g\log(hj)}{\log Y}+(\log Y)^{-B}\right)
\]
by taking \(M>2D+B+2\). The diagonal is exactly \(m\). Dividing by \(\sqrt{mn}\), using \(\log(hj)/\sqrt{hj}\le2/e\), gives the claimed uniform *absolute* phase error. A uniform relative phase error of order \(1/\log Y\) does not follow from this estimate. [Gonek, Corollary 1](../../sources/classical63_lfunctions/gonek1984.pdf)

For primes \(p,q\le P\le (\log Y)^D\), set \(Q_p=p e_p-1\) and \(Z_p=Q_p/\sqrt{p-1}\). Using the exact monomial diagonal and \(g=1\) in off-diagonal prime entries shows that the second-moment matrix differs from \(I\) by entries bounded by \(C_D\log(2P)/[\log Y\sqrt{(p-1)(q-1)}]\). The same bound holds after subtracting the mean product, since each mean before division by \(\sqrt{p-1}\) is \(O_D(\log(2P)/\log Y)\). For any unit vectors \(u,v\), the bilinear form of the error is at most the coefficient \(C_D\log(2P)/\log Y\) times the product of two sums of the form \(\sum_p |u_p|/\sqrt{p-1}\). Cauchy–Schwarz bounds this product by \(\sum_{p\le P}(p-1)^{-1}\ll\log(2P)\), using just the harmonic series. The covariance operator error is therefore \(O_D((\log(2P))^2/\log Y)\). Scaling instead by the actual standard deviations gives the same order by diagonal conjugation, since the relative variance errors satisfy the diagonal estimate. This controls phase coefficients indexed by admissible reduced rationals and prime-block covariances. The fixed-\(Y\) all-prime obstruction remains: along consecutive primes with ratio tending to 1, continuity of the fixed-tail characteristic function gives values tending to 1, while \(1/\sqrt{pq}\) tends to zero.
