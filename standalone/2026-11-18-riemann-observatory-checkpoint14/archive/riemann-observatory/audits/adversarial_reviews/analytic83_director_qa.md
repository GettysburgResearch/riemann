## Required scope correction

At review the joint note inferred strict negativity for “nonnegative nonzero \(f,g\)” under the hypothesis of bounded Riemann-integrable weights. Such a function can be nonzero on a null set. Take \(f=\mathbf1_{\{x_0\}}\) at a single positive \(x_0\), and a positive interval sine test on a disjoint band. The weighted cosine sum has at most one term, bounded by \(Y^{-x_0/2}\), and tends to zero. The mixed cubic formula correctly gives zero. Thus its inference of dependence fails for this example.

For \(f,g\ge0\), the conclusion with \(h=g\) holds when each has strictly positive integral. Then \(\int f(x)(x^2-2)\,dx<0\) and \(\int g(y)y\,dy>0\), and the negative mixed cubic is inherited by any weak subsequential vector limit because of fourth uniform integrability. Independence would force it to vanish. The original prime81 proof uses the proper nonzero-integral qualifier. The exact formula remains valid for all bounded Riemann-integrable weights in the specified common band. [Original weighted proof](prime81_joint_third.md)
