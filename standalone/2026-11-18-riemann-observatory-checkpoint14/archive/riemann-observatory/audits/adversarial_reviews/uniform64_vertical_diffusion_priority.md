# Antecedents and source versions for the Selberg vertical-shift diffusion

18 November 2026. The central Gaussian variance, degree-one vertical shifts, compensated characteristic pairs, and infinitesimal Poisson-to-Gaussian limit are established antecedents. In the primary sources checked here, I found no explicit statement combining them into the weakly continuous family for \(F_\tau(s)=F(s+i\tau)\) through a zero crossing, with the translated \(K_\tau\) and a quantitative exponent comparison. This is a bounded search result. Publication priority of the combined application remains unassessed. [Vertical-shift derivation](uniform64_vertical_shift_diffusion.md) · [Earlier central-drift adjudication](director_central_drift_round10.md)

## Direct arithmetic and probabilistic antecedents

- Kaczorowski–Perelli, *On the structure of the Selberg class, I*, Acta Math. 182 (1999), 207–241, state the degree-one family \(L(s+i\theta,\chi)\) in the introduction and Theorem 3, p. 212. The nonprincipal character case and the special zeta case are already distinguished. Thus the use of a nonprincipal degree-one vertical translate within the class is classical. More generally, for pole-free \(F\in\mathcal S\), the defining axioms immediately preserve the class under translation. Substituting in the gamma factors gives the chosen completion and root-number phase \(Q^{-i\tau}\), \(\omega Q^{-2i\tau}\). The phase does not affect the law. I did not find a law-continuity statement in the degree-classification source. [Kaczorowski–Perelli original article](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02392574) · [Public PDF copy inspected](https://archive.ymsc.tsinghua.edu.cn/pacm_download/117/6577-11511_2006_Article_BF02392574.pdf)
- Nakamura–Suzuki, arXiv:2307.02027v1, §§2.3–2.4, equations (2.7)–(2.8), Theorem 2.2 and the paragraph immediately following it, explicitly identify the central multiplicity \(m_0\) with Gaussian variance and the noncentral signed atoms with the finite Lévy measure. They give a static convolution law and central examples in §3. This is the closest direct antecedent. The required regular-part convention at a central zero is already addressed in the preceding director note. The inspected six-page version contains no varying-\(\tau\) law, centered diverging-rate moving atom, or crossing limit. [Nakamura–Suzuki v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.02027v1)
- Suzuki, arXiv:2209.12832, §5.1, defines the static counting measure \(\tau_F=\sum_{\gamma}m_\gamma\delta_{-\gamma}\) under GRH and uses \(\int(1+x^2)^{-1}\,d\tau_F(x)<\infty\). Thus the static finite measure \(K_F=(1+x^2)^{-1}\tau_F\) is already implicit and exact in that source, including the central atom. Suzuki's *Analytic theories around the simplest screw*, arXiv:2308.11860v2, Theorem 4.1, (4.1)–(4.2), and Appendix B, give the corresponding general spectral-measure and Lévy–Khintchine normalization, citing Kreĭn–Langer Theorem 5.1. Appendix B's example has a Gaussian convolved with two fixed Poisson terms. It illustrates the static decomposition and does not move the underlying zeros. [Suzuki Selberg-class screw source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.12832v2) · [Suzuki screw-function survey v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.11860v2) · [Kreĭn–Langer bibliography](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00020-013-2091-z)
- Lindner–Pan–Sato, arXiv:1701.02400v1, §1 (1.5)–(1.8) and Theorem 4.2, give the location change between truncations, the finite characteristic pair \(a\delta_0+(1\wedge x^2)\nu\), and its equivalence with weak convergence for a continuous truncation. Theorem 4.2 credits Gnedenko and cites Gnedenko–Kolmogorov §19, Theorem 1. The finite-measure representation in this specific application uses \(x^2/(1+x^2)\) instead of \(1\wedge x^2\). These are equivalent through multiplication by the bounded continuous positive ratio \(r(x)=(1+x^2)(1\wedge x^2)/x^2\), with \(r(0)=1\) and \(1\le r\le2\). Khartov, arXiv:2204.13667v1, eq. (1) and Theorem 3, uses exactly the \(x^2/(1+x^2)\) spectral-measure weighting, with sine rather than rational truncation. The sine-versus-rational shift correction is integration of a bounded continuous kernel against the finite measure, because the truncations differ by \(O(x^3)\) at zero. Hence this probability mechanism does not depend on the choice between the checked equivalent representations. [Lindner–Pan–Sato, §§1, 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.02400v1) · [Khartov, (1), Theorem 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.13667v1)
- Durrett's *Probability: Theory and Examples*, checked 2010 source PDF, Example 3.4.4 p. 109, gives the standard normal approximation of a centered Poisson variable for arbitrary positive rate tending to infinity. Section 3.8, p. 146, gives an explicit infinitesimal-jump compound-Poisson approximation to a Gaussian. The crossing's single signed jump, centered at rate \(m/\delta^2\), is this standard normal approximation under multiplication by \(-\operatorname{sgn}(\delta)\sqrt m\). Its characteristic expansion can also be calculated directly. This limit is a probability antecedent, irrespective of the arithmetic source of the shift. [Durrett, Example 3.4.4 and §3.8](https://www.stat.rice.edu/~dobelman/courses/texts/qualify/Prob.Theory.Examples.Durrett.2010.pdf)

The translated finite measure and compensated drift follow directly once these static ingredients are put together. With \(x_\gamma(\tau)=-\gamma-\tau\) and under GRH,
\[
 K_\tau=a_\tau\delta_0+\frac{x^2}{1+x^2}\nu_\tau(dx)
       =\sum_\gamma \frac{m_\gamma}{1+(\gamma+\tau)^2}\delta_{x_\gamma(\tau)}.
\]
Every term acts continuously on bounded continuous tests. On a compact \(\tau\)-interval its large-\(\gamma\) mass is dominated by a fixed multiple of \(m_\gamma/|\gamma|^2\), so the finite measure varies weakly continuously even as an atom reaches zero. It need not vary in total variation, since an atom changing position need not approach the limit in that topology. For square-compensated truncation \(h(x)=x/(1+x^2)\), \(c_\tau=b_\tau+\int h\,d\nu_\tau\), with \(b_\tau\) the uncompensated shift. The raw difference formula then gives
\[
 c_\tau-c_0=\sum_\gamma m_\gamma[f(\gamma+\tau)-f(\gamma)],\qquad
 f(u)=\frac{u}{1+u^2}.
\]
The term across a central \(u=0\) is filled continuously, and the differences are absolutely summable locally uniformly. The \(m/\delta^2\) Poisson jump and \(m/\delta\) shift combine to \(-\delta[\mathrm{Pois}(m/\delta^2)-m/\delta^2]\). Its limiting Gaussian variance is \(m\), which also equals the limiting \(K_\tau\)-mass at zero contributed by that root. This is the added moving-zero interpretation, compared with the static arithmetic interpretation of Theorem 2.2.

## Related primary factorizations and scope of search

- Polson, arXiv:1806.07964v7, equations (3)–(5) and §2 Theorem 1, factorizes the reciprocal of \(\xi\) in a Laplace variable. Under RH the central Thorin atoms are indexed by squared positive ordinates, with exponential/gamma contributions, rather than the signed atomic jumps of the screw exponent. Polson's later current arXiv:1804.10043v9, Theorem 12, Lemma 14, and §§6–7, pursues the Hadamard/Wald duality. Its Theorem 30 uses ratios \(\Lambda(\alpha,\chi)/\Lambda(\alpha+\sqrt s,\chi)\) with real basepoint \(\alpha>1\) and an absolutely continuous positive-line Thorin measure built from Poisson kernels. Remark 32 treats passage toward a zero line as \(\alpha\) varies. This is a change of real basepoint in a different transform. Those statements do not give the signed-ordinate shift \(x=-\gamma-\tau\) at a critical-line zero or the compensated law above. This comparison addresses what is stated in the inspected sources and does not certify their stronger global claims. [Polson GGC v7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.07964v7) · [Polson Wald/Thorin v9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.10043v9)
- Ostrovsky, arXiv:1402.6163, §7 Theorem 7.1, constructs a positive-variable family from sums of scaled gamma laws and a Barnes-beta factor, recovering \(\xi\) through a limit of Mellin-transform combinations. Its parameter limit is not a vertical translate of a Selberg completion, and its underlying law is not the signed zero-ordinate jump law. [Ostrovsky, §7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1402.6163)
- I also searched the current Suzuki sources arXiv:2308.11860v2, 2606.09096v4 and 2610.32704v1 for moving or translated zero jumps, Poisson limits and compensated shifts. The first treats general screw representations and fixed examples. The latter two concern Weil forms, model-space operators, and localization or spectral convergence, rather than weak continuity of a varying arithmetic Lévy law. Searches paired the titles and author names with “vertical shift”, “translated zeros”, “central zero”, “Poisson”, “Gaussian”, “diffusion limit”, and “canonical measure”. No exact moving-zero result surfaced in this checked scope. [Weil quadratic form v4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.09096v4) · [Spectral convergence v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32704v1)

The rendered current v2 §5.1 displays an outer \(+iC_Ft\), the measure \(\tau_F=\sum m_\gamma\delta_{-\gamma}\), and the coefficient \(C_F=B_F+\sum_{\gamma\ne0}m_\gamma/[\gamma(1+\gamma^2)]\). Compare using the exact same \(B_F\) from (2.5)–(2.6), whose drift term in (1.1) is \(-iB_Ft\). Each noncentral atom in the compensated integrand contributes the extra linear term \(+it\,m_\gamma/[\gamma(1+\gamma^2)]\), since its location is \(-\gamma\). Therefore compatibility with (1.1) requires
\[
 C_F=-B_F-\sum_{\gamma\ne0}\frac{m_\gamma}{\gamma(1+\gamma^2)}
\]
when the outside sign is \(+iC_Ft\). Equivalently one may retain the printed coefficient and use outer \(-iC_Ft\). A check with \(E(z)=z-\delta\), real nonzero \(\delta\), gives \(B_F=-1/\delta\) and required \(C_F=\delta/(1+\delta^2)\). This simple test is algebraic rather than an example asserted to be in the Selberg class. The corrected shift formula shows why the canonical drift is continuous, while a global reversal of its sign would change the phase unless the drift vanishes.
