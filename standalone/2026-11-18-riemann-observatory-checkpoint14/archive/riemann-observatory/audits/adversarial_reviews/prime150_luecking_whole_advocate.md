## The theorem and its proof

On the unit disk use normalized area \(dm=dx\,dy/\pi\) and \(A^p=\mathcal O\cap L^p(dm)\). For \(0<q<p<\infty\), set \(s=p/(p-q)\). For a positive Borel measure \(\mu\) and fixed pseudohyperbolic radius \(0<r<1\), let \(k_r(z)=\mu(D_r(z))/m(D_r(z))\). The bounded embedding \(A^p\to L^q(\mu)\) holds iff \(k_r\in L^s(dm)\). Section 1 assumes finite positive Borel measures. The theorem statement leaves finiteness implicit, and testing the embedding on \(1\) forces it. Sufficiency also implies it by applying the Tonelli bound to \(1\). For necessity the proof truncates to \(|z|<R\) and passes monotonically to \(R\uparrow1\). Norm expressions for exponents below one are quasi-norms. The earlier \(0<p\le q\) range, including equality, is cited in the introduction. The proof here is for strict \(q<p\). The separate radius lemma covers \(1\le s\le\infty\). Neither \(p=\infty\) nor \(q=0\) enters the main theorem.

The Euclidean radius of \(D_r(z)\) is \(r(1-|z|^2)/(1-r^2|z|^2)\), and its normalized area is the square of that expression. A disk centered at \(z\) with a fixed fraction of that radius fits inside \(D_r(z)\). Subharmonicity of \(|f|^q\) bounds the value at \(z\) by its average on \(D_r(z)\) times a fixed constant. For \(u\in D_r(z)\) the areas at \(z,u\) are comparable. Tonelli thus bounds \(\int |f|^q\,d\mu\) by a constant times \(\int |f|^qk_r\,dm\). Hölder with \(p/q,s\) proves sufficiency.

For the radius lemma with \(0<\delta<\epsilon<1\), \(k_\delta\ll k_\epsilon\) by inclusion and normalized-area comparison. In the other direction average \(k_\delta\) over \(D_\epsilon(z)\). This averaging operator is bounded on \(L^\infty\) and \(L^1\), the latter by Tonelli and area comparability, hence on all intermediate \(L^s\). Its value dominates \(k_\epsilon(z)\) up to a fixed constant. In fact, for \(t\in D_\epsilon(z)\), the Fubini kernel \(\int_{D_\epsilon(z)\cap D_\delta(t)}dm(w)/m(D_\delta(w))\) has a uniform positive lower bound. Send \(z\) to zero by a disk automorphism. The overlap remains positive even at \(|t|=\epsilon\) in these coordinates, so compactness and continuity give a uniform fraction. The Jacobian of the inverse automorphism has a bounded ratio on the fixed compact region involved. This verifies the geometric step without using the printed proof's numerical shorthand as a universal constant.

For necessity, fix \(\eta<1\) sufficiently near one for Amar's uniform interpolation theorem. Every \(\eta\)-separated sequence \(a_j\) interpolates data \(c_j\) with \(\sum |c_j|^p(1-|a_j|^2)^2\le1\) by an \(A^p\) function of norm at most \(M(p,\eta)\). The restriction map is bounded by disjoint-disc mean values. Mere surjectivity for each sequence individually would leave a gap because the proof needs the uniform \(M\). Choose a small \(\delta\), construct a maximal \(\delta/2\)-separated covering lattice, and split it into finitely many \(\eta\)-separated sequences by Amar. The disks \(B_j=D_{\eta/2}(a_j)\) in one separated sequence are disjoint.

Put \(\mu_j=\mu(D_\delta(a_j))\) and \(V=(\sum_j\mu_j^s m(B_j)^{1-s})^{1/s}\). To obtain (3.2), send \(a_j\) to zero, apply the local analytic derivative bound from Cauchy and mean values, and undo the map. For \(z\in D_\delta(a_j)\),
\[
 |f(z)-f(a_j)|^q\ll_{q,\eta}
 \frac{\delta^q}{m(B_j)}\int_{B_j}|f|^q\,dm.
\]
Integrate in each small disk against \(\mu\). Local Hölder followed by sequence Hölder and disjointness of the \(B_j\) gives
\[
 \sum_j\int_{D_\delta(a_j)} |f(z)-f(a_j)|^q d\mu(z)
 \ll_{q,\eta}\delta^q\|f\|_{A^p}^q V .
\]
On the unit weighted interpolation data the supremum of \(\sum_j\mu_j |c_j|^q\) is exactly \((\sum_j\mu_j^s (1-|a_j|^2)^{2(1-s)})^{1/s}\) by ordinary positive sequence duality with exponents \(p/q,s\). This is comparable to \(V\). Apply the embedding to an interpolating \(f\), split \(|c_j|^q\) using subadditivity for \(q\le1\) and \(2^{q-1}\) times the sum for \(q>1\), and absorb the \(\delta^q M^q V\) term by choosing \(\delta\) small. The resulting bound is uniform in compactly supported truncations of \(\mu\). Sum the finite coloring bounds. For \(z\in D_{\delta/2}(a_j)\) one has \(D_{\delta/2}(z)\subset D_\delta(a_j)\). Integrate the corresponding upper bound for \(k_{\delta/2}^s\) over the cover to obtain the assertion at that radius. The radius lemma gives every fixed radius. This proof does not require randomized tests.

## Multipliers and product scope

For measurable \(g\), multiplication \(gA^p\subset L^q(dm)\) is equivalent by closed graph to the embedding with \(d\mu=|g|^qdm\). Evaluation on \(1\) gives finiteness, and local uniform convergence in \(A^p\) and an almost-everywhere subsequence from \(L^q\) convergence give the closed graph, also in the quasi-Banach cases. For analytic \(g\), the theorem recovers the characterization \(g\in A^r\), \(1/r=1/q-1/p\), attributed by the source to Attele with a related Axler citation. Necessity uses \(|g|^q\ll k_\delta\) and \(qs=r\), and sufficiency also follows directly from Hölder. The product inclusion \(A^p A^r\subset A^q\) follows. The source gives no surjectivity or norm-controlled factorization of arbitrary \(A^q\) functions. The weighted-ball and bounded symmetric domain extensions are discussed as remarks, with the latter formulation left to the reader.

## Strip transfer and scope of endpoint ancestry

For the strip \(S=\{a<\Re z<b\}\), \(a<0<1<b\), and a conformal map \(\Phi:U\to S\), write \(F=(hG)\circ\Phi\cdot\Phi'\), where \(G\) is holomorphic and zero-free with \(|G|^2\asymp w\). Then \(F\) ranges over all \(A^2\) as \(h\) ranges over the weighted strip area space, and norms correspond up to a fixed factor and the normalization \(1/\pi\). For \(w(t)=(1+t^2)^\sigma\) use \(G=(C+z)^\sigma\). For \(w(t)=\sqrt{1+t^2}\log^p(e+|t|)\) use \(G=(C+z)^{1/2}(\operatorname{Log}(C+z))^{p/2}\). Taking \(\Re(C+z)>e\) guarantees both holomorphic powers and the asserted uniform comparisons.

An atom of mass \(1/|G(\rho)\Phi'(\Phi^{-1}\rho)|\) at each preimage of a zero gives exact equality of the absolute sample sum and disk \(L^1(\mu)\), counting multiplicities. The measure is finite. The exponential-to-half-plane and Cayley maps give \(1-|\Phi^{-1}\rho|^2\ll e^{-\pi|\Im\rho|/(b-a)}\), and the conformal-radius identity bounds \(|\Phi'|(1-|\Phi^{-1}\rho|^2)\) above and below uniformly for \(0\le\Re\rho\le1\). The remaining factors grow at most polynomially or logarithmically, as do zero counts per unit height.

Any fixed hyperbolic radius ball meeting the zero substrip has center in a fixed interior substrip and spans bounded height. On it the disk area is comparable to \((1-|z|^2)^2\), and the atom masses are comparable to \((1-|z|^2)w(\Im\Phi(z))^{-1/2}\). In the squared density integral the residual factor \((1-|z|^2)^{-2}\) cancels with the change to strip area up to uniform constants. The criterion then becomes \(\sum_j N_j^2/w(j)<\infty\). The upper direction uses a bounded number of neighboring unit cells. For the lower direction a single larger radius, allowed by the radius lemma, covers the zero rectangle \(0\le\Re\rho\le1,\ j\le\Im\rho<j+1\) from all centers in a fixed small disk at \(1/2+i(j+1/2)\). These integrating disks can be chosen disjoint with the same positive area.

The Riemann–von Mangoldt upper bound on unit cells and lower bound on dyadic shells give precisely \(\sigma>1/2\) or logarithmic \(p>3\). The lower direction uses Cauchy–Schwarz against the \(O(T)\) cells in each \([T,2T)\). The zero geometry requires neither RH nor simplicity. A failed embedding implies an actual analytic strip sample sequence outside \(\ell^1\) by the closed graph theorem and continuity of point evaluation. Luecking's necessity handles atomic measures and obtains arbitrary nonnegative size sequences through deterministic interpolation. It is direct provenance for ambient analytic sharpness, but supplies neither randomized carriers nor the archive's smooth real inverse Mellin function with physical-space support, decay and convergence constraints. It also supplies no signed endpoint convergence conclusion.
