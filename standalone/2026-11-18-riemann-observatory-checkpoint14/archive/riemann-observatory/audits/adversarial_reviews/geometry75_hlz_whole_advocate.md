## 1. Main theorem and assumptions

With \(k=a/b>0\) fixed rational, pointwise Hölder uses \(a/(a+b)\) and \(b/(a+b)\). The powers of \(|\zeta'|\) cancel because \(k b=a\), and the power of the polynomial is \(2a\). On RH the pairs \(P(\rho)P(1-\rho)\) and \(\zeta'(\rho)\zeta'(1-\rho)\) become squared moduli. Simplicity permits the finite negative powers in the second Hölder factor. Raising the inequality to \((a+b)/b\) gives \(J_{-k}\ge S_1^{k+1}/S_2^k\). The propositions have respective log exponents \(k^2+1,k^2+3\), yielding \((k+1)(k^2+1)-k(k^2+3)=(k-1)^2\). For \(k=0\), use the zero count. Propositions 2–3 are unconditional statements with paired zeros and multiplicity. The lower bound is neither an upper bound nor an asymptotic for negative moments. The Gonek–Hejhal and random matrix remarks are contextual predictions. The polynomial \(P\) heuristic equates the two Hölder inputs after the suggested shifts and cancels log factors by homogeneity. The rational construction itself uses only finite convolutions. Choose \(B=\lceil14+12k\rceil\) for the required integer smoothing and fix \(0<\theta<1/2\) once for the constants.

## 2. Proposition 2 | independent Mellin reconstruction

The weight in (7) is \((\log(x/n)/\log x)_+^B\). Formula (8) follows by inverting \(s^{-B-1}\), with value zero at \(n=x\). For \(R=P^a\), its coefficients \(r(n,x)\) are bounded by \(\tau_a(n)\) since \(|\tau_{-1/b}(p^j)|\le1\). Ng 2008a, Proposition 4(i), directly gives (9) for length \(x^a<T^{1/2}\), with its \(o(T)\) remainder, using coefficients satisfying \(O_\epsilon(T^\epsilon)\). The formula is about the paired zero sum, so it does not need RH. Use \(N(T)=T\log(T/(2\pi e))/(2\pi)+O(\log T)\).

For \(S_{11}\), imposing equal products on the two groups of \(a\) variables gives each first prime term from a pair in opposite groups, with coefficient \(1/b^2\). Factor out the \(a^2\) copies of \(\zeta(1+s_i+s_{a+j})^{1/b^2}\) in (10). The absolute majorant on positive lines has the same first prime terms. The normalized remaining product has no first-order prime terms and converges normally in a fixed smaller polydisc about zero. There \(\log\zeta(1+u)\) is fixed by the Euler log from \(\Re u>0\), or by \(-\log u+\log(u\zeta(1+u))\) with the right-half-plane branch. Only absolute upper bounds are needed for distant imaginary parts. For \(S_{12}\), express \(\Lambda\) as the auxiliary derivative of \(\mu*n^\gamma\) at zero. At a prime the extra balanced unit may occupy one of the last \(a\) variables. This produces the factor \(\prod_j[\zeta(1+s_{a+j}-\gamma)/\zeta(1+s_{a+j})]^{1/b}\) and leaves \(B(\mathbf s,0)=A(\mathbf s)\). Differentiate, then \(\zeta'/\zeta(1+s_{a+j})\sim-1/s_{a+j}\) gives the negative leading sign for \(S_{12}\).

Scale all \(s_j\) by \(1/\log x\) and truncate at \(|\Im s_j|\le\sqrt{\log x}\) in the scaled variables. The full absolute integrands before the cut are bounded by the appropriate powers \((\log x)^{k^2}\) or \((\log x)^{k^2+1}\), times integrable smoothing denominators. The error from a single omitted tail is a factor \((\log x)^{-B/2}\). In the central region replace the Euler factor by its value at zero and \(\zeta(1+u)\) by \(u^{-1}(1+O(|u|))\). The absolute uniform relative error for the products is \(O((\log x)^{-1/2})\). For the log derivative one can use \(\zeta'/\zeta(1+u)=-1/u+O(1)\), which suffices for (14) without the stronger uniform error printed in the journal. Extending the model integrals back to full lines has the same tail bounds. This proves (13)–(14) with the indicated orders.

At zero \(A(0)=\prod_p(1-p^{-1})^{k^2}\sum_{m\ge0}\tau_{-k}(p^m)^2/p^m>0\). Replace each \((s_i+s_{a+j})^{-1/b^2}\) by its Laplace gamma integral. The multivariate interchange is absolute because each \(s_j\) has real part 1, and \(B\ge1\). Mellin inversion gives the integral on the nonnegative matrix polytope with every row and column sum at most 1. The integrand is the product of positive slack powers and \(x_{ij}^{1/b^2-1}/\Gamma(1/b^2)\). For \(\gamma(a,b)\) one slack exponent rises to \(B+1\) and is divided by \(B+1\). The interior has positive volume and the singular coordinate weights are integrable. Thus \(\beta(a,b),\gamma(a,b)>0\). Substituting (13) and (14) in (9) retains \(1/\pi\) on both leading terms relative to the display at the start of §2.3. That common factor does not affect the claimed existence or positivity of the leading constant.

## 3. Proposition 3 | direct uses of the smoothed formula

Let \(N=a+b\) and \(Q=P^N\). Its coefficients satisfy \(|a(n)|\le\tau_N(n)\). One must not infer the *pointwise* submultiplicative hypothesis of Theorem 4 from this bound. A concrete check is \(a=b=2\), \(N=4\), \(B=26\), and fixed prime \(p\). If \(u=\log p/\log x\), the coefficient at \(p^e\), for \(e\le6\) and large \(x\), is \([z^e](\sum_{j=0}^6(-1)^j\binom{1/2}{j}(1-ju)^{26}z^j)^4\). For \(e=3,6\) the coefficients through degree 2 in \(u\) vanish and degree 3 is 13 for both. This follows by retaining the four combinations of the zeroth through third derivatives of the factors, or by exact coefficient expansion. Thus \(|a(p^6)|/|a(p^3)|^2\to\infty\). For using Theorem 4, extend its quadratic identity to this array by padding with \(D(n)=4\tau_N(n)\mathbf1_{n\le y}\), applying it to \(D,D+a,D-a\), and canceling with the parallelogram identity on the entire formula before estimating. Each padded array lies between \(3\tau_N(n)\) and \(5\tau_N(n)\) on support and satisfies the submultiplicative bound even with constant 1, because \(\tau_N(mn)\le\tau_N(m)\tau_N(n)\). The support \([1,y]\) is divisor-closed. Common divisor/log majorants give uniform bounds. The same technique applies to any divisor-envelope array with suitably chosen padding. This supplies the application as a repaired deduction without positing submultiplicativity of the mollifier.

The mixed \(\alpha,\beta\) derivative gives \(S_2\). Take Cauchy circles of radii \(\asymp1/\log T\) with a larger log saving before differentiation. For (16), integrate on separated \(z_1,z_2\) circles enclosing both \(\alpha\) and \(-\beta\). Repeated selections vanish by the squared Vandermonde even where \(\zeta(1+z_1-z_2)\) has a pole. The two distinct assignments have residue sign minus for the Vandermonde quotient, canceling the outer minus. Their powers are the two displayed continuous contributions. The pole at \(\alpha+\beta=0\) is removable in their sum.

For \(F(x,z_1,z_2)\), unfold the \(2N\) variables. First prime terms of the gcd factor in §3.1 arise from one occupied variable on either side or a single variable on each side. The two singleton families contribute \(-1/b\) at \(1+s_i-z_2\) and \(1+s_{N+j}+z_1\); the opposite double family contributes \(1/b^2\) at \(1+s_i+s_{N+j}\). This gives (17). Requiring \(|z_i|\) sufficiently small relative to \(1/\log x\) keeps the reciprocal factors on the right of 1 on the innermost \(s\)-lines. Uniform tails of the \(2N\) Mellin variables use crude \((\log T)^{N^2/b^2+2N/b}\) and cost \((\log T)^{-B/2}\). Local pole extraction gives \(\ll(\log T)^{N^2/b^2-2N/b}=(\log T)^{k^2-1}\) after integration, with factors \((1+|s_j|)^{1/b}\) absorbed by \(B\). Combining derivative, two contour lengths, Vandermonde and \(\log t\) gives \(T(\log T)^4\) times this bound.

For (21), apply the same residue rule with nodes \(\alpha,-\beta,\gamma\). The squared Vandermonde quotient has sign \((-1)^3=-1\), each central-slot selection has two outside-slot permutations, and \(f_{z_1,z_3}\) is symmetric. After including the external \(t\)-power inside the \(t\) integral the three central slots give multipliers \(1,(t/2\pi)^{-\alpha-\beta},(t/2\pi)^{-\beta-\gamma}\). Use mutually separated integration circles with external shifts inside the smallest circle. For the smoothed formula use a kernel depending symmetrically on the node triple as described in §4 below. The numerator Vandermonde squared cancels the node-diagonal denominators of the symmetric \(H_{\mathbf z}\) identically on the initial \(w\)-line. This ensures that repeated-node residue contributions still require care: at repeated selections the canceled factors alone no longer force vanishing. For the unsmoothed \(Z\) kernel, the local zeta pole has order at most one per pair, so the Vandermonde does force the repeated selections to vanish. One can therefore apply the Vandermonde identity first to \(Z\), and replace \(Z\) by its smoothed diagonal uniformly on the separated \(z_j\) contours, rather than prove (21) by taking residues of the smoothed \(G\) itself. This sequence supplies analytic confluence and the same \(o(T)\) term. The derivative costs \(L^3\) and the base triple integral costs \(L^9 L^{-6}L^{-3}=1\), with \(L=\log T\).

For (22), first prime terms on the diagonal \(h_1\cdots h_Nm_1m_2m_3=k_1\cdots k_Nn\) are pairs with one unit on each side. The three \(m\) versus \(n\) pairs give the two numerator zetas and one reciprocal. The \(h_i\) versus \(k_j\) pairs yield the \(N^2\) factors of exponent \(1/b^2\). The remaining four pair families have exponents \(+1/b,-1/b,-1/b,-1/b\) as in (22). Normal convergence of the post-factor product is required only near the working contours, where second prime terms have exponent \(>1\) with a fixed margin. For the \(w\) move take \(\Re s_i=\kappa=1/\log\log T\), \(\Re w\) from 1 to \(-2\kappa/3\), and height \(\asymp\sqrt{\log T}\). Then \(\Re(s_i+w)\ge\kappa/3\) and all shifted versions with \(z_i=O(1/L)\) stay to the right of zero. The only unpaired reciprocal with real part below 1 is \(\zeta(1-z_2+2w)^{-1}\), controlled by the classical zero-free region. Cancel the two nonzero \(w\)-poles by the compatible \(H\). Polynomial \(L\) losses are dominated by \(T^{(-4/3+2\theta)\kappa}\) on the new line and by the Gaussian on the horizontal lines. The residue at zero leaves the original \(s\) contours. Move those to \(1/\log x\), then localize at \(\sqrt{\log x}\) after scaling. The crude bound loses at most \(L^{k^2+6k+6}\) before the tail factor \(L^{-B/2}\). Inside the cutoff, combine the four fractional exponent families and the bare zeta quotient to get \(L^{N^2/b^2-2N/b+1}=L^{k^2}\), times controlled powers of \((1+|s_j|)\). This gives \(|S_{22}|,|S_{23}|\ll T L^{k^2+3}\). The replacement of the lower integration limit \(0\) by 1 contributes only powers of \(L\), using separated shifts and the integrability of \(t^{-O(1/L)}(\log t)^j\).

## 4. Shifted formula and its smoothed version

The rectangle for \(\zeta'/\zeta\) in §4 has only the desired zero residues, with multiplicities. Use a good positive height within bounded distance of \(T\), standard convexity and logarithmic derivative bounds, and the reflected edge via \(\chi'/\chi\). For complex coefficients keep the source's outer conjugation \( \overline{I(\bar\beta,\bar\alpha,T)}\) and the matching conjugated coefficient slot. Changing the height back costs \(O(yT^{1/2+\epsilon})\) using bounded-window zero counts, the convexity-bound product \(O(T^{1/2+\epsilon})\) throughout the strip, and fixed-envelope polynomial products. The \(\chi'/\chi\) integral is shifted to the center. Stirling gives \(-\log(t/(2\pi))+O(1/t)\). The journal invokes a sharp-interval, slightly rephrased Pratt–Robles theorem for the general divisor-enveloped coefficients to furnish the shifted continuous moment for length \(y< T^{1/2}\); its errors \(T^{3/20}y^{33/20}\) and \(T^{1/2+\epsilon}y^{1/2}\) are power savings. The sharp-interval conversion and complex-shift interpretation are separate interface inputs. They are not proved here from the cited smooth statement, and a separate direct approximate-functional-equation check of that interface is in progress. Partial integration inserts the logarithm and handles the \(1/t\) remainder, and the lower-end extension costs polylogs.

For \(K\) in (27), the functional equation in the journal's own convention \(\zeta(s)=\chi(s)\zeta(1-s)\) gives \(\zeta(1-s+\beta)=\chi(1-s+\beta)\zeta(s-\beta)\). The printed \(\chi(s-\beta)\), and \(\chi(s)\) in the next expressions after translation, must be the reciprocal factors. This is independently settled by the stationary-phase input: original Conrey–Ghosh–Gonek 1998 Lemma 2, pp. 504–505, and Ng 2007 Lemma 4.1 use \(\chi(1-s)\), giving \(e(-m/k)\) with \(m\le Tk/(2\pi)\). Lemma 7 itself has the correct gamma factor and uses an inconsistent \(n\) for \(k\) in its inner bound. Author v1 Lemma 4 preserves this same cutoff typo. Use \(m^{O(1/L)}\) in infinite shifted Dirichlet series and choose the shift radii with room for absolute convergence on the initial line. The oscillatory estimate in Ng may be summed with this factor, or first truncated where it is bounded. The complex vertical endpoint movement is absorbed by the stationary-phase error \(O(yT^{1/2+\epsilon})\).

For the inverse kernel in Lemma 6, \(hm=kn\) means \(m=k\ell,n=h\ell\). The arithmetic factor in the resulting Mellin integral is
\[
\frac{T^{2s}}{(hk)^{1+s}h^\beta}
\frac{\zeta(1+\alpha+\beta+2s)\zeta(1+\beta+\gamma+2s)}
{\zeta(1+\beta+2s)}
\prod_{p^e\parallel k}\frac{\sum_{j\ge0}f_{\alpha,\gamma}(p^{j+e})p^{-j(1+\beta+2s)}}{\sum_{j\ge0}f_{\alpha,\gamma}(p^j)p^{-j(1+\beta+2s)}}.
\]
This gives the exact \(hk\) factor in (25) and recovers (6) on ungrouping \(gh,gk\). The actual numerator pole offsets in journal coordinates are \(2s=-\alpha-\beta,-\beta-\gamma\). The rendered conditions \(2s=\beta-\alpha,\beta-\gamma\) in Theorem 4 and Lemma 6 do not in general cancel them. Author v1 Lemma 3 gives \(z_2-z_1,z_2-z_3\), which are the correct offsets when the shifted term is written \(S_{z_1,-z_2,z_3}\). For a simultaneous choice across all three permutations, use
\[
 H_{\mathbf z}(s)=e^{s^2}\prod_{i\ne j}\left(1-\frac{2s}{z_i-z_j}\right).
\]
This kernel is normalized, entire in \(s\), symmetric in the nodes, and has the requisite uniform Gaussian bound with polynomial \(L\) losses on separated circles. This suffices in the Vandermonde formula and by confluence for Proposition 3. Statements at colliding nodes are interpreted for the combined integral or residue sum. Requiring \(H(0)=1\) and cancellation at \(s=0\) literally at a collision would be impossible.

The exponent in the rendered journal (25) is \(1/\log_3 T\) with a **subscript**, hence an iterated logarithm. The earlier interpretation as \(1/(\log T)^3\) is withdrawn. Truncate at height \(A'\sqrt{\log_2 T}\), move the contour to \(-c/\log_3 T\) with small fixed \(c>0\), and apply the classical zero-free region including the \(O(1/L)\) shifts. Uniform Gaussian decay at the truncation height and the factor \((T^2/(hk))^{-c/\log_3 T}\) bound the error by every fixed negative power of \(L\) for \(hk\le y^2\). Standard strip estimates for the zetas and divisor bounds in \(k\) suffice. This uses the exponent's iterated-log scale, with an explicit small constant for the contour margin. The separated circles and the Cauchy derivatives absorb the necessary fixed powers of \(L\). Without pole cancellation, the omitted residues are not controlled by this decay. The argument establishes the version with the compatible weight above, not invariance under arbitrary weights satisfying the printed sign conditions.

For (29)–(35), decompose \(e(-m/k)\) first by \((m,k)\), then by principal and nonprincipal characters; the principal coefficient is \(\mu(k')/\varphi(k')\). The induction from imprimitive characters to primitive conductors with the divisor weight \(\delta\) uses Conrey–Ghosh–Gonek (5.10). In the principal term, extract \((h,k)\), then \((n,k)\). Lemma 8 is obtained by assigning each prime power in the fixed divisor \(D\) to the convolution factors in order, peeling off all available valuation until the valuation budget of \(D\) is spent. Leftover variable factors are coprime to the already spent factors in the equivalent reversed ordering. This gives the finite Euler products (31)–(34). The needed holomorphy and divisor bounds are near \(\Re s=1\), with the tiny shifted arguments kept inside their valid region.

For \(x=Tk/(2\pi h)\) one has \(\log x\asymp\log T\). ## 6. Large primitive conductors

In \(\delta(q,kq,d,\psi)\), write \(k=k'k_q\) with \(k_q\mid q^\infty\) and \((k',q)=1\). Character support forces \(e=k_qe'\), then \(d=k_qd'\) and \((d',q)=1\); the latter also uses the character factor involving \(d/e\). The formula reduces to \(\delta(q,k'q,d',\psi)\). Bound by \((\log\log T)^2d'/(k'q)\) using \(\varphi(n)\gg n/\log\log(3n)\) and \(\sigma(n)\ll n\log\log(3n)\), and take absolute values outside the character sums to retain cancellation inside. Regrouping the \(q\)-part of \(k\), then dyadically splitting (44), leaves \(KQD\ll y\), \(q\asymp Q\), \(d\asymp D\) and maximum cutoff \(x\ll KQT\). The outer prefactor after summing \(k\) is \(L^C/(KQ^{3/2-\epsilon})\) times a divisor-weighted \(d\)-sum. This step is the extension beyond squarefree support in Bui–Heath-Brown 2013.

Heath-Brown's identity in §6.2 follows by expanding \(1-(1-\zeta M)^J\). The remainder has no coefficients through \(z=T^{3/2}\), since \(1-\zeta M\) starts beyond \(z^{1/J}\). Insert the original four other convolution factors (three shifted unit sequences and \(a\)), use dyadic intervals, and distribute the fixed divisor \(d\) by Lemma 8, giving at most \(2J+3\) variables. The resulting factors have divisor envelopes and lengths \(G_i\asymp F_i/d_i\) for nonempty dyadic ranges. The small cutoff \(x\ll(yT)^{1/2}\) gives \(O(yT^{1/2+\epsilon})\) by the trivial divisor estimate and \(O(Q^2)\) characters. For large cutoff, choose \(W=(KQT)^{2/3}\) with constant slack to accommodate the dyadic endpoints. If a \(G_i\gg W\), it cannot be an \(a\) factor or one of the short Möbius factors since \(y\ll x^{2/3}\) and \(J\) can be large. This is Type I. Otherwise group the factors to achieve both products \(O(W)\). More explicitly, omit empty dyadic boxes, cap \(x\) at a constant multiple of the support-product upper end, and take the full group if the product is already \(O(W)\). Otherwise a greedy partial product reaching \(\prod G_i/W\) cannot overshoot \(O(W)\), because its last small factor is below this threshold unless a single factor already works, and \((KQT/W)^2\asymp W\).

Type I uses Möbius removal of coprimalities and Pólya–Vinogradov for each primitive nonprincipal \(\psi\), with partial summation for the complex powers \(n^c\). The bound per long variable is \(O(\tau(D_i)\sqrt q\log q)\); the product of remaining lengths is \(O(x/W)\), leading to \(Q^2T^{1+\epsilon}/W\). Type II use Perron at height \(T^{20}\), moving a sharp real upper cutoff to the next half-integer if needed, or absorbing a possible single coefficient in \(O(T^\epsilon)\). With product length \(AB\ll KQT\), dyadic integration and the hybrid large sieve of Iwaniec–Kowalski Theorem 7.17 give for each averaged block
\[
 V^{-1}\sqrt{(Q^2V+A)(Q^2V+B)AB}
\]
times fixed divisor factors and logs. This is after Cauchy–Schwarz and the divisor squared-norm bounds for the two grouped polynomials. Summation over \(d\) absorbs fixed divisor weights in \(L^C\). The displayed three contributions in (52) are \(T^{1/2}W^{1/2}K^{-1/2},QT^{1/2},TQ^{-1/2}\), up to logs and small powers. Inserting \(W\) gives Proposition 11's \(yT^{1/2+\epsilon}\), \(y^{4/3}T^{1/3+\epsilon}\), \(y^{1/3}T^{5/6+\epsilon}\) and \(L^C T\eta^{-1/2+\epsilon}\) terms. The three powers are all strictly \(o(TL^{-A})\) for fixed \(\theta<1/2\) and sufficiently small \(\epsilon\). Choose a large fixed log-power split \(\eta\) after the desired \(A\). Combining with Proposition 10 proves the needed conductor saving without a hypothesis on Dirichlet zeros.

## Disposition and version boundaries

The proof strategy and conditional negative-moment exponent survive the local reconstructions above. The items meriting independent adjudication are the printed cancellation signs for the smoothed diagonal conversion, its confluence convention, the unsupported direct mollifier submultiplicativity application, and the functional-equation reciprocal in §4.2. The \(H=\sqrt U\) choice is already reflected in the surrounding Perron estimates. The iterated logarithm was checked visually and carries no log-decay issue. External inputs remain at their stated boundaries. The revised journal separates the full smoothed Theorem 4 from the explicit Theorem 5, whereas author v1 gives its explicit shifted theorem as Theorem 2 and smoothing inside Proposition 2's proof. The reviewed source-dependent derivative-tail work uses the explicit Theorem 5 with separated shifts and coefficient padding, so it does not require the disputed smoothing passage. This is an advocate report submitted for independent comparison. No source or corpus status is altered.
