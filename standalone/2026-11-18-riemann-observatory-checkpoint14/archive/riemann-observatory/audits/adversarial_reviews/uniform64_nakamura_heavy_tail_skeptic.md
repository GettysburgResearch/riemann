# Independent audit of the Nakamura–Suzuki tail and scaling deductions

18 November 2026. Under RH, the symmetric compound Poisson law has the stated two-term tail with error \(O(\log^3Y/Y^2)\), dense countable atoms, moment threshold \(p=1\), and the standard symmetric Cauchy scaling. The numerical enclosure at zero uses the rate, a certified finite cancellation sum, and a separate upper bound for the remaining multiplicities. The local cusp is unconditional and alone does not make the formal scaled functions characteristic functions. ## Counting and the strict Lévy tail

I checked Hasanalizade–Shen–Wong arXiv:2107.06506v1 p. 1 inclusive count, p. 2 Corollary 1.2 (1.5), and its argument-principle positive-sign count convention. The explicit remainder after subtracting \(M_0(v)=v\log(v/(2\pi e))/(2\pi)\) is bounded by \(a\log v+b\) for \(v\ge e\), \(a=.3611,\ b=9.3675\), since \(\log\log v\le\log v\). Titchmarsh §9.4 (9.4.3) supplies the same two main terms with \(O(\log v)\). Under RH the count applies to all positive zero ordinates in the Lévy law. The atom locations use the opposite sign to the zero coordinate, which preserves the two-sided and reflected counts. [Hasanalizade–Shen–Wong v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.06506v1) · [Titchmarsh](../../sources/equivalence_round2/titchmarsh1986.pdf)

The strict two-sided sum is \(-2N(Y)/Y^2+4\int_Y^\infty N(v)v^{-3}dv\), subtracting the inclusive count at \(Y\). The smooth part has derivative \(\log(v/(2\pi))/(2\pi)\), so its contribution is \([\log(Y/(2\pi))+1]/(\pi Y)\). The bound for the discrepancy contribution is
\[
 2(a\log Y+b)/Y^2+4\int_Y^\infty(a\log v+b)v^{-3}dv
 =(4a\log Y+4b+a)/Y^2.
\]
An additive constant in the smooth count would cancel between the endpoint and the integral. The error formula is unaffected when \(Y\) is exactly an ordinate.

## Compound Poisson transfer at the stated precision

Normalize the jump distribution by the finite rate \(S\) and let \(F(x)=\mathbb P(|J|>x)\). The preceding estimate implies \(F(x/m)\ll m\log x/x\) for all \(m\ge1\), \(x\ge2e\). Below \(x/m=e\) use \(F\le1\) and \(m>x/e\). Also
\[
 |F(x\pm v)-F(x)|\ll (v+1)\log x/x^2
 \quad(0\le v\le x/2),
 \qquad
 \mathbb E[|J|\mathbf1_{|J|\le x}]\ll\log^2x.
\]
The first is a smooth main-term difference plus separately bounded remainders. It holds for the strict tail even at endpoints with mass, and does not require a density. The second uses the tail integral for \(\min(|J|,x)\), which dominates the truncated expectation.

For \(n\ge2\) put \(u=x/[2(n-1)]\). The union bound for at least two jumps exceeding \(u\) is at most \(\binom n2 F(u)^2\ll n^4\log^2x/x^2\), uniformly even when \(n\) is large. If none exceeds \(u\), the signed sum cannot be greater in absolute value than \(nu\le x\). For each possible single extreme index \(i\), condition on all other jumps, let \(R_i\) be their signed sum and let \(G_i\) indicate that all their magnitudes are at most \(u\). Then \(|R_i|\le x/2\) on \(G_i\). The event \(G_i, |J_i|>x+|R_i|\) certifies the strict absolute sum and is disjoint across \(i\). If the strict absolute sum occurs outside the event of two or more large jumps, it lies in some event \(G_i, |J_i|>x-|R_i|\). These inequalities include possible cancellations and require no concentration assumption on the signs.

Independence from the other jumps makes the sums of lower and upper probabilities respectively \(\sum_i \mathbb E[\mathbf1_{G_i}F(x+|R_i|)]\) and \(\sum_i \mathbb E[\mathbf1_{G_i}F(x-|R_i|)]\), with the two-large-jump exceptional probability added in the upper bound. Each summand differs from \(F(x)\) by at most
\[
 \mathbb E[\mathbf1_{G_i}(|R_i|+1)]\,O(\log x/x^2)
   +F(x)\mathbb P(G_i^c).
\]
The expectation is \(O(n\log^2x)\), and \(\mathbb P(G_i^c)\le(n-1)F(u)\ll n^2\log x/x\). Hence the conditional absolute tail is \(nF(x)+O((1+n)^4\log^3 x/x^2)\), with no changing implicit constant in \(n\). The \(n=0,1\) cases are exact. Averaging over the Poisson count uses only its finite fourth moment and \(\mathbb E K=S\). Thus the leading term is precisely the Lévy tail and the compounded error is of the claimed order. A separate treatment of large \(n\) is unnecessary because the uniform tail bound below \(e\) was included.

By symmetry the one-sided strict tails are half the absolute tail. Tail integration for absolute moments gives convergence exactly at \(0<p<1\), since the principal contribution at infinity has integrand proportional to \(y^{p-2}\log y\). Positive and negative parts of the first moment both diverge. For each fixed \(z\ge1\), the tail ratio at \(zY\) and \(Y\) tends to \(z^{-1}\), giving the stated Pareto conditional limit.

## Intensity, cancellation at zero, and the atomic subgroup

The genus-one logarithmic derivative, differentiated locally at \(s=1/2\), gives \(-\sum_\rho m_\rho(1/2-\rho)^{-2}\). Normal convergence there follows from the inverse-square sum, since \(\xi(1/2)\ne0\). The functional equation gives \(\xi'(1/2)=0\), so this second logarithmic derivative is \(\xi''(1/2)/\xi(1/2)\). Under RH every \(1/2-\rho=-i\gamma\) and the sign turns the sum into the Lévy intensity \(S\) for both signs. The analytic ratio can be independently evaluated without RH. It is not the probability of no jump or of total cancellation.

Let \(w_\gamma=m_\gamma/\gamma^2\) at each distinct \(\gamma>0\). There is no jump at zero. At \(K=2\) only pairs of opposite equal ordinates sum to zero, with both orders counted. The contribution to the atom is \(e^{-S}(S^2/2)\cdot 2\sum_{\gamma>0}(w_\gamma/S)^2=e^{-S}\sum w_\gamma^2\). The contribution from \(K=0\) is \(e^{-S}\), and the remainder lies between zero and \(\mathbb P(K\ge3)\). Therefore the atom is strictly larger than \(e^{-S}\), while \(1-Se^{-S}\) is also an upper bound by disregarding all information except the impossible \(K=1\) case. This needs neither simplicity beyond the expressly certified finite block nor assumptions about additive relations at three or more jumps.

For \(\gamma>100\), the jump of the inclusive count at \(\gamma\) is at most \(2(a\log\gamma+b)\): take the left limit in the explicit count bound and subtract from the right count, cancelling the continuous \(M_0\). Its quotient by \(\gamma^2\) is decreasing for \(\gamma\ge100\), since its derivative is \(2\gamma^{-3}(a-2a\log\gamma-2b)<0\). Hence \(\sum_{\gamma>100}w_\gamma^2\le 2(a\log100+b)100^{-2}(S/2-\sum_{0<\gamma\le100}w_\gamma)\). This uses multiplicities globally and is independent of a simplicity conjecture.

An exact finite representation by signed ordinates has positive probability, by prescribing one Poisson count and one ordered sequence of jumps. Conversely every realization is in the subgroup of finite sums because \(K<\infty\) almost surely. The subgroup is countable, and the topological support is its closure. In the original Acta Arithmetica 150.1 (2011), p. 35, Bui–Conrey–Young define \(N_0^*\) to count **simple critical-line zeros**, and Theorem 1.1 gives \(\liminf N_0^*(T)/N(T)\ge .4058\). Thus there are \(\gg T\log T\) distinct positive ordinates, precluding containment in a lattice of fixed spacing. An additive subgroup of \(\mathbb R\) with arbitrarily small positive elements is dense by taking their integer multiples, and otherwise it is a discrete lattice (the infimum of positive elements is then attained by the difference property). This proves dense atoms without asserting linear independence. [BCY journal PDF](../../sources/classical63_lfunctions/bcy2011print.pdf)

## Local cusp, Cauchy limit, and the distinct torus laws

I checked the independent zero-sum cusp argument without RH. Pair opposite zeros, and compare \(k(u\gamma)\) with \(k(u\Re\gamma)\), \(k(z)=(1-\cos z)/z^2\), \(u=|t|\). No zero has real part zero in the ordinate coordinate, by the eta-series sign and the strip. For \(u\Re\gamma\le1\), the entire derivative satisfies \(k'(z)=O(|z|)\) on the short vertical segment because \(k'(0)=0\). For \(u\Re\gamma\ge1\), differentiation and bounded imaginary parts give \(k'=O((u\Re\gamma)^{-2})\). After multiplying by \(u^2\) and the short segment length \(O(u)\), the two ranges contribute \(O(u^2\log(1/u))\) using the unconditional Riemann–von Mangoldt count and partial summation. On the real-ordinate Stieltjes expression the same bound follows from \(\int_0^\infty |k'(v)|(1+|\log v|)dv<\infty\), with cancellation of the lower-order terms at zero. Thus both unconditional cusp coefficients follow by inserting \(M_0'(v)\) as a density. The Mellin transform at exponent \(\alpha\) is \(\pi/[2\Gamma(1+\alpha)\sin(\pi\alpha/2)]\) for \(0<\alpha<2\), by the stated Laplace and beta integrals. Negating the derivative at \(\alpha=1\) gives \((\pi/2)(1-\gamma_{\rm E})\) for the logarithmic integral, so no sign or constant changes between these two derivations.

The arithmetic formula independently gives a sharper small-time check. For \(r=e^{-t/2}\), \(0<t<\log2\), the Lerch derivative is \(-2\sum_{j\ge0}r^{4j+1}/(4j+1)=-\operatorname{arctanh}r-\arctan r\). Here \(\operatorname{arctanh}(e^{-t/2})=\tfrac12\log\coth(t/4)=\tfrac12\log(4/t)+O(t^2)\), and \(\arctan(e^{-t/2})=\pi/4-t/4+O(t^3)\). The digamma special value \(\psi(1/4)=-\gamma_{\rm E}-\pi/2-3\log2\) follows from reflection and duplication at \(1/4,3/4\). Combining the linear term and the elementary even exponential part in (1.5) yields the stated cusp coefficient and the further term \(-7t^2/8+O(|t|^3)\). No prime term contributes in this small interval. [Nakamura–Suzuki, (1.5)](../../sources/suzuki_model63_positivity/suzuki_2306_candidate.pdf)

Under RH the normalized sums have characteristic functions \(e^{n g_\zeta(t/a_n)}\), \(a_n=\tfrac12 n\log n\), with zero centering by symmetry. For fixed \(t\ne0\), \(n|t|/(2a_n)=|t|/\log n\) and \(\log(a_n/|t|)/\log n\to1\), while the linear and smaller errors vanish after this scaling. The case \(t=0\) is exact. The limit \(e^{-|t|}\) is the characteristic function of the standard Cauchy law, so Lévy continuity applies. The formula only gives formal pointwise convergence without RH. Adding an off-real quartet to the real-zero model changes the cusp by \(O(t^2)\), yet phase-selecting the leading \(e^{bt}e^{-iat}/\omega^2\) term makes the full exponent arbitrarily large and positive. For any \(n\ge2\) and every positive power the scaled function is still unbounded on the entire real line because scaling the argument is surjective. Thus no probabilistic converse follows from the formal limit.

The pushforward to the prime torus is well defined by the continuous coordinate characters \(e^{ix\log p}\). Its coefficients lie in \([e^{-2S},1]\) because the finite symmetric Lévy exponent is \(\int(\cos(tx)-1)\nu(dx)\). The atom at zero gives an atom at the torus identity. By contrast the derivative-weighted law has a continuous Poisson-circle marginal and coefficients tending to zero along powers of any one prime. This distinction persists after putting both objects on the same compact group.
