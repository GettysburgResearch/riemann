## Exact spaces and limiting rates

Write \(Z^+\) for the multiset of nontrivial zeros with \(\Re\rho>1/2\). Finiteness, together with reflection across the critical line, means all exceptional off-line zeros have bounded height and positive minimum horizontal separation from the line. On \(\Re s=1/2\), let \(\|f\|_2\) denote the norm in measure \(dt/(2\pi)\). The boundary Mellin transform is unitary on \(L^2((0,\infty),dx)\), and support in \((0,1]\) gives \(H^2(\Re s>1/2)\). Its closed step subspace \(S\) comes from the cells \((1/(n+1),1/n]\), or from \([n,n+1)\) in the reciprocal physical coordinate with weight \(x^{-2}dx\). The Mellin image of its constant target is \(E=1/s\). The generator

\[
 G_n(s)=\frac{\zeta(s)}s(n^{-s}-n^{-1})
\]

is the transform of \(\lfloor x/n\rfloor-n^{-1}\lfloor x\rfloor\) in the reciprocal coordinate. Direct integration for \(\Re s>1\) followed by the bounded floor-difference formula proves the identification. Thus \(V_N=\operatorname{span}\{G_2,\ldots,G_N\}\), \(V=\overline{\cup_N V_N}\), \(d_N=\operatorname{dist}(E,V_N)\), and \(D_\infty=\operatorname{dist}(E,V)\). Anchoring is precisely \(A(1)=0\) for an integer Dirichlet polynomial \(A\) of length at most \(N\). Also \(\|f\|_* =\|f/s\|_2\), which fixes the Cauchy weight and all factors of \(2\pi\).

For \(u=P_VE\), the anchored conclusion is

\[
 \lim_N(\log N)(d_N^2-D_\infty^2)=
 \sum_{\rho\,\mathrm{distinct},\ \Re\rho=1/2}m_\rho^2|u(\rho)|^2.
\]

For the unrestricted distance \(\Delta_N=\inf_A\|(1-\zeta A)/s\|_2\), set \(H_0=\zeta/s-1/(s-1)\), \(h_S=P_SH_0\), \(\alpha=1+\|H_0-h_S\|_2^2\), and \(\Pi=P_{S\ominus V}\). Define

\[
 F(c)=\alpha|c|^2+\|\Pi(E-ch_S)\|_2^2,
 \qquad c_* =\frac{\langle\Pi E,\Pi h_S\rangle}{\alpha+\|\Pi h_S\|_2^2},
 \qquad u_c=P_V(E-ch_S),
\]

with the inner product linear in its first argument. Then \(\Delta_\infty^2=F(c_*)\) and

\[
 \lim_N(\log N)(\Delta_N^2-\Delta_\infty^2)=
 \sum_{\rho\,\mathrm{distinct},\ \Re\rho=1/2}m_\rho^2|u_{c_*}(\rho)|^2.
\]

These sums take both signs of the ordinates. Projection and conjugation \(f(s)\mapsto\overline{f(\bar s)}\) commute for the relevant real spaces and targets, so \(c_*\) is real and the two signed contributions at conjugate zeros agree. The spaces and minimizations use complex coefficients.

## Why the discrete projection is available

At each distinct \(\rho\in Z^+\), the jets \(f^{(j)}(\rho)\), \(0\le j<m_\rho\), are continuous by the half-plane Hardy kernel \(1/(s+\bar\rho-1)\) and its parameter derivatives. They vanish on \(V\) since all \(G_n\) contain the zeta factor. Independence on the dense step span of \(n^{-s}/s\) follows as in RST001 Lemma 2. A dependence summed against \(n^{-w}\) becomes an identity between jets of \(\zeta(s+w)/s\) for \(\Re w>1\). On continuation the pole at \(w=1-\rho\) of highest order isolates the highest nonzero jet coefficient at that \(\rho\). This gives surjectivity onto the finite jet data space and invertibility of its Gram matrix.

The reverse inclusion \(V=\ker J\) in the finite case needs approximation on the discrete span. Here is the check of RST001 Proposition 5. For a Dirichlet polynomial \(P\) vanishing at the exceptional jets, \(Q=P/\zeta\) is holomorphic on the right half-plane and subpower at high heights uniformly on each fixed smaller right half-plane. The high-height logarithm estimate used here is justified below. Finite-height poles cancel and \(Q(1)=0\). Take fixed \(0<r<1/4\) below the minimum exceptional gap when the set is nonempty, and a smooth cutoff \(\phi\) equal to 1 on \((-\infty,1]\) and 0 on \([2,\infty)\). For the Dirichlet series of \(Q\), multiply the \(n\)-th coefficient by \(\phi(\log n/\log R)\) to obtain \(Q_R\). The Mellin kernel is \(z^{-1}\int_1^2-\phi'(v)R^{zv}dv\), with residue 1 at zero and rapid vertical decay by integration by parts. Shift to \(\Re z=-r/2\) on \(\Re w=1/2+r\). This gives \(Q_R(w)-Q(w)=O_{r,\theta}(R^{-r/2}(2+|\Im w|)^\theta)\) for fixed \(\theta>0\). The anchored polynomials \([Q_R(s+r)-Q_R(1+r)]\zeta(s)/s\) converge in boundary norm to the same expression with \(Q\), using a sufficiently small \(\theta\) and the high-height bound for zeta on the line. The limit belongs to \(V\).

Let \(r\downarrow0\) with its upper bound fixed below the exceptional gap. The centered even Hadamard product for \(\xi(1/2+z)\) pairs roots \(\pm\gamma\). Each purely imaginary pair has distance factors nondecreasing from \(it\) to \(r+it\). For an exceptional pair choose its right member \(b+i\tau\), \(b>r\). The distance to the left member is nondecreasing, and the ratio of distances to the right member is at most \(b/(b-r)\). The product of these finitely many bounds, and the gamma factor by Stirling, give \(|\zeta(s)/\zeta(s+r)|\ll (2+|t|)^{r/2}\) on the line with a uniform constant for \(r\) in the fixed smaller range. A possible centered zero factor would satisfy the same monotone ratio. Dominated convergence gives \([Q(s+r)-Q(1+r)]\zeta(s)/s\to P(s)/s\) in boundary norm. Thus every such \(P/s\) belongs to \(V\). Choose finitely many Dirichlet polynomials whose jets are a basis, subtract their combinations from the dense Dirichlet-polynomial step span, and then use closedness of \(V\) plus a finite-dimensional span. This proves \(V=\ker J\) and \(S\ominus V=\operatorname{span}\{r_{\rho,j}\}\), where the latter are the step jet representers. That is the part of RST001 Theorems A and C needed here. [RST001 v2, §§2–4](../../sources/ratio62_rstation48/rstation_1_v2.md)

The defect is an actual step-space Gram projection. If \(q_a=E^{(j)}(\rho)\), \(a=(\rho,j)\), and \(\Gamma_{ab}=\langle r_b,r_a\rangle\), then \(D_\infty^2=q^*\Gamma^{-1}q\). The continuous Hardy projection into the Blaschke space has a different target when the exceptional set is nonempty. In that case the continuous projection of \(E\) is a nonzero multiple of \(B(s)/s\) for a nonconstant finite Blaschke product \(B\). Any \(f\in S\) satisfies \(\sigma f(\sigma)=f_1+O(\sqrt\sigma 2^{-\sigma})\) as real \(\sigma\to\infty\), by splitting the first cell from the rest and applying Cauchy–Schwarz. A nonconstant rational \(B(\sigma)\) cannot have this exponential approach to its limit. [RST001 v2, Theorem C](../../sources/ratio62_rstation48/rstation_1_v2.md)

## The unrestricted scalar and numerator regularity

In the full Mellin space \(H_0\) transforms \(-\{1/x\}\mathbf1_{(0,1]}\), while \(1/(s-1)\) transforms \(-x^{-1}\mathbf1_{(1,\infty)}\), orthogonal to \(H^2\) with norm 1. These signs follow by direct integration. Each \(\zeta A/s\) decomposes uniquely as \(v+cH_0+c/(s-1)\) with \(v\in V_N\), \(c=A(1)\). Every pair \((v,c)\) is realized since the coefficient at \(n=1\) remains free after selecting the generator combination. Orthogonal splitting gives the exact identity

\[
 \Delta_N^2=\min_c\{F(c)+\operatorname{dist}(u_c,V_N)^2\},
 \qquad d_N^2=D_\infty^2+\operatorname{dist}(u,V_N)^2.
\]

The scalar function satisfies \(F(c)-F(c_*)=(\alpha+\|\Pi h_S\|_2^2)|c-c_*|^2\). Density of the union of \(V_N\) in \(V\), tested at fixed \(c_*)\), gives \(\Delta_N^2\to F(c_*)\). If \(Z^+\ne\varnothing\), evaluation at any such zero shows \(\Pi E\ne0\), and \(F(c_*)\ge \alpha\|\Pi E\|_2^2/(\alpha+\|\Pi h_S\|_2^2)>0\). With empty \(Z^+\), both baselines vanish, \(V=S\), \(c_*=0\), and the targets coincide with \(E\). [RST001 v2, Proposition 6](../../sources/ratio62_rstation48/rstation_1_v2.md)

The reciprocal physical value of \(r_{\rho,j}\) on \([n,n+1)\) is \(n(n+1)\int_n^{n+1}(-\log x)^j x^{-\bar\rho-1}dx\). Substitution \(x=n+v\) gives size \(O(n^{1-\Re\rho}(1+\log n)^j)\). Differentiation in real \(n\) under this fixed-interval integral gives successive differences \(O(n^{-\Re\rho}(1+\log n)^j)\). The cell values of \(h_S\) are exactly \(-n(n+1)(\log(1+1/n)-1/(n+1))=-1/2+1/(6n)+O(n^{-2})\), so their differences are \(O(n^{-2})\). For either \(f=u\) or \(f=u_c\), step telescoping gives \(W(s)=sf(s)=\sum_{n\ge1}b_nn^{-s}\) with \(b_n\) the differences of cell values, including the first cell value at \(n=1\). Hence \(W\) and every fixed derivative are absolutely convergent and bounded on every closed half-plane strictly inside \(\Re s>1-\beta_*\), where \(\beta_* =\min_{Z^+}\Re\rho\). The empty-set case follows directly from the constant target and the \(h_S\) differences when needed. This gives genuinely analytic boundary values \(f(\rho)\). Because \(f\in V\), \(W\) cancels every right exceptional zero with its full multiplicity. The derivation uses only the strip part of RST004 Lemma 1. ## High-height bounds and the retained contour

For fixed \(\eta>0\) small and large \(|t|\), use the analytic logarithm of zeta in the disc about \(2+it\) of radius \(3/2-\eta/4\). The finite exceptional hypothesis ensures absence of any zero or pole there. Classical polynomial growth and Borel–Carathéodory give \(O_\eta(\log|t|)\) on the disc of radius \(3/2-\eta/2\). The Euler logarithm is uniformly bounded on a smaller disc about the same center of radius \(1/2\). Three circles then give \(\log\zeta=O_\eta((\log |t|)^{q_\eta})\), \(q_\eta<1\), on the disc of radius \(3/2-\eta\). This supplies both zeta and reciprocal subpower bounds on every fixed right substrip. The functional equation and strip interpolation between \(1/2\pm\eta\), taking \(\eta\) sufficiently small for the requested exponent, give the growth bound in (7) on and to either side of the line. Cauchy's estimate also gives \(\zeta'/\zeta=o(\log|t|)\) on any fixed right line.

The required local count can be checked without transferring the full RH error term in (8). At \(s=1/2+h+it\) for fixed \(h>0\), the real part of the centered Hadamard logarithmic derivative and Stirling give

\[
 \sum_{\gamma\,\mathrm{on\ line}}\frac{h}{h^2+(t-\gamma)^2}
   =\left(\frac12+o(1)\right)\log|t|.
\]

The finite exceptional factors contribute \(O_h(t^{-2})\) to the real part. Each zero with \(|\gamma-t|\le h\) contributes at least \(1/(2h)\), including multiplicity. With \(h=2D/3\) this gives \(k_C\le(2D/3+o(1))\log T_C\le D\log T_C\), the needed margin in (14). The leading coefficient \(h/\pi\) for a general fixed interval can also be obtained by taking the Poisson width \(\eta\) fixed and integrating the identity over \(t\pm(h+d)\) with \(d>0\). Each root in \(t\pm h\) contributes at least \(2\arctan(d/\eta)\), so the limsup coefficient is at most \((h+d)/(2\arctan(d/\eta))\). Let \(\eta\downarrow0\) and then \(d\downarrow0\). For good horizontal heights at a fixed scale, the classical local partial fractions for \(\zeta'/\zeta\) contain only on-line zeros in the bounded nearby window at high height. Integrate horizontally from \(1/2+\eta\) and average \(\log(1+\eta^2/(t-\gamma)^2)\) as in the source. The error integrates to \(O(\eta\log|t|)\), and the nonnegative sum averages \(O((\eta/\ell+\eta^2)\log|t|)\) on a fixed interval of length \(\ell\). Choose \(\eta\) after \(\ell\) and the desired exponent. The fixed right reciprocal estimate and the functional equation complete the good-height bound. Compact-height rectangles can keep the same spacing and avoid the finitely many ordinates. This proves the inputs used by the adaptive core under the finite exception hypothesis. The quotient \(Q=W/\zeta\) has only critical-line poles in \(\Re s>1/2-2\delta\). Every cluster is indexed by its full zeta multiplicity, including lowered or canceled poles of \(Q\). On the good contours \(|Q|\ll_W (2+|t|)^e\) with \(D=e=10^{-4}\), since \(W\) is bounded throughout them. With \(a=1/2-\delta\), define upward integrals \(J_0,J_N\) as in (94). ## Transfer of the upper construction

The numerator modifies the contour bounds on \(R_C\), the first-order indicator, and the inverse Laplace representation used in quadrature. All three changes use one fixed prefactor, because their initial contour expressions are linear in \(Q\). The near-zero division by cluster roots still uses \(\zeta\), its growth, and all its zeros with multiplicity. Thus the corrected-cluster tail \(\ll L^{-1}\sum T_C^{-2+1/100}\) from (41) and the first-order high-strip bound (49) still apply. The collective correction from (54) is the Dirichlet polynomial with coefficients \(n^{-1}\sum_{|\tau_C|\le N^{1/20}}h_C(\log n)\psi_C(\log n/L)\), where \(h_C\) is the contour inverse Laplace transform for \(Q\). The correction profile \(\psi_C\) vanishes through \(N^{1/4}\) in the integer variable and at \(N\). Its continuous counterpart and its discretization have the same derivative and boundary estimates (56)–(60) with the fixed prefactor. Taking a smaller \(\delta\) improves the powers in those bounds. This is ordinary integer support at most \(N\) throughout. ## Matching lower bound from the primary cutoff vectors

Burnol uses the inner multiplier \(\mathcal I=(s-1)/s\) to map anchored generators into cutoff dilates. Indeed with \(Z=(s-1)\zeta/s^2\), \(\mathcal I G_n=(n^{-s}-n^{-1})Z\), a combination of the dilates at \(1/n\) and 1 in Mellin space. Hence \(\mathcal I V_N\subset C_{1/N}\). The primary Corollary 4.4 makes \(Y^{1/N}_{\rho,k}\), \(k<m_\rho\), orthogonal to this space for each actual on-line zero and its multiplicity. Burnol's Theorem 5.3 gives the limiting Gram matrix of \(X_{\rho,k}=L^{-k-1/2}Y^{1/N}_{\rho,k}\) over a fixed finite zero set. Its blocks are \((1/(j+k+1))_{0\le j,k<m_\rho}\), with off-block limit zero. Neither input requires RH. [Burnol §§3–5](../../sources/equivalences/burnol.pdf)

The remaining pairing step works directly for any projected \(f=W/s\) above. Let \(\mathcal V(s)=(s/(1-s))^3\zeta(1-s)/\zeta(s)\) be Burnol's unitary multiplier. The functional equation turns the ratio into \(\pi^{1/2-s}\Gamma(s/2)/\Gamma((1-s)/2)\), analytic across the line in the strip under consideration. On the strip \([1/2-a_1,1/2]\) for sufficiently small fixed \(a_1>0\), \(\mathcal V\mathcal I f=-sW(s)(1-s)^{-2}\zeta(1-s)/\zeta(s)\) is analytic and uniformly \(O((2+|t|)^{-1})\) by Stirling. Strip Mellin–Plancherel therefore shows that the physical function \(g=(\mathcal V\mathcal I f)^\vee\) has \(x^{-a_1}g\in L^2\). Equivalently, shifting the Mellin line to \(1/2-a_1\) is ordinary Fourier–Plancherel after multiplication by \(x^{-a_1}\) in the logarithmic coordinate.

Burnol's definition and Theorems 4.7–4.11 give \(\mathcal VY^{1/N}_{\rho,k}=\mathbf1_{[1/N,\infty)}\Phi_{\rho,k}\) with \(\Phi_{\rho,k}=O(x^{-1/2}(1+|\log x|)^k)\) near zero and \(O((1+\log x)^2/x)\) at infinity. The bounds hold on compact parameter sets approaching \(\rho\) from the left, by applying the Hardy iterates to the original expressions. Cauchy–Schwarz with \(x^{-a_1}g\) at zero and \(g\in L^2\) at infinity gives the finite limit of \(\langle\mathcal I f,Y^{1/N}_{\rho,k}\rangle\). For \(k=0\), take parameters \(w\) to \(\rho\) from \(\Re w<1/2\). Then \(\Phi_{w,0}=\mathcal V(x^{-w}\mathbf1_{(0,1]})\), so unitarity identifies the limit before approaching the boundary as \((\mathcal I f)(1-\bar w)\). The same domination passes to the boundary and gives \((\mathcal I f)(\rho)\). Thus \(\sqrt L\langle\mathcal I f,X_{\rho,0}\rangle\to(\mathcal I f)(\rho)\), and for \(k\ge1\) it tends to zero by the extra \(L^{-k}\). Projection onto the finite witness span yields

\[
 \liminf_N L\operatorname{dist}(f,V_N)^2\ge
 \sum_{\rho\in R}m_\rho^2|f(\rho)|^2
\]

for every finite set \(R\) of critical zeros. The first inverse-block entry is \(m_\rho^2\), by the shifted Legendre evaluation kernel for polynomials of degree less than \(m_\rho\), and \(|\mathcal I(\rho)|=1\) on the line. As the distance is already to the projected target, there is no extra exceptional-jet projection of these witnesses. This recovers the RST001 Theorem F lower constant. [Burnol §§4–5](../../sources/equivalences/burnol.pdf) · [RST001 v2, Theorem F](../../sources/ratio62_rstation48/rstation_1_v2.md)

For the unrestricted limit, let \(c_N\) minimize the exact finite scalar objective. The quadratic gap and \(\Delta_N^2\to F(c_*)\) imply \(c_N\to c_*\). For each fixed witness set, the scaled target pairings converge uniformly on bounded \(c\) because \(u_c=u_0-cP_Vh_S\) and the pairing proof applies individually to these two targets. The Gram matrix is independent of \(c\). Therefore the same finite witness bound at \(c_N\) has limit \(\sum_{\rho\in R}m_\rho^2|u_{c_*}(\rho)|^2\). Dropping the nonnegative \(F(c_N)-F(c_*)\) and exhausting finite sets proves the lower bound matching the upper at fixed \(c_*\). This reproduces Corollary F′ without a rate estimate for \(c_N-c_*\). [RST001 v2, Corollary F′](../../sources/ratio62_rstation48/rstation_1_v2.md)

Twelve exact symbolic controls covered the sign of the derivative residues and the first-order taper identity at pole orders 1–6. A separate exact complex quadratic fixture checked the scalar normalization. The conclusion is the rate of convergence to the discrete projected baselines in the finite-exception setting. It leaves both the finiteness and the emptiness of the exceptional set unresolved.
