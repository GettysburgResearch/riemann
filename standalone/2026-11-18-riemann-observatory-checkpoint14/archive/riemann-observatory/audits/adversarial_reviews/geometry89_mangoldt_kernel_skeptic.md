## Kernel, sign, and best cubic coefficient

For a zero \(\alpha=\beta+i\gamma\) in the original \(t\)-plane, set \(d=T-\beta\), \(m=a+\gamma\) and \(F_d(x)=\arctan(x/d)\) on the principal real branch. The path on p. 6 has \(d\ne0\). Its continuous argument on the vertical line differs from the principal \arctangent expression by a constant on each side of the imaginary axis. The source's subtraction of the lower upward edge from the upper upward edge gives \(Q_a=2F_d(m)-F_d(m-a)-F_d(m+a)\). By differentiating the \arctangent and pairing points about the common edge,
\[
Q_a(d,\gamma)
=\int_0^a [F'_d(m-u)-F'_d(m+u)]\,du
=4dm\int_0^a \frac{u\,du}{[d^2+(m-u)^2][d^2+(m+u)^2]} .
\]
Because \(a>1/2\) and \(|\gamma|<1/2\), \(m>0\). Every term has the sign of \(d\), and
\[
 |Q_a(d,\gamma)|<\frac{2a^2(a+\gamma)}{|d|^3},
 \qquad \lim_{|d|\to\infty}|d|^3|Q_a(d,\gamma)|=2a^2(a+\gamma).
\]
The strict bound follows from the product of the two denominators being strictly greater than \(d^4\) for \(u>0\). The limit follows by dominated convergence in this finite integral. It proves sharpness as a uniform coefficient over unbounded \(|d|\) at each fixed \(a,\gamma\). The original p. 8 mean-value expression alone requires care for \(a<1\) and negative \(\gamma\), since its factor \(a(1+2\vartheta)+2\gamma\) can take either sign over hypothetical \(\vartheta\in(0,1)\). The integral settles the sign of the actual expression for the full stated range of \(a\).

The single-zero uniform coefficient is \(a^2(2a+1)\) from \(|\gamma|\le 1/2\). Each one-sided far multiset is closed under \(\gamma\mapsto-\gamma\), with equal multiplicities, so the \(\gamma\)-linear term cancels in its upper bound. This gives the smaller coefficient \(2a^3\) per far zero in (6):
\[
|\Phi_2-\Phi_3|
 \le \frac{\pi}{2}K+
    2a^3\max\!\left\{
        \sum_{\beta\le T-2k}|T-\beta|^{-3},
        \sum_{\beta\ge T+2k}|T-\beta|^{-3}
      \right\}.
\]
Every occurrence includes multiplicity. The pairing coefficient \(2a^3\) is sharp for the cubic bound on an isolated pair or a real zero, by the same limit. This sharpness concerns the kernel estimate and leaves room for cancellation in sums over different \(\beta\). The opposite-factor product on pp. 5–6 supplies the initial logarithmic increments. The cubic estimate plus local counts supplies absolute convergence for the edge difference, allowing the separation by side in p. 9.

## Near-window geometry and endpoints

Take \(d>0\), since replacing \(d\) by \(-d\) negates every increment. For one real zero, each edge increment lies in \((0,\pi/2)\), so their difference has magnitude \(<\pi/2\). For a conjugate pair, the upper increment sum is \(F_d(a+\gamma)+F_d(a-\gamma)\in(0,\pi)\). Both lower increments are positive and each is \(<\pi/2\), since \(a\pm\gamma>0\). Thus the magnitude of the pair's difference is \(<\pi\). A single nonreal factor can exceed \(\pi/2\). For fixed negative \(\gamma\), it tends to \(\pi\) as \(d\downarrow0\). Grouping conjugates is therefore essential to the \(\pi/2\) bound per zero counted. Each side's near contribution has a fixed sign and magnitude bounded by its own count times \(\pi/2\). Taking the absolute difference gives the larger of the two count bounds. The literal strict sign in the separate near estimate on pp. 7–8 needs \(K>0\). If \(K=0\), the near sum equals zero.

The inequalities in pp. 6 and 9 place \(|d|=2k\) in the far sum. The two near counts use the open intervals \((T-2k,T)\) and \((T,T+2k)\). The shells in (9) include the inner distance endpoint and exclude the outer one. The 1895 local-count lemma bounds a closed interval, so it covers either shell orientation without a boundary correction. On p. 2 the vertical line through \(T\) must avoid every zero projection \(\beta\). At an ordinate \(T_0\), when one holds \(a\) fixed and crosses \(T_0\), each real zero at this height changes its kernel contribution by \(\pi\). For an off-axis conjugate pair the two right-minus-left jumps are \(2\pi\) for the member with \(\gamma<0\), and zero for the member with \(\gamma>0\). This follows by taking the one-sided arctangent limits, since \(a\pm\gamma>0\). With multiplicity this gives exactly \(\pi m(T_0)\), where \(m(T_0)\) is the total order of all zeros at the ordinate. All exterior terms are continuous and the other kernel terms are continuous locally, by finiteness on compact sets and the summable uniform tail. Consequently the right count includes all copies, the left count excludes them, and the midpoint convention adds \(m(T_0)/2\) to the left count. The printed numerical inequality passes to either limit with a weak inequality. [1905 contour and split](../../sources/geometry81/mangoldt1905_gdz.pdf#page=3)

## The logarithmic order and its dependencies

Section 3 fixes \(a=3/2\) and \(k=\kappa=\tan1\). The cited 1895 lemma gives a count \(<k\log h\) on \([h-k,h+k]\) for \(\tan1\le k\le h-4\). This directly covers the large positive intervals of length \(2\kappa\). For negative centers reflect about zero and for centers with \(|h|<4+\kappa\) use the finite count in one compact interval. The latter contribution fits below the positive-center bound on p. 11 for all sufficiently large \(T\), uniformly in the shell index. This makes explicit the small-center qualification in the invocation of symmetry on that page. Near counts are \(O(\log T)\). Each far shell has count \(O(\log(2+T+\nu))\) and contributes \(O(\nu^{-3}\log(2+T+\nu))\). Summing gives \(O(\log T)\), since \(\sum_{\nu\ge1}\nu^{-3}\log(1+\nu)\) converges. Formula (8) then yields the asserted \(O(\log T)\) count error, with the exterior Euler logarithms uniformly bounded at the two fixed abscissae. The local multiplicity estimate is an input. Subtracting the 1895 global \(O(\log^2 T)\) count at two nearby heights would only give an \(O(\log^2 T)\) upper bound for the local count. In the general paired-product formulation, the local count and bounded strip suffice for the kernel estimate. The count asymptotic also requires the argument principle and exterior asymptotics for the function in question. ## Numerical relevance and current interfaces

In the historical §4 bound, keeping the original near-window estimate and substituting the conjugate-paired far coefficient replaces the limiting coefficient \(5/8\) by \(1/4\) in the far term as \(a\downarrow1/2\). At \(k=0.675\) this would change the leading logarithmic coefficient from approximately \(0.43194\) to \(0.38976\). This calculation concerns the leading term, with no new finite-height decimal envelope certified. Even the near term alone has leading coefficient \(k/[2\arctan(2k)]>1/4\) at each fixed \(k>0\). For comparison, Hasanalizade–Shen–Wong v1, Corollary 1.2 (1.5), supplies the inclusive count with leading error coefficient \(0.1038\) for \(T\ge e\). Centering that bound at the historical \(7/8\) costs at most \(7/8\) in its additive constant. The new kernel bound therefore improves a historical intermediate estimate without improving the modern total-count input used in the observatory.
