## Ye's windows and the weighted maximum

Ye defines \(N(\sigma;U,U+U^a)\) using the strict real-part threshold \(\beta>\sigma\), closed ordinate interval and analytic multiplicity. For Theorem 1.1 and the fourth moment, set \(\ell=2\) and \(\theta=\max(a,2/3)\). The uniform hypothesis for \(a<2/3\) follows by taking the positive fourth moment integral over the larger window with the identical real start. The Type II denominator is exactly 2. Thus the exponent is \((1-\sigma)\max(15a/(3+5\sigma),2\max(a,2/3))\) for \(\sigma\ge3/5\). For \(a=3/5\), the weighted exponent has derivative \(2-72/(3+5\sigma)^2\ge0\) up to \(3/4\) and then equals \(1-2(1-\sigma)/3\) as a function of \(\sigma\). The total count covers the initial segment through \(3/5+1/L\). Multiplying \(O(T^{3/5}L)\) by its largest weight \(O(T^{1/5}/L^2)\) gives the displayed \(O(T^{4/5}/L)\). Each remaining cell uses a counted threshold below its left endpoint, which protects equality cases. On that range the squared denominator has order \(L^2\) and \(O(L)\) cells give the claimed net factor \(L^{-1}\). [Ye Theorem 1.1 and (2.17)](../../sources/latest62_density_frontier/2610.15989.pdf)

Ye's Theorem 1.2 has loss \(\ll_{b,\varepsilon}\) for fixed \(2/3\le b\le1\) and a piecewise profile \(b\mathcal B(\sigma)\). On \([1/2,7/10]\), \(\mathcal B=3(1-\sigma)/(2-\sigma)\) and the derivative of the weighted exponent \(2\sigma-1+b\mathcal B\) is \(2-3b/(2-\sigma)^2>0\). On \([7/10,v]\) with \(3/4<v<4/5\), \(\mathcal B=15(1-\sigma)/(3+5\sigma)\) and \(\mathcal B''=1200/(3+5\sigma)^3>0\). The values of \(\mathcal B\) agree at \(7/10\) and equal \(9/13\). It follows that the maximum through \(v\) is the larger of \(2/5+9b/13\) and \(2v-1+15b(1-v)/(3+5v)\). At \(b=13/15\) the first is exactly 1 while the second at \(v=3/4\) is \(53/54\). For each fixed smaller \(b\) there is a fixed \(v>3/4\) with strict slack. A mesh absorbs the strict threshold shift, and the unit-window bound near \(1/2\) fits because \(2/5+9b/13>b\) for \(b\le1\). Add the all-height upper tail once for a union of windows. Since all weights are positive, nesting inside a single \(U^{2/3}\) window also proves the same \(o(T)\) result for every fixed \(0<b<2/3\). Covering a length \(O(T^b)\) window at height \(U\asymp T\) takes only a bounded number of the native windows. If that interval extends above \(T\), apply the upper tail result at a constant multiple of \(T\), whose weight is uniformly comparable. [Ye Theorem 1.2](../../sources/latest62_density_frontier/2610.15989.pdf)

For full dyadic coverage, \(1-b+b\mathcal B(\sigma)\) is minimized at \(b=1\) since \(\mathcal B\le1\). On \([1/2,3/4]\) the displayed branch coefficient of \(1-\sigma\) is at least 2. Likewise \(1-a+F_a(\sigma)\) for Theorem 1.1 is the maximum of the Type I affine term with slope \(-1+15(1-\sigma)/(3+5\sigma)\le0\) and Type II terms with slopes \(-1\) and \(-1+2(1-\sigma)\le0\). This covering step cannot improve the global weighted conclusion.

## Two persistent curves in one all-height model

Put \(z=\log t\). For the near-line curve use \(\beta_c=1/2+z^{-3/4}\) and \(\lambda_c=(1+\sqrt z)e^{-2z^{1/4}}\). For the join curve use \(\beta_j=3/4+1/z\) and \(\lambda_j=e^{-2}t^{-1/2}z^2/32\). For large fixed \(t_0\) these rates are decreasing and less than 1. The first logarithmic derivative in \(z\) is at most \((2z)^{-1}-(1/2)z^{-3/4}<0\), and the second is \(-1/2+2/z<0\). With \(F_i(t)=\int_{t_0}^t\lambda_i\), the cumulative count of crossings is exactly \(\lfloor F_i(t)\rfloor\) and the count in a closed real interval is at most \(1+F_i(B)-F_i(A)\) when both ends lie above \(t_0\). Intervals touching the initial height are handled by restricting to \([t_0,\infty)\). Adding same-height reflected points doubles each positive-ordinate crossing's contribution. Conjugation separately adds the negative ordinates.

Fill each half-open unit interval \((n,n+1]\) with distinct line ordinates to achieve the difference of consecutive floors of \(R(t)=\tfrac{t}{2\pi}(\log(t/(2\pi))-1)\). This difference is asymptotic to \((2\pi)^{-1}\log n\) and eventually exceeds the bounded prescribed count. At integer endpoints this gives the floor with a bounded initial offset. At arbitrary real height, both the count and \(R(t)\) change by \(O(\log(2+t))\) over a unit interval, proving the Riemann–von Mangoldt main term with its \(O(\log T)\) remainder and the unit-window bound. Distinct locations may share an ordinate when prescribed by symmetry, so all points can have multiplicity 1. The two curves occupy disjoint real-part ranges for the chosen \(t_0\).

For the near-line sequence, at an occupied strict local threshold \(\sigma\le\beta_c(U)\), Ye's \(\mathcal B\) is at least \(1-(3/2)(\log U)^{-3/4}\). Therefore \(U^a\lambda_c(U)\ll U^{a\mathcal B(\sigma)}\) for \(0<a\le1\), uniformly in such thresholds, since the exponential margin against the logarithmic factor is at least \((1/2)(\log U)^{1/4}\). The curve is below \(3/5\), so Theorem 1.1 counts none. For an inclusive global threshold above \(1/2\), monotonicity reduces each nonempty inverse image to an initial height interval ending at \(X\le T\). Since \((t\lambda_c(t))'/\lambda_c(t)\to1\), its count is \(O(X\lambda_c(X))\). The same estimate with \(a=1\) is at most the first Palomar branch at \(X\), which is also at most the corresponding power of \(T\). The occupied real parts can be taken below \(0.55\), where both quoted Guth–Maynard exponents and the uniform \(39/17\) exponent exceed 1. At \(\sigma=1/2\) the inclusive global count is \(O(T\log T)\), fitting the fixed positive loss. The strict local threshold at \(1/2\) omits the fillers.

For the join sequence the local count is \(O(1+U^{a-1/2}(\log U)^2)\). At occupied thresholds the first term of Theorem 1.1's maximum is at least \(5a/9-O(1/\log U)\), and Theorem 1.2 has the same lower bound. The exponent slack is at least \(1/18\), since \(5a/9-(a-1/2)=1/2-4a/9\ge1/18\). In the global inclusive count, truncate at the last allowed height \(X\) as above. Its count is \(O(X^{1/2}(\log X)^2)\). The right Palomar branch decreases with derivative of magnitude at most \(8/3\) there, so the exponent at every occupied threshold is at least \(1/2-(8/3)/\log X\). Its left branches are all at least \(9/16\) for \(\sigma\le3/4\), by their explicit decreasing formulas. This proves even a uniform fixed-\(\varepsilon\) estimate for this model across the exact piecewise curve, because the exponential loss is a fixed factor and \((\log X)^2\ll_\varepsilon X^\varepsilon\). All quoted Guth–Maynard and uniform \(39/17\) exponents are above \(1/2\) through \(0.77\). The Pintz exponent there is also above \(1/2\) on its occupied range. The upper ranges covered by the no-loss bound and by the near-one input are empty. Both ordinate signs in the inclusive estimates add only a fixed factor.

For the limiting weights, write \(w_i(t)=G(L(\beta_i(t)-1/2))\) with \(L=\log T\). This decreases in \(t\). Stieltjes integration by parts and \(|\lfloor F_i\rfloor-F_i|\le1\) bound the discretization error by \(w_i(T)+\int_{t_0}^T|w_i'(t)|dt=w_i(t_0)=o(T)\). The minimum displacements at \(t=T\) tend to infinity after multiplication by \(L\). For \(t\le\sqrt T\), the near-line contribution is \(O(T^{1/2+1/10})\) using its largest real part and bounded rate. The join contribution is \(O(T^{1/4}L^2T^{1/2+2/\log t_0})=o(T)\). For \(t=uT\ge\sqrt T\), the near-line integrand after division by \(T\) is

\[
 \frac{1+\sqrt z}{1+L^2z^{-3/2}}e^{2(L-z)z^{-3/4}},\qquad z=\log(uT).
\]

It tends to 1 for fixed \(u>0\). The fraction is at most 1 since \(z\le L\), and the exponential is at most \(u^{-1/2}\) for large \(T\) on the given range. The join integrand is

\[
 \frac{e^{-2}}{32}u^{-1/2}\frac{z^2 e^{2L/z}}{1+L^2(1/4+1/z)^2},
\]

bounded by a constant times \(u^{-1/2}\) since \(L/2\le z\le L\), and with pointwise limit \(u^{-1/2}/2\). Dominated convergence gives 1 from each curve and \(M_T/T\to2\). The displaced count is \(O(T\lambda_c(T)+T^{1/2}L^2)=o(T)\), hence \(D_T(1/2)=o(T)\) and compact-uniform \(r_T\to0\) follow. This construction fixes all ordinates once by the two primitives, so each of these limits is along all real heights.

## The endpoint window model

At \(X_n=4^n\), place \(K_n=\lfloor X_n^{3/5}(\log X_n)^2/25\rfloor\) equally spaced right copies at \(\beta=7/10\) in the block of length \(X_n^{13/15}\). The spacing tends to infinity, so the same reflection and line-fill construction works. A length-\(U^a\) interval meeting a block requires \(U/2\le X_n\le2U\), hence encounters at most two such blocks. The number from each is \(O(1+\log^2 U\min(U^{3/5},U^{a-4/15}))\). The exponent minimum is at most \(9a/13\) on both sides of \(a=13/15\) and equals it there. Both Ye bounds are at least this exponent on their applicable occupied thresholds, and every fixed positive power loss absorbs the logarithm. The global sum of the \(K_n\) through height \(T\) is \(O(T^{3/5}(\log T)^2)\) by geometric summation, including thresholds weaker than \(7/10\). The smallest exact Palomar exponent in that range is \(54/79>3/5\), and the Guth–Maynard and separate uniform exponents also give strict slack. The global count at \(1/2\) includes the line fillers and still fits the positive loss.

At the sequence of heights \(T=X_n+X_n^{13/15}\), the selected block's weight per copy is \(G((\log T)/5)\sim25X_n^{2/5}/(\log X_n)^2\). Multiplying by \(K_n\) gives a ratio to \(T\) tending to 1, because the single floor error contributes \(o(T)\). The construction is global even though the lower bound tests only a subsequence of windows. This is enough to rule out a uniform \(o(T)\) deduction at the endpoint from the stated inequalities.

## Remaining weighted-tail condition

The proposed dyadic criterion suffices. A uniform bound \(\int_Y^{2Y}r_T^{<}(y)\,dy\ll Y^{2-\kappa}\) contributes \(O(Y^{-\kappa})\) to the weighted integral. Sum over dyadic shells beginning at a fixed large \(Y_0\) to get \(O(Y_0^{-\kappa})\) uniformly in height. On each fixed compact range, \(D_T^{<}(1/2)=o(T)\) bounds \(r_T^{<}\) uniformly by \(o(1)\). The layer identity then gives the claimed little-oh. The counting models exclude deriving the full weighted moment from the inspected envelopes alone. The BGSTB signed far-pair proof and the independent Simple673 proof have separate hypotheses and verification scope.
