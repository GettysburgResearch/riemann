## Uniform Perron deformation

Fix x large, X=floor(x)+1/2, U=log X, and |t|≤x^(3/4). The difference between x and X changes only absolute constants in the final estimates. Apply truncated Perron at vertical height X in the variable z−it to the Dirichlet series 1/ζ(z), with Re z=c=1+1/U. The series coefficients in the shifted variable are μ(n)n^(−it) and have absolute value≤1. To check the O(log X) error, bound the usual truncation sum by Σ (X/n)^c min(1,(X|log(X/n)|)^(−1)). For X/2≤n≤2X, this is O(Σ 1/|X−n|)=O(log X), because the endpoint is half integral. Outside that range, it is O(X^(c−1)ζ(c))=O(log X). After recentring the two endpoints to ordinates ±X, the added integrals have length |t| and denominator |z−it|≍X. Their size is O(|t|log X), by |1/ζ(c+iv)|≤ζ(c)≪log X. Multiplication by |ζ(1/2+it)| and use of RH Lindelöf with a fixed η<1/6 absorb this error into √X(1+|t|)^(1/2), since for t≥1 its quotient by that expression is O(log X·X^(−1/2+(3/4)(1/2+η))). The same is immediate for bounded t.

All contours lie in Re z>1/2. Under RH the reciprocal is holomorphic there, with a zero rather than a pole at z=1. The kernel pole z=it is outside the deformation. The powers X^(z−it) use the real logarithm of X. Conjugation handles negative t, so take t≥0. Let X₀=exp(φ(U)), φ(U)=√(U/log U). Use the BFH baseline contour with shift b(X₀) centrally and b(Y) at dyadic panels of height Y, where b(Y) is as above.

On a baseline vertical panel away from v≈t the denominator is ≫Y and its integral is O(√X exp(b(X₀)U+U^(1/3))) by the reciprocal first moment on [Y,2Y]. This holds also for a partial terminal panel by positivity of the reciprocal moment. There are O(U) panels and b(X₀)U=O(φ(U)); both U^(1/3) and log U are o(φ(U)). Old dyadic connectors away from v≈t have shifts β comparable to b(Y), |1/ζ(1/2+β+iv)|≤exp((1/2+o(1))log Y) by (2.8), and bounded length. Their denominator is likewise ≫Y, so they fit the first contribution √X exp(C_*φ(U)).

For the central segment and boundedly many adjacent panels within 16X₀ if t<4X₀, use reciprocal bound exp(O(φ(U))). On fixed bounded v this follows by local factorization at finitely many critical-line zeros, with at worst a fixed power of b(X₀)^(−1). On larger v up to 16X₀ it follows from BFH (8.1), or from the full (2.8) since log(1/b(X₀))=O(log U) and the largest log v is φ(U)+O(1). This use of the full source error is valid even when K is not O(log log v) at the lower end. Uniformly in center t, integrating (1+|v−t|)^(−1) on an interval of length O(X₀) costs O(1+log X₀). Thus these pieces also fit the first contribution.

At v=±X the final connector's denominator is ≍X. Its shift runs from b≈1/(U log U) to 1/2+1/U. For 0<b≤α≤1/4, the modulus of ξ at fixed v is nondecreasing with horizontal shift α under RH. This follows termwise from the paired product used in C103 Proposition 4. Combining reciprocal bounds at b, Stirling for the change in completion factor and the Perron factor X^(α−1/2) gives a bound exp((3/2)αU+O(U/log U)) for the connector integrand. Its maximum is X^(3/8+o(1)). For 1/4≤α≤1/2+1/U, start the monotonicity comparison at 1/4, where the reciprocal is exp(o(U)), and use X^(α−1/2)≤e. The completion ratio costs at most X^(1/8+o(1)), so this part also fits the first contribution. This directly checks both terminal connectors.

## Near t, both choices of the replacement shift

When t≥4X₀, select dyadic endpoints a₁∈(t/4,t/2] and a₂∈[2t,4t) and replace all positive panels between them. The endpoints exist since t/4≥X₀ and 4t<X for large x. Outside the replacement the positive baseline denominator is ≫|v|, because v≤t/2 or v≥2t. The same holds on all negative panels. Put w=log t and a=min(U^(−1/2),w/(U log w)) on the single replacement vertical. There log v=w+O(1), φ(U)≤w+O(1)≤U+O(1), and log w≍log U. The little-o condition a log log v=o(1) and the condition K=O(log log v) hold uniformly on this entire range. In using (2.8), replacing log v by w=log t changes the bound by no more than the retained O(w/log w) loss. RH critical-line ζ growth contributes another O(w/log w) to the exponent after multiplying by |ζ(1/2+it)|.

After division by √X t^(1/2), the exponential upper bound is
    aU+[w/(2log w)](log(1/a)−log log w−log w+O(1)).
In the uncapped branch a=w/(U log w), this is w/(2log w)(log(U/w²)+O(1)). If the bracket is positive, bound the positive part by O(√U/log U), since r log(K/r²) is bounded above on r>0 for any fixed K, taking r=w/√U. If it is nonpositive the same bound is automatic. In the capped branch a=U^(−1/2), one has w≥√U log w. Hence the bracket is at most −2log log w+O(1), which absorbs the cost aU=√U uniformly once U is sufficiently large. The kernel integral over the replacement vertical costs O(1+w), absorbed into exp(D_*√U/log U).

On the two connecting horizontals at v=a₁,a₂, the shifts β lie between a and a baseline neighbor b(Y) with Y≍t. Every such β has β log w=o(1) and log(1/β)≤log w+O(log log U). For a in the uncapped branch, the latter inequality follows from U/w²≪log U; in the capped branch it follows from w≥√U log w; at b(Y) it follows directly. Therefore (2.8) gives |1/ζ(1/2+β+iv)|≤exp(2w/3) for all large U. Since |v−t|≫t, the denominator contributes exp(−w), leaving exp(−w/3). The Perron shift loss is at most exp(Cφ(U)+w/log w) by βU≤Cφ(U)+w/log w. Bounded length, and absorption of the w/log w cost by the remaining negative term, place both connectors in the first contribution. This exhausts the deformed contour and proves the candidate's uniform bound (3), with absolute constants C_*,D_* depending on the fixed source inputs.

## Independence of A>C from the contour constant

Put L=log N, ε=A/√(L log L), and use the Mertens exponent C in the untwisted Abel and Plancherel argument. It bounds the Fourier norm by a polynomial in ε^(−1) times exp(−(A−C)φ(L)). The separate Abel endpoint term uses the full finite norm of ζ(s)/s. For T₀=exp(Bφ(L)) with any fixed B, the RH critical-line pointwise bound gives sup_{|t|≤T₀}|ζ(1/2+it)|=exp(o(φ(L))). Thus for any A>C the low-height truncation norm is exponentially small in φ(L). Since φ(L)=o(L), the cutoff T₀ lies below N^(3/4) for every fixed B and sufficiently large N.

From T₀ to N^(3/4), the twisted Abel formula is obtained by factoring n^(−it) into the summands. Its upper endpoint vanishes and the integral converges for each fixed t,ε. This also follows directly from (3) when ζ(s)≠0, or from untwisted partial summation with any fixed t; at zeros the multiplied expression vanishes. For the whole application (3) is eligible throughout x≥N. Its first contribution in the Abel integral uses φ(U)≤φ(L)√(U/L), so
    C_*φ(U)−εU≤[C_*²/(2A)]φ(L)−εU/2
for U≥L. Endpoint and integral then cost at most exp(B₁φ(L)) times |ζ(s)| with fixed B₁. Convexity gives ∥ζ(s)/s∥_{2,|t|≥T₀}≪T₀^(−1/8) on choosing the auxiliary convexity exponent small. Picking B>8(B₁+1) makes this contribution exponentially small. The large choice of fixed B still permits the low-height estimate and does not change ε or the requirement A>C.

In the second contribution D_*√U/log U≤(A/4)φ(U) uniformly for U≥L large. The supporting tangent to φ gives exponential decay in U−L with rate ≍ε. Both the endpoint and integral therefore cost at most (1+ε^(−1))exp(−3Aφ(L)/4)(1+|t|)^(1/2). The weighted squared norm over these middle heights adds a factor O(L) before taking the square root. This is also exponentially small. An equivalent cutoff suggested by the proponent is T₀=exp(√L). At this height the low supremum still has log bound O(√L/log L)=o(φ(L)), and the first middle contribution has factor exp(B₁φ(L)−√L/8), which is smaller than every fixed power of L. Proposition 13 on the remaining heights has polynomially decaying norm in N. All three regions combined yield classical truncation o(√ε).

Minkowski and C103 Proposition 2 now give the candidate d₁(P_N)=O((log N log log N)^(−1/4)) on the full sequence for each fixed A>C. With the separately adjudicated exact ratio slope, the same o(√ε) comparison yields d₁(P_N)²∼ε S₁. This latter inference depends on that separate slope result and concerns this prescribed array.
