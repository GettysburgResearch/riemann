Theorems 1.1–1.2 restate continuity on a closed disc and holomorphy inside but omit nonvanishing, which is explicit in the earlier quoted Theorem A. Both new proofs take log f, requiring that additional target restriction. The surrounding goal of shortening Theorem A explains the intended class but does not supply it in the literal restated quantifiers. Classification: missing or insufficiently stated hypothesis, with a specific corrected class. This is not a claim to invalidate the intended nonvanishing-target theorem.

For an exact witness let s0=3/4, r=1/8, f(s)=s-s0, epsilon=1/16 and B=20. The target meets the literal stated assumptions. Rouché forces each good shifted disc to contain a zero with real part greater than 5/8. Under RH the good-shift set is therefore empty for every window, in conflict with the literal RH-conditional Theorem 1.1 conclusion for this target. Unconditionally the cited zero-density input gives total good-shift measure O(X^(15/16)log^5 X)=o(X), and a polylog-window covering argument excludes good shifts for density-one starts. This does not exclude exceptional short-window starts and DOES NOT refute the unconditional limsup of Theorem 1.2 by that counting argument. A limsup over short exceptional windows is not a global upper density.

The repaired logarithmic proof uses targets nonvanishing on the closed disc; dilation extends it to zero-free interior targets continuous on the boundary. The identically zero function is separately a uniform limit of nonzero constants, so 'every target with a zero fails' would also be an overstatement.
