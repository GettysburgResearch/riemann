# Independent audit of the finite off-line extension in RST048 v2

The scoped reconstruction supports both limits in Theorem 6 under its finite off-line zero hypothesis. The projection is onto the discrete integer-cell span. The retained contour term is \(O(1/\log N)\) in the full boundary norm, and the unrestricted problem has an exact extra scalar penalty from the pole at \(s=1\). This review uses the previously reconstructed adaptive profile estimates and gives an independent lower-bound transfer using the compact tests of paper 47. It derives the high-height cluster count needed by the extension directly, without requiring the source's exact RH argument for the error in (8). ## Discrete projection and the full boundary norm

Use the critical-line boundary Hilbert space \(L^2(dt/2\pi)\) and \(\|F\|_* = \|F/s\|\). In the physical Mellin coordinates \(x=1/t\) for the original variable \(t\in(0,1)\), the Hardy space corresponds to \(x\ge1\) with measure \(dx/x^2\). Let \(S\) be its closed subspace of functions constant on integer cells, \(E=1/s\), \(V_N=\operatorname{span}\{G_2,\ldots,G_N\}\) for \(G_n=\zeta(s)(n^{-s}-n^{-1})/s\), and \(V=\overline{\bigcup V_N}\). A polynomial \(A\) of length \(N\) gives \(\zeta(A-A(1))/s\in V_N\), and every element of \(V_N\) arises this way with the same length. Writing \(u=P_VE\), the exact formula is
\[
 d_N^2=D_\infty^2+\operatorname{dist}(u,V_N)^2.
\]

The finite jet synthesis ingredient from paper 1 can be seen as follows. Every jet of order below multiplicity at a right-hand zero vanishes on \(V\). The jets are independent on the dense span of \(n^{-s}/s\) in \(S\). Indeed sum a vanishing linear combination on these test functions with weights \(n^{-w}\). The result is the same linear combination on \(\zeta(s+w)/s\), analytically zero for \(\Re w>1\) and then meromorphically zero. At each \(w=1-\rho\), the highest nonzero jet coefficient would give a unique highest-order pole. To get the reverse inclusion, choose a finite family of Dirichlet polynomials whose jet vectors span the finite jet space. A Dirichlet polynomial \(P\) whose jets at those zeros vanish gives \(P/s\in V\). For this step, \(q=P/\zeta\) is holomorphic in \(\Re s>1/2\), including the finite exceptions as removable points. For fixed sufficiently small \(\epsilon>0\), the smooth Dirichlet cutoffs of \(q(s+\epsilon)\) converge after subtracting their values at \(1+\epsilon\) and multiplying by \(\zeta(s)/s\). More explicitly a smooth log-length cutoff equal to 1 through length \(M\) and zero from \(M^2\) has Perron kernel \(\Phi_M(z)/z\), with \(\Phi_M(0)=1\) and arbitrary inverse powers of \(1+|z|\log M\) from integration by parts. Shift to \(\Re z=-\epsilon/2\). The fixed right-abscissa bound for \(q\) gives the difference \(O_{\epsilon,\theta}(M^{-\epsilon/2}(1+|t|)^\theta)\), with arbitrarily small fixed \(\theta>0\). This is square integrable after multiplication by \(\zeta(s)/s\). The anchored limit therefore lies in \(V\).

To send \(\epsilon\downarrow0\), pair the zeros of the even function \(\xi(1/2+z)\) as \(\pm\alpha\) in its Hadamard product. The factors from critical-line zeros have modulus at \(z=it\) at most their modulus at \(z=\epsilon+it\). For each off-line pair with horizontal distance \(b>0\) the ratio is at most \(b/(b-\epsilon)\), uniformly in \(t\), when \(\epsilon<b\). The finite product of these ratios is bounded for \(\epsilon\) smaller than half the least such distance. The gamma quotient is \(O((2+|t|)^{\epsilon/2})\). Thus dominated convergence applies to \(P(s+\epsilon)\zeta(s)/(s\zeta(s+\epsilon))\) on the line. The anchor term tends to zero since \(q(1+\epsilon)\to0\). Subtracting the fixed spanning polynomials from a dense family and taking closure now gives \(V=\ker J\) in \(S\), where \(J\) is the full finite jet map. In particular \(S\ominus V\) is spanned by the step-projected jet representers, including all jets through one less than the multiplicity. [Paper 1, Lemmas 1–4, Proposition 5 and Theorems A and C](../../sources/ratio62_rstation48/rstation_1_v2.md)

For the unrestricted problem, \(H_0=\zeta/s-1/(s-1)\) is the Mellin transform of \(-\{x\}\) on \(x\ge1\). The summand \(1/(s-1)\) is the boundary transform of \(-x\mathbf1_{(0,1)}(x)\), orthogonal to the Hardy space and of squared norm 1. With \(h_S=P_SH_0\), \(\kappa=\|H_0-h_S\|^2\), and \(\Pi=P_{S\ominus V}\), the full residual splits orthogonally because
\[
 \frac{\zeta A}{s}=v_N+cH_0+\frac{c}{s-1},
 \qquad c=A(1),\quad v_N\in V_N .
\]
The unrestricted first coefficient makes \((v_N,c)\) range over exactly \(V_N\times\mathbb C\). Projection in \(S\) then gives (101). Set \(u_c=P_V(E-ch_S)\), and
\[
 F(c)=(1+\kappa)|c|^2+\|\Pi(E-ch_S)\|^2,\qquad
 \Lambda=1+\kappa+\|\Pi h_S\|^2.
\]
There is a unique minimizing \(c_*\). Since \(V_N\) increases to \(V\) in closure, the exact finite identity becomes
\[
 \Delta_N^2-\Delta_\infty^2
 =\min_c\left\{\Lambda|c-c_*|^2+\operatorname{dist}(u_c,V_N)^2\right\}.
\]
In particular \(\Delta_\infty^2=F(c_*)\). A right zero forces \(D_\infty>0\) by bounded evaluation at that zero, and \(F(c)=0\) would force both \(c=0\) and \(\Pi E=0\). The positive limiting distance is included on both sides of the rate statement. [Paper 1, Proposition 6](../../sources/ratio62_rstation48/rstation_1_v2.md)

A finite half-plane Blaschke product \(B\) acts isometrically on the boundary, but the continuous projection of \(E\) is a scalar multiple of \(B/s\). In the nonempty case \(B/s\notin S\). For \(f\in S\), its first cell and Cauchy–Schwarz on the cells \(x\ge2\) give \(\sigma f(\sigma)=f_1+O_f(\sqrt\sigma\,2^{-\sigma})\) as \(\sigma\to\infty\), incompatible with a nonconstant rational \(B(\sigma)\). The scalar is nonzero because \(B(1)\ne0\). Thus replacing the discrete projected target by the Blaschke target changes the geometry. [Paper 1, Theorem C](../../sources/ratio62_rstation48/rstation_1_v2.md)

## Target regularity and high-height contour inputs

A representer for the \(j\)-th jet at \(\rho\) has cell value as in (92). Extending the index \(n\) to a real \(y\ge1\), that expression is
\[
 y(y+1)\int_0^1(-\log(y+v))^j(y+v)^{-\bar\rho-1}\,dv .
\]
Differentiation bounds the value by \(O(y^{1-\Re\rho}(1+\log y)^j)\) and its derivative by \(O(y^{-\Re\rho}(1+\log y)^j)\). The cell values of \(h_S\) are \(-\int_0^1 v(1+1/n)/(1+v/n)^2\,dv\). Their successive differences are \(O(n^{-2})\) since the integrand is differentiable with bounded derivative as a function of \(1/n\). Both \(u\) and \(u_*=u_{c_*}\) are finite linear combinations of these representers, \(E\), and \(h_S\), as follows from finite jet synthesis. Denote either by \(U\). If \(\beta_*\) is the least real part of the finitely many right-hand zeros, then its successive cell differences satisfy (93), so \(W=sU\) and all fixed derivatives are bounded on each \(\Re s\ge1-\beta_*+\epsilon\). The numerator vanishes to full multiplicity at every right-hand zero because \(U\in V\). In the empty case \(U=E\) for both targets. [RST048 v2, (92)–(93)](../../sources/ratio62_q1_priority/rstation_48_v2.md) · [Paper 4, Lemma 1](../../sources/ratio62_rstation48/rstation_4_v1.md)

Fix \(0<\delta\le10^{-6}\) with \(4\delta<\beta_*-1/2\) in the nonempty case. With \(a=1/2-\delta\), \(G=W/\zeta\) has only critical-line poles to the right of \(a\). The reflected exceptions lie to its left. A boundary zero where \(W\) has order \(r\) has pole order at most \(m-r\). The cluster polynomial still includes all \(m\) copies of that zero. This keeps the Cauchy divided-difference bound for \(\zeta\) and handles all reduced and canceled Laurent coefficients.

Here are the contour inputs with the finite exceptions kept explicit. At large \(|t|\) choose a zero-free disk about \(2+it\) contained in \(\Re s>1/2\), with radius just below \(3/2\). Borel–Carathéodory for the branch of \(\log\zeta\) anchored at the center gives \(O(\log |t|)\) in a smaller disk by unconditional polynomial growth. On a fixed disk of radius below 1 the Euler series gives \(O(1)\). Three circles on the logarithm gives \(O((\log|t|)^q)\) with some \(q<1\) on any fixed narrower disk reaching \(1/2+\eta\), for fixed \(\eta>0\). Cauchy on a still smaller fixed neighborhood gives \(\zeta'/\zeta(1/2+\eta+it)=o(\log|t|)\). This also supplies the subpower reciprocal bound on fixed right abscissae away from the compact exceptional configuration. Interpolating between \(1/2\pm\eta\) with the functional equation on the left and then taking \(\eta\) sufficiently small gives subpower bounds for \(\zeta\) on the critical line. The functional equation and the high fixed right-line estimates yield the weighted strip bound (7).

For the size of the clusters, the real completed logarithmic derivative at \(1/2+\eta+it\) gives at high height
\[
 \sum_{\Re\rho=1/2}\frac{\eta}{\eta^2+(t-\gamma)^2}
  = (1/2+o(1))\log |t|.
\]
The finite off-line terms are \(O((1+|t|)^{-2})\) and the gamma derivative supplies the leading term. Integrate this positive kernel over a fixed interval of length at most \(4D/3\) enlarged by padding \(D/3\) at both ends. Each in-cell copy contributes at least \(2\arctan(D/(3\eta))\). For fixed \(\eta\le D/3\) the resulting coefficient is at most \(2D/\pi+o(1)<D\). This proves the exact \(k_C\le D\log T_C\) needed in (14), with the source \(D=10^{-4}\). It uses no moving interval or exact finite-exception version of the error in (8).

For the horizontal sides, the average of the kernel in (10) over a fixed small height interval is \(O((\eta/\ell+\eta^2)\log T)\) by the unconditional unit-window count. The logarithm of the finite off-line factors changes by \(O_{Z^+}(T^{-2})\) along the bounded horizontal displacement at high height. Use the right abscissa, the real Hadamard identity (12) for the critical-line factors, and the functional equation for the left side. Choosing \(\eta\) sufficiently small relative to the fixed interval and to \(e=10^{-4}\) gives a height with the source's \(T^e\) reciprocal bound. On the two fixed vertical sides, the high reciprocal bounds and the choice of \(\delta\) apply. Low sides avoid critical zeros by their height choices and the right and left exceptional zeros by their abscissae. There are only finitely many low cells. Uniform boundedness of \(W\) over every contour gives \(|G|\ll_W T_C^e\), with a single prefactor. ## Retained edge, collective quadrature and fixed Laurent corrections

The coefficients of \(G\) on an absolute-convergence line are \(c_n=(b*\mu)(n)\). Use \(V_N^W=\sum_{n\le N}c_nn^{-s}(1-\log n/L)\) with \(L=\log N\). On the upward left edge \(\Re z=a\), write \(J_0,J_N\) as in (94). Integrating \(G(z)/(z-s)^2\) around the strip gives (95) with minus \(J_0\) because the positively oriented left side is downward and the sum of residues at the cluster poles is \(-\sum_C R_C'(s)\). For the exponential kernel, the Perron residue at \(z=s\) is \(LG(s)+G'(s)\) and retaining the upward left edge gives (96) with plus \(J_N\). On the right the absolutely convergent Dirichlet series is bounded for \(\Re z\ge2\), and the derivative kernel kills the integral when that edge goes to infinity. Fixed \(N,s\) and good heights justify sending the horizontal sides to infinity, with grouped remote residues \(O_{N,s}(T_C^{-2+e})\). The cluster regularization goes only to \(a\).

Combining the formulas, the left error is \(\zeta(J_0-J_N)/L\). Its integrand on the left is bounded by the contour majorant times \(2/(\delta^2+(t-y)^2)\). Convolution with \((2+|y|)^e\) costs \(O_{\delta,e}((2+|t|)^e)\), so \(\|E_{\rm left}\|_*^2\ll L^{-2}\int(2+|t|)^{-2+4e}dt\). The residue at \(z=s\) in the complete first-order cluster kernel is \(LG(s)\) before multiplication by \(\zeta/L\), giving the indicator multiplied by \(W(s)\) in (98). It is essential to carry it through telescoping. Its squared tail is \(O(H^{-1})\). The vertical edges after telescoping give \(O(N^{2\delta}H^{-1+4e}/L^2)\) in squared norm by Cauchy–Schwarz with the same positive integrable convolution kernel. At the two horizontal connectors keep
\(e^{L(z-s)}-1=(z-s)\int_0^Le^{v(z-s)}dv\).
The residual horizontal kernel is bounded by \(2\operatorname{arsinh}(\delta/|h-t|)\). Its square is integrable at the logarithmic singularity and has a quadratic tail. The squared connector bound is \(O(N^{2\delta}H^{-2+4e})\). These estimates also show convergence in the full weighted norm when the outer good heights first tend to infinity at fixed \(N\). No normalization uses only a compact \(t\) interval. [RST048 v2, (44), (46)–(49), (94)–(98)](../../sources/ratio62_q1_priority/rstation_48_v2.md)

The Newton coefficients have the same bound \(O_W(T_C^e(2D)^r)\) for a denominator of length \(r\), even when some coefficients vanish. The bound is from the contour length and one power of the supremum of \(G\). Retain the previously reconstructed divided-difference and profile estimates (34)–(41). The corrected-cluster series over any subset still has squared norm at most \(C_W L^{-1}\sum_C T_C^{-2+1/100}\). To verify coefficient length for the growing set, use the actual summand
\[
 n^{-s-1}\sum_{|\tau_C|\le H}h_C(\log n)\,
       [w_{k_C}(\log n/L)-(1-\log n/L)]
 \qquad(n\le N),
\]
where now \(h_C(x)=\oint_C G(z)e^{zx}\,dz/(2\pi i)\). The bracket vanishes before \(N^{1/4}\) and at \(N\). The integral analog exactly replaces the required cluster profile. Bounds on the contour and its derivative give collective size \(O(H^{1+e}y^{1/2+\delta})\) and derivative in \(\log y\) of size \(O(H^{2+e}y^{1/2+\delta})\). On the critical line the quadrature integrand has variation at most \(O(H^{2+e}(1+|t|)N^{-(1-\delta)/4})\). Split the full norm at \(K=N^{3/20}\) and choose \(H=N^{1/20}\). The two squared errors have exponents bounded by \(-0.1499595\) and \(-0.049958\) at the maximum allowed \(\delta\), while the slowest high strip exponent is \(-0.049978\). Shrinking \(\delta\) improves all three. The growing correction's value at 1 is \(O(H^{1+e}N^{-(1/2-\delta)/4})\). At any fixed zero \(\rho\) of multiplicity \(m\), a Laurent term of order \(r\le m\) locally contributes a fixed analytic factor times
\((s-\rho)^{m-r}e_{r,w}(L(t-\gamma))\), with \(e_{r,w}(v)=O((1+|v|)^{-1})\) for the admissible profile. For \(r<m\), put \(j=m-r\ge1\). Its squared local integral is \(O(L^{-2j-1}\int_{|v|\le cL}|v|^{2j}(1+|v|)^{-2}dv)=O(L^{-2})\). The same Laurent term away from its fixed separate neighborhood has norm \(O(L^{-1})\) by the far single-pole estimate in (26). This retains the fixed-Laurent qualification of the earlier audit and avoids any use of uniform lower bounds for zeta derivatives at a growing set of zeros.

The top term gives the leading local constant \(|W(\rho)|^2c_m(w)/|\rho|^2\), since its coefficient is \(W(\rho)m!/\zeta^{(m)}(\rho)\). A canceled top coefficient gives zero. Repair it at a fixed finite family by (63) with \(q(u)=(1-u)^m-w_{k_C}(u)\) times that Laurent coefficient. The fixed-knot quadrature lemma gives \(O(L^{-1})\) error and anchor and uses only \(n\le N\). The optimal repaired value is \(c_m=m^2\) by (73). The uniform cluster tail permits exhaustion after taking \(N\to\infty\) for each fixed family. In particular, estimating the difference between full and finite repaired norms at scale \(\sqrt L\) by the square root of the tail bound yields the claimed upper constant without any assumption that individual cluster norms are absolutely summable. The resulting series converges since \(W\) is bounded, and in each unit ordinate window \(\sum m_\rho^2=O(\log^2(2+|t|))\).

For the base anchor, \(G(1)=0\), \(G'(1)=W(1)\), and the grouped residues in (96) at \(s=1\) sum to \(O_W(N^{-1/2+\delta})\) by summing \(T_C^{-2+e}\). The left integral is \(O_W(N^{-1/2-\delta})\). Thus (100) holds. Together with the growing and fixed corrections it gives \(A(1)=O_W(L^{-1})\) for every fixed repaired family. Subtracting \(A(1)\) affects only coefficient 1 and changes the full residual norm by at most \(|A(1)|\,\|\zeta\|_*=O_W(L^{-1})\). Since the target error here is already \(O(L^{-1/2})\), its squared leading term is preserved. This proves
\[
 \limsup_N L\operatorname{dist}(U,V_N)^2
 \le \sum_{\Re\rho=1/2}^{\rm distinct} m_\rho^2|U(\rho)|^2 .
\]
Apply it to \(u\) and \(u_*\). The two exact norm identities then give the two upper bounds of Theorem 6. [RST048 v2, §§7, 9.3](../../sources/ratio62_q1_priority/rstation_48_v2.md)

## Matching lower bounds directly in the discrete space

The following independent transfer uses paper 47's analytic compact tests in the form already checked for the Theorem 5 lower bound. For fixed \(s\) near any selected critical-line zero, choose \(k=\lceil L\rceil\), \(R=\lceil4NL\rceil\), \(B_k(z)=e^{-z}z^k/\Gamma(k)\), \(\chi_k(z)=e^{-z}\sum_{j<k}z^j/j!\), and
\[
 \mathcal L_{s,N}(f)=\int_1^{R+1} f(x)x^{-s-1}
       \bigl(\chi_k(x/N)+B_k(x/N)/s\bigr)\,dx .
\]
For a sequence \(v_N\in V_N\) of bounded full norm, the finite Möbius inversion of its unit jumps bounds its coefficient sum by \(N\sqrt{4N+1}\|v_N\|\). The kernel outside the smooth cutoff has zero Mellin mean and the nonzero frequency transform bound of paper 47 (14). At all frequencies of an integer generator with index at most \(N\), \(|\omega N|\ge2\pi\). Summing the absolutely convergent Fourier bounds gives error exponent \(3/2-\Re s-\tfrac12\log(1+4\pi^2)\). On the remaining hard physical tail the incomplete gamma rate gives exponent \(3/2-\Re s-(3-\log4)\). Both are negative in a fixed neighborhood of the line. Hence \(\mathcal L_{s,N}(v_N)\) agrees with its analytic Mellin transform up to a vanishing power error there. Cauchy estimates transfer this to each fixed derivative jet. The genuine transform vanishes to the full order at each selected zero, so the corresponding compact jets tend to zero.

For either projected target its physical cell values grow by at most \(O(x^{1-\beta_*}(1+\log x)^J)\), by the representer bound above. On \(1\le x\le N\) the difference between the cutoff and 1 is factorially small. On \(x\ge N\) the cutoff is bounded by \(O(1+\sqrt k)\), and the absolute Mellin tail tends to zero uniformly in a fixed neighborhood with \(\Re s>1-\beta_*\). This proves \(\mathcal L_{s,N}(U)\to U(s)\) locally uniformly, including the derivatives by Cauchy's formula. This convergence also applies uniformly to bounded linear combinations of the two fixed functions \(P_VE,P_Vh_S\). In the empty case the same conclusion follows directly from bounded target cell values.

For a selected zero \(\rho=1/2+i\gamma\), use the physical representers of \(L^{-j}\partial_s^j\mathcal L_{s,N}|_{s=\rho}\) for \(0\le j<m_\rho\). They differ by \(o(\sqrt L)\) in norm from the sharp tests
\[
 (-\log(x)/L)^j x^{1-\bar\rho}\mathbf1_{[1,N]}(x).
\]
Indeed the smooth-to-sharp discrepancy near and above \(N\) costs \(O(\log L)\) in squared norm from the retained sharp-length change and \(O(\sqrt L)\) from the squared gamma bump, since \(\int_0^\infty B_k(z)^2 dz/z=\Gamma(2k)/(2^{2k}\Gamma(k)^2)=O(\sqrt k)\). Derivatives of \(1/s\) and the normalized log factors are bounded on the retained domain. Their Gram matrix divided by \(L\) therefore tends to signed Hilbert blocks \(((-1)^{i+j}/(i+j+1))_{0\le i,j<m_\rho}\), with distinct ordinates asymptotically orthogonal by integrating the fixed polynomials against \(e^{i(\gamma-\gamma')Lr}\), \(0\le r\le1\). In each block the inverse entry at \((0,0)\) is \(m_\rho^2\), by the shifted Legendre reproducing kernel at zero. For a residual \(U-v_N\) the compact pairings converge to \((U(\rho),0,\ldots,0)\) in each block. Orthogonal projection onto the selected tests gives the desired lower bound at scale \(L\) for an arbitrary finite family, then exhaustion gives the full series. [Paper 47, Lemma 3 and (9)–(23)](../../sources/ratio62_rstation48/rstation_47_v1.md) · [Compact-test Gram review](director_rst048_prefix_round8.md)

For the unrestricted problem let \(c_N\) be the exact finite scalar minimizer. Inserting the fixed \(c_*\) and the upper construction gives excess \(O(1/L)\). The exact positive scalar penalty forces \(|c_N-c_*|=O(L^{-1/2})\). The corresponding best approximants to \(u_{c_N}\) have bounded norm, and the compact tests for \(u_{c_N}\) give the limiting right sides with \(u_*\) by the uniform bounded-linear-combination argument. Discard the nonnegative scalar penalty when taking the lower bound. This proves (90) and (91), retaining the full-line normalization and the actual finite polynomial length. The direct lower-bound route avoids any need to infer uniform estimates for a moving scalar from the source's pointwise upper construction.

If \(Z^+=\varnothing\), then \(V=S\), \(c_*=0\), and both target constants reduce to the one in Theorem 1. For a nonempty finite \(Z^+\), the positive limiting distances and the discrete target are part of the result. The argument is a finite-exception conditional theorem, with scope separate from the fixed Möbius prefix theorem. The hypothesis that the exceptional set is finite or empty remains unresolved here.
