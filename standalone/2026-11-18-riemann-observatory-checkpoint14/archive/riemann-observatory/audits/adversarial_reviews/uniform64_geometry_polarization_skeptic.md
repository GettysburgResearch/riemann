# Independent audit of the finite Frobenius power tests

17 November 2026. The finite-spectrum equivalence, sparse prime-degree counterexample, and comparison with the three original sources are sound. The draft I first checked contained broken TeX escapes, including embedded backspace bytes. This audit distinguishes two-term tests along all powers from the joint degree-\((r-1)\) test and from prime-degree sampling. [Candidate frontier](../../frontiers/geometry64_minimal_polarization.md)

Normalize each of the finitely many nonzero roots by the fixed \(\rho>0\). Multiplicities are positive integers, reciprocal pairing preserves them, and conjugation preserves them. Then \(s_n=s_{-n}\in\mathbb R\) and \(s_0=d\). Real Laurent polynomials vanishing on all distinct roots form a \(*\)-stable ideal because inversion preserves the set. The trace descends to the quotient because it is a sum of evaluations. The quadratic form on \(a+b x^n\) has matrix \(\begin{pmatrix}d&s_n\\s_n&d\end{pmatrix}\), so is nonnegative for all real \(a,b\) exactly when \(|s_n|\le d\). If all roots lie on the unit circle, the full trace form is a sum of squared moduli.

For a density-one set \(S\) satisfying the one-sided subexponential upper bound, let \(R=\max|z|\). Use the tuple of all phases at \(R\), each with multiplicity, and its compact cyclic subgroup closure \(G\). The neighborhood in which all coordinate real parts exceed \(1/2\) receives returns at bounded gaps: the translates by powers cover \(G\) by density, and a finite subcover bounds the magnitude of the necessary index correction. This gives positive lower density of positive returns, so \(S\) meets it unboundedly. The contribution from smaller moduli is exponentially small after dividing by \(R^n\), and the real part of the top block is at least half its size. Along the selected subsequence \(s_n\ge cR^n>0\). If \(R>1\), this violates the upper bound for fixed \(0<\varepsilon<\log R\). The argument also identifies the limsup of the positive part to power \(1/n\) as \(R\) on every density-one set, since \(|s_n|\le dR^n\). Reciprocals then give equality of every modulus to one. Positive multiplicity is needed here: a signed coefficient at the positive real root \(R>1\) can make its contribution negative on every power. Without reciprocal pairing the upper part of the argument controls the closed disk only.

For a fixed multiset with \(r\) distinct points, the degree-\((r-1)\) positivity test has a separate proof. For any off-circle point \(z\), its conjugacy class and the conjugacy class of \(z^{-1}\) are disjoint, including when either is a real point. The interpolated values \(+1,-1,0\) on the first class, second class, and remaining points yield a degree-\((r-1)\) polynomial, real because the conjugated polynomial solves the same interpolation problem. The pairing is minus the total multiplicity in those two classes. This proves that the entire \(r\times r\) Toeplitz condition detects every off-circle pair. Its individual \(2\times2\) minors check only finitely many \(|s_n|\le d\) tests and cannot be substituted for the joint condition. A fixed degree-four family witnessing this distinction is \(R^{\pm1}e^{\pm i\theta}\), all sign choices, with \(\theta/\pi\) irrational and \(R\) approaching 1 from above. At \(R=1\) the finitely many required strict inequalities hold, and persist for some \(R>1\). Distinctness is required only at that latter \(R\).

For \(P(X)=(X^4+32)(X^4+8)\), the weight-one radius at \(q=4\) is 2. If \(\alpha^4=-32\), then \((4/\alpha)^4=-8\), so the two factors are exchanged and give the required positive multisets. The normalized fourth powers are \(-2\) and \(-1/2\), each four times. Thus \(s_4=-10\) and \(s_8=17\), while \(s_n=0\) unless \(4\mid n\) by summing four equally spaced unit phases. Every prime degree, including 2, misses the violation. The integral companion matrix supplies one compatible unnormalized power sequence, with no geometric claim. This is also a counterexample to an arbitrary infinite set of tests. One-sided tests on density one avoid that failure by recurrence.

For the comparison of signed spectra, group identical roots and let \(R\) be the largest modulus with a nonzero grouped coefficient. Divide the difference of power sums by \(R^n\). The lower block tends to zero, while the upper block \(V_n=\sum_j c_j u_j^n\), for distinct unit \(u_j\), satisfies \(\lim_{M\to\infty}M^{-1}\sum_{n\le M}|V_n|^2=\sum_j|c_j|^2>0\) by geometric sums. Deleting a density-zero set changes this average by \(o(1)\), since \(V_n\) is uniformly bounded. If the exact differences vanished on density one, their top block there would tend to zero, a contradiction. With a known cardinality bound on the distinct union, consecutive power traces yield the same result by the Vandermonde matrix after multiplying each column by the nonzero initial power. Neither argument allows the input spectrum itself to change with the tested power.

## Source checks

- Weil 1941, pp. 345–346, defines correspondences on the product of the nonsingular curve with itself, the transpose, and the quotient by principal and degenerate correspondences. On p. 346 the generic complementary correspondence of degree \((g,m)\) gives \(2m=\sigma(yy')\) and hence positivity, and the Frobenius correspondence gives \(FF'=q\) with degree-one coincidence multiplicities for its powers. The count is \(1+q^n-\sigma(F^n)\). On p. 347 the reciprocal roots, their power traces, reciprocity, and real polynomial interpolation establish the radius. Weil uses the full positivity form there. The restriction to \((1,F^n/q^{n/2})\) is the present sufficient reduction, with real scalars justified by the integral quadratic form, homogeneity on rationals, and continuity. The genus-zero empty spectrum is vacuous. [Weil 1941, pp. 345–347](../../sources/classical63_automorphic_geometry/weil1941.pdf)
- Grothendieck, pp. 195–197, gives the weak projector conjecture \(C(X)\) and identifies the extra cycle-independence refinement, frames \(A(X)\) and algebraicity of the lowering operator \(B(X)\), and derives the algebraicity part of \(C(X)\) from \(B(X)\). Section 4 Remark (2) explicitly invokes \(B(X)\) and \(\mathrm{Hdg}(X\times X)\) for the Weil–Serre implication about an endomorphism scaling the hyperplane class. Replacing that sign assumption by the required finite power tests is a sufficiency statement in the already realized trace algebra. No separate geometric construction is inferred. [Grothendieck, §§2–4](../../sources/classical63_automorphic_geometry/grothendieck1968.pdf)
- Katz–Messing, pp. 73–75, take Deligne's integral pure polynomials D1 and the reconstruction D2 over permissible smooth pencil sections after finite base extensions. Theorem 1 uses duality, a high-degree weak Lefschetz condition, and the zeta determinant formula on the other theory. The comparison cancels degrees at distance at least two from the middle and obtains neighboring divisibilities before the polynomial residual identity in the three remaining degrees. Disjoint radii then force the residual factors to equal 1. On pp. 76–77, Theorem 2 adds its stated hypotheses on the cohomology theory before producing the Künneth projectors. Its polynomial construction follows from pairwise coprime factors and Cayley–Hamilton. These source arguments do not give either a classical zeta trace realization or a general infinite-spectrum transfer. [Katz–Messing, Theorem 1 and Theorem 2](../../sources/classical63_automorphic_geometry/katzmessing1974.pdf)

The radius criteria concern eigenvalue multisets and are insensitive to Jordan blocks. In the classical setting, the analogy still needs an explicit compatible trace on the proposed test algebra and a theorem turning the trace bound into control of individual zeros. A finite characteristic polynomial in \(q^{-s}\) would give a vertically periodic zero set with \(O(T)\) zeros up to height \(T\), incompatible with the classical \(T\log T\) count. This rules out that direct finite model.
