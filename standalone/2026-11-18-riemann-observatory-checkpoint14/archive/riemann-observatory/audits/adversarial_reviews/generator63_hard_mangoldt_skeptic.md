# The hard Mangoldt cutoff with a moving correction

17 November 2026. The displayed truncation in the factorial-target antecedent audit matches every cell before the cutoff and diverges in the step norm. In fact its squared error has order \(M\) for every fixed \(k\ge2\), unconditionally. The cutoff \(M\) ranges over all integers \(M\ge2\). The proof uses elementary prime-power valuations and applies to composite \(k\). [Candidate under review](jump_generator63_antecedents.md#the-exact-von-mangoldt-coefficients-and-a-failed-direct-truncation)

## Exact definition and first mismatching cell

On \(x\ge0\), define \(f_j(x)=j\lfloor x/j\rfloor-\lfloor x\rfloor\) for integer \(j\ge1\), with \(f_1=0\). Let \(\Lambda\) be the von Mangoldt function with \(\Lambda(1)=0\), and set \(\psi(x)=\sum_{d\le x}\Lambda(d)\). All cells are right-continuous \([n,n+1)\), so a summand with \(d=x\in\mathbb N\) is included.

The target and candidate are
\[
 \begin{split}
 R_k(x)&=k\log(q!)+kq\log k-\log(n!),
      \qquad n=\lfloor x\rfloor,\quad q=\lfloor x/k\rfloor,\\
 A_{k,M}(x)&=\log k\,[f_k(x)-f_M(x)]+\sum_{d\le M}\Lambda(d) f_k(x/d).
 \end{split}
\]
The dilation identity \(d f_k(x/d)=f_{kd}(x)-f_d(x)\) follows exactly from the floors. Thus \(A_{k,M}\) belongs to the anchored span generated by \(f_2,\ldots,f_{kM}\). Here \(M\) cuts off the sum in \(d\), while \(kM\) is the resulting upper bound on the ordinary dilation index.

Using \(\log j=\sum_{d\mid j}\Lambda(d)\) gives \(\sum_{d\le y}\Lambda(d)\lfloor y/d\rfloor=\log(\lfloor y\rfloor!)\). Apply this for \(y=x/k\) and \(y=x\) to obtain
\[
 R_k(x)=k\log k\,\lfloor x/k\rfloor+\sum_{d\le x}\Lambda(d) f_k(x/d).
\]
Both formulas then give the exact global error identity
\[
 A_{k,M}(x)-R_k(x)
 =-M\log k\,\lfloor x/M\rfloor-\sum_{M<d\le x}\Lambda(d)f_k(x/d).
\]
Terms at \(d>x\) vanish since \(f_k(y)=0\) on \(0\le y<1\). Therefore the error is zero for \(x<M\), including every unit cell with \(n<M\). For \(M\le x<2M\) and an omitted term \(M<d\le x\), one has \(1\le x/d<2\). Thus \(\lfloor x/d\rfloor=1\) and \(\lfloor x/(kd)\rfloor=0\) for every integer \(k\ge2\). In this range the error is
\[
 A_{k,M}(x)-R_k(x)=\psi(x)-\psi(M)-M\log k .
\]
In particular on \([M,M+1)\) it is exactly \(-M\log k\), so the pointwise matching stops before cell \(M\). At \(x=2M\) the correction changes to the second multiple of \(-M\log k\), which explains the half-open range.

## Lower and upper bounds in the step norm

On a window of length \(c_k M/\log M\) immediately after \(M\), the estimate \(\Lambda(d)\le\log(2M)\le2\log M\) for \(d\le2M\) gives \(\psi(x)-\psi(M)\le M\log k/2\) for sufficiently small fixed \(c_k>0\). Integrating the squared error against \(dx/x^2\) on this window proves the original \(\gg_k M/\log M\) claim for the squared norm. A longer window follows from the same elementary coefficient structure.

For integers \(0\le h\le M\), the prime powers \(p^r\in(M,M+h]\) all contribute one to the corresponding Legendre summand
\[
 \left\lfloor\frac{M+h}{p^r}\right\rfloor
 -\left\lfloor\frac{M}{p^r}\right\rfloor
 -\left\lfloor\frac{h}{p^r}\right\rfloor
\]
in \(v_p\binom{M+h}{h}\). Every other such summand is nonnegative by the basic floor inequality. Consequently
\[
 0\le\psi(M+h)-\psi(M)\le\log\binom{M+h}{h}.
\]
For \(1\le h\le M/16\), use \(\binom{M+h}{h}\le [e(M+h)/h]^h\), obtained from \(h!\ge(h/e)^h\). The function \(h\log(e(M+h)/h)\) is increasing for \(h>0\). Thus the logarithmic upper bound is at most \((M/16)\log(17e)<(M/2)\log2\). At \(h=0\) the difference is zero. Taking \(h=\lfloor x\rfloor-M\) gives \(|A_{k,M}(x)-R_k(x)|\ge M\log k/2\) on \(M\le x\le17M/16\). In particular
\[
 \|A_{k,M}-R_k\|_S^2\ \ge\ \frac{M(\log k)^2}{68}.
\]
This uses an upper bound on prime-power mass in the window. No prime-free interval, prime gap theorem or primorial subsequence is needed.

For an upper bound, the same valuation argument at \(h=M\) gives \(\psi(2M)-\psi(M)\le2M\log2\). Telescoping at powers of two and then using monotonicity gives \(\psi(M)\ll M\). Each \(f_j\) is bounded by \(j-1\) in absolute value. Therefore \(|A_{k,M}(x)|\ll_k M\) on the entire half-line. The jumps of \(R_k\) are \((\log n)(k1_{k\mid n}-1)\), whose unweighted factors have bounded partial sums \(f_k(n)\). Summation by parts gives \(|R_k(x)|\ll_k1+\log x\) for \(x\ge1\). Using the exact match for \(x<M\),
\[
 \|A_{k,M}-R_k\|_S^2
 \ \ll_k\ \int_M^\infty (M^2+(1+\log x)^2)\frac{dx}{x^2}
 \ \ll_k M .
\]
Thus the squared error is \(\Theta_k(M)\), and the norm itself is \(\Theta_k(\sqrt M)\). This also bounds the tail beyond the window used for the lower bound.

## Cutoff conventions and the prior divergence example

The arithmetic floor coefficients of the full target are
\[
 w_k(j)=k1_{k\mid j}\Lambda(j/k)-\Lambda(j)+k\log k\,1_{j=k}.
\]
By expansion of the finite-span candidate, its coefficient of \(f_j\), \(j\ge2\), is
\[
 (\log k)(1_{j=k}-1_{j=M})
 +1_{k\mid j,\ j/k\le M}\frac{\Lambda(j/k)}{j/k}
 -1_{j\le M}\frac{\Lambda(j)}j .
\]
In the shifted term the cutoff is imposed on \(j/k\). In particular the coefficients for \(j<M\) equal \(w_k(j)/j\), while the coefficient for \(j=M\) includes the moving correction \(-\log k\). A direct hard truncation of the ordinary coefficients would instead be \(\sum_{j\le M}(w_k(j)/j)f_j(x)\). On \(x<M\), its error is \(-\lfloor x\rfloor\sum_{j\le M}w_k(j)/j\), and for \(M\ge k\) the sum equals \(\log k-\sum_{M/k<d\le M}\Lambda(d)/d\). That is a distinct error identity. The previously studied logarithmic taper uses \(w_k(j)(1-\log j/\log N)/j\) and a different anchor convention. The divergence proof here concerns \(A_{k,M}\). The target there is 1, and the recurrence fixes this value on \([1,n+1)\). The divergence uses successive differences with coefficient \(c_n=n/2\) at powers of two, whose \(L^1(dx/x^2)\) norms are bounded away from zero by dilation. It is a distinct sequence and proof.
