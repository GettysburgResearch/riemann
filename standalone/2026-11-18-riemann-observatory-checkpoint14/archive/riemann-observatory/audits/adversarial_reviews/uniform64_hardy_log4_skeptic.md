## Continuous input and Hardy phase

Fix \(T\ge16\), \(U=T+1\), \(H=\log(4T)\), \(r=1/H\), and \(M=\lceil2U\rceil\). Then \(2U\le M\le3T\), \(M\le3U\), \(r<1/4\) and \(H>4\). The intended Euler formula holds with \(s=\sigma+it\), initially for \(\sigma>1\) and then for \(\sigma>0,\ s\ne1\) by the absolute convergence of the periodic remainder. With integer \(M\) and the sum including \(M\), the correction is \(-M^{-s}/2\). Integration by parts against the sawtooth multiplied by the derivative of \(x^{-s}\) gives the sign \(-s\) in front of its integral.

For either sawtooth frequency \(\pm n\), the phase has \(|\phi'|\ge2\pi n-U/M\ge5n\). With \(a(x)=x^{-\sigma-1}\), the boundary and \(a'\) terms after integration by parts cost at most \(2M^{-\sigma-1}/(5n)\), and the term involving \(\phi''\) is at most \(U M^{-\sigma-2}/[25n^2(\sigma+2)]\). Their sum is at most \(M^{-\sigma-1}/(2n)\). Abel damping gives valid interchange by the uniformly bounded sawtooth convolution and summable final majorant, independently of discontinuities at the integers. Summing the two coefficient signs gives total at most \(M^{-\sigma-1}/\pi\), since \(\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-2}\le2\). As \(|s|\le M\), the Euler remainder plus endpoint correction is bounded by \(M^{-\sigma}\). Its squared integral is at most \(2U<3T\). The pole term has squared integral at most \(\pi M^{2-2\sigma}/(1-\sigma)\le4\pi e^2M<300T\), using \(\log M\le H\). The pole stays off the closed strip, including for \(t=0\).

Gaussian weighting gives the exact matrix with entries \(\sqrt\pi U \exp[-U^2(\log(n/m))^2/4]\). Since \(|\log(n/m)|\ge |n-m|/M\) and \(M\le3U\), the normalized row sums are bounded by \(1+2\sum_{j\ge1}e^{-j^2/36}\le1+6\sqrt\pi<12\), including the diagonal exactly once. The harmonic coefficient bound is \(e^2(1+\log M)\le10H\). Removing the Gaussian on \([-U,U]\) costs \(e\), so the polynomial's squared integral is at most \(12e\sqrt\pi U\cdot10H\le640TH\). The sum of squared contributions times 3 is at most \(3(640TH+303T)\le3000TH\) since \(H>4\).

The ratio of gamma functions defining the Hardy phase has the stated holomorphic square root. Explicitly use \(\exp\big[-(s-1/2)\log\pi/2+(\log\Gamma(s/2)-\log\Gamma((1-s)/2))/2\big]\) with \(\log\Gamma\) real on the positive real axis and analytic on the right half-plane. On the strip \(A(s)A(1-s)=1\), and the functional equation gives \(\zeta(1-s)=A(s)^2\zeta(s)\). Thus \(A(s)\zeta(s)\) is invariant under \(s\mapsto1-s\), and conjugation shows it is real on the critical line. The induced \(\mathcal Z(w)\) is holomorphic for \(|\Im w|<1/4\), is real and even on the real axis, and obeys \(\mathcal Z'(0)=0\).

For the modulus bound off center, the gamma arguments in the closed strip obey \(\Re z\ge1/8\), \(|z-1|\le U\). In the digamma series with \(J=\lceil U+2\rceil\), the real part of \(1/(n+z)\) lies between 0 and \(1/n\) for \(n\ge1\). The initial term for \(n=0\) lies in \([-7,1]\). The lower bound on the real subtotal for \(1\le n<J\) is \(-1\) by telescoping and its upper bound is \(\log J\) by integrating \(1/x\). The tail is at most \(U/(J-1)\le1\), and the Euler constant adds at most 1. Thus \(-10\le\Re\psi(z)\le\log J+3\) and \(|\Re\psi(z)|\le H+6\). The derivative \(\partial_\sigma\log|A|\) is bounded by \(\frac12\log\pi+\frac12(H+6)<H/2+4\). Integrating from \(|A(1/2+it)|=1\) gives the required \(|A|^2\le e^3<21\) for \(r=1/H\), uniformly at low and high ordinates alike.

## Discrete derivative sum and both signs

At each \(0\le t\le T\), the circle \(w=t+re^{i\theta}\) stays in the holomorphic phase strip and corresponds to \(\sigma=1/2-r\sin\theta\), ordinate \(t+r\cos\theta\in[-U,U]\). Cauchy on that circle and Cauchy–Schwarz in the angle give \(\int_0^T|\mathcal Z^{(j)}|^2\le63000(j!)^2TH^{2j+1}\le65000(j!)^2TH^{2j+1}\) for \(j=1,2\), since the ordinate shift has Jacobian 1. The argument includes \(t=0\) and \(t=T\) with the same strip margin. At a zero the derivative of the phase times \(\zeta\) disappears, leaving exactly the modulus of the zeta derivative.

Real evenness supplies a stationary point at 0 ahead of the first positive zero. Rolle supplies a point between each successive pair of distinct positive zeros. The intervals from these points to the zeros are disjoint in their interiors. On each interval the fundamental theorem applied to \(\mathcal Z'^2\) bounds the squared endpoint derivative by the integral of \(2|\mathcal Z'\mathcal Z''|\). There are finitely many real zeros on each compact segment because the function is holomorphic and nonzero as a function. Multiple zeros contribute 0, and neither close pairs nor a multiple zero between other roots spoils Rolle. Cauchy–Schwarz then gives \(2\sqrt{65000\cdot260000}\,TH^4=260000TH^4\) for positive ordinates. Reflection contributes exactly 2 and \(|2^{1/2-i\gamma}-1|^2\le(1+\sqrt2)^2<6\), giving \(F(T)\le3.12\cdot10^6TH^4\).

When integrating the positive counting function for both signs against \(f(t)=(t^2+1/4)^{-1}\), the strict-tail lower term is \(-f(Y)F(Y)\le0\), even when \(Y\) is a zero ordinate. The upper endpoint vanishes and \(-f'(t)\le2/t^3\). This proves the leading constant \(6.24\cdot10^6\) multiplying \(\int_Y^\infty t^{-2}\log^4(4t)\,dt\). Integration by parts gives the claimed polynomial divided by \(Y\). Its ratio to \(H_Y^4/Y\) decreases with \(H_Y>4\) and is at most \(103/32\), giving the relaxed bound with \(2.1\cdot10^7\). No ordinate at zero affects this strict tail.
