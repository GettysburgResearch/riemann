## Pintz, A remark on density theorems

The notation is \(N(1-\eta,T)\ll_\epsilon T^{\eta B(\eta)+\epsilon}\), counting zeros with \(\beta\ge1-\eta\) and \(0\le\gamma\le T\). Put \(\alpha_\ell=1-2^{-\ell}\), \(u_\ell=\mu(\alpha_\ell)/(2^{-\ell}-\eta)\), and \(v_\ell=\mu(\alpha_{\ell-1})/[2(2^{-\ell}-\eta)]\). Theorem 1, (2.7), gives \(B(\eta)\le\max(4u_\ell,3v_\ell)\) in the range used by the proof, \(0<\eta<\min(1/3,2^{-\ell})\). PDF p. 5 was visually inspected and explicitly prints **max** in the *range* condition, inconsistent with (3.1)'s **min**, the denominators, and the displayed equivalence preceding the theorem. The printed outside range is not usable as written. For \(\ell\ge2\), the stated corollaries use the narrower range. The Hungarian institutional repository points to the same arXiv v1 PDF, so it does not establish whether the typeset journal printing corrects the range.

The proof uses a maximal 1-separated set of zero ordinates in \([T/2,T]\), recovering all zero copies through the standard local \(O(\log T)\) count. With \(X=T^\epsilon\), the convolution \(M_X\zeta\) vanishes in coefficients at \(2\le n\le X\). A Gaussian Perron detector (3.7) moves to \(\alpha_\ell-\beta_j\), costing \(T^{\mu(\alpha_\ell)+o(1)}Y^{\alpha_\ell-\beta_j}\), \(Y=T^{u_\ell+\epsilon}\) with the successive small losses chosen inside the available margin. The pole at \(1-\rho_j\) is exponentially suppressed. The Gaussian on the shifted contour depends on its integration height, and the part where the shifted zeta height approaches zero lies in the suppressed tail. Back on the coefficient side the weight for \(n=1\) is \(1+o(1)\), terms \(n>e^3Y\) are negligible, and dyadic pigeonholing extracts a large polynomial. The sign after subtracting the \(n=1\) term in (3.9) is immaterial for the subsequent absolute value.

Bounded powering of the polynomial brings its length to the threshold \(T^{v_\ell+\epsilon}\). A block already above \(T^{(v_\ell+\epsilon)/2}\) can be squared. A shorter block needs at most the minimum power crossing the threshold, with exponent at most \(3(v_\ell+\epsilon)/2\). Thus the maximum resulting length is essentially \(\max(Y^2,T^{3v_\ell/2})\). Halász's split in (3.14) yields from Cauchy the scalar sum over \(n\) at \(\sigma_0=1-\eta_j-\eta_\nu\) and \(\gamma_j-\gamma_\nu\). The Perron deformation for that sum follows to \(\alpha_{\ell-1}\) because \(1-2\eta>\alpha_{\ell-1}\), and the threshold \(v_\ell\) suppresses its vertical integral. The pole contributes \(M^{2\eta}/|\gamma_j-\gamma_\nu|\); separation makes its row sum \(O(M^{2\eta}\log T)\). The diagonal contributes \(KM^{2\eta}\). This gives (3.16) and explains exactly the factors 4 and 3. Lemma 1 specifies \(s=\sigma_0+t\) in the extracted text, where the contour integrand and bound use \(s=\sigma_0+it\).

For \(\ell=2\), the classical bounds in (2.6) give Corollary 4, \(B(\eta)\le1/(1-4\eta)\) for \(0<\eta<1/4\). This is strict against the density-hypothesis exponent for \(\eta<1/8\), with equality of the displayed power at \(1/8\). Corollary 3 gives \(B(\eta)\le (4\log2+o(1))/\log(1/\eta)\) as \(\eta\to0\), an exponent statement rather than a uniform bound with a single \(T\)-constant at moving \(\eta\). Under Lindelöf, \(\ell=2\) gives \(N(\sigma,T)\ll_{\sigma,\epsilon}T^\epsilon\) for fixed \(\sigma>3/4\). Theorem 2's alternative proof of Montgomery's \(B(\eta)\le2/(1-\eta)\) uses the variant \(\ell=0\), \(v_0=u_0(2\eta)\), and yields that entry directly up to \(\eta=1/5\). For larger \(\eta\), Ingham's \(3/(1+\eta)\le2/(1-\eta)\) supplies the stated bound. The paper's concluding uniform exponent \(5/2\) is recovered by this same envelope. These are classical-style derivations and fixed-real-part power estimates.

A weighted-tail consequence is limited to a fixed right slab. For fixed \(d>0\) and a nonempty slab \(7/8+d\le \beta\le 1-d\), set \(y=(\beta-1/2)\log T\) and sum \(e^{2y}/(1+y^2)\) over \(0<\gamma\le T\). The exponent gap at a left counting threshold \(\sigma=1-\eta\) in this slab is \(\eta[2-1/(1-4\eta)]>0\). Its minimum on \(\eta\in[d,1/8-d]\) is positive. Cover the slab by finitely many fixed bins whose width is sufficiently small relative to this minimum, and choose \(\epsilon\) smaller still. Counts at the left endpoints and weights at the right endpoints give \(o(T)\) on this slab, even over the whole height block. This does not reach the moving scaled boundary \(\beta-1/2\asymp1/\log T\), nor does it justify uniform removal of the final strip approaching \(\beta=1\) from the fixed-\(\eta\) theorem alone. It is a scoped observation, not a new strong-density theorem.

## Dong–Wang–Wang–Zhang, Large zeta sums and zeros

Write \(S(x,t)=\sum_{n\le x}n^{it}\). In Theorem 1.1, absolute \(c,T_0\) work whenever \(T\ge T_0,\ T\le |t|\le2T,\ \exp(\sqrt{\log T})\le x\le\sqrt T,\ |S(x,t)|=x/N,\ 1\le N\le(\log x)^{1/100}\). There is a single \(\phi\) with \(|\phi-t|\le cN\) such that for every \(cN^6\le L\le(\log x)/2\), the open disk centered at \(1+i\phi\) of radius \(L\log T/(\log x)^2\) contains at least \(L/360\) zero copies. This is unconditional. The individual frequency witness inside Proposition 4.1 can vary with \(L\), while the disk center \(\phi\) is fixed by the original large sum. The radius only forces off-critical zeros if it is \(<1/2\).

Section 2 chooses the maximizing twist \(t_0\) on \(|t_0|\le\log x\). Halász and the comparison/Lipschitz estimates yield \(|t_0|=O(N)\), the bound on pretentious distance, and a \(1/3\)-Hölder difference for logarithmic sums after untwisting. The comparison formula inserts \(x^{-it_0}(1+it_0)\) and incurs error \(O(x/(\log x)^{3/4})\). In §3, an elementary \(|\zeta(1-\lambda+iu)|\ll(2+|u|)^\lambda/\lambda\) controls the contour tails. The completed Hadamard product compares \(1-\lambda+iu\) and \(1+\lambda+iu\); the gamma power cancels against the real logarithmic derivative sum and leaves the exponential of \(2\lambda^2\sum_\rho |1+\lambda+iu-\rho|^{-2}\). The Gaussian Fourier transform includes a pole residue with exact factor \(2\pi(1+i\tau)^{-1}\exp((\lambda+i\tau)^2/(2\mathcal T))\), where \(\tau=t-t_0\) and \(\mathcal T\) is the Gaussian parameter. The sign follows by contour continuation below the moving pole at \(\tau-i\lambda\), and the pole of the denominator stays above. At the choice \(\mathcal T=\lambda/\log x\) the residue is exponentially small since \(|\tau|\asymp T\).

With \(y_0=\log x\), §4's Gaussian average around \(y_0\) has normalized mean error of size \((\lambda y_0)^{-1/6}\), forcing \(\lambda y_0\gg N^6\). The resulting large \(\zeta\) value sits at offset \(|\xi|\le2\lambda\sqrt{\log T/y_0}\) because outside that range its Gaussian weight swamps the polynomial estimate. Hadamard then gives \(\sum_\rho\lambda/|1+\lambda+i(\phi+\xi)-\rho|^2\ge y_0/4\), with an immaterial sign change in the offset through conjugation. With \(\lambda=L/(40y_0)\) and \(a=20\lambda(\log T)/y_0\), each remote term is bounded by the corresponding positive real logarithmic-derivative summand at \(1+a+i\phi\), times \(5\lambda/a=y_0/(4\log T)\). Its total is at most \(5y_0/36\). Near terms give at least \(y_0/9\), each at most \(1/\lambda\), yielding \(L/360\). The inequalities include the boundary of the remote set and leave an open disk for the near terms.

Theorem 1.2 fixes \(A>0,\ 0<\delta\le1/4\). For \(T\le|t|\le2T,\ \varepsilon>(\log T)^{-1/3}\), assume for each \(|u-t|\le C(\log T)^{1/100}\) the region \(\Re s\ge1-\delta,\ |\Im s-u|\le\delta\) contains at most \(\delta\varepsilon^2\log T/400\) zero copies. Then \(S(x,t)\ll_A x/(\log x)^{1/100}\) uniformly for \(T^\varepsilon\le x\le T^A\) beyond the fixed-parameter threshold. In the short range use \(L=\delta\varepsilon^2\log T\), which makes the radius at most \(\delta\) and the lower count \(L/360\) strictly larger than the permitted count. For \(x\ge\sqrt T\), §5 instead uses the exponent pair \((1/6,2/3)\) up to length \(t\), and first derivatives beyond \(t\), to get \(O(xT^{-1/13})\). The fixed polynomial upper length is needed when passing to a logarithmic saving. A density estimate may make the local zero condition fail only on a small set of centers, with the polylogarithmic enlargement accounted for. The implication runs from large arithmetic sums to local zero clusters and does not supply a global upper count that could resolve the BGSTB bottleneck.
