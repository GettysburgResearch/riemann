\begin{abstract}
We reconstruct a parity-resummed factor--67 proposal from its literal arithmetic source to its reciprocal-zeta Mellin transform. Two mandatory adversarial inputs alter the claimed conclusion. Correct owner incidence and Cauchy--Binet propagate determinant signs but do not imply the global target capacity required by Hall. Exact matching of the scalar $5Q(2)+3Q(3)$ generally decreases row two while increasing row three, so scalar exactness cannot be promoted to two-row feasibility. We prove the complete finite parity owner ledger, the exact completed-parity scalar Hall problem as a fractional-knapsack/Lorenz linear program with one-parameter dual, the precise scope of the compact-plus-MPFR terminal certificate, and the full Mellin--Landau implication. The first unsupported arrow is the uniform completed-parity scalar Lorenz inequality, denoted \textup{CPSL67}. Its dual already contains the conclusion-producing scalar sign. Thus the manuscript gives a sharp conditional reduction and an exact downgrade, not a proof of the Riemann hypothesis.
\end{abstract}

\begin{theorem}[Finite scalar Lorenz theorem]\label{thm:lorenz}
The completed-parity scalar problem is feasible if and only if
\[
 T_O\le T_E:=\sum_i a_it_i,\qquad R_O\le\PhiX(T_O).
\]
Order atoms by $\theta_i=r_i/t_i$ decreasing and put $A_k=\sum_{i\le k}a_it_i$. For $A_{k-1}\le T\le A_k$,
\[
 \PhiX(T)=\sum_{i<k}a_ir_i+\theta_k(T-A_{k-1}).
\]
Moreover,
\[
 \PhiX(T)=\min_{\lambda\in\R}\left[\lambda T+\sum_i a_i(r_i-\lambda t_i)_+\right].
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
This is the fractional-knapsack exchange argument. If $\theta_i>\theta_j$ and both coefficients are interior, transfer a small target amount from $j$ to $i$; the target equality is fixed and the objective increases. Thus all larger ratios saturate first and at most one atom is fractional. Weak duality is immediate; equality holds at the threshold $\lambda=\theta_k$.
\end{proof}

\section{The scalar Mellin transform}

Since $|a_*(n)|\le24$,
\[
 |R_X|\le24\log X\sum_{n\le X}n^{-1/2}
 \le60\sqrt X(1+\log X),\qquad X\ge2.
\]
Thus the Mellin integral converges absolutely for $\Re s>1/2$. With $z=s+1/2$, substitution $X=kY$ contributes $k^{-s-1/2}$. Fubini gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mellin}
\int_1^\infty R_X X^{-s-1}\,dX
=\frac6{s^2}-\frac{3(1-2^{-z})(2-2^{-z})}{s^2\zeta(z)}.
\end{equation}
The $6/s^2$ term is the unit source.

For real $s>0$, $\zeta(s+1/2)$ has no real zero. At $z=1$, $1/\zeta(z)$ vanishes, so \eqref{eq:mellin} is removable. The finite numerator is zero-free for $\Re z>0$, because there $|2^{-z}|<1$. A zeta zero of arbitrary multiplicity with real part greater than $1/2$ therefore gives a nonremovable pole in $\Re s>0$.
