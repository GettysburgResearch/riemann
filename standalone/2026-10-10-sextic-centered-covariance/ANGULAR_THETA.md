## 1. Why this is a different adapter from a formal A2 Weyl orbit

The earlier A2 packet supplied an exact arithmetic completion but left the literal infinity type and sextic row twists outside the cited classical A2 local-data theorem. The source's existing cubic theta realization already handles every local exponent of the sextic twist, with a fixed finite family of cusps after ray splitting. Its only explicitly treated horizontal derivative has order one.

The needed extension can instead be made within that rank-one theta realization. A pure horizontal derivative of any fixed positive order transforms exactly into the opposite pure derivative at the center of a cusp inversion. The conductor exponent stays unchanged. The two-Poisson transfer then preserves the angular order, so no growing list of angular types is generated by its iteration.

This does not by itself construct a centered rank-two scattering theorem. It does supply a native fixed-angle input for the angular Möbius coefficient exposed in PR #915.

## 2. Exact cusp derivative identity

Write \(\alpha(z)=z/|z|\) for \(z\ne0\). Let

\[
g=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathcal O_K),
\qquad c\ne0,
\]

and set \((z',v')=g^{-1}(z+a/c,v)\). The source's cusp coordinates are

\[
z'=-\frac dc-\frac{\overline z}{c^2(|z|^2+v^2)},\qquad
v'=\frac{v}{N(c)(|z|^2+v^2)}.                    \tag{2.1}
\]

For \(r\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}\), put \(m=|r|\) and

\[
\mathscr D_r=
\begin{cases}
\partial_z^m,&r>0,\\
\partial_{\overline z}^m,&r<0.
\end{cases}
\]

### Lemma 2.1

For every smooth function \(F\) of cusp coordinates,

\[
\boxed{
\mathscr D_r\{F(z',v')\}\big|_{z=0}
=(-1)^m\alpha(c)^{2r}N(c)^{-m}v^{-2m}
\mathscr D_{-r}F\!\left(-\frac dc,\frac1{N(c)v}\right).
}                                                       \tag{2.2}
\]

The identity contains no height derivatives and no lower derivative orders.

**Proof.** In the Taylor jet for a pure \(\partial_{\overline z}\) derivative at zero, set \(z=0\) while retaining \(\overline z\) as the independent Wirtinger variable. Formula (2.1) then becomes exactly

\[
z'=-d/c-\overline z/(c^2v^2),\qquad
\overline{z'}=-\overline d/\overline c,\qquad
v'=1/(N(c)v).
\]

Thus only a linear change in \(z'\) remains. The chain rule of order \(m\) is precisely \((-c^{-2}v^{-2})^m\partial_{z'}^m\), which is the negative-\(r\) case of (2.2). The pure \(\partial_z\) case is its conjugate coordinate calculation. This Taylor-jet argument applies to smooth, not necessarily holomorphic, \(F\). \(\square\)

The imported theta is scalar under the relevant automorphy map: its multiplier \(\overline{\kappa(g_1)}\) is constant in \((z,v)\), as displayed in `eq:theta-cusp-automorphy`. Hence differentiating that multiplier introduces no extra terms. This scalar automorphy is an explicit input here, not inferred from a weight convention.

## 3. Mellin transform with the exact angular and conductor factors

Retain the source's finite periodic twist \(\Psi\), extended by zero at its excluded primes, and its function \(\Theta_\Psi\). Its finite additive-translate realization is unchanged. Define, initially for \(\Re s>1\),

\[
\mathcal T_r(s,\Psi)
=\left(\sum_n^*\alpha(n)^r\gamma_2(n)\Psi(n)(Nn)^{-s}\right)
 \left(\sum_b\alpha(b)^{3r}\Psi(b)^3(Nb)^{-3s+1/2}\right), 
                                                               \tag{3.1}
\]

where the primary-generator and bad-prime conventions are exactly those of the source. The associated smoothed completion is

\[
T_r(X;\Psi)
=\sum_n^*\sum_b
\frac{\alpha(n)^r\gamma_2(n)\Psi(n)\alpha(b)^{3r}\Psi(b)^3}
     {\sqrt{Nn}\,Nb}
 V_*(Nn(Nb)^3/X),
\qquad V_*(x)=\sqrt x\,W(x).                    \tag{3.2}
\]

Thus the source's \(T\) is \(T_{-1}\). Define

\[
\mathcal J_r(s)=\int_0^\infty
\mathscr D_r\Theta_\Psi(0,v)\,v^{2s+m-2}\,dv,
\qquad
G_m(s)=\Gamma(s+m/2-1/6)\Gamma(s+m/2+1/6).
\]

### Lemma 3.1. Exact Mellin normalization

Put \(\varepsilon_r=i^{m+r}\), so \(\varepsilon_r=1\) for negative \(r\) and \(\varepsilon_r=(-1)^m\) for positive \(r\). Then

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal J_r(s)=
\frac{3^{5/2}\varepsilon_r}{4}
\left(\frac{27}{(2\pi)^2}\right)^s
G_m(s)\mathcal T_r(s,\Psi).
}                                                       \tag{3.3}
\]

**Proof.** A Fourier mode acquires \((2\pi i)^m|\ell|^m\alpha(\ell)^r\) under \(\mathscr D_r\). The Bessel integral is

\[
\int_0^\infty v^{2s+m-1}K_{1/3}(4\pi|\ell|v)\,dv
=\frac{2^{2s+m-2}}{(4\pi|\ell|)^{2s+m}}G_m(s).
\]

Multiplication by the derivative factor gives exactly

\[
\frac{i^m}{4(2\pi)^{2s}}G_m(s)
\alpha(\ell)^r N(\ell)^{-s}.
\]

On the source's initial theta support \(\ell=\lambda^{-3}nb^3\), one has \(\alpha(\lambda^{-3})=i\) and \(N(\lambda^{-3})=1/27\). Write \(\phi_\Psi(x)=\chi_x(\lambda)^2\Psi(x)\), with its source periodic extension and excluded-prime zeros; this is the full periodic multiplier, including the moving twist, rather than just the fixed factor denoted by \(\phi\) in (3.7). The explicit coefficient formula gives
\(d_0(\ell)\phi_\Psi(\lambda^3\ell)=3^{5/2}|b|\gamma_2(n)\Psi(n)\Psi(b)^3\).
Substitution proves (3.3). Absolute convergence for \(\Re s>1\) justifies the termwise integration. \(\square\)

### Lemma 3.2. Reflection and absence of a constant-mode residue

For each of the source's additive translates, let \(c\) be its reduced denominator, let \(c_{\mathrm F}\) be its finite Fourier coefficient, and let \(\kappa(g_1)\) be its fixed scalar automorphy multiplier. Then

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal J_r(s)
=(-1)^m\sum_{\boldsymbol h}
c_{\mathrm F}(\boldsymbol h)\overline{\kappa(g_{1,\boldsymbol h})}
\alpha(c_{\boldsymbol h})^{2r}N(c_{\boldsymbol h})^{1-2s}
\mathcal J^{\vee}_{-r,\boldsymbol h}(1-s).
}                                                       \tag{3.4}
\]

The cusp transform on the right uses the opposite pure derivative and the same height exponent \(2s+m-2\) in its defining variable. Both sides continue to entire functions of \(s\).

**Proof.** Apply (2.2) in each finite translate and substitute
\(v=(N(c)v')^{-1}\). The product of the radial factors is

\[
N(c)^{-m}v^{-2m}v^{2s+m-2}\,dv
\longmapsto N(c)^{1-2s}(v')^{2(1-s)+m-2}\,dv'.
\]

This proves the exact conductor exponent and (3.4). For \(v\to\infty\), the original nonzero Fourier modes and all their fixed-order derivatives decay exponentially. For \(v\to0\), use the transformed cusp expansions and (2.2). Because \(m\ge1\), the pure horizontal derivative annihilates every constant Fourier mode. All remaining modes decay exponentially at the transformed height \(1/(N(c)v)\). The extra powers of \(v\) from differentiation are harmless against that decay. For each fixed finite twist this proves absolute convergence of \(\mathcal J_r(s)\) on every compact set of \(s\), hence entireness. Division by the gamma factors in (3.3) also makes \(\mathcal T_r\) entire. \(\square\)

The restriction \(r\ne0\) is essential to this argument. With no horizontal derivative, a transformed constant mode need not vanish; its possible polar term cannot be deleted. No claim about that case follows from (3.4).

### Theorem 3.3. Full fixed-angular reflection

Define

\[
V_{*,m}^{\sharp}(x)=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{(0)}
\widehat V_*(-t)
\frac{\Gamma((m+1)/2-1/6+t)\Gamma((m+1)/2+1/6+t)}
     {\Gamma((m+1)/2-1/6-t)\Gamma((m+1)/2+1/6-t)}
\left(\frac{(2\pi)^4x}{27}\right)^{-t}dt.
                                                               \tag{3.5}
\]

Use the source's active-prime sets, local factors \(B_{p,j}\), and fixed finite triples \((d_\sigma,\psi,c_0)\). Then \(T_r(X;\Psi)\) is the corresponding sum of terms

\[
\boxed{
C_r\sum_{\ell\ne0}
\frac{d_\sigma(\ell)\alpha(\ell)^{-r}}{\sqrt{N\ell}}
\psi(\lambda^4\ell)
\prod_{p\in\mathcal A}B_{p,j_p}(\lambda^4\ell)
V_{*,m}^{\sharp}\!\left(\frac{N\ell X}{N(c)^2}\right),
}                                                       \tag{3.6}
\]

with exact scalar

\[
\boxed{
C_r=\frac{i^r}{81}\alpha(c)^{2r}
\widehat\phi(h_0)\overline{\kappa_0}
\prod_{p\ {\rm inactive}}(1-(Np)^{-1})
\prod_{p\ {\rm active}}\chi_p(\sigma_p)^{-2}\omega_{p,j_p}.
}                                                       \tag{3.7}
\]

Here \(\sigma_p,\omega_{p,j}\) are exactly the source's local quantities. Thus \(|C_r|\le1/81\); all moving local exponents and nonunit zeros are unchanged. For \(r=-1\), (3.5)–(3.7) recover the source's formulas exactly.

**Proof.** The opposite cusp Mellin series in (3.4) has normalization

\[
\mathcal J_{-r}^{\vee}(s)
=\frac{i^mG_m(s)}{4(2\pi)^{2s}}
\sum_{\ell\ne0}d_\sigma(\ell)\alpha(\ell)^{-r}
N(\ell)^{-s}\breve e(-d\ell/c).
\]

Insert this and (3.3) into (3.4), then into the Mellin integral for (3.2). The radial change \(t=1/2-s\) produces (3.5). Its constant is

\[
\frac{(-1)^mi^m}{81\varepsilon_r}=\frac{i^r}{81},
\]

which proves (3.7) after the unchanged source local finite Fourier sums. Those sums do not involve the horizontal derivative order; the only new index factor is \(\alpha(\ell)^{-r}\), common to the local finite sum.

For the contour justification, the initial and dual series are absolutely convergent at \(\Re s>1\) and \(\Re s<0\), respectively. The entire Mellin transform, its finite-order bounds obtained by splitting the height integral at one, and Stirling's formula give polynomial vertical growth through the intervening strip by the same Phragmen–Lindelof argument as in the source. The fixed smooth test gives rapid Mellin decay. The first possible pole of the numerator gamma factors of (3.5) occurs at \(t=-(m+1)/2+1/6\le-5/6\), so shifting the dual kernel contour to zero crosses none. Its reciprocal gamma factors are entire. This proves the stated identity with the same type of rapidly decreasing large-\(x\) bounds and positive-power small-\(x\) bounds as the source. Fixed derivative seminorms may depend on \(m\), but introduce no power of the arithmetic scales. \(\square\)

## 4. The completed mean square keeps its conductor powers

For fixed \(r\ne0\), fixed ray character \(\xi\), and the source twist

\[
\Psi_{k,f}(n)=\xi(n)\chi_n(k)\chi_n(f)^4,
\]

write \(T_r(X;k,f)=T_r(X;\Psi_{k,f})\).

### Theorem 4.1. Fixed-angle analogue of the source's completed mean square

Assume the coefficient, fixed-ray uniformity, and classical quadratic large-sieve inputs used to prove the source's Proposition `prop:R`. For polynomially bounded positive scales and every \(\epsilon>0\),

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{0<Nk\le\mathcal H}|T_r(X;k,f)|^2
\ll_{r,\xi,S,\epsilon,C_0,I}
D^\epsilon\|W\|_{C^J(I)}^2
\left(\mathcal H+\frac{\mathcal H^2Nf}{X}\right).
}                                                       \tag{4.1}
\]

The rows are all nonzero elements, with every row unit and repeated-prime pattern retained. The integer \(J\) may depend on the fixed angular order, but not on \(k,f,X,\mathcal H\).

**Proof of the adapter.** In the source's squarefree-row preparation, each denominator has the form \(c=c_0k_0r_{\mathrm{fix}}\). The new scalar in (3.7) supplies the phase
\(\alpha(k_0)^{2r}\), times a factor independent of the varying squarefree row \(k_0\). It has modulus one and is a row contraction. It cannot change the number of fixed-ray branches or any conductor norm.

On each theta support \(\ell=u\lambda^jnb^3\), the factor \(\alpha(\ell)^{-r}\) is a product of separate fixed-unit, squarefree-index, and cube-index phases. All are independent of \(k_0\). The source's proof applies its quadratic sieve to arbitrary bounded coefficient vectors, so these phases do not alter its coefficient-energy estimates. Every local factor \(B_{p,j}\), including its zero values, and every active/inactive prime decomposition are identical to the source. The new smooth transform has precisely the uniform derivative/tail bounds needed in that preparation, by Theorem 3.3.

Consequently the source's prepared squarefree-row estimate retains exactly the form

\[
\sum_{Nk_0\le\mathcal H_0}^*
|T_r(X;u_0tk_0,g)|^2
\ll D^\epsilon\|W\|_{C^J}^2
\left(\mathcal H_0+
\frac{\mathcal H_0^2(Nt)^2Ng}{X}\right).
\]

Its absolutely convergent sums over cube dyads and ramified valuations are unchanged, since only unit-modulus phases were added to their coefficients. Finally write every nonzero row uniquely as \(k=u_0sv^2\), with \(s\) squarefree and no condition \((s,v)=1\). The exact zero-extended identity
\(\chi_n(u_0sv^2)\chi_n(f)^4=\chi_n(u_0s)\chi_n(fv^2)^4\)
does not involve \(r\). Apply the squarefree estimate with row bound \(\mathcal H/(Nv)^2\), auxiliary \(fv^2\), and sum \((Nv)^{-2}\). This is the convergent ideal sum \(\zeta_K(2)\), proving (4.1). \(\square\)

## 5. The angular type is preserved by the finite two-Poisson iteration

Define

\[
a_{r,\xi}(n)=\alpha(n)^r\gamma_2(n)\xi(n),\qquad n\text{ squarefree}.
\]

Its CRT identity is the source's identity with the same cross-symbol, because \(\alpha\) is multiplicative on the chosen generators. The cube completion and cube removal are

\[
X^{-1/2}\sum_n^*a_{r,\xi}(n)\chi_n(k)\chi_n(f)^4W(Nn/X)
=\sum_h\frac{\mu_K(h)\alpha(h)^{3r}\Psi_{k,f}(h)^3}{Nh}
T_r(X/(Nh)^3;k,f).
                                                               \tag{5.1}
\]

This is finite on the relevant smoothed supports, or follows first from absolutely convergent Dirichlet convolution. Its coefficient bounds are exactly those of the source's inverse cube projection.

The only other angular-sensitive interface is the first paired Gauss identity. Since

\[
a_{r,\xi}(n)=\alpha(n)^{r+1}a_{-1,\xi}(n),
\]

the source's `eq:paired-first-gauss` becomes

\[
\boxed{
a_{r,\xi}(u_1)\overline{a_{r,\xi}(u_2)}
\gamma(\chi_{u_1}\overline{\chi_{u_2}})
=\mu(u_1)\mu(u_2)(\xi G)(u_1u_2^{-1})
\alpha(u_1u_2^{-1})^{r+1}.
}                                                       \tag{5.2}
\]

Thus after its finite ray expansion the intermediate coefficient is exactly

\[
\mu(n)\xi_1(n)\alpha(n)^{r+1}
\mathbf1_{(n,t)=1}\overline{\chi_n(y)}\chi_n(C)^4\chi_n(d).
                                                               \tag{5.3}
\]

The factors at the common divisors still cancel against their conjugates. In the second Poisson identity, multiplying the source's paired identity by
\(\alpha(z_1z_2^{-1})^{r+1}\) returns precisely

\[
a_{r,\xi'}(z_1)\overline{a_{r,\xi'}(z_2)}.
                                                               \tag{5.4}
\]

Every conductor, exclusion, auxiliary update, child row range, and smooth norm kernel is therefore the same as in `eq:common-poisson-output` and `lem:second-transfer`. The phase in (5.3) stays in the coefficient; it is never declared a ray character. Equations (5.2)–(5.4) prove closure at the same fixed \(r\).

### Theorem 5.1. Source-conditional fixed-angle canonical extension

For fixed \(r\ne0\), define the literal source energy with the new coefficient by

\[
\mathcal E_r(\mathcal H,X,F;\xi,W)
=\frac1{XF}\sum_{\substack{F\le Nf<2F\\(f,S)=1}}^*
\sum_{0<Nk\le\mathcal H}
\left|\sum_{(n,S)=1}^*a_{r,\xi}(n)\chi_n(k)\chi_n(f)^4
W(Nn/X)\right|^2.                                      \tag{5.5}
\]

Assume the imported finite completion/transfer inputs specified above. Fix \(\kappa>0\), \(C_0\ge1\), a compact \(I\subset(0,\infty)\), and the fixed ray group. For every \(\epsilon>0\) there is an integer \(J=J(r,\kappa,C_0,\epsilon)\) such that, for every \(D\ge2\), smooth \(W\) supported in \(I\), and scales

\[
\mathcal H,X,F\ge1,\qquad \Sigma=XF\le D^{C_0},\qquad
\mathcal H\le\Sigma D^{-\kappa},
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{\mathcal E_r(\mathcal H,X,F;\xi,W)
\ll_{r,I,S,\xi,\kappa,C_0,\epsilon}
D^\epsilon\Sigma\|W\|_{C^J(I)}^2.}                       \tag{5.6}
\]

**Proof.** Use Theorem 4.1 in place of the source's order-one completed estimate and (5.1)–(5.4) in place of its angular-sensitive formulas. The cube inverse weights retain modulus \((Nh)^{-1}\); every common-divisor extraction and exclusion update retains the same norm and zero mask; and the two Poisson transformations return exactly the coefficient in (5.5). All remaining inequalities in the source's finite induction involve only those unchanged scales, coefficient moduli, and smooth seminorms. In particular its child domain has \(\Sigma'=X'F'\), \(\mathcal H'/\Sigma'\le\mathcal H/\Sigma\), and strict row-scale contraction by the same positive gap. Its initial counting case and finite number of steps therefore prove (5.6), with the derivative orders enlarged for the fixed order \(r\). This reproduces the source's canonical domain \(\Sigma=XF\); the independent majorant used inside its transfer lemma is not a strengthening of that domain. \(\square\)

This is an extension of the specified imported proof mechanism, not an inference from its theorem restricted to finite-order twists. The nonzero derivative order is the additional hypothesis that makes its reflection entire.

## 6. Deriving the angular scalar input, including moving conductors

This section derives the exponent needed below from Theorem 5.1. It does not import the stronger exponent available elsewhere in the repository for finite-order characters.

### Theorem 6.1. The original angular mean square and its scalar extraction

Fix an integer \(t\ne-1\), a finite-order Hecke character \(\nu_0\), and a finite set \(S\) containing the source bad primes and the conductor primes of \(\nu_0\). Define

\[
A_{u,t}(D)=\sum_{(n,S)=1}\mu(n)\nu_0(n)\alpha(n)^t
\chi_n(u)W(Nn/D).                                      \tag{6.1}
\]

Under the same source inputs as Theorem 5.1, for fixed \(0<\vartheta\le1/10\) and every \(\epsilon>0\), some finite \(J\) gives

\[
\boxed{\sum_{0<Nu\le D^{1+\vartheta}}|A_{u,t}(D)|^2
\ll_{t,\nu_0,S,I,\vartheta,\epsilon}
D^{2+\vartheta+\epsilon}\|W\|_{C^J(I)}^2.}               \tag{6.2}
\]

Consequently, for every fixed smooth compactly supported \(W\),

\[
A_{1,t}(D)\ll_{t,\nu_0,S,W,\epsilon}D^{11/12+\epsilon}.
                                                               \tag{6.3}
\]

**Proof.** In the source's initial Poisson calculation the square is expanded after conjugating its inner sum. The new factor at its two coprime indices is therefore \(\alpha(z_1z_2^{-1})^{-t}\). Multiplying the source's `eq:initial-paired-gauss` by that factor gives exactly

\[
\mu(z_1)\mu(z_2)\overline{\nu_0(z_1)}\nu_0(z_2)
\alpha(z_1z_2^{-1})^{-t}
\gamma(\overline{\chi_{z_1}}\chi_{z_2})
=\sum_\xi c_\xi a_{-t-1,\xi}(z_1)
\overline{a_{-t-1,\xi}(z_2)}.                            \tag{6.4}
\]

The finite ray expansion involves only \(\nu_0\) and the original ray factors. Common angular factors cancel with their conjugates. Thus the exclusion identity, signed initial-Poisson expression, norm kernels, and initial scales are unchanged. They satisfy

\[
X=D/(BF),\qquad \Sigma=XF=D/B,\qquad
\mathcal H\ll D^{1-\vartheta}/B^2,
\qquad \mathcal H/\Sigma\ll D^{-\vartheta}.
\]

Apply Theorem 5.1 with \(r=-t-1\ne0\), \(\kappa=\vartheta/2\), and the source's bounded-scale counting cases. The rest of its initial reduction uses coefficient moduli and the common smooth-weight principle, so it proves (6.2) with the stated seminorm. Every nonzero row, including sixth powers and repeated-prime rows, is included.

For extraction set \(H=D^{1+\vartheta}\), \(Y=H^{1/6}\), and average over primary primes \(Y/2<Np\le Y\) outside fixed \(S\). The prime ideal theorem for the fixed field, as used in the source, supplies \(\gg Y/\log Y\) such primes. The literal identity \(\chi_n(p^6)=\mathbf1_{p\nmid n}\) and unit modulus of the angular factor give

\[
|A_{1,t}(D)-A_{p^6,t}(D)|\ll_W D/Y.
\]

The mean square (6.2) consequently implies

\[
|A_{1,t}(D)|^2\ll D^{11/6+5\vartheta/6+\epsilon}
+D^{5/3-\vartheta/3}.
\]

Choose \(\vartheta\) and the mean-square loss sufficiently small for any requested final loss. This proves (6.3). The constants here may depend on the fixed finite character; no conductor-uniform pointwise assertion has yet been made. \(\square\)

### Lemma 6.2. A common zero-free half-plane at the fixed angular type

For each fixed \(t\ne0,-1\), (6.3) implies that every Hecke Euler product with angular type \(t\) and an arbitrary finite-order finite twist has no zero in

\[
\Re s>\beta,\qquad \beta=11/12.                         \tag{6.5}
\]

The character on ideals is defined using the primary generators in (6.1). Its infinity factor is \(\alpha(n)^t\). On all six generators of an ideal, the compensating unit factor can be placed in the finite part modulo \(3\). Thus every integer \(t\) is allowed with this ray convention; the infinity factor is not being declared finite order.

**Proof.** The scalar proof is the source's Mellin argument applied to (6.3), which has now been proved for this angular type. If \(\rho\) were a zero with \(\Re\rho>\beta\), choose \(W(y)=y^{-\rho}\varphi(y)\) for nonzero smooth nonnegative \(\varphi\) supported in \((1,2)\). Then \(\widehat W(\rho)>0\). The integral

\[
\int_0^\infty A_{1,t}(D)D^{-s}\frac{dD}{D}
\]

is holomorphic for \(\Re s>\beta\) by (6.3), and equals \(\widehat W(s)/L^S(s,\nu_0\alpha^t)\) for \(\Re s>1\). The product identity continues to the whole half-plane and contradicts its value at \(\rho\). For nonzero \(t\), the elementary continuation to \(\Re s>1/2\) proved in Lemma 6.3 below suffices for this step; no new automorphic continuation theorem is required. Omitted finite Euler factors have no zero for \(\Re s>0\). The boundary \(\beta\) is common to every fixed finite twist because no norm of its conductor occurs in the exponent of (6.3). This observation does not assert uniformity of that equation's implied constant. \(\square\)

### Lemma 6.3. Uniform reciprocal bounds for the fixed nonzero infinity type

Fix \(t\ne0,-1\). A modulus \(\mathfrak q\) here includes the fixed primary-generator modulus and any moving excluded primes. Under (6.5), for every \(\delta,\epsilon>0\),

\[
\boxed{|L(\sigma+i\tau,\eta\alpha^t)^{-1}|
\ll_{t,\delta,\epsilon}
[N\mathfrak q(2+|\tau|)]^\epsilon,
\qquad\sigma\ge\beta+\delta,}                           \tag{6.6}
\]

uniformly in the finite character and its imprimitive zero extension modulo \(\mathfrak q\).

**Proof of the needed polynomial growth.** After extending from the primary generator to all six generators, the ideal sum is one sixth of a lattice sum of the form

\[
\sum_{0<|z|\le\sqrt x}\kappa(z)\alpha(z)^t,
\]

where \(\kappa\) is periodic modulo an ideal of norm \(Q\ll_t N\mathfrak q\), has modulus at most one, and includes every nonunit zero. The unit compensator described above is periodic modulo \(3\) and can be absorbed into \(Q\). For each residue class, the underlying lattice has spacing comparable to \(\sqrt Q\). A fundamental-cell comparison gives

\[
\sum_{\substack{z\equiv a\ (\mathfrak q)\\0<|z|\le R}}
\alpha(z)^t
=\frac1{\operatorname{covol}(\mathfrak q)}
\int_{|z|\le R}\alpha(z)^t\,dz
+O_t(R/\sqrt Q+1).                                    \tag{6.7}
\]

For completeness, cells meeting the boundary or a disk of radius \(O(\sqrt Q)\) about zero contribute \(O(R/\sqrt Q+1)\). On all other cells use \(|\nabla\alpha(z)^t|\ll_t|z|^{-1}\). Summing their errors over annuli of width \(\sqrt Q\) gives another \(O_t(R/\sqrt Q+1)\). This proves (6.7) uniformly in the translated lattice. Because \(t\) is a nonzero integer, its disk integral is zero. Sum over the \(O(Q)\) residue classes, with their bounded weights, to obtain

\[
\left|\sum_{Nn\le x}(\eta\alpha^t)(n)\right|
\ll_t\sqrt{Qx}+Q\ll_t Q\sqrt x\qquad(x\ge1).           \tag{6.8}
\]

Partial summation continues the Euler Dirichlet series holomorphically to \(\Re s>1/2\), and for every fixed \(a>1/2\) bounds it by \(O_{t,a}(Q(1+|s|))\) on \(\Re s\ge a\). This applies directly to imprimitive characters because their excluded-prime zeros were already included in \(\kappa\).

**From growth and zero-freeness to (6.6).** On the simply connected half-plane \(\Re s>\beta\), choose the holomorphic branch \(g(s)=\log L(s)\) agreeing with the Euler branch on \(\Re s>1\). This exists by (6.5); no pole is present because \(t\ne0\) and (6.8) gives holomorphy. Fix \(\beta<a<\beta+\delta<b\) with \(b>1\). The polynomial bound, Borel–Caratheodory on fixed-radius disks centered at \(2+i\tau\), and the uniformly bounded Euler logarithm there give

\[
|g(a+i\tau)|\ll\log[Q(2+|\tau|)],\qquad
|g(b+i\tau)|\ll_b1.
\]

For a fixed \(\tau_0\), apply the three-lines theorem to \(g(s)e^{(s-i\tau_0)^2}\) on \([a,b]\). The Gaussian absorbs the logarithmic vertical growth and supplies the hypotheses on the unbounded strip. Hence, uniformly for \(\beta+\delta\le\sigma\le b\),

\[
|g(\sigma+i\tau_0)|\ll(\log[Q(2+|\tau_0|)])^{\omega},
\qquad \omega\le\frac{b-\beta-\delta}{b-a}<1.
\]

Exponentiation of \(-\Re g\) gives \(Q_0^\epsilon\) for \(Q_0=Q(2+|\tau_0|)\). The region \(\sigma\ge b>1\) follows directly from the Euler product. This proves (6.6). This is the analytic-log argument of PR #913, `FOURTH_MOMENT_ATTACK.md`, Lemma 8.1, with its polynomial-growth premise now proved for the actual angular type. \(\square\)

### Corollary 6.4. The exact negative-branch Möbius sum

Fix the ray character \(\eta\) from PR #915. For squarefree \(k\), an arbitrary exclusion ideal \(\mathfrak d\), and polynomially bounded \(Nk,N\mathfrak d,G\) with \(G\ge1\), define

\[
M_{k,\mathfrak d}(G;V)=
\sum_{(g,S\mathfrak d)=1}\mu(g)\eta(g)\alpha(g)^{-3}
\chi_k(g)^3 V(Ng/G).
\]

Then, uniformly in these moving indices,

\[
\boxed{|M_{k,\mathfrak d}(G;V)|
\ll D^\epsilon G^{11/12}\|V\|_{C^J(I)}.}                \tag{6.9}
\]

The character already vanishes when \((g,k)>1\); this zero is retained.

**Proof.** The finite part \(\eta(g)\chi_k(g)^3\), with its exclusion by \(\mathfrak d\), is a finite-order ray character with modulus norm \(O_S(Nk\,N\mathfrak d)\), after the fixed primary/ray convention. Apply Lemma 6.3 with \(t=-3\), which uses the newly derived reflected order \(r=2\). Mellin inversion of the reciprocal Euler series and a shift to \(\Re s=\beta+\delta\) give \(G^{\beta+\delta}Q^\epsilon\) times a fixed smooth seminorm. Choose the small margins in terms of the polynomial scale ceiling and the final loss. This proves (6.9). The same proof bounds norm-modulated tests by a polynomial in their Mellin frequency, which is absorbed by the rapidly decreasing majorants in the weight separations below. \(\square\)

## 7. A strict analytic saving in the coupled standard-cusp face

We now use the exact object of PR #915, `COUPLED_THETA_COMPLETION.md`, Sections 1–3 and 6. Its original axes have norm scales \(A,B\), its squarefree rows satisfy \(H\le Nk<2H\), and its Ramanujan allocation is \(a=efg\) with pairwise coprime squarefree factors of norms \(E,F,G\), where \(EFG\asymp A\).

**Smooth allocation convention.** Fix once and for all a smooth dyadic partition of unity on norm values at least one, with profiles supported in a fixed compact subinterval of \((0,\infty)\) and uniform rescaled derivative bounds. Define an \(E,F,G\) component by inserting the three corresponding smooth factors at \(Ne/E,Nf/F,Ng/G\). Thus summing these components recovers the complete allocation expansion exactly. This convention applies to every theorem in this section. In particular the \(g\)-cutoff is smooth; (6.9) is not being applied to a sharp interval indicator. The previously proved quadratic/cubic coefficient estimates allow these bounded profiles, so all PR #915 bounds remain available for these components.

First restrict to its explicit standard-cusp coefficient face

\[
\ell=\lambda^{-3}nb^3,\qquad (nb,S)=1,\qquad
d_0(\ell)=3^{5/2}|b|\overline{\chi_n(\lambda)^2}\gamma_2(n).
                                                               \tag{7.1}
\]

This restriction is part of the theorem. We do not assert that every other cusp coefficient has this formula.

The reindexing \(n=en'\), \(b=fb'\) gives the scale

\[
Y=\frac{H^2EG^2}{BF},\qquad Nn'\asymp U,
\quad U(Nb')^3\lesssim Y                                \tag{7.2}
\]

up to fixed constants and rapidly decreasing tails. Its amplitude before the \(n',b'\) sums is \((AFG)^{-1/2}\). Most importantly, the negative factor is exactly

\[
\mu(g)\eta(g)\alpha(g)^{-3}\chi_k(g)^3,                  \tag{7.3}
\]

and it has no condition \((g,n'b')=1\). Such an extra mask would destroy the factorization used below and would change the original Ramanujan identity.

### Theorem 7.1. Angular cancellation survives the coupled cubic sieve

Under the source inputs already named, the standard-cusp allocation component satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{k\sim H}^*|\mathcal C^{(0)}_{E,F,G}(k)|^2
\ll D^\epsilon G^{-1/6}
\left[HE+Y+(EY)^{2/3}\right],
\qquad Y=\frac{H^2EG^2}{BF}.
}                                                       \tag{7.4}
\]

More generally, the proof gives the factor \(G^{2\beta-2}\) in place of \(G^{-1/6}\) from any conductor-uniform bound of the form (6.9) with exponent \(\beta>1/2\). All fixed ray-class branches in the specified face are included. The rows remain squarefree and primary.

**Proof.** We give the separation of the moving mask explicitly. Fix a cube dyad, a cube index \(b'\), and a finite ray branch. Smoothly separate the norm kernel by Mellin inversion before taking a pointwise bound or applying either sieve. The transformed-weight estimates of the source, already used in PR #915, give an integrable majorant with arbitrarily many polynomial frequency moments. The same is true after the changes of variables below, because all variables are divided by their own dyadic scales. It therefore suffices to estimate each separated term with those polynomial frequency factors; the final integration changes only the smooth seminorm and \(D^\epsilon\).

For fixed \(f,b'\), after bounded separate norm ratios have been absorbed in the coefficient vectors, the remaining sum has prefactor \((AFG)^{-1/2}/Nb'\) and arithmetic part

\[
\sum_{e,g,n'}^*
\frac{u_e v_{n'}}{\sqrt{Nn'}}
\mu(g)\eta(g)\alpha(g)^{-3}
\chi_k(gn'b')^3\chi_{n'}(e)^4
\mathbf1_{(k,ef)=1}\mathbf1_{(g,ef)=1}.                 \tag{7.5}
\]

Before (7.5), expand every fixed residue restriction and every \(g\)-dependent fixed-ray factor into the characters of the corresponding fixed finite unit group. In particular, the additive phase as a function of \(en'(fb')^3\) factors after this finite expansion, since all these standard-face indices are units at its fixed bad modulus. The \(g\)-factor is therefore a fixed finite family of the characters \(\eta\) admitted in (6.9), not an arbitrary bounded function of \(g\).

Here \(|u_e|,|v_{n'}|\le1\) after a fixed rescaling. They include \((e,f)=1\), \((n',f)=1\), all fixed ray phases, and the separated smooth weights. The mask \((e,n')=1\) is exactly the zero of \(\chi_{n'}(e)^4\). Formula (7.5) follows from the explicit standard coefficient and Gauss CRT
\(\gamma_2(en')=\gamma_2(e)\gamma_2(n')\chi_{n'}(e)^4\).
In particular there is no moving coefficient depending jointly on \(g,n'\).

The restriction \((g,e)=1\) must not be discarded or hidden in a coefficient that depends on \(e\). Expand it exactly:

\[
\mathbf1_{(g,e)=1}=\sum_{d\mid g,\ d\mid e}\mu(d),
\qquad g=dg',\quad e=de'.                              \tag{7.6}
\]

We apply this to the factorized expression (7.5). Its product expression provides an extension to the off-mask pairs; only its values on the original coprime support are used by (7.6). Squarefreeness retains \((g',d)=(e',d)=1\). There is now no mask \((g',e')=1\): it has been replaced by the signed divisor sum. The product \(\mu(d)\mu(dg')\) equals \(\mu(g')\) on this retained support. All other \(d\)-factors have modulus at most one. Specifically, \(\chi_{n'}(d)^4\) enters the \(n'\)-vector; the Gauss CRT factor \(\chi_{e'}(d)^4\) enters the \(e'\)-vector; and \(\chi_k(d)^3\mathbf1_{(k,d)=1}\) is a row contraction. The remaining row mask \((k,e')=1\) is retained.

For fixed \(d,f\), the \(g'\)-sum is consequently (6.9) at scale \(G/Nd\) and exclusion \(df\), independent of both \(e'\) and \(n'\). It is bounded, uniformly for each row, by

\[
D^\epsilon (G/Nd)^\beta.                               \tag{7.7}
\]

Nonempty dyads with scale between a fixed positive constant and one are included in a fixed bounded annulus and satisfy the same estimate with a changed constant. Empty dyads contribute zero.

The remaining double sum over \(e'\) and \(n'\), whose scales are \(E'=E/Nd\) and \(U\), is \(\sqrt{E'}\) times the quadratic–cubic composition in PR #915, Lemma 6.1. Its exact surviving row mask is \((k,e')=1\). Its added restrictions involving \(d,f\) enter the separate coefficient vectors, so that lemma applies and gives

\[
\sum_{k\sim H}^*|Q_k(E',U)|^2
\ll D^\epsilon\frac{H+U}{U}
\left[E'+U+(E'U)^{2/3}\right].                          \tag{7.8}
\]

That lemma itself expands \((k,e')=1\) by inclusion–exclusion and uses divisor-Cauchy, followed by the quadratic and cubic sieves. Thus no moving zero mask is replaced by one at this step. Applying the pointwise bound (7.7) before this row norm is legitimate because the \(g'\)-factor no longer depends on either of the two column indices.

There are \(O(F)\) choices of the frozen \(f\); apply Minkowski to them. The square of the total prefactor, including (7.7) and \(\sqrt{E'}\), is

\[
\frac{F^2}{AFG}\left(\frac G{Nd}\right)^{2\beta}
\frac E{Nd}
\asymp G^{2\beta-2}(Nd)^{-2\beta-1},                   \tag{7.9}
\]

using \(EFG\asymp A\). The cube sum with weight \(1/Nb'\) has bounded mass on each dyad. For \(1\le U\ll Y\), expansion of (7.8), with \(H,E'\gg1\), bounds it by a constant times

\[
HE'+Y+(E'Y)^{2/3}
\ll HE+Y+(EY)^{2/3}.                                   \tag{7.10}
\]

The dyadic tails are controlled by the same transformed-weight decay as in PR #915; if \(Y<1\), every nonzero-frequency term is in that decay range and the displayed bound still follows. Summing the relevant norm dyads costs a subpower.

Finally apply Minkowski over the signed divisor label \(d\). By (7.9), its square-root cost is

\[
\sum_d (Nd)^{-\beta-1/2}<\infty                         \tag{7.11}
\]

because \(\beta>1/2\). This is an absolutely convergent ideal sum, so the number of possible common divisors causes no arithmetic power loss. Equations (7.8)–(7.11) prove the general factor \(G^{2\beta-2}\). Corollary 6.4 supplies \(\beta=11/12\), giving (7.4). \(\square\)

### Corollary 7.2. Balanced scales and a larger proved component range

At \(A=B=D\), sum the standard-cusp allocation components with their original signs. Then

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{k\sim H}^*|\mathcal C^{(0)}_{D,D}(k)|^2
\ll D^\epsilon
\left[HD+H^2D^{5/6}+H^{4/3}D^{2/3}\right].
}                                                       \tag{7.12}
\]

In particular the canonical target \(D^{2+\epsilon}\) holds for this specified face throughout

\[
1\le H\le D^{7/12}.                                    \tag{7.13}
\]

**Proof.** Since \(EFG\asymp D\), (7.2) gives \(Y\asymp H^2G/F^2\) and \(EY\asymp H^2D/F^3\). Thus the three terms in (7.4) are bounded respectively by

\[
HD,\qquad H^2D^{5/6},\qquad H^{4/3}D^{2/3}.
\]

There are only logarithmically many dyadic allocation blocks and finitely many fixed ray branches. Minkowski absorbs their sum in \(D^\epsilon\). Substitution of \(H\le D^{7/12}\) proves (7.13). \(\square\)

For the all-negative corner \(E=F=1,G=A\), the same proof gives the sharper direct quadratic-sieve form

\[
\sum_{k\sim H}^*|\mathcal C^{(0)}_{1,1,A}(k)|^2
\ll D^\epsilon\left[HA^{-1/6}+\frac{H^2A^{11/6}}B\right].
                                                               \tag{7.14}
\]

Here, after smooth separation, the Möbius factor is simply bounded by \(A^{\beta-1}\), and the remaining normalized squarefree theta sum has quadratic-sieve energy \(\ll D^\epsilon(H+Y)\), \(Y=H^2A^2/B\). No cubic composition is needed. The prior bound \(H/A+H^2A^2/B\) from PR #915 remains available as well. The new long-dual term gains \(A^{1/6}\); its first term need not improve every earlier bound.

### Theorem 7.3. A weaker improved bound at every reflected cusp

For the full reflected allocation component, including all three source cusp sequences, their theta units and ramified valuations, the smooth-block convention above gives

\[
\boxed{\sum_{k\sim H}^*|\mathcal C_{E,F,G}(k)|^2
\ll D^\epsilon E G^{-1/6}(H+Y),
\qquad Y=\frac{H^2EG^2}{BF}.}                           \tag{7.15}
\]

The more general factor is \(EG^{2\beta-2}\). In particular (7.14) holds for the full all-negative component at every cusp, as well as for the standard face. The stronger two-column bound (7.4) is still restricted to (7.1).

**Proof.** Use the exact full scalar cancellation of PR #915 before discarding any phases. Fix a ray branch, theta unit \(u\), valuation \(m\ge-4\), and indices \(e,f,b'\). The remaining negative \(g\)-factor is exactly (7.3), now with the fixed exclusion \(ef\). The other coefficient is the source's prepared value

\[
A_{u,m}(en',fb')
=\frac{d_\sigma(u\lambda^m en'(fb')^3)
\alpha(u\lambda^m en'(fb')^3)
\psi(u\lambda^{m+4}en'(fb')^3)}
{3^{m/6}\sqrt{N(fb')}}.
\]

It is bounded by 27 and is independent of \(g,k\) after the fixed ray split. This is exactly the source's `eq:prepared-theta-sum` coefficient and `lem:theta-bounds`; no factorization into Gauss coefficients at the other cusps is assumed. All finite dependence of the branch on the ray class of \(g\) is expanded into a fixed finite family of characters before applying (6.9). The same applies to its dependence on the row ray class.

Mellin-separate the smooth norm kernel first. Corollary 6.4 then bounds the \(g\)-sum by \(D^\epsilon G^\beta\), independently of \(n'\). The residual character is \(\chi_k(n'b')^3\mathbf1_{(k,ef)=1}\). The fixed \(b'\)-character and \((k,ef)\)-mask are row contractions. The quadratic large sieve applies to the arbitrary bounded coefficient vector in \(n'\), divided by \(\sqrt{Nn'}\), and gives \(D^\epsilon(H+U)\). If \(n'\) meets \(S\), first split its finitely many squarefree \(S\)-parts; this changes only a fixed scale and row contraction. The cube index remains unrestricted at \(S\).

Minkowski over the \(O(EF)\) choices of \(e,f\), together with the original squared prefactor, costs exactly

\[
\frac{E^2F^2}{AFG}G^{2\beta}
\asymp EG^{2\beta-2}.
\]

On each cube dyad \(Nb'\asymp C\), the mass of \(1/Nb'\) is bounded. The valuation \(m\) supplies the amplitude \(3^{-m/3}\), while the effective \(n'\)-length is \(O(Y/(3^mC^3))\). Taking square roots, both the \(H\) and \(Y\) contributions are summable over \(m\ge-4\). The transformed-weight tail bounds then permit the same dyadic summation as the source. This proves (7.15), with all nonunit zeros and reflected bad-prime support intact. \(\square\)

## 8. What this still does not prove

The genuine gain here is (7.4), and at balanced scales the enlarged standard-cusp range (7.13). It comes from cancellation in the exact angular Möbius sum (7.3), with its conductor uniformity proved before use. The nonzero angular derivative adapter is essential: the finite-order zero-free theorem by itself did not cover this coefficient.

The following boundaries remain explicit.

- The results are deductions from the named analytic inputs of the imported October 5 proof. The derivative, Mellin normalization, lattice/log adapter, and moving-mask composition are native proofs here; they are not an independent reconstruction of the imported theta automorphy or both large sieves.
- The stronger coupled estimate (7.4) uses the explicit standard-cusp coefficient face (7.1) and squarefree primary rows. The weaker estimate (7.15) covers all source cusps, but does not give the same whole-face range. No arbitrary-row coupled estimate is supplied by these theorems.
- The statement concerns the reflected completed balanced sum. Returning to its literal two-factor squarefree face still requires the exact completion inversions and a norm theorem that controls their full family at the required scales.
- The initial fourth-moment dual range is of order \(D^{3-\vartheta}\) at product length \(D^2\), far beyond (7.13). No centered sesquilinear transformation reaching that range has been proved here.
- The signed product-column diagonal identified in PR #914 can be removed exactly, but a positive Hilbert norm bound such as (7.4) does not automatically bound the resulting centered covariance. Distinct correction triples produce cross terms with product-column equality; factor-pair equality is not the correct diagonal.
- No classical rank-two positive-root zeta normalizer or finite angular scattering closure has been proved for the literal centered family. The present route bypasses that missing interface for one concrete scalar factor; it does not resolve it.

Thus the generalized \(2k\)-th moment estimate and RH remain unproved by this note.
