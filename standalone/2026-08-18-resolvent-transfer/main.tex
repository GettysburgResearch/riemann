\begin{abstract}
A recent factor--67 programme obtained a rigorous all-real finite-$P_{61}$
scalar bias and a genuinely subcritical safe-child coefficient budget.  The two
statements do not yet compose: the native rough recursion carries a raw
parity-swapped child coefficient $r=p^{-1/2}$, whereas the safe recursive ledger
carries a smaller coefficient $t$.  We give the exact statement-to-use theorem
for this interface.

On every finite rough-history DAG, the native parity-resummed scalar is
$f=(I+R)^{-1}b$, where $R$ is the raw child operator.  Replacing $R$ by a
contracted positive operator $T$ forces the current
\[
 g=(I+T)(I+R)^{-1}b=b+(T-R)f.
\]
The current is therefore nonlocal in the complete child scalar.  A two-node
separator shows that if $t<r$, no universal source-local one-channel current can
be coefficientwise nonnegative: a positive datum at the child forces current
coefficient $t-r<0$ at the parent.  More generally, the exact transfer has an
alternating nonzero coefficient at every available history depth.

We then repair the literal source identity.  In the positive paired parity
cone, for any $0\le t\le r$, the leftover $(r-t)$ copy of the swapped child is
placed in the current and the $t$ copy in the recursive ledger.  The source is
positive, first-owner exact, activation exact, and the raw coefficient is spent
once.  Two parity channels are minimal: no nonzero positive scalar functional
can intertwine a positive swap with the sign character.

Finally we prove an exposure-conservation theorem.  Contracting the recursive
ledger relocates, but cannot reduce, the total raw signed child exposure.  The
repaired $P_{61}$ interval $1/42\le F/M\le1/8$ therefore cannot be composed with
the $<1/8$ recursive mass by replacing raw exposure with safe exposure.  The
correct remaining theorem is one nonlocal Bellman current inequality, denoted
$\NCBI$.  It is a resolvent form of the common CPSL67/GPHT*/GABPT/TFPE/ACBI
frontier.  We prove the reduction but not $\NCBI$.  The Riemann Hypothesis
remains unproved.
\end{abstract}

\section{Finite rough-history operators}

Let $V$ be a finite directed acyclic graph, ordered so every child follows its
parent.  For a scalar vector $f=(f_v)_{v\in V}$ define the nonnegative,
strictly upper-triangular raw child operator
\[
 (Rf)_v=\sum_{v\to w}r_{v,w}f_w,
 \qquad r_{v,w}\ge0.
\]
The raw coefficient is the coefficient occurring in the literal native source;
for a single rough prime it is $p^{-1/2}$ together with the parity swap.

Let $b_v$ be the local complete grouped scalar before future rough factors.  The
signed parity recursion is
\begin{equation}
 f+Rf=b.
 \label{eq:rawrec}
\end{equation}
Nilpotence gives the finite expansion
\[
 f=(I+R)^{-1}b=(I-R+R^2-\cdots)b.
\]
No convergence assumption is involved.

\section{The exact resolvent transfer}

\begin{theorem}[Raw-to-contracted transfer]
\label{thm:transfer}
Let $T$ be any other nonnegative strictly upper-triangular operator.  The same
$f$ satisfies
\[
 f+Tf=g
\]
if and only if
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 g=(I+T)(I+R)^{-1}b=b+(T-R)f.
 }
 \label{eq:forcedcurrent}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Substitute $f=(I+R)^{-1}b$ into $g=(I+T)f$.  Conversely,
\eqref{eq:forcedcurrent} implies $(I+T)f=g$.
\end{proof}

Equation \eqref{eq:forcedcurrent} is the complete statement-to-use contract.
The current is not a freely selected finite-$P_{61}$ object.  If a raw
coefficient is reduced, the omitted coefficient reappears in the current and
multiplies the complete child scalar.

\subsection{Chain expansion}

On a chain $0\to1\to\cdots\to L$, write the edge coefficients as $r_i$ and
$t_i$.  Backward substitution gives
\[
 f_i=b_i-r_if_{i+1},
 \qquad
 g_i=b_i+(t_i-r_i)f_{i+1}.
\]
Hence the coefficient of $b_k$, $k\ge1$, in $g_0$ is
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 (-1)^{k-1}(t_0-r_0)r_1\cdots r_{k-1}.
 }
 \label{eq:chaincoef}
\end{equation}
For constant $r,t$ this becomes
\begin{equation}
 g=b+(t-r)\sum_{k\ge1}(-r)^{k-1}S^kb,
 \label{eq:nonlocalseries}
\end{equation}
where $S$ is the forward shift and the series ends at the finite chain depth.
Unless $t=r$, every available depth occurs.

\section{A universal one-channel positivity obstruction}

\begin{theorem}[Two-node separator]
\label{thm:separator}
Suppose $0\le t<r$.  There is a positive local datum for which the exact
contracted one-channel current is negative.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take two nodes and $b=(0,1)$.  Equation \eqref{eq:rawrec} gives
$f=(-r,1)$.  Therefore
\[
 g_0=f_0+tf_1=t-r<0.
\]
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Safe-child local no-go]
A universal source-local one-channel positive current cannot replace the raw
factor--67 edge $r=p^{-1/2}$ by the safe coefficient
$t=\alpha=\lambda r<r$ while retaining exact parity and arbitrary positive
child data.
\end{corollary}

\begin{corollary}[No fixed-depth repair]
Any current formula depending only on histories of depth at most $m$ fails on a
chain of depth $m+1$ whenever $t\ne r$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}
The theorem is deliberately scoped.  The actual arithmetic local datum is not
arbitrary.  Arithmetic correlations may make the forced nonlocal current
positive.  What is excluded is a generic local source theorem deduced solely
from $t<r$, positivity of the input cone, or the finite-$P_{61}$ bias.
\end{remark}

\section{Exact positive paired-source repair}

The scalar obstruction does not prevent a positive source identity.  It shows
why the parity state must remain two-channel.

Let $\Src_+=\R_+^2$ and define the positive swap
\[
 \mathsf S(x,y)=(y,x).
\]
Signed observation is $\ell(x,y)=x-y$, so
$\ell(\mathsf SP)=-\ell(P)$.

\begin{theorem}[Positive contracted paired-source identity]
\label{thm:paired}
Suppose the literal raw first-owner identity is
\begin{equation}
 P_v=B_v\oplus\bigoplus_w r_{v,w}\mathsf SP_w,
 \label{eq:rawsource}
\end{equation}
with source-disjoint summands.  For arbitrary
$0\le t_{v,w}\le r_{v,w}$ put
\begin{equation}
 C_v(t)=B_v\oplus\bigoplus_w(r_{v,w}-t_{v,w})\mathsf SP_w.
 \label{eq:currentpacket}
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 P_v=C_v(t)\oplus\bigoplus_wt_{v,w}\mathsf SP_w.
 }
 \label{eq:contractedsource}
\end{equation}
Every term is positive, every raw coefficient is spent exactly once, and all
activation and first-owner labels are unchanged.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The source supports are inherited from \eqref{eq:rawsource}.  On each edge the
leftover and recursive coefficients add to the raw coefficient:
$(r-t)+t=r$.
\end{proof}

Applying signed observation gives
\begin{align}
 f_v&=b_v-\sum_wr_{v,w}f_w,\label{eq:signedraw}\\
 c_v&=b_v-\sum_w(r_{v,w}-t_{v,w})f_w,\label{eq:signedcurrent}\\
 f_v&=c_v-\sum_wt_{v,w}f_w.\label{eq:signedcontracted}
\end{align}
Thus the positive source current is exact, but its signed observation is the
nonlocal current \eqref{eq:forcedcurrent}.

\begin{theorem}[Two parity channels are minimal]
There is no nonzero positive scalar functional $\phi:\R_+^2\to\R_+$ satisfying
$\phi(\mathsf SP)=-\phi(P)$ for every positive $P$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Both $\phi(P)$ and $\phi(\mathsf SP)$ are nonnegative, while the displayed
identity makes them negatives.  Hence both vanish for every $P$.
\end{proof}

This explains why a paired source can be positive while every exact
one-channel signed current fails positivity on some input.

\section{Why the safe-child budget does not pay raw scalar debt}

Let a local base packet have unsigned mass $M$ and scalar at least $\ell M$.
For child $w$, assume $0\le f_w\le u m_w$.  Splitting the raw coefficient
$r_w$ into current coefficient $r_w-t_w$ and recursive coefficient $t_w$ gives
\begin{equation}
 \sum_w(r_w-t_w)f_w+\sum_wt_wf_w=\sum_wr_wf_w.
 \label{eq:exposure}
\end{equation}
Thus the strongest interval-only lower bound is
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 f_v\ge \ell M-u\sum_wr_wm_w,
 }
 \label{eq:rawbound}
\end{equation}
independently of the contracted budget.

With the repaired constants from PR \#576,
\[
 \ell=\frac1{42},\qquad u=\frac18,
\]
so the local interval argument needs
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\sum_wr_wm_w}{M}\le\frac{\ell}{u}=\frac4{21}.
 \label{eq:criticalraw}
\end{equation}
The statement
\[
 \sum_wt_wm_w<\frac18M
\]
does not imply \eqref{eq:criticalraw}.  For example raw fraction $1/4$ and
contracted fraction $1/10$ satisfy the safe budget while
\[
 \frac1{42}-\frac18\cdot\frac14=-\frac1{1344}<0.
\]

\begin{firewall}
A safe-child coefficient theorem controls the recursive ledger.  It does not
change the raw coefficient appearing in the native Möbius source.  The leftover
$r-t$ must be present in the current and must be paid in the same signed
coordinate.
\end{firewall}

\section{The exact remaining Bellman inequality}

Let $f=(I+R)^{-1}b$ be the native scalar and let $T$ be a chosen contracted
operator.  The source current \eqref{eq:currentpacket} has scalar
\[
 c=b-(R-T)f.
\]
A sufficient positive-resolvent condition is
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \NCBI:\qquad c\ge Tc.
 }
 \label{eq:ncbi}
\end{equation}
Indeed,
\[
 (I+T)^{-1}=(I-T)(I-T^2)^{-1},
\]
and $c\ge Tc$ gives
\[
 f=(I+T)^{-1}c=(I-T)(I-T^2)^{-1}c\ge0.
\]

The condition is global because $c$ contains the complete child scalar $f$.
The two-node separator proves this dependence cannot be removed in a universal
one-channel source-local theorem.

At scalar scope, \eqref{eq:ncbi} is a resolvent normal form for the common
frontier appearing elsewhere as CPSL67, GPHT*, ASHP67, GABPT, TFPE, ACBI, or
RJTE.  Those formulations use different source or dual coordinates, but each
must produce the nonlocal correlation missing from the interval estimate
\eqref{eq:rawbound}.
