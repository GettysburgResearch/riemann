\begin{abstract}
A parity-aware reconstruction of a projective rough-prime route reduced the
positivity of native M\"obius rows to a global common-source problem, but proved
that every fixed local parity reset fails. We continue that route in three
steps. First, the unique conclusion-producing combination
\(\RR_X=5c_X(2)+3c_X(3)\) collapses the two-row cone to one reciprocal state
whose Mellin numerator has no zero in the relevant half-plane. Second, an
adaptive Bonferroni theorem proves that variable even depth restores positivity
in the complete homogeneous rough product, while a repeated-integral formula
proves that every smooth-interior rough finite difference is nonnegative.
Hence the remaining sign is supported only on histories crossing a row
activation boundary. Third, we construct an exact positive \(2\times2\) Julia
lift in which cumulative rough-history parity acts by diagonal conjugation and
the off-diagonal is precisely the \(5{:}3\) scalar. The positive trace retains
the real zeta pole, so matrix positivity alone cannot feed Landau. The entire
unresolved producer is localized to one globally owned activation-boundary
functional, denoted GABPT. We prove GABPT implies the Riemann Hypothesis through
one fixed Mellin transform. GABPT is not proved here; RH remains unproved.
\end{abstract}
\begin{center}\fbox{\parbox{0.92\textwidth}{\textbf{Status.} This is an
unconditional reduction and interface repair, not a proof of RH. The remaining
arithmetic theorem is stated explicitly as GABPT.}}\end{center}

\section{The native rows and the unique scalar}
For the canonical component row \(Q_X(j)\), write
\[
c_X(j)=\sum_{k\le X/j}\frac{\mu(k)}{\sqrt{k}}Q_{X/k}(j).
\]
The parity audit of the predecessor shows that cumulative rough history changes
the sign of a terminal observation by \((-1)^{|h|}\); magnitudes and activations
remain unchanged. The two fixed rows have an exact conclusion-producing
combination.

\begin{theorem}[Single-scalar collapse]\label{thm:scalar}
Put \(\RR_X=5c_X(2)+3c_X(3)\). Then
\[
\RR_X=\sum_{n\le X}\frac{a_*(n)}{\sqrt n}\log\frac Xn,
\]
where
\[
a_*(n)=6\1_{n=1}-6\mu(n)+9\1_{2\mid n}\mu(n/2)
-3\1_{4\mid n}\mu(n/4).
\]
The unsieved dictionary \(q_*=1*a_*\) is
\[
q_*(1)=0,\quad q_*(2)=15,\quad q_*(3)=6,\quad q_*(4)=3,
\quad q_*(m)=6\ (m\ge5).
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Insert the exact coefficient dictionaries of rows two and three and collect the
M\"obius shifts. Convolution with the constant-one sequence gives the displayed
finite values; for \(m\ge5\) every boundary correction has entered and the
constant tail is six.
\end{proof}

Let \(b=(\delta_1-\delta_2)*\mu\) and
\[
G(X)=\sum_{n\le X}\frac{b(n)}{\sqrt n}\log\frac Xn.
\]
Direct convolution gives
\[
a_*=6\delta_1-3(2\delta_1-\delta_2)*b,
\]
hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recip}
\RR_X=6\log X-3\left(2G(X)-2^{-1/2}G(X/2)\right).
\end{equation}
This is the first simplification: the global parity-Hall problem has only one
scalar output and one reciprocal state.

\section{The fixed-depth obstruction and adaptive depth}
The predecessor proved that every fixed even depth eventually makes the current
Euler block negative, whereas every fixed odd depth leaves the recursive parity
reversed. This rules out a fixed local reset, not a variable stopping rule.

Let \(0\le r_i\le r_{\max}<1\), let \(e_k\) be the elementary symmetric sums,
and put \(z=\sum_i r_i\). The complete rough product is
\[
P=\prod_i(1-r_i)=\sum_{k\ge0}(-1)^ke_k>0.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Adaptive Bonferroni positivity]\label{thm:bonf}
For an even integer \(L>z\), put
\[
S_{L-1}=\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}(-1)^ke_k.
\]
If
\[
L\log\frac{L}{ez}>\frac{z}{1-r_{\max}},
\]
then \(S_{L-1}>0\). For rough-prime weights \(r_i=p_i^{-1/2}\), the smallest
even integer with \(L\ge8(z+1)\) is sufficient.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Since \(-\log(1-r)\le r/(1-r_{\max})\),
\[
P\ge \exp\left(-\frac{z}{1-r_{\max}}\right).
\]
Maclaurin's inequality gives \(e_k\le z^k/k!\). A Chernoff bound for the
exponential tail yields
\[
\sum_{k\ge L}e_k\le \sum_{k\ge L}\frac{z^k}{k!}
\le\left(\frac{ez}{L}\right)^L.
\]
The assumed inequality makes the absolute omitted tail smaller than \(P\), so
\(S_{L-1}=P-\sum_{k\ge L}(-1)^ke_k>0\). For
\(r_{\max}\le67^{-1/2}\), the explicit linear choice has ample room.
\end{proof}

The theorem explains why the fixed-depth asymptotic does not settle the global
problem: the correct depth may grow with the accumulated rough hazard.

\section{Smooth-interior positivity}
Critical conjugation turns the logarithmic ramp into
\[
\phi(u)=u e^{u/2}\1_{u\ge0}.
\]
\begin{lemma}[Positive derivatives]\label{lem:der}
For \(r\ge1\) and \(u>0\),
\[
\phi^{(r)}(u)=e^{u/2}\left(\frac{u}{2^r}+\frac{r}{2^{r-1}}\right)>0.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Differentiate \(u e^{u/2}\) and induct on \(r\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Smooth-interior finite differences]\label{thm:interior}
If \(a_i\ge0\) and \(u\ge a_1+\cdots+a_r\), then
\[
\prod_{i=1}^r(I-T_{a_i})\phi(u)
=\int_0^{a_1}\!\cdots\!\int_0^{a_r}
\phi^{(r)}(u-v_1-\cdots-v_r)\,dv_1\cdots dv_r\ge0.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Apply \(f(u)-f(u-a)=\int_0^a f'(u-v)\,dv\) repeatedly. The activation
assumption keeps the complete integration cube in the smooth region, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:der} supplies the sign.
\end{proof}

Thus a rough history can contribute negatively only when its translation cube
crosses an activation boundary. This localizes the producer sharply.

\section{Exact activation-boundary localization}
Fully expand the finite parity-labelled source at fixed endpoint \(X\). Assign
every history to the smooth-interior class if its complete translation cube
stays within one active row cell; assign it to the boundary class otherwise.
One-use ownership gives the exact decomposition
\begin{equation}\label{eq:IB}
\RR_X=\II_X+\BB_X.
\end{equation}
Theorem~\ref{thm:interior} gives \(\II_X\ge0\). The remaining statement is:

\begin{definition}[GABPT]
The Global Activation-Boundary Parity Transport theorem is
\[
\BB_X\ge-\II_X
\]
for every sufficiently large \(X\), with every cumulative history parity and
source coefficient used exactly once.
\end{definition}

GABPT is equivalent to eventual nonnegativity of \(\RR_X\). It is a global,
variable-depth theorem. Separate leafwise transports cannot prove it, because
odd histories reverse orientation and independent leaf budgets duplicate the
same source reserve.

\section{A positive parity-covariant Julia lift}
Let \(g_2(n)=v_2(n)+1\) and
\[
h_*(n)=6g_2(n)+3\1_{2\mid n}g_2(n/2).
\]
Define
\[
\mathcal J_*(n)=\begin{pmatrix}h_*(n)&a_*(n)\\a_*(n)&h_*(n)\end{pmatrix}.
\]
\begin{theorem}[Julia positivity and parity conjugation]\label{thm:julia}
For every \(n\), \(h_*(n)\ge|a_*(n)|\), so \(\mathcal J_*(n)\succeq0\).
If \(D=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\), then for every rough history \(h\),
\[
D^{|h|}\mathcal J_*(n)D^{|h|}\succeq0,
\]
and its off-diagonal is \((-1)^{|h|}a_*(n)\).
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For an odd squarefree core \(d>1\), the only nonzero dyadic levels have
\[
(|a_*|,h_*)=(6,6),(15,15),(12,24),(3,33).
\]
The unit core is checked directly, and nonsquarefree odd cores have zero scalar
coefficient. Diagonal conjugation preserves the PSD cone and flips the
off-diagonal once per parity swap.
\end{proof}

The theorem repairs the parity interface exactly: one need not positively
realize both a scalar datum and its negative. Both are off-diagonal faces of one
positive matrix state.

\section{Why the positive trace does not finish Landau}
The Dirichlet series of \(g_2\) is
\[
\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{g_2(n)}{n^z}=\frac{\zeta(z)}{1-2^{-z}}.
\]
After excluding the nonphysical unit column, the Julia diagonal has symbol
\[
H(z)=3(2+2^{-z})\frac{\zeta(z)}{1-2^{-z}}-6.
\]
It retains the pole at \(z=1\).

\begin{firewall}[Source-blind Julia promotion]
The positive eigenchannels \(H(z)\pm A(z)\) retain the same real pole. Their
Mellin abscissa is therefore the shifted value \(1/2\), not zero. PSD Julia
gluing preserves parity but cannot, by its trace alone, provide the Landau
continuation needed for RH. The trace-free off-diagonal scalar remains the
conclusion-producing quantity.
\end{firewall}

\section{The annular reserve}
The complete \(P_{61}\) scale-four annular theorem supplies strong local lower
bounds for rows two and three. Its local certificate is useful, but its
parity-blind root composition cannot be imported after the odd-history
counterexample. In the present architecture an annular reserve may be used only
as one globally owned budget against \(\BB_X\). It may not be copied among
leaves, used in canonical orientation after odd histories, or reclassified as
recursive source mass. Proving the required one-use domination is one possible
route to GABPT.

\section{The Mellin--Landau consumer}
\begin{theorem}[Exact scalar transform]\label{thm:mellin}
For \(z=s+1/2\), initially in the absolute half-plane,
\[
\int_1^\infty\RR_X X^{-s-1}\,dX
=\frac6{s^2}-\frac{3(1-2^{-z})(2-2^{-z})}{s^2\zeta(z)}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Integrate each logarithmic ramp using
\[
\int_n^\infty\log(X/n)X^{-s-1}\,dX=\frac{n^{-s}}{s^2},
\]
then insert the Dirichlet series of \(a_*\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[GABPT implies RH]\label{thm:rh}
If GABPT holds, then the Riemann Hypothesis holds.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
GABPT makes \(\RR_X\) nonnegative for all sufficiently large \(X\). Removing a
finite initial interval changes its Mellin transform by an entire function.
Landau's theorem forces the tail transform to be holomorphic through
\(\Re s>0\), since the displayed meromorphic expression has no positive-real
singularity. A zero \(\rho\) of \(\zeta\) with \(\Re\rho>1/2\) would create a
pole at \(s=\rho-1/2\). The numerator cannot cancel it: its zeros require
\(2^{-\rho}=1\) or \(2\), which force \(\Re\rho=0\) or \(-1\). This is a
contradiction. Functional symmetry gives RH.
\end{proof}

\section{Hostile reconstruction checklist}
Reject the route at the first occurrence of any of the following:
\begin{enumerate}
\item a rough history whose cumulative parity is recorded but not applied;
\item an activation-boundary history counted in both \(\II_X\) and \(\BB_X\);
\item an annular reserve copied into more than one global owner;
\item a PSD carry column promoted to a positive arithmetic node source;
\item the Julia trace used as though its Mellin abscissa were zero;
\item a finite replay represented as a proof of GABPT;
\item a claim that this paper proves RH unconditionally.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
The fixed-depth no-go is not the end of the parity-Hall programme. Adaptive
depth proves positivity in the homogeneous product, and complete monotonicity of
the critical ramp proves every smooth-interior history positive. The parity
cocycle admits an exact PSD Julia implementation. These results compress the
open producer to one globally owned activation-boundary scalar. That scalar is
the correct target for the next proof or exact separator. GABPT remains open,
and the Riemann Hypothesis remains unproved.
