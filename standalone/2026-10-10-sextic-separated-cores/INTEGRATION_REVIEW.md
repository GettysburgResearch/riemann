## 1. The cross-side theorem keeps its original selectors

I independently derived the central two-factor identity before receiving the final author's proof. In the polynomial \(F_{q,C}\), set \(t=(q,m)\), \(d=(a,m)\), \(a=da_0\), \(m=dtm_0\). Since \(a\) avoids \(q\), \(d\) is prime to \(q\), \(t\mid q\), and \(a_0,m_0\) are pairwise coprime and avoid \(dq\). Conversely these conditions reconstruct exactly the original squarefree pair.

The original side gcd is \(td\), so its strict cutoff remains \(Ntd<C\). The long and short scales are \(D/(Nd\,Nt)\) and \(X/Nd\), with \(X=D/Nq\). The former is at least the latter because \(t\mid q\). The native/pointwise anisotropic bound therefore gives norm weights \((Nd)^{-b-1/2}(Nt)^{-1/2}\). The \(d\)-sum converges for \(b>1/2\), and the \(t\)-sum is a subpower divisor factor. This proves the stated uniform \(HDX^{2b}\) energy bound. The single zero row after shortening is handled separately by a bounded contribution.

For the cross gcd, first fix its exact value \(c\), then remove the remaining mutual coprimality by \(\sum_{e\mid(a,b)}\mu(e)\). Reindexing gives exactly
\[
\sum_{c,e}\mu(e)\sum_u\Phi(u/\sqrt H)
\mathbf1_{(u,ce)=1}|F_{ce,C}(u)|^2.
\]
The indicator is the product of a character and its conjugate; division by a possibly zero character is never needed. Taking absolute values after this identity leaves two convergent ideal tails and yields \(HD^2R^{2b-1}\).

The complete covariance is estimated before imposing the hard-conductor condition. The complement is removed using the pinned positive absolute accounting, which is valid under the added tuple selectors. That inherited accounting, rather than a new norm bound for an arbitrarily selected Möbius polynomial, justifies this step.

I checked the two intersection exclusions independently. Disjoint cross matches have \(\mathcal E\le(\beta D/T)^4\). A star with both cross gcds at least \(T\) forces the opposite side gcd to be at least \(T^2/(\beta D)\). With the strict within-side cutoff, these exclude all intersections in the stated range. At \(C=D^{(6-h)/7}\), the strict exponent condition is \(r<(1+h)/14\). Thus the four-event union has the single-event cost.

The note correctly distinguishes a sharp reduced-factor condition from a cross-gcd tail. They are not interchanged using signed monotonicity. Its residual average remains an explicit unproved premise.

## 4. The extension to all rows is an actual additional proof
