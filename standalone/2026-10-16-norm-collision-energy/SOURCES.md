## Primary external sources

1. Andrew V. Sutherland, MIT 18.785, Lecture 19, November 15, 2021,
   "The analytic class number formula":
   https://math.mit.edu/classes/18.785/2021fa/LectureNotes19.pdf
   Page 1's theorem (including its formula) was read as text and screenshot.
   The lecture's fixed-field ideal-count argument was inspected. Imported
   facts: I_K(x)=O_K(x), Euler convergence for real s>1, and the simple pole
   at one with a positive residue. No zero-free theorem is used. The exact
   analytic class number formula is more than the proof needs.

2. J. S. Milne, Algebraic Number Theory, version 3.08 (July 19, 2020):
   https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/ANT.pdf
   Theorem 1.18, printed page 24 (PDF index 25), read in text and screenshot:
   the tensor of a finite separable extension with a field is a product of
   finite separable fields. The finite-field tensor splitting follows by
   separable polynomial factorization and CRT, as reconstructed in Section 3.
   Milne is not cited as the source of the analytic zeta-pole theorem.

3. Martin Herdegen, Gechun Liang, Osian Shelley, arXiv:2205.13075,
   "A continuity theorem for generalised signed measures with an application
   to Karamata's Tauberian theorem": https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.13075
   Only the primary abstract was accessible. It is cited for classical
   context, NOT as an imported signed-measure theorem. TAUBERIAN.md supplies
   the entire positive-measure argument used here, including uniqueness and
   the continuity-point passage. Hardy--Littlewood/Karamata theory, Gaussian
   Fourier integration, Schur bounds and Euler-product comparison are
   classical; no invention of those general mechanisms is claimed.
