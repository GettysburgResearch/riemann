# Exact scalar local-filter classification and a sharp amplitude budget

9 November 2026. These elementary statements delimit the scalar filter class;
they are not a prohibition on arithmetic cancellation. The positive
two-channel finite-field adapter in `TWO_CHANNEL_LOCAL_ADAPTER.md` is a
different operator and is the main result of this lane.

## 1. The full prime-divisibility defect

Let a(n)=mu(n)chi(n), with chi completely multiplicative and |chi(n)|<=1,
and let alpha=chi(p). Put
L(s,chi)=sum_n chi(n)n^(-s)=product_p(1-chi(p)p^(-s))^(-1)
initially in Re(s)>1. A finite-order Dirichlet or unitary Hecke character
is the intended example; at later target points the pole assertions
assume the usual meromorphic continuation of this L-function there.
On coefficient sequences put

\[
 (D_pf)(n)=\mathbf1_{p\mid n}f(n/p),\qquad
 (U_pf)(n)=f(pn),\qquad (E_pf)(n)=\mathbf1_{p\mid n}f(n).
\]

Then U_p D_p=I and D_p U_p=E_p, and

\[
 U_pa=-\alpha(I-E_p)a. \tag{1}
\]

For p not dividing n, this is the usual Mobius sign flip. For p dividing n,
the left side vanishes because p^2 divides pn, and the right side vanishes
by its mask. The often-used unmasked equation U_pa=-alpha a is false.
The same statements hold for ideals and a prime ideal p.

## 2. Classification of finite operators supported at finitely many primes

Fix a finite set P of primes. Consider the complex algebra generated by
D_p,U_p and masks depending on v_p(n), for p in P. Every operator here is a
finite linear combination of finite words in those generators, with fixed
scalar coefficients. Norm-dependent weights, residue-symbol twists in n,
additive Fourier transforms, and moving prime sets are not part of this
fixed algebra statement.

### Proposition 1

For every such operator O there is a finite polynomial B_O in variables z_p
such that, initially in Re(s)>1,

\[
 \sum_{n\ge1}(Oa)(n)n^{-s}
 =\frac{B_O((p^{-s})_{p\in P})}
        {\prod_{p\in P}(1-\chi(p)p^{-s})}\frac1{L(s,\chi)}. \tag{2}
\]

The polynomial need not factor prime by prime. If O is a tensor product
of one-prime operators, it does.

**Proof.** Write n=m product_p p^(k_p), with (m,product P)=1. The original
local coefficient tensor is product_p [1,-chi(p),0,0,...], multiplied by
a(m). Each D_p shifts one local index right, U_p shifts it left, and a mask
multiplies its entries. A finite word therefore leaves a finite local
tensor, independent of m. A finite linear combination does the same.
Its generating polynomial is B_O. The untouched m-series is
[1/L(s,chi)] / product_p(1-chi(p)p^(-s)), proving (2). QED.

This is stronger than the statement for commuting pure dilations, but
narrower than a theorem about all Hecke operators. In particular genuine
residue-symbol channel mixing is outside its hypotheses.

### Corollary: exact source-pole preservation

The denominator in (2) is nonzero in Re(s)>0. At a zero rho
of L of order m in that half-plane, the filtered reciprocal has a pole of
order max(0,m-ord_rho B_O). Thus B_O(rho)!=0 preserves the entire original
pole. A vector of such probes preserves every pole, including multiplicity,
if their numerators have no common zero at any target point.

The operator algebra alone has supplied no bound on this reciprocal and
no new zero-free region. A filter can nevertheless be useful if a new
signed estimate is proved for its complete output. This is consistent
with the already existing two-scale source-preservation construction in
PR #1002; no scalar-no-go result here invalidates that construction.

Also, on integers coprime to P, Oa is a fixed scalar times a. It cannot
manufacture an arbitrary nonconstant free-column multiplier on that set.
This sharply distinguishes a finite local valuation operation from the
Gauss-coefficient change excluded in PR #1020's transfer audit.

## 3. A sharp amplitude price for killing a real mode

Let T_dF(X)=F(X/d), with 1<=d<=R, R>1. Consider a finite scalar probe

\[
 OF(X)=\sum_d c_d F(X/d),\qquad M_O(s)=\sum_d c_d d^{-s}.
\]

The old estimate |F(X)|<=C X^beta implies only

\[
 |OF(X)|\le C X^\beta\,\mathcal C_\beta(O),\qquad
 \mathcal C_\beta(O)=\sum_d |c_d|d^{-\beta}. \tag{3}
\]

### Theorem 2: optimal total-variation cost

Let lambda>beta be real, Delta=lambda-beta, and impose
M_O(beta)=1 and M_O(lambda)=0. Then

\[
 \boxed{\mathcal C_\beta(O)\ge
 \frac{1+R^{-\Delta}}{1-R^{-\Delta}}
 =\coth\!\left(\frac{\Delta\log R}{2}\right).} \tag{4}
\]

The bound is attained by a probe supported at d=1,R. It holds for complex
coefficients as well as real ones.

**Proof.** Set b_d=c_d d^(-beta), y_d=d^(-Delta), and r=R^(-Delta).
Then sum b_d=1, sum b_d y_d=0, and r<=y_d<=1. The affine function

\[
 \phi(y)=\frac{1+r-2y}{1-r}
\]

has absolute value at most one on [r,1]. Consequently

\[
 \sum|b_d|\ge\left|\sum b_d\phi(y_d)\right|
 =\frac{1+r}{1-r}.
\]

Equality is attained with b_1=-r/(1-r), b_R=1/(1-r). QED.

The general inequality C_beta(O)>=|M_O(beta+it)| also follows immediately
from the triangle inequality. Normalizing the target multiplier to one
cannot produce a power saving from (3), even when the support grows with X.
Equation (4) additionally quantifies the cost of separating two close
real Mellin modes. If Delta log R is small, the cost is asymptotic to
2/(Delta log R).

### Product filters and repeated annihilation

For a first-order factor supported at 1,q, normalized to preserve beta
and kill lambda, its exact weighted coefficient mass is
coth(Delta log q/2). For products over **distinct rational primes**, the
factorizations of support integers are unique, so their exact masses
multiply. For prime-ideal indexed shifts the same is true of the
ideal-indexed mass before identifying equal norms; collecting equal norm
shifts can lower that mass and must be checked separately. In the
collision-free case, if there are r factors and their total support is
R=product q_i, then

\[
 \mathcal C_\beta(O)=\prod_{i=1}^r\coth(\Delta\log q_i/2)
 \ge\coth\!\left(\frac{\Delta\log R}{2r}\right)^r. \tag{5}
\]

The lower bound follows from Jensen's inequality because
f(x)=log coth(Delta x/2) satisfies
f''(x)=Delta^2 cosh(Delta x)/sinh(Delta x)^2>0 for x>0.
The same formal convolution-mass bound holds for repeated scales, though
coefficient collisions must then be checked before asserting equality.

This exhibits a real tradeoff among repeated notches, available support,
and coefficient mass. It does not by itself price a genuinely arithmetic
Hecke conductor. Scalar norm shifts leave the original coefficients
intact and change the profile; any claimed automorphic conductor change
requires its own exact formula.
