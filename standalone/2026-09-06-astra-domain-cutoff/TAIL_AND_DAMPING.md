# Exact future-energy cost and the nearby-damping construction

Status: PROPOSED COMPONENT PROOFS; no domain-completion or RH claim.
All notation, Hilbert norms and source locks are those of CONSTRUCTION.md
and its pinned CSM26 parent. No synthetic function is substituted for xi
in the damping result below.

## 1. All multiplicities and any finite selected zero packet

Suppose lambda_i are distinct zeros of D in Re z>0, with multiplicities m_i.
For a fixed T>0 index pairs alpha=(i,r), 0<=r<m_i, and put

    k_alpha(t)=t^r exp(-conj(lambda_i)t) 1_[T,infinity)(t),
    v_alpha=-integral_0^T t^r exp(-lambda_i t)f(t)dt,
    C_T(alpha,beta)=<k_alpha,k_beta>
        =integral_T^infinity t^(r+s)
                          exp(-(lambda_i+conj(lambda_j))t)dt.     (T1)

The finite Gram C_T is positive definite: distinct exponential polynomials
are linearly independent on every open interval. All its entries are exact
incomplete-gamma integrals, reducible to finite exponential-polynomial sums.
Since Ly_T=D Lh_T, derivatives up to order m_i-1 vanish at lambda_i.
Therefore the actual future tail satisfies all constraints

    <k_alpha,y_T 1_[T,infinity)>=v_alpha.

The Hilbert projection theorem, or finite Cauchy--Schwarz applied after a
Gram solve, gives

    ||y_T 1_[T,infinity)||_2^2 >= v* C_T^(-1) v.    (T2)

This is the sharp minimum over vectors satisfying THIS selected finite
constraint set. It is not claimed that y_T attains it, nor that other zeros
can be deleted. Adding other constraints cannot reduce the minimum.

### Single zero, any multiplicity: a sharp leading cost

For one lambda with d0=Re lambda>0 and multiplicity m, change variables
u=t-T and use the basis u^r exp(-conj(lambda)u), 0<=r<m. Its fixed Gram is

    C(r,s)=(r+s)!/(2d0)^(r+s+1).                   (T3)

The new constraint vector is

    w_r=-exp(lambda T) integral_0^T
                         (t-T)^r exp(-lambda t)f(t)dt.

Since F(lambda) is nonzero,

    w_r=-exp(lambda T)[(-T)^r F(lambda)+O(T^(r-1))]

for r>=1, with the obvious convergent formula for r=0. The exact minimum
cost for this packet is consequently asymptotic to

    [(2d0)^(2m-1)|F(lambda)|^2/((m-1)!)^2]
            T^(2m-2) exp(2d0 T).                  (T4)

To verify the constant, factor the factorial moment matrix in (T3).
The identity binom(r+s,r)=sum_k binom(r,k)binom(s,k) factors its normalized
matrix as two unit-triangular Pascal matrices. Thus

    det C_m = product_(r=0)^(m-1)(r!)^2 / (2d0)^(m^2),
    (C_m^(-1))_(m-1,m-1)=(2d0)^(2m-1)/((m-1)!)^2.

This also shows why simplicity cannot simply be assumed: a repeated zero
adds the polynomial factor T^(2m-2) to the necessary future energy.

## 2. Nearby positive sources really are total

For any b>0 and any delta>0 set epsilon_j=delta/(j+1), j=1,2,... . Then

    span{d_(b+epsilon_j):j>=1} is dense in L2(0,infinity).          (T5)

Indeed d_(b+epsilon)(t)=exp(-epsilon t)d_b(t). If u is orthogonal to every
such vector, the function

    A(z)=integral_0^infinity conj(u(t))d_b(t)exp(-zt)dt

is analytic on Re z>-b. Cauchy--Schwarz and g<=1+t justify both convergence
and differentiation on compact substrips. It vanishes at epsilon_j->0,
an interior accumulation point. The identity theorem and Laplace uniqueness
give conj(u)d_b=0 almost everywhere, hence u=0 since d_b>0.

This is unconditional even at b=1/2. It fills the space by CHANGING the
generating family. It does not say that the parent's fixed source domain
M_b, generated by causal translations/all-pass shifts of ONE d_b, is full.

## 3. The corresponding simple output rule is NOT contractive

Fix the parent's actual pair 0<a<1/2, b=1/2+a, c=1/2-a, d_b and n_a.
The parent's exact arithmetic/functional-equation construction gives

    ||d_b||_2=||n_a||_2,
    n_a=r_a*r_a*r_a*j_a*d_c,
    r_a=delta_0-2a exp(-bt)dt,
    j_a(t)=2pi^a/Gamma(a) exp(-ct)(1-exp(-2t))^(a-1).

The tempting dense-domain map sends

    d_(b+epsilon) -> exp(-epsilon t)n_a(t).         (T6)

On a finite sum p(t)=sum_j q_j exp(-epsilon_j t), this map is just
p d_b -> p n_a. It is a time-varying multiplication rule, not automatically
the parent's stationary convolution k_a*.

**Theorem T7.** For EVERY delta>0 and this prescribed sequence epsilon_j,
there are a finite J and real coefficients q_1,...,q_J such that

    ||p n_a||_2^2 > ||p d_b||_2^2.                 (T7)

Thus (T6) does not even extend to a contraction on the whole space.
This is an existence theorem for the ACTUAL arithmetic pair; no explicit
finite coefficient vector is claimed certified in this packet.

Proof. As t decreases to zero, d_b(t)->1, whereas beta-kernel integration gives

    n_a(t) ~ (2pi)^a t^a/Gamma(a+1) ->0.

The r_a convolutions change only the higher-order terms near zero. Hence
|n_a|^2-|d_b|^2 is strictly negative on a short interval. Its integral on
the whole half-line is zero by the exact norm identity, so it is strictly
positive on some positive-measure set E, which can be chosen bounded.

Put omega(t)=|d_b(t)|^2+|n_a(t)|^2. For every 0<sigma<c,

    integral exp(2sigma t)omega(t)dt < infinity.

For n_a, this follows by weighted Young inequalities applied to its literal
convolution factors; their weighted L1/L2 norms are finite for sigma<c.
The proof of (T5), now with measure omega(t)dt, shows that the same finite
linear combinations p are dense in L2(omega dt). Approximate 1_E there.
The functional p -> integral |p|^2(|n_a|^2-|d_b|^2) is continuous for this
convergence because the absolute density is at most omega. Its value at
1_E is strictly positive, so a finite approximant has positive value.
Taking real parts of approximants gives real coefficients if necessary.
This proves (T7).

This does NOT disprove global contractivity of the actual Xi convolution.
It refutes the SUBSTITUTE output rule (T6), whose omitted commutator is next.

## 4. The exact omitted term, and its coefficient cost

The parent's locally integrable causal impulse satisfies k_a*d_b=n_a.
Let d_epsilon=exp(-epsilon t)d_b. Direct multiplication inside a convolution
shows

    k_a*d_epsilon-exp(-epsilon t)n_a
       =[(1-exp(-epsilon t))k_a]*d_epsilon.         (T8)

No continuation theorem is required for this local identity. On a finite
interval [0,T], Young's inequality and 1-exp(-epsilon t)<=epsilon t give

    ||k_a*d_epsilon-exp(-epsilon t)n_a||_(L2(0,T))
      <=epsilon ||d_b||_2 integral_0^T t|k_a(t)|dt.                 (T9)

For a signed combination, the safe bound contains sum_j |q_j|epsilon_j.
It is invalid to drop this coefficient mass merely because each epsilon_j
is small.

There is an exact obstruction to that cheap-coefficient strategy. Let
u in H, delta_u=dist(u,M_b), and v=sum_j q_j d_(b+epsilon_j). Since
(sum_j q_j)d_b belongs to M_b,

    delta_u <= ||u-v||_2 + C_b sum_j |q_j|epsilon_j,               (T10)
    C_b^2=1/(4b^3)+3/(4b^4)+3/(4b^5).

Here ||t d_b||_2<=C_b follows from g<=1+t. Therefore approximants to a target
with delta_u>0 CANNOT have the weighted coefficient mass tending to zero.
This is a lower bound on the coefficient cost, not a lower bound on the
actual commutator norm: cancellation in that commutator would have to be
proved separately.

For u=sqrt(2eta)exp(-eta t), the parent identifies delta_u^2=1-|B_b(eta)|^2.
A hypothetical shifted zero makes this positive. Thus the domain issue
cannot be erased by the combination of (T5) and the first-order bound (T9).

## 5. Construction attempts and their outcomes

1. Nearby damping fills a genuinely dense input span, unconditionally.
2. Its simplest source-matched output rule fails contractivity on actual data.
3. Correcting it to the stationary Xi map creates the explicit signed
   commutator (T8), with a non-negligible coefficient obligation in (T10).
4. The pole-neutral cutoff construction in CONSTRUCTION.md keeps the ORIGINAL
   domain and matches a zero-safe target locally. Equation (T2) retains its
   complete unavoidable future cost if any zero is off the line.

Neither procedure proves the missing subexponential energy bound. A new
arithmetic cancellation estimate is still required; no domain is silently
replaced by a larger one in the claimed RH consequence.
