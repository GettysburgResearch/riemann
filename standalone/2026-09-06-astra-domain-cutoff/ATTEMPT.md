# Attempted global completion: what was constructed and what failed

Status: RESEARCH RECORD, not a proof of RH.

## The decisive goal

The parent's critical source map is norm-preserving on M_b=B_b H2. Extending
that same stationary Xi transfer to every input requires control of its
uncanceled pole/domain obstruction. Merely producing a different dense
family or a bounded nonstationary map would not accomplish it.

## First attempt: add arbitrarily nearby source parameters

The inputs d_(b+delta/(j+1)) really are total. This is an unconditional theorem
with a direct Laplace-uniqueness proof, not an assumption about zeros.
However, assigning their outputs by the corresponding exponential damping
of n_a changes the map. The proposed dense-domain map fails contraction on
some finite real combination for EVERY delta>0. This is established using
the ACTUAL sources, their near-zero asymptotics, and their equal full norms.
It is not a planted rational counterexample.

To obtain the intended critical convolution one must add (T8), the exact
signed commutator. Small damping alone does not control it after coefficient
synthesis; (T10) records the coefficient cost. No upper bound with vanishing
combined error was found. The fact that the input span is dense cannot be
used in isolation.

## Second attempt: eliminate the inverse's artificial growing pole first

The inverse formula in the parent contains a real exponential from 1/(z-1/2).
The present choice F(z)=(z-1/2)^2/(z+1)^4 cancels that deterministic pole while
remaining nonzero at every hypothetical shifted nontrivial zeta zero.
The resulting inverse kernel phi is a stable cubic exponential, not a
sum with an exponentially growing individual kernel.

This gives explicit vectors y_T in the ORIGINAL domain, matching a fixed
RH-sensitive target exactly on [0,T]. All coefficients are ordinary Mobius
values, fixed before the endpoint is chosen. It eliminates finite matrix
optimization from this candidate synthesis.

The remaining future tail cannot be omitted. Formula (19), and the full jet
version (T2), show what a hypothetical off-line zero would require that tail
to carry. There is no appeal to a rightmost zero, separation of ordinates,
simplicity, or phase independence. A single zero gives an exponential lower
bound for every sufficiently large T; multiplicity adds the exact
polynomial factor in (T4).

The hoped-for closure was a subexponential upper bound for this explicit
family. The stable rational filter and its three exact null moments are
insufficient by themselves. The diagonal of the input energy is O(T), but
the complete signed off-diagonal correlation (14) is still unbounded by the
required subexponential expression in this work. The unconditional estimate
actually established is only 64(exp T-1) for ||h_T||^2.

This is not a hidden scalar positivity assumption. It is the exact named
open estimate DCP26.OPEN1, which must remain excluded from proof reachability.

## Third attempt: remove the Gram-conditioning uncertainty

GRAM_FLOOR.md supplies an explicit lower bound for every finite parent Gram,
uniformly for b in [1/2,1]. Its floor is exponentially small in rank; a very
large explicit source cutoff pays the corresponding inverse perturbation.
This closes a finite conditioning/error contract, not domain cyclicity.
Even a rank-independent positive Gram floor would not force the projection
limit to be zero, as the parent's exact synthetic control demonstrates.

## Relationship to earlier mathematics

These constructions belong to the classical Mobius/Nyman--Beurling/Hardy
cyclicity programme. Burnol's projection-product and causality analyses,
and Suzuki's actual Xi quotient, are essential prior context. The current
work contributes explicit constructions, energy/conditioning bounds, and a
source-level diagnosis of the damping attempt; it claims no literature
priority or independent acceptance.

The all-scale positive Jordan theorem is preserved but not used as a
substitute for the signed estimate. Its positive source lives on a different
safe-side observation. There is no proved norm comparison from that source
to the unbounded correlation in (14).
