## 1. Conventions and the exact source

Use H=L2(0,infinity), Lebesgue measure, inner product conjugate-linear in its
first slot, and Lf(z)=integral exp(-zt)f(t)dt. The Hardy norm is
(1/(2pi)) integral |Lf(iy)|^2 dy. Retain the parent's literal source

    g(t)=1-{exp(t)}+integral_0^t {exp(u)}du
        =floor(exp(t))(1-t)+log(floor(exp(t))!),
    d(t)=exp(-t/2)g(t),
    D(z)=(z-1/2)zeta(z+1/2)/(z+1/2)^2.             (1)

Integer activations are right-continuous; values at isolated knots have no
L2 consequence. In particular 0<g<=1+t, d is in L1 and L2, and ||d||_1<=6.
The apparent pole at z=1/2 in (1) is removed, with value 1.

Let M be the closed causal-translation cyclic subspace generated by d. This
is the same space as the parent's span of all nonnegative powers of one
causal all-pass filter R_eta. That identification uses the parent’s Hardy
cyclicity theorem. Its inner factor B consists of precisely the shifted
zeta zeros in Re z>0, with full multiplicities, with no singular inner factor.
We import this source/domain identification, not outerness of D.

## 2. Cancel the deterministic inverse pole, not a possible zeta zero

Choose the fixed target and its stable inverse kernel

    f(t)=exp(-t)[t-(3/2)t^2+(3/8)t^3],
    phi(t)=exp(-t)[1-(5/2)t+(7/8)t^2-t^3/16], t>=0.

Their exact transforms are

    F(z)=(z-1/2)^2/(z+1)^4,
    Phi(z)=(z-1/2)(z+1/2)^2/(z+1)^4.              (2)

The target is NOT outer: it has a double zero at the positive real point
1/2. That is intentional. No nontrivial zeta zero is real; consequently
F(rho-1/2) is nonzero for every nontrivial right-of-line zero rho. For a real
0<s<1, the alternating eta series is positive and
zeta(s)=eta(s)/(1-2^(1-s))<0; for s>1 the ordinary series is positive.
Thus the claimed exclusion of real nontrivial zeros is elementary. It is
therefore zero-safe for this argument. The canceled real point is the
removable zeta-pole point of D, not one of D's zeros.

The extra vanishing in Phi has explicit moment meaning:

    integral exp(-t/2)phi(t)dt=0,
    integral exp(+t/2)phi(t)dt=0,
    integral t exp(+t/2)phi(t)dt=0.                (3)

These identities remove designated exponential modes; they do not assert
cancellation for arbitrary Möbius coefficients. Direct gamma integration gives

    ||f||_2^2=29/512,     ||phi||_2^2=385/2048.     (4)

## 3. Completely prescribed locally finite Möbius synthesis

Put, without any optimization or choice of zero locations,

    h(t)=sum_(n<=exp(t)) mu(n)/sqrt(n) phi(t-log n). (5)

This is a finite sum on every compact time interval. For Re z>1/2 the
absolutely convergent reciprocal-zeta series and (2) give

    Lh(z)=Phi(z)/zeta(z+1/2),
    D(z)Lh(z)=F(z).                                (6)

There is also a direct finite proof, stronger than relying only on analytic
continuation. Put d0(t)=exp(-t/2)(1-t) for t>=0, zero otherwise. Then

    d(t)=sum_(m<=exp t) m^(-1/2)d0(t-log m),
    d0*phi=f.

The first equation follows by summing 1-t+log m and the factorial formula;
the second is the rational transform identity
[(z-1/2)/(z+1/2)^2] Phi(z)=F(z), or direct elementary integration.
On each compact interval the double convolution is a FINITE sum. Grouping
by k=mn gives coefficient sum_(n|k)mu(n), which is zero unless k=1.
Hence d*h=f locally, with every coefficient and activation retained. The
initial-half-plane calculation (6) supplies an independent check. Nothing
evaluates a divergent boundary Dirichlet series.

Here is an elementary bound sufficient for uniqueness and every local use.
Let P(v)=1-(5/2)v+(7/8)v^2-v^3/16. For v>=0,

    exp(-v/2)|P(v)|<8.                             (7)

Indeed the individual upper bounds are 1, 5/e, 14/e^2 and (27/2)/e^3;
the inequality e>5/2 puts their sum below 8. Each summand in (5) has absolute
value at most 8 exp(-t/2), so

    |h(t)|<=8 exp(t/2),
    ||h 1_[0,T]||_2^2<=64(exp(T)-1).               (8)

This bound uses only |mu|<=1 and is not subexponential.

Define

    h_T=h 1_[0,T],       y_T=d*h_T.                 (9)

Then h_T is compactly supported L1 intersection L2. Young's inequality gives

    y_T in H,     ||y_T||_2<=48 sqrt(exp(T)-1).     (10)

Moreover y_T belongs to M. To see this without assuming D outer, write
it as the Bochner integral of causal translates of d against h_T; approximate
that integral by finite simple-function/Riemann sums, using strong continuity
of translations. Finally, causality and (6) give the exact local match

    y_T(t)=f(t),        0<t<T, almost everywhere.  (11)

Thus an explicit vector in the ORIGINAL source domain matches the fixed
zero-safe target on every prescribed finite interval. This is a local
construction, not norm convergence on the whole half-line.

The coefficients in (5) are fixed ordinary Möbius values. There is no Gram
inverse and no unbounded-order choice of an annihilator in this construction.

## 4. Exact finite arithmetic energy and a complete diagonal bound

Write

    H(T)=||h_T||_2^2.

For v,L>=0 set

    R_L(v)=integral_0^L phi(u)phi(u+v)du.           (12)

This is an explicit finite polynomial-exponential integral. If m<=n<=exp(T),
then the (m,n) entry of the truncated Gram is

    (mn)^(-1/2) R_(T-log n)(log(n/m)).              (13)

Consequently the following identity retains EVERY cross term and every
truncation endpoint:

    H(T)=Ddiag(T)+Ccross(T),
    Ddiag(T)=sum_(n<=exp T) mu(n)^2/n R_(T-log n)(0),
    Ccross(T)=2 sum_(m<n<=exp T) mu(m)mu(n)/sqrt(mn)
                                R_(T-log n)(log(n/m)).              (14)

The diagonal is unconditional:

    0<=Ddiag(T)<= (385/2048)(1+T).                 (15)

Here sum_(n<=exp T)1/n<=1+T. Since H(T)>=0, (14) also gives the
unconditional lower bound Ccross(T)>=-(385/2048)(1+T). The missing
estimate is its upper bound, not a lower bound already supplied by the Gram.
The complete untruncated correlation is

    R_infinity(v)=exp(-v)(385-639v+162v^2-9v^3)/2048.                (16)

For example R_infinity(1)=-101/(2048 e)<0. Formula (16) must NOT replace
R_(T-log n) in (14) without its exact tail correction. That correction is

    R_infinity(v)-R_L(v)
      = exp(-v) integral_L^infinity exp(-2u)P(u)P(u+v)du.            (17)

Every monomial tail is

    integral_L^infinity u^k exp(-2u)du
      =exp(-2L) sum_(j=0)^k k!/(j! 2^(k-j+1)) L^j.                (18)

Equations (12)--(18) define finite entries without numerical quadrature.
They supply exact finite algebra, not the sign or size of the entire signed
cross sum. In particular the Gram is PSD because it is a Gram; that fact
is not an upper energy estimate for its actual Möbius vector.

## 5. A single hypothetical zero forces exponential future cost

Let lambda=rho-1/2 have d0=Re lambda>0 and suppose zeta(rho)=0.
Because y_T belongs to M, its Laplace transform vanishes at lambda. Put

    F_T(lambda)=integral_0^T exp(-lambda t)f(t)dt.

Then the complete future tail must satisfy

    integral_T^infinity exp(-lambda t)y_T(t)dt=-F_T(lambda).

Cauchy--Schwarz, with the exact tail norm of exp(-conj(lambda)t), gives

    ||y_T 1_[T,infinity)||_2^2
         >=2d0 exp(2d0 T)|F_T(lambda)|^2.            (19)

F_T(lambda) tends to F(lambda), which is nonzero by §2. In particular

    liminf_(T->infinity) (log ||y_T||_2)/T >=d0.     (20)

This applies to EVERY sufficiently large T, not merely a favorable
subsequence of phases. It does not assume simplicity or a dominant-zero
asymptotic. All other zeros are harmless to the lower bound because y_T
vanishes at the selected one as well.

For multiplicities and finite packets, see TAIL_AND_DAMPING.md. It gives the
exact Cauchy-jet Gram lower bound, and a sharp asymptotic for the minimum norm
required by each selected finite set of constraints.

## 6. End-to-end consequence and the estimate NOT proved

Each of the following statements is equivalent to RH:

(A) For every epsilon>0, H(T)=O_epsilon(exp(epsilon T)).
(B) For every epsilon>0, ||y_T||_2^2=O_epsilon(exp(epsilon T)).
(C) For every epsilon>0, Ccross(T)<=C_epsilon exp(epsilon T) for all T>=0.

Proof of the implications towards RH:
(C) implies (A) by (14)--(15); (A) implies (B) by ||d||_1<=6.
If (B) holds, (19) rules out any right-of-line zero. Reflection then gives
RH. This direction uses only the entire/meromorphic zeta setup and the exact
source identities, not any estimate for Mertens sums.

For the converse ONLY, import Littlewood's classical implication

    RH => M(x):=sum_(n<=x)mu(n)=O_epsilon(x^(1/2+epsilon)).          (21)

It is not asserted unconditionally. Partial summation in (5), with x=exp t,
uses the weight w_x(u)=x^(-1)sqrt(u)P(log(x/u)). Its endpoint and derivative
obey

    w_x(x)=x^(-1/2),
    |w_x'(u)|<=C x^(-1)u^(-1/2)(1+log(x/u)^3).

Thus, under (21), for any epsilon>0,

    |h(t)|<=C_epsilon exp(epsilon t),

since x^(-1) integral_1^x u^epsilon(1+log(x/u)^3)du=O_epsilon(x^epsilon).
After reducing epsilon, this gives (A), and (14)--(15) give (C).
The standard implication (21), not a new proof of it, is the only conditional
arithmetic input in this reverse direction. See EXTERNAL_INPUTS.md.

In the variable X=exp T, condition (C) is a subpower upper bound for the
fully written signed Möbius correlation (14). It is not a diagonal estimate,
a fixed-height result, or a condition on chosen free coefficients.

I attempted to bound (14) using the null moments (3), full correlation (16),
Cauchy--Schwarz, and the parent's positive Jordan measure. None supplied the
needed signed upper bound. The unconditional bound established in this
packet remains H(T)<=64(exp T-1). It is exponentially too large.

Accordingly the original domain is NOT proved complete here. The construction
removes Gram inversion and the artificial pole from a candidate approximant;
it does not remove the RH-bearing cancellation estimate. These formulas are
in the classical Möbius/Nyman--Beurling approximation lineage, and no external
novelty or priority claim is made.
