\begin{abstract}
PR~\#565 proposed to prove eventual nonnegativity of a scale-four annular scalar by combining a finite $P_{61}$ signed-bias certificate, a factor-67 causal decomposition, exact low-child recombination, and a strict parity contraction.  The downstream scalar has an exact reciprocal-zeta Mellin transform whose finite numerator cannot cancel a nontrivial zeta zero, so the intended Landau consumer is exceptionally compact.

We reconstruct the entire argument at exact frozen heads, with PRs~\#574 and~\#575 imposed as mandatory global-Hall, parity, scalar-lift, and ownership firewalls.  Two independent failures occur before the consumer.  First, the asserted universal inequality
\[
  \frac1{40}\le \frac{F(x)}{M(x)}\le\frac18\qquad(x\ge67)
\]
is false.  A 256-bit MPFR enclosure at $x=184$ gives
\[
  \frac{F(184)}{M(184)}
  =0.023984293558766304673\ldots <\frac1{40}.
\]
Second, the finite $P_{61}$ packet and the complete rough-history source cannot be the same object at the point where the finite bias is used.  If $P_x$ denotes the finite packet, its scalar is $F(x)$ but it omits the native rough histories.  If it denotes the complete source, the advertised currents retain unresolved rough histories and are not controlled by $F/M$; moreover the low-child recombination returns the same-rank full packet rather than a terminal packet.

We replace the compressed tree by the exact ordered all-history recursion, prove first-owner uniqueness, cumulative parity, coefficient preservation, base cases, and finite termination, and run the $X=61841$ odd-history witness through the literal construction.  We also give a complete proof of the single-scalar annular Mellin transform, below-support convention, continuity, growth, Fubini step, nonvanishing scale factor, zero noncancellation, Landau hypotheses, and functional-equation finish.

The strongest surviving statement is fail-closed: eventual positivity of one explicit all-history scalar implies RH, but neither a finite $P_{61}$ bias interval nor the raw $<1/8$ coefficient budget proves that positivity.  The first unsupported arrow and the exact corrected frontier are stated explicitly.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Exact first-owner theorem}
\begin{theorem}[All-history first-owner recursion]\label{thm:all-history}
For every $j$ and $x$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
 \Source_j(x)=\Base_x\oplus
 \bigoplus_{\substack{k\ge j\\p_k\le x/2}}
 p_k^{-1/2}S\Source_{k+1}(x/p_k).
}
\label{eq:all-history-source}
\end{equation}
Every atom has exactly one owner, coefficients and activations are preserved, cumulative parity is correct, and the recursion terminates after finitely many steps.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
An atom has either empty rough history, in which case it belongs to $\Base_x$, or it has a unique least rough prime $p_k$.  Remove that prime.  The remaining history lies in $\Source_{k+1}(x/p_k)$.  Conversely, placing $p_k$ reconstructs the unique original history.

Coefficient preservation is exact:
\[
 p_k^{-1/2}
 \frac{q_\star(m)H_{x/p_k}(n)}{\sqrt n}
 =
 \frac{q_\star(m)H_x(p_kn)}{\sqrt{p_kn}}.
\]
The channel swap contributes the missing factor $-1$ in the signed coefficient.  The owner sets are disjoint because the least rough prime is unique.  Finite termination follows from the product bound $2\cdot67^r\le x$.
\end{proof}

\begin{align}
 R_j(x)&=F(x)-

 p_k^{-1/2}R_{k+1}(x/p_k),\label{eq:Rrec}\\

\section{Complete single-scalar annular consumer}

\subsection{Support and continuity}
The convention $R_X=0$ for $0<X<1$ makes $R_{X/4}$ unambiguous for every $X>0$.  At $X=n$, an entering term has logarithm zero.  At $X=4n$, the entering term of $R_{X/4}$ also has logarithm zero. \subsection{Growth and absolute Fubini}
Since $|a_\star(n)|\le24$,
\[
 |R_X|
 \le24\sum_{n\le X}n^{-1/2}\log(X/n)
 =O(\sqrt X),
\]
and the same is true of $\Ann_X$.  Thus the Mellin integral has finite abscissa and converges absolutely for $\Ree s>1/2$.

For $n\ge1$ and $\Ree s>0$,
\[
 \int_n^\infty\log(X/n)X^{-s-1}\,dX=\frac{n^{-s}}{s^2}.
\]
Absolute convergence for $\Ree s>1/2$ permits termwise integration.

\subsection{Dirichlet numerator and annular factor}
Put $z=s+1/2$.  Direct summation gives
\begin{align}
 \sum_{n\ge1}\frac{a_\star(n)}{n^z}
 &=6-\frac{6-9\,2^{-z}+3\,4^{-z}}{\zeta(z)}\\
 &=6-\frac{3(1-2^{-z})(2-2^{-z})}{\zeta(z)}.
\end{align}
Therefore
\[
 \int_1^\infty R_XX^{-s-1}\,dX
 =\frac6{s^2}
 -\frac{3(1-2^{-z})(2-2^{-z})}{s^2\zeta(z)}.
\]
The below-support convention permits the substitution $X=4Y$ without a boundary term:
\[
 \int_1^\infty R_{X/4}X^{-s-1}\,dX
 =4^{-s}\int_1^\infty R_YY^{-s-1}\,dY.
\]
Hence
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
 \int_1^\infty \Ann_XX^{-s-1}\,dX
 =(1-4^{-s})
 \left[
 \frac6{s^2}
 -\frac{3(1-2^{-z})(2-2^{-z})}{s^2\zeta(z)}
 \right].
}
\label{eq:mellin}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Noncancellation}
If $\Ree s>0$, then $|4^{-s}|<1$, so $1-4^{-s}\ne0$.  The finite numerator vanishes only if
\[
 2^{-z}=1\quad\text{or}\quad2^{-z}=2.
\]
These force $\Ree z=0$ and $\Ree z=-1$, respectively.  No nontrivial zero in $0<\Ree z<1$ is cancelled.

\subsection{Landau's theorem and the functional equation}
\begin{theorem}[Direct scalar consumer]\label{thm:consumer}
If $\Ann_X\ge0$ for all sufficiently large $X$, then the Riemann Hypothesis holds.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Removing a compact initial interval changes the Mellin transform by an entire function.  Under $X=e^t$, the remaining transform is a Laplace transform of a nonnegative locally integrable function with finite abscissa of convergence.  Landau's theorem states that a finite abscissa is a singularity on the real axis; see \cite[Ch.~II]{titchmarsh} or \cite{landau1905}.

The right side of \eqref{eq:mellin} has no positive-real singularity.  At $s=1/2$, $1/\zeta(s+1/2)$ vanishes because zeta has a pole at 1; zeta has no positive real zero.  Therefore the abscissa is at most zero, and the transform is analytic throughout $\Ree s>0$.

If $\zeta(\rho)=0$ with $\Ree\rho>1/2$, then $s=\rho-1/2$ lies in $\Ree s>0$.  The noncancellation result forces a pole of \eqref{eq:mellin}, contradiction.  The functional equation maps a zero with real part below $1/2$ to one with real part above $1/2$.  Hence every nontrivial zero lies on the critical line.
\end{proof}

All hypotheses of the consumer are now explicit.  No $J_\Lambda/P_\Lambda/F_\Lambda$ endpoint bridge is used.
