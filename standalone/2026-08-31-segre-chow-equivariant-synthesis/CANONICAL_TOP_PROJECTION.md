## 1. Setting

Let

\[
 K_7=(\ker D_2)_7
\]

in PR #769's marked degree-seven source. Let \(K_{(7,7,7)}\) be its central
torus-weight subspace. The diagonal \(GL_3\)-action differentiates to an
\(\mathfrak{sl}_3\)-action. Write

\[
 E_{12},E_{21},E_{23},E_{32}
\]

for the four simple-root raising and lowering operators, and let \(S_3\)
permute the three Segre factors.

PR #769 proves

\[
 \operatorname{Tor}_{3,7}^{S_W}(R,\mathbf Q)
 =[777]\otimes\mathbf1
\]

with multiplicity one. The first PR #782 packet proves that the old
subspace generated by the degree-five and degree-six top syzygies contains no
copy of \([777]\otimes\mathbf1\).

## 2. Canonical-line theorem

Define

\[
 \boxed{
 L_{\mathrm{can}}=
 K_{(7,7,7)}
 \cap\ker E_{12}\cap\ker E_{21}
 \cap\ker E_{23}\cap\ker E_{32}
 \cap K_7^{S_3}.
 }
\]

### Theorem 2.1

\(L_{\mathrm{can}}\) is one-dimensional. Its image in the quotient

\[
 K_7/(S_{>0}K)_7
 =\operatorname{Tor}_{3,7}^{S_W}(R,\mathbf Q)
\]

is an isomorphism onto \([777]\otimes\mathbf1\).

### Proof

A polynomial \(GL_3\)-representation of central weight \((7,7,7)\) is a copy
of \(\det^7=[777]\) exactly when its \(SL_3\)-part is trivial. In a
semisimple rational representation, the trivial \(SL_3\)-subrepresentation is
precisely the simultaneous kernel of the simple positive and negative root
operators. Intersecting with the factor-permutation invariants therefore
extracts the \([777]\otimes\mathbf1\)-isotypic subspace.

The complete Tor table says that the quotient contains this representation
once. The old-space representation calculation says that the old subspace
contains it zero times. Semisimplicity then implies that the corresponding
subspace of \(K_7\) is one-dimensional and maps isomorphically to the quotient
line. ∎

This is stronger than saying merely that a chosen kernel vector has nonzero
quotient class: it characterizes the canonical representative line by
intrinsic equivariance equations.

## 3. Primitive integral normalization

The marked source has a distinguished integral coordinate lattice
\(K_{7,\mathbf Z}\). Define the saturated canonical lattice

\[
 L_{\mathrm{can},\mathbf Z}
 =L_{\mathrm{can}}\cap K_{7,\mathbf Z}.
\]

It has rank one. Hence it has a unique primitive generator up to sign.
Order the frozen source descriptors lexicographically and require the first
nonzero coefficient to be positive. This defines a literal vector

\[
 \boxed{w_{\mathrm{can}}}
\]

without a remaining scalar ambiguity.

This normalization is source-bound: changing the marked integral lattice may
change the primitive representative, while the rational equivariant line and
its quotient class remain canonical.

## 4. Relation to the published 379-term witness

Let \(w_{379}\) be the accepted primitive marked witness. PR #769 proves

\[
 [w_{379}]\ne0
 \quad\text{in}\quad
 \operatorname{Tor}_{3,7}^{S_W}(R,\mathbf Q).
\]

Therefore there are unique

\[
 \alpha\in\mathbf Q^\times,
 \qquad
 w_{\mathrm{old}}\in(S_{>0}K)_7
\]

such that

\[
 \boxed{
 w_{379}=\alpha w_{\mathrm{can}}+w_{\mathrm{old}}.
 }
\]

The first packet's Hessian-duality theorem identifies the induced map of the
canonical line with the dual transpose of the ternary-cubic Hessian component.
Thus \(w_{\mathrm{can}}\), not an arbitrary marked lift, is the literal
Hessian-dual/canonical-module tensor.

It remains possible that \(w_{379}=\pm w_{\mathrm{can}}\); it is also possible
that a nonzero old correction is present. Neither conclusion follows from
its 379-term support, primitivity, central weight or nonmembership certificate.

## 5. Exact comparison contract

A complete executable comparison needs the following frozen inputs in the
original degree-seven coordinates:

1. the accepted matrix \(D_2\);
2. the complete 49-column central old space;
3. the selected 379-term vector;
4. matrices for \(E_{12},E_{21},E_{23},E_{32}\);
5. matrices for two generators of the factor-permutation \(S_3\).

Then:

1. solve the simultaneous exact system defining \(L_{\mathrm{can}}\);
2. certify its rational nullity is one;
3. saturate the resulting rational line in the integral coordinate lattice;
4. apply the descriptor-order sign convention;
5. solve
   \(w_{379}=\alpha w_{\mathrm{can}}+M_{\mathrm{old}}c\) exactly;
6. report \(\alpha\), the old coordinates \(c\), support comparison, and all
   integer residuals.

Acceptance must use all original rows and exact integer/rational arithmetic.
A modular kernel may propose the vector but may not be the final equality
certificate.

## 6. Current boundary

The accepted artifact contains the top vector, original residuals, complete
old columns and the old-space coordinate obstruction. It does not currently
expose accepted matrices for the marked \(\mathfrak{sl}_3\) and factor-action
operators required by the simultaneous-equivariance projection. Those maps
can be generated from the source descriptors, but that generation needs its
own frozen contract and review.

Accordingly this note proves the canonical line and specifies a deterministic
normalization/comparison. It does not silently relabel the published
379-term vector as the already projected canonical representative.
