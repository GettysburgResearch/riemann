# 4. New Theorem III: the canonical top syzygy and its Hessian-dual identification

## Theorem III

In the rank-three, power-three Chow-base resolution of PR #769:

1. The top Tor module
   \[
   B_{3,7}=[777]\otimes\mathbf1
   \]
   has a unique \(GL_3\times S_3\)-equivariant lift inside the degree-seven kernel of \(D_2\), up to scalar.

2. Under the \(S_3\)-invariant projection, the resolution is the classical resolution of the normalization of the ternary-cubic Chow variety. In this invariant subcomplex, the top differential
   \[
   S(-7)\otimes[777]
   \longrightarrow
   S(-4)\otimes[552]
   \]
   is, up to scalar, the Gorenstein dual transpose of the cubic Hessian component
   \[
   S(-3)\otimes[522]\longrightarrow S
   \]
   of the first differential.

3. Consequently the canonical top map is a **Hessian-dual/canonical-module map**. It is not literally a scalar discriminant, Chow form, Aronhold invariant, or hyperdeterminant.

4. The published 379-term integral relation is a nonzero marked lift of the canonical quotient class. Without an explicit equivariant projection or coefficient comparison, the literal 379-term vector is not proved to be the canonical equivariant representative. It may differ from that representative by an old degree-seven relation.

After projection to the unique equivariant line, a primitive integral normalization is unique up to sign; fixing one coefficient sign removes the remaining ambiguity.

## Proof

Let \(K_7=(\ker D_2)_7\). The old degree-seven subspace is generated by lower-degree third-syzygy generators, hence is a quotient of

\[
\operatorname{Sym}^2W\otimes B_{3,5}
\;\oplus\;
W\otimes B_{3,6}.
\]

Here

\[
W=[300]\otimes\mathbf1,
\]

\[
\operatorname{Sym}^2W=([600]\oplus[420])\otimes\mathbf1,
\]

\[
B_{3,5}=[555]\otimes\mathbf1+[744]\otimes\varepsilon,
\]

and

\[
B_{3,6}=[765]\otimes\sigma+[666]\otimes\varepsilon.
\]

The \(S_3\)-types immediately exclude every summand except the possible product
\(\operatorname{Sym}^2W\otimes[555]\otimes\mathbf1\). But

\[
[777]=[555]\otimes[222],
\]

and \([222]\) does not occur in
\(\operatorname{Sym}^2([300])=[600]\oplus[420]\). Therefore the old subspace contains no copy of
\([777]\otimes\mathbf1\).

The quotient

\[
K_7/(\text{old degree-seven subspace})
\]

is \(B_{3,7}=[777]\otimes\mathbf1\), with multiplicity one. Since
\(GL_3\times S_3\) is reductive in characteristic zero, \(K_7\) contains one and only one copy of this irreducible representation, and the quotient map restricts to an isomorphism on it. This proves the unique equivariant lift.

Now take \(S_3\)-invariants. Exactness of invariants in characteristic zero gives the invariant subresolution

\[
0\to
([555]S(-5)\oplus[777]S(-7))
\to
[552]S(-4)
\to
[522]S(-3)
\to
(S\oplus[222]S(-2))
\to
R^{S_3}\to0.
\]

This is exactly the classical ternary-cubic Chow-normalization resolution described by [Raicu–Sam–Weyman](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.10910). Its middle map is skew-self-dual; the first map has a linear coefficient-vector component and a cubic component given by the coefficients of the Hessian of the generic ternary cubic. Gorenstein duality identifies the third map with the transpose of the first. The summand dual to the scalar target \(S\) is the top
\([777]S(-7)\) summand, while the summand dual to \([522]S(-3)\) is \([552]S(-4)\). Therefore the top differential is the dual transpose of the Hessian component.

A scalar invariant is a map to a one-dimensional representation. The top differential instead has the nontrivial ten-dimensional target \([552]\) and cubic polynomial entries. Hence it is not literally a scalar discriminant, Chow form, Aronhold invariant, or hyperdeterminant. The Aronhold invariant enters a different part of the classical description: the square relation for the extra degree-two normalization generator.

Finally, any marked witness outside the old subspace has nonzero projection to the unique top quotient, but may carry an old component. Thus the 379-term witness is canonical modulo old relations, not necessarily as a literal vector. Equivariant projection removes the old component. The resulting rational line meets the marked integral lattice in a rank-one lattice, whose primitive generator is unique up to sign.

\(\square\)
