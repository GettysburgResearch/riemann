## 1. Definitions

Work in the stable symmetric-function ring

\[
 \Lambda_{\mathbf Q}=\mathbf Q[e_1,e_2,\ldots]
\]

on an alphabet \(X\). Put

\[
 H_X(t)=\sum_{r\ge0}h_r(X)t^r.
\]

Define the stable square-defect numerator by

\[
 N_{2,X}(u)
 =D_{\operatorname{Sym}^2X}(u)
  \sum_{r\ge0}h_r(X)^2u^r
 =\sum_{j\ge0}N_j(X)u^j,
\]

where

\[
 D_{\operatorname{Sym}^2X}(u)
 =\det(1-u\operatorname{Sym}^2X).
\]

Let

\[
 D_{\wedge^2X}(u)
 =\det(1-u\wedge^2X)
 =\sum_{q\ge0}(-1)^qe_q[\wedge^2X]u^q.
\]

The Gauss-sign term in layer \(j\) is

\[
 G_j(X)=(-1)^{j(j-1)/2}e_j[\wedge^2X],
\]

and the stable correction is

\[
 \operatorname{corr}_j=N_j-G_j.
\]

It is homogeneous of weight \(2j\).

For \(0\le k<j\), let \(H_{j,k}\) be defined by the unique decomposition

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{corr}_j
 =\sum_{k=0}^{j-1}e_{2j-k}H_{j,k}
  +R_j,
 }
\]

where \(H_{j,k}\) has weight \(k\), and every elementary factor occurring in
\(R_j\) has index at most \(j\). Uniqueness follows from the elementary
monomial basis: a weight-\(2j\) monomial cannot contain two parts strictly
larger than \(j\).

## 2. Master head theorem

Set

\[
 s_j=(-1)^{j(j+1)/2}.
\]

### Theorem 2.1

For every \(j\ge1\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{k=0}^{j-1}H_{j,k}t^k
 \equiv
 H_X(-t)\left(
 N_{2,X}(t^2)+s_jD_{\wedge^2X}(t^2)
 \right)\pmod{t^j}.
 }
\]

Thus the complete large-part region of every stable layer is generated by
shallower square-defect coefficients and the exterior-square determinant.

## 3. Proof

Fix \(k<j\) and set

\[
 D=2j-k>j.
\]

To extract the coefficient of \(e_D\), retain arbitrary shallow elementary
variables of total weight at most \(k\), set every other deep elementary
variable to zero, and introduce a first-order parameter

\[
 e_D=u,\qquad u^2=0.
\]

Write \(S\) for the shallow alphabet. The elementary generating series is

\[
 E_X(z)=E_S(z)+uz^D.
\]

Since

\[
 E_X(z)=\exp\left(
 \sum_{n\ge1}(-1)^{n-1}p_n(X)\frac{z^n}{n}
 \right),
\]

linearization gives

\[
 \delta\log E_X(z)
 =\frac{uz^D}{E_S(z)}
 =uz^DH_S(-z).
\]

Comparing coefficients yields, for \(q\ge0\),

\[
 \boxed{
 \delta p_{D+q}(X)
 =(-1)^{D-1}(D+q)u h_q(S).
 }
\]

The plethystic power sums are

\[
 p_n[\operatorname{Sym}^2X]
 =\frac{p_n(X)^2+p_{2n}(X)}2,
 \qquad
 p_n[\wedge^2X]
 =\frac{p_n(X)^2-p_{2n}(X)}2.
\]

Because \(D>j\), the variation of \(p_n(X)^2\) cannot contribute to a layer
of index at most \(j\). Only \(p_{2n}(X)\) contributes. Therefore

\[
 \delta\log D_{\operatorname{Sym}^2X}(v)
 =(-1)^D u
   \sum_{2n\ge D}h_{2n-D}(S)v^n,
\]

while

\[
 \delta\log D_{\wedge^2X}(v)
 =(-1)^{D+1} u
   \sum_{2n\ge D}h_{2n-D}(S)v^n.
\]

The factor \(\sum h_r(X)^2v^r\) has no first-order variation in coefficient
index at most \(j\), again because \(D>j\). Hence

\[
 [u]N_j(X)
 =(-1)^D
 \sum_{q=0}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}
 N_q(S)h_{k-2q}(S).
\]

Since \((-1)^D=(-1)^k\), this is exactly

\[
 [t^k]\,H_S(-t)N_{2,S}(t^2).
\]

For the Gauss term, note that

\[
 G_j=(-1)^{j(j-1)/2}e_j[\wedge^2X]
 =s_j\,[v^j]D_{\wedge^2X}(v).
\]

The exterior variation therefore contributes

\[
 -s_j[t^k]H_S(-t)D_{\wedge^2S}(t^2)
\]

to \([u]G_j\), and subtracting \(G_j\) from \(N_j\) changes the sign once
more. Thus

\[
 H_{j,k}
 =[t^k]H_S(-t)
 \left(N_{2,S}(t^2)+s_jD_{\wedge^2S}(t^2)\right).
\]

The result is a universal symmetric-function identity, so we may replace the
shallow alphabet \(S\) by \(X\). Doing this simultaneously for every
\(k<j\) proves the theorem. ∎

## 4. First five head coefficients

Using

\[
 N_0=1,
 \qquad N_1=e_2,
 \qquad N_2=-e_2[\wedge^2X],
\]

and

\[
 D_{\wedge^2X}(u)
 =1-e_2u+e_2[\wedge^2X]u^2+\cdots,
\]

we obtain the following.

If \(s_j=+1\), equivalently \(j\equiv0,3\pmod4\), then

\[
 (H_{j,0},\ldots,H_{j,4})
 =(2,-2e_1,2h_2,-2h_3,2h_4)
\]

whenever the indicated index is below \(j\).

If \(s_j=-1\), equivalently \(j\equiv1,2\pmod4\), then

\[
\begin{aligned}
H_{j,0}&=H_{j,1}=0,\\
H_{j,2}&=2e_2,\\
H_{j,3}&=-2e_1e_2,\\
H_{j,4}&=2(e_2h_2-e_2[\wedge^2X])\\
 &=2(e_4-e_3e_1-e_2^2+e_2e_1^2).
\end{aligned}
\]

These are exactly the shifted six-term heads observed at depths 5 and 6.

## 5. Period-four revival and corrected statement

The coefficient of \(e_{2j}\) is

\[
 \boxed{1+s_j}
\]

and the coefficient of \(e_{2j-1}e_1\) is

\[
 \boxed{-(1+s_j)}.
\]

Consequently:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
j\bmod4&\text{linear head}\\ \hline
0,3&2e_{2j}-2e_{2j-1}e_1+\cdots\\
1,2&0.
\end{array}
\]

Depths 3 and 4 lie in the first class. Depths 5 and 6 lie in the second.
Depths 7 and 8 return to the first class. In particular,

\[
 [e_{14}]\operatorname{corr}_7=2,
 \qquad
 [e_{16}]\operatorname{corr}_8=2.
\]

Therefore the finite observations “no \(e_{10},e_9e_1\)” and
“no \(e_{12},e_{11}e_1\)” are correct, but the conclusion that the linear
head has permanently terminated is false. The correct law is period-four
vanishing and revival.
