# Small character-detecting primes and ray-class generators

Status: a complete classical explicit-formula deduction from an imported
uniform zero-free half-plane. This note does not improve the zero-free
boundary, independently certify Liu's analytic proof, or claim a new
method for small-prime bounds. The point is to spell out an arithmetic
consequence, with its field, conductor, ramification and infinite-place
dependence retained.

## 1. The imported hypothesis and the conclusions

Fix 1/2 <= B < 1. Assume the following statement, denoted H_B:

> For every number field K and every primitive finite-order Hecke
> character chi of K, L_K(s,chi) has no zero in Re(s)>B. The principal
> simple pole at s=1 is permitted.

Liu's arXiv:2610.26835v1, Theorem 17.1, supplies this statement with
B=B_flat=0.874886647039964... as an imported theorem. The standard Hecke
functional equation gives the complementary inequality 1-B <= Re(rho)
for every nontrivial zero. In particular all nontrivial zeros lie in
the closed strip [1-B,B]. Zeros on its boundary are allowed.

Write n=[K:Q], D=|disc(K)| and N for absolute ideal norm. Every constant
in the following statements is uniform in K, the modulus, the character
and its order; it may depend on B. No numerical value of this constant
is optimized or computed here.

**Theorem A: detecting a character.** Let chi be a nontrivial primitive
finite-order Hecke character with finite conductor f. Under H_B there is
a prime ideal p, prime to f, such that chi(p) != 1 and

\[
 Np\le C_B\bigl(\log(DNf)+n\bigr)^{1/(1-B)}.           \tag{1}
\]

The prime ideal may additionally be required to have residue degree one
over Q. If chi is given as a character modulo a larger ray modulus m,
one may instead require p to be prime to m_0, replacing Nf by Nm_0
in the bound. Here m_0 is the finite part of m. The infinite part can
include any subset of the real places; its contribution is covered
by the n term.

**Theorem B: generators.** Under H_B, the ray class group Cl_m(K) is
generated by the classes of degree-one prime ideals p prime to m_0 with

\[
 Np\le C_B\bigl(\log(DNm_0)+n\bigr)^{1/(1-B)}.        \tag{2}
\]

This includes ordinary ideal class groups and narrow class groups.

There is a useful stronger version. If H is a nontrivial subgroup of
G=Cl_m(K), put i=[G:H]. Then H is generated by the degree-one prime
ideals p whose classes lie in H and whose norms satisfy

\[
 Np\le C_B\bigl(i\{\log(DNm_0)+n\}\bigr)^{1/(1-B)}. \tag{3}
\]

The full group is the case i=1. Requiring the small generators to lie
in a particular subgroup is genuinely stronger than merely generating
the ambient group.

## 2. The size of the arithmetic improvement

For Liu's boundary the resulting exponent is

\[
 p_{\flat}=\frac1{1-B_{\flat}}
 =7.99275198323094567584\ldots .                       \tag{4}
\]

It is the root near 7.993 of

\[
 116p^4-5691p^3+55674p^2-157356p+133488=0.             \tag{5}
\]

Equation (5) follows by substituting B=1-1/p in Liu's defining quartic
and multiplying by p^4. The decimal in (4) is explanatory; the exact
expression 1/(1-B_flat) is the theorem's exponent.

| Zero-free input B | Exponent 1/(1-B) in (1)-(3) |
|---|---:|
| 31/32 | 32 |
| 7/8 | 8 |
| B_flat | 7.992751983230945... |
| 1/2, if assumed | 2 |

Thus the all-field input improves the corresponding polylogarithmic
exponent from 32 to just below 8. Over Q, this gives a prime p not
dividing q with chi(p) != 1 at p <<_B (log q+1)^(1/(1-B)) for every
nontrivial Dirichlet character modulo q. For a quadratic character this
is a small quadratic-nonresidue prime. This is not the same statement as
finding a small prime in every prescribed residue class.

## 3. A smoothed explicit formula with uniform constants

Choose the fixed nonnegative weight

\[
 f(u)=
 \begin{cases}
 960(u-1/2)^2(1-u)^2,&1/2\le u\le1,\\
 0,&\text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}                                        \tag{6}
\]

It and its first derivative vanish at both endpoints, and

\[
 \int_0^{\infty} f(u)\,du=1,\qquad \|f\|_{\infty}=15/4.
\]

Define the entire Mellin transform

\[
 F(s)=\int_{1/2}^{1}f(u)u^{s-1}\,du.
\]

After u=exp(v), two integrations by parts, with zero endpoint terms,
give

\[
 |F(\sigma+it)|\ll_f(1+|t|)^{-2}
 \quad(-1/2\le\sigma\le2).                          \tag{7}
\]

The piecewise-polynomial second derivative is integrable, which is all
this argument requires. In particular F(1)=1 and F has no pole at zero.
Using an annular weight avoids the double-pole terms at zero that can
arise from a Perron kernel 1/[s(s+1)].

Let chi be primitive, including the principal character, and put

\[
 Q_\chi=DNf_\chi,\qquad A_\chi=\log Q_\chi+n,
 \qquad
 S_\chi(X)=\sum_{\mathfrak a}
 \Lambda_K(\mathfrak a)\chi(\mathfrak a)f(N\mathfrak a/X).
\]

The character is extended by zero at the primes of its finite
conductor. The assertion needed below is

\[
 \boxed{S_\chi(X)=\delta_\chi X+
 O_f\bigl(X^B A_\chi\bigr)\qquad(X\ge2).}            \tag{8}
\]

Here delta_chi is one for the principal character and zero otherwise.
The implied constant in (8) is independent of field, conductor and
character. The proof has two parts.

### 3.1. Uniform weighted zero sum

The standard real-part logarithmic-derivative identity for primitive
Hecke L-functions is

\[
 \sum_{\rho}\Re\frac1{s-\rho}
 =\frac12\log Q_\chi+
 \Re\left\{
 \delta_\chi\left(\frac1s+\frac1{s-1}\right)
 +G_\chi(s)+\frac{L'_\chi}{L_\chi}(s)
 \right\}.                                         \tag{9}
\]

Zeros are counted with multiplicity. G_chi is the logarithmic derivative
of the archimedean gamma factor. For finite-order characters,

\[
 G_\chi(s)=\frac a2\psi(s/2)+\frac b2\psi((s+1)/2)
             -\frac n2\log\pi,
 \quad a,b\ge0,\quad a+b=n.                         \tag{10}
\]

The real-place sign pattern changes a and b, not these uniform bounds.
Equation (9) follows from the completed Hadamard product and the
functional equation. A primary printed source with this normalization
is Wesolowski [W19], Section 2, equations (2-2)-(2-3).

At s=2 the gamma term is O(n). The absolutely convergent Euler series
gives

\[
 \left|\frac{L'_\chi}{L_\chi}(2)\right|
 \le-\frac{\zeta'_K}{\zeta_K}(2)
 \le n\left(-\frac{\zeta'}{\zeta}(2)\right)\ll n.
\]

The middle inequality follows prime by prime from
sum_(p|rational p) e_p f_p=n and Np=p^(f_p); it is uniform in K.
For 0<beta<1,

\[
 \frac1{1+\gamma^2}
 \le2\frac{2-\beta}{(2-\beta)^2+\gamma^2}.
\]

Consequently (9) proves

\[
 \sum_{\rho}\frac1{1+\gamma^2}\ll \log Q_\chi+n.     \tag{11}
\]

This avoids introducing a spurious factor n*log X from an unnecessarily
crude zero-count cutoff. Under H_B, (7) and beta<=B therefore give

\[
 \sum_\rho |X^\rho F(\rho)|
 \ll_f X^B(\log Q_\chi+n).                          \tag{12}
\]

### 3.2. Contour shift and the trivial-zero terms

Mellin inversion and absolute convergence on Re(s)=2 give

\[
 S_\chi(X)=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{(2)}
   -\frac{L'_\chi}{L_\chi}(s)F(s)X^s\,ds.
\]

Shift to Re(s)=-1/2, crossing the principal pole at one, all nontrivial
zeros and possibly a trivial zero at zero. The exact formula is

\[
 S_\chi(X)=\delta_\chi X-
 \sum_\rho X^\rho F(\rho)-r_\chi(0)F(0)+I_\chi(X), \tag{13}
\]

where r_chi(0) is the order of vanishing of the primitive L-function at
zero and 0<=r_chi(0)<=n. There are no other trivial-zero locations between
-1/2 and zero. The standard residue table is [W19], Section 2, Table 1.
It includes the principal case and all real-place sign patterns.

On Re(s)=-1/2 the functional equation reflects L'/L to Re(1-s)=3/2,
where its Euler series is absolutely convergent. Gamma logarithmic
derivatives on this line remain away from their poles. Hence, uniformly,

\[
 \left|\frac{L'_\chi}{L_\chi}(-1/2+it)\right|
 \ll \log Q_\chi+n\log(2+|t|).
\]

Together with (7), this gives

\[
 |I_\chi(X)|\ll_f X^{-1/2}(\log Q_\chi+n).
\]

The horizontal sides vanish along the usual sequence of heights avoiding
zeros; (7) and the standard logarithmic-derivative estimates suffice.
Equivalently one may first use finite-height smoothed explicit formulas
and pass to the absolutely convergent zero sum in (12). No height- or
field-dependent constant is left in the final estimates. Equations
(12)-(13) prove (8).

## 4. Imprimitive characters and missing Euler factors

Let eta now be any character of Cl_m(K), and eta* its primitive inducing
character. Let S_eta^m(X) use coefficients eta(a)=0 whenever
(a,m_0)!=1. The only difference from S_(eta*)(X) is at powers of primes
dividing m_0. Since the weight is bounded,

\[
 |S_\eta^{\mathfrak m}(X)-S_{\eta^*}(X)|
 \le \|f\|_\infty
 \sum_{\mathfrak p\mid\mathfrak m_0}
 \sum_{k\ge1,\ N\mathfrak p^k\le X}\log N\mathfrak p
 \ll \omega(\mathfrak m_0)\log X.
\]

Use omega(m_0)<=log Nm_0/log 2 and log X << X^(1/2)<=X^B for X>=2.
Since f_(eta*) divides m, (8) gives the uniform incomplete formula

\[
 S_\eta^{\mathfrak m}(X)
 =\delta_\eta X+O_f\left(X^B\{\log(DNm_0)+n\}\right).
                                                               \tag{14}
\]

This explicitly pays for ramified and additionally excluded primes;
they are not declared absent without cost. The argument applies equally
to narrow ray characters because their archimedean sign contributions
were retained in (10).

## 5. Character detection and generation

First take H=G. If eta is nontrivial, subtract (14) for eta from its
principal instance. If every prime ideal p prime to m_0 with Np<=X
satisfies eta(p)=1, then every prime-power coefficient in the difference
is zero. Thus

\[
 X\ll X^B\{\log(DNm_0)+n\}.
\]

Choosing X beyond a fixed sufficiently large multiple of
(log(DNm_0)+n)^(1/(1-B)) contradicts this inequality and proves (1)
without the degree-one restriction. The stronger restriction follows
from the argument below.

For a subgroup H of index i and a character chi of G whose restriction
to H is nontrivial, use its annihilator

\[
 H^\perp=\{\eta\in\widehat G:\eta|_H=1\},
 \qquad |H^\perp|=i.
\]

On ideals prime to m_0, character orthogonality gives

\[
 T_{H,\chi}(X):=
 \sum_{\substack{(\mathfrak a,\mathfrak m_0)=1\\
                  [\mathfrak a]\in H}}
 \Lambda_K(\mathfrak a)(1-\chi(\mathfrak a))f(N\mathfrak a/X)
 =\frac1i\sum_{\eta\in H^\perp}
       (S_\eta^{\mathfrak m}(X)-S_{\eta\chi}^{\mathfrak m}(X)).
\]

Exactly one eta on the right is principal. None of eta*chi is principal,
because chi is nontrivial on H. Therefore (14) gives

\[
 T_{H,\chi}(X)=\frac Xi+
 O_f\left(X^B\{\log(DNm_0)+n\}\right).              \tag{15}
\]

Suppose every degree-one prime ideal p prime to m_0, with [p] in H and
Np<=X, satisfies chi(p)=1. Its first-power contribution vanishes.
All remaining contributions in (15) are at prime ideal powers whose
absolute norms are rational prime powers p^j with j>=2. Their absolute
total is bounded by

\[
 2\|f\|_\infty\,n
 \sum_{j\ge2}\sum_{p^j\le X}\log p\ll_f n\sqrt X.  \tag{16}
\]

To see the uniform field factor, group prime ideals above a rational
prime p and use sum f_p<=n. For each ideal with residue degree f_p,
the relevant integers j=f_p k form a subset of all j>=2. The rational
estimate in (16) is elementary: Chebyshev's bound theta(y)<<y gives a
leading sqrt X term; the at most log_2 X terms with j>=3 contribute
O(X^(1/3)log X)=O(sqrt X). A fixed enlargement of the constant covers
the bounded X range. No unproved prime number theorem is needed here.

Since B>=1/2 and n<=log(DNm_0)+n, (15)-(16) imply

\[
 X^{1-B}\ll_f i\{\log(DNm_0)+n\}.
\]

This is impossible for X beyond the bound in (3). Hence some
degree-one prime ideal in H at that bound detects chi.

Finally let J be the subgroup of H generated by these small prime
classes. If J were proper, choose a nontrivial character of H/J and
extend it to G. Such extension exists for finite abelian groups, and
its values have finite order. The resulting chi is nontrivial on H and
trivial on all the chosen prime classes, contradicting the detection
statement. Thus J=H. This proves (3), then (2), and also the
degree-one version of Theorem A.

## 6. Attribution and limits

This is the classical small-prime explicit-formula mechanism of
Ankeny/Bach and later ray-class work, applied with the general exponent
B in place of the critical-line exponent. The subgroup character
projection follows the same established strategy as Wesolowski's
Theorems 1.1-1.2 and Section 3. The new input for this packet is Liu's
reported all-field boundary, with its original analytic dependencies.

What is proved here is the implication H_B -> (1)-(3), with an
unspecified field-uniform constant C_B. It neither computes practical
search limits nor gives a prime in every prescribed class at that same
bound. The latter is a different counting statement. No algorithmic
running-time theorem, actual class-group computation, new zero-free
boundary or independent review of Liu's upstream proof is claimed.
