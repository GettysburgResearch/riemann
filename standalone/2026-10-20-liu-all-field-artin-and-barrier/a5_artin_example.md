# An explicit nonmonomial A5 consequence of the all-field Hecke theorem

Status: exact finite character identity and a source-qualified analytic
corollary. The finite identity is independently reconstructed below and by
`verify_a5_artin.py`. The analytic corollary imports Liu's all-number-field
Hecke nonvanishing theorem; it does not certify its proof or improve its
numerical boundary. No worldwide novelty claim is made for the character
identity or for Brauer induction.

## 1. The statement

Let E/K be any finite Galois extension with Galois group A5. Identify A5
with the even permutations of five letters. Let H be a point stabilizer,
so H is A4 and [A5:H]=5, and let C be a cyclic subgroup generated by a
five-cycle c, so [A5:C]=12. Write K5=E^H and K12=E^C. Let lambda be either
nontrivial character of H/V4, of order three. Let theta(c)=exp(2*pi*i/5).
We use the same letters for the corresponding finite-order Hecke
characters over K5 and K12, through class field theory and Artin formalism.

There are two nonisomorphic irreducible degree-three representations rho3
and rho3prime of A5, and

\[
 L(s,\rho_3,E/K)
 =\frac{L_{K_{12}}(s,\theta)\,\zeta_K(s)}
 {L_{K_5}(s,\lambda)\,\zeta_{K_5}(s)}.                 \tag{1}
\]

Replacing theta by theta^2 gives rho3prime. The formula is an identity of
meromorphic L-functions with all ramified local factors retained. No
assumption of Artin holomorphy is used in deriving it.

Assume every finite-order Hecke L-function over every number field is
nonzero on Re(s)>B, with only a simple principal pole at one. Then each
degree-three Artin function in (1) is **holomorphic and nonzero throughout
Re(s)>B, including s=1**. In particular Liu's Theorem 17.1 supplies
B=B_flat=0.874886647039964... as an imported theorem.

Neither degree-three representation is monomial. Consequently this is a
concrete example outside the monomial/direct-sum-of-monomials scope that
Liu explicitly lists in Corollary 19.2(2). It is an example of the more
general Brauer-induction consequence, rather than a proof of a smaller
zero-free boundary or full Artin holomorphy.

## 2. Exact character identity

Let 1 denote the trivial character of A5. Set

\[
 A=\operatorname{Ind}_{C}^{A_5}\theta,\qquad
 D=\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{A_5}\lambda,\qquad
 P=\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{A_5}1.
\]

The identity to prove is

\[
 \boxed{\chi_3=A-D-P+1.}                              \tag{2}
\]

Put phi=(1+sqrt(5))/2 and phi'=1-phi. Label the two five-cycle classes
5A and 5B so that c lies in 5A and c^2 in 5B. The following complete
table follows directly from the induction formula:

| Class | 1 | 2A | 3A | 5A | 5B |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Class size | 1 | 15 | 20 | 12 | 12 |
| A=Ind_C theta | 12 | 0 | 0 | phi-1 | -phi |
| D=Ind_H lambda | 5 | 1 | -1 | 0 | 0 |
| P=Ind_H 1 | 5 | 1 | 2 | 0 | 0 |
| A-D-P+1 | 3 | -1 | 0 | phi | phi' |

Here is a derivation that does not presume the A5 character table.
For any subgroup J and character nu of J,

\[
 (\operatorname{Ind}_J^{A_5}\nu)(g)
 =\frac1{|J|}\sum_{x\in A_5,\ x^{-1}gx\in J}\nu(x^{-1}gx).
                                                               \tag{3}
\]

The point stabilizer contains three double transpositions and eight
three-cycles. Lambda is one on V4 and takes the values omega and omega^2
on the two other cosets of V4, each containing four elements. Thus its
sum over the eight three-cycles is 4*(omega+omega^2)=-4. The centralizer
orders in A5 are respectively 4 and 3, so (3) gives D(2A)=1 and
D(3A)=-1. The same count with the trivial character gives P(2A)=1 and
P(3A)=2. Neither induced H-character sees a five-cycle. Their degrees
are both 60/12=5.

The cyclic group C has two nonidentity elements in each five-cycle
class: c,c^-1 in 5A and c^2,c^-2 in 5B. The centralizer has order five,
so A(c)=theta(c)+theta(c)^-1=phi-1 and A(c^2)=-phi. It vanishes on
the classes of orders two and three and has degree 60/5=12. These facts
give the displayed table and (2) as an equality of virtual characters.

The virtual character in the last row has positive degree three and
exact norm one:

\[
 \frac1{60}\{3^2+15(-1)^2+12\phi^2+12(\phi')^2\}
 =\frac{9+15+12\cdot3}{60}=1.
\]

An integral virtual character decomposes into irreducible characters
with integer coefficients. Squared norm one forces one coefficient to
be plus or minus one and all others zero; the positive degree fixes
the sign. Hence this is an actual irreducible character. Theta^2
interchanges the two five-cycle values and gives a distinct degree-three
irreducible. Also P-1 and D have respective degrees four and five and
are irreducible; the independent program verifies all 25 inner products
of the five characters of degrees 1,3,3,4,5. Their squared degrees sum
to 60, so this recovers the complete irreducible character table.

## 3. Why these representations are nonmonomial

If a degree-three representation were induced from a one-dimensional
character of a subgroup J, then [A5:J]=3. This would give a nontrivial
action of A5 on three cosets and hence a nontrivial homomorphism to S3.
But A5 is simple and 60>6, so no such homomorphism exists.

For completeness, simplicity here needs no black-box group data. The
five conjugacy class sizes are 1,15,20,12,12. A normal subgroup is a
union of classes containing the identity, and its order must divide
60. Checking the sixteen subsets of the four nonidentity classes
leaves only the orders 1 and 60. The exact program performs this check.

## 4. Analytic deduction and the pole at one

Artin L-functions are multiplicative under direct sum, hence under
virtual character identities as meromorphic functions. Inductivity
identifies an induced one-dimensional character with its Hecke
L-function on the fixed field of the subgroup. Applying these identities
to (2) proves (1) initially in the Euler-product half-plane and then
meromorphically. This is a classical use of Artin formalism and class
field theory, with the convention at ramified places kept consistent.

On Re(s)>B away from one, every factor on the right of (1) is holomorphic
and nonzero under the imported all-field theorem. Their quotient is
therefore holomorphic and nonzero. At one, both Hecke characters theta
and lambda are nontrivial, so their L-values are finite and nonzero.
Both zeta functions have simple poles with positive residues. The
singularity cancels, and

\[
 L(1,\rho_3,E/K)=
 \frac{L_{K_{12}}(1,\theta)}{L_{K_5}(1,\lambda)}
 \frac{\operatorname{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_K(s)}
 {\operatorname{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_{K_5}(s)}\ne0.          \tag{4}
\]

The same argument applies to rho3prime. There is no need to choose a
particular polynomial realizing E/K for this theorem: it holds for
every finite Galois extension with the stated Galois group. Field- or
discriminant-uniform numerical constants are not asserted.

## 5. Computation and verification boundary

`verify_a5_artin.py` constructs A5 from all even permutations, constructs
H, V4, C, lambda and theta, and verifies their closure and character
homomorphisms. It evaluates (3) for every g and every conjugating x.
Cyclotomic arithmetic is exact in

\[
 \mathbb Z[z]/(z^8-z^7+z^5-z^4+z^3-z+1),\qquad
 \theta(c)=z^3,\quad\lambda((123))=z^5.
\]

The quotient is the standard cyclotomic integer ring for a primitive
fifteenth root. The script also checks the root order and inverse
relations directly. It uses no floating-point calculations, imported
character table, approximate roots, external package or L-value data.
Required checks use explicit exceptions and remain active under Python
optimization.

Fresh executions in isolated ordinary and optimized Python produced
identical `A5_CERTIFICATE.json` bytes. This certifies the finite
character identities, norms, conjugacy classes, and nonmonomial group
argument. The source-qualified infinite-family analytic deduction is
the written argument of Section 4, not a conclusion of finite enumeration.
