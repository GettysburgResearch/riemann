# CD26: compact causal inversion and the global cost of domain completion

Status: PROPOSED COMPONENT THEOREMS WITH COMPLETE PROOFS; independent review pending.
Date: 2026-09-06. Author: Astra. This is research, not a Reviewer D verdict.
The original domain is NOT proved complete. Theorems below construct admissible
locally exact approximants and isolate their still-unproved global growth bound.
No finite computation supplies an infinite quantifier.

## 1. Source, space and normalization

Use H=L2(0,infinity), with inner product conjugate-linear in the first slot,
and Laplace transform Lf(z)=integral exp(-zt)f(t)dt. Its Hardy norm is
(1/(2pi)) integral_R |Lf(iy)|^2 dy. For b>0 put

    g(t)=1-{exp(t)}+integral_0^t {exp(u)}du
        =floor(exp(t))(1-t)+log(floor(exp(t))!),
    d_b(t)=exp(-bt)g(t),
    D_b(z)=(z+b-1)zeta(z+b)/(z+b)^2.
                                                        (1)

The source is that of CSM26 at the exact predecessor listed in SOURCES.json.
For completeness, integration of the floor function gives (1) first for
Re(z+b)>1 and then for Re z>0 by analytic continuation; the zeta pole at
z+b=1 is removable. Also 0<g(t)<=1+t, so

    ||d_b||_1 <= K_b:=1/b+1/b^2,     d_b in L1 intersect L2.       (2)

Let M_b be the closed Hardy invariant subspace generated by D_b, transported
to H. This is the same closed space as the predecessor's all-pass dictionary.
Equivalently its Hardy image is closure(D_b H2). The standard inner-outer
factorization identifies it with B_b H2; the predecessor excludes singular
inner factors. B_b's zeros are exactly rho-b for zeta zeros Re rho>b, with
multiplicity. The explicit constructions below need no zero as input.

Our target is

    h_b(t)=t exp(-bt),      H_b(z)=1/(z+b)^2,
    ||h_b||_2^2=1/(4b^3).                                      (3)

H_b is outer. At b=1/2 its squared norm is exactly 2. It is not the predecessor's
unit exponential target; all finite error ratios below use (3).

## 2. Compact inverse: no delta derivatives and no unremoved unstable residue

Write m(x)=sum_(n<=x) mu(n)/n. For an integer N>=1 put X=N+1, T=log X, and

    v_(b,N)(t)=exp((1-b)t)m(exp(t)) 1_[0,T)(t),
    f_(b,N)=d_b*v_(b,N).                                       (4)

These are genuine functions. v has compact support and belongs to L1 intersect
L2. Thus f belongs to H, with ||f||_2<=K_b ||v||_2, and f belongs to M_b since
its Hardy transform is D_b Lv and Lv is in H2.

Define the finite rational coefficients

    c_n=mu(n) (1<=n<=N),       c_X=-X m(N),
    P_N(w)=sum_(n=1)^X c_n n^(-w).                             (5)

In particular P_N(1)=0 EXACTLY. Integrating the steps of m gives

    Lv_(b,N)(z)=P_N(z+b)/(z+b-1),
    Lf_(b,N)(z)=zeta(z+b)P_N(z+b)/(z+b)^2.                      (6)

The apparent pole in the first formula is removable, not ignored. The second
formula is likewise analytic on Re z>0. Its derivation starts in an absolute
convergence half-plane. The boundary coefficient c_X is the exact consequence
of cutting off the input; it is not a freely chosen main-term subtraction.

**CD26.T1 (local exact reproduction).** For every b>0 and N>=1,

    f_(b,N)(t)=h_b(t),                 0<=t<=log(N+1).           (7)

To prove this and obtain a literal arithmetic output, set

    a_N(k)=sum_(n|k, n<=X) c_n,
    A_N(x)=sum_(k<=x) a_N(k) log(x/k),    x>=1.

Dirichlet multiplication in (6), followed by Laplace uniqueness, gives

    f_(b,N)(t)=exp(-bt) A_N(exp(t)).                            (8)

Every sum is finite at a finite t. Ordinary Mobius inversion gives a_N(1)=1
and a_N(k)=0 for 2<=k<=N. The k=X term has log(X/X)=0 at activation.
This proves (7), including its endpoint. The convolution and (8) are continuous.

The input norm has the exact formula

    ||v_(b,N)||_2^2=integral_1^X x^(1-2b)m(x)^2 dx.              (9)

In particular

    ||v_(1/2,N)||_2^2=sum_(k=1)^N m(k)^2=:S_N.                 (10)

There is no approximate inverse, Gram inversion or conditioning assumption
in (4)-(10). The cost has moved to a stated global output norm, not vanished.

### A complete safe-range check and the critical endpoint distinction

For b>1 the elementary |m(x)|<=1 (proved in Section 5) puts the uncut
v_infinity=exp((1-b)t)m(exp(t)) in H. Laplace uniqueness then gives
 d_b*v_infinity=h_b. Therefore

    ||f_(b,N)-h_b||_2 <= K_b (N+1)^(1-b)/sqrt(2b-2).          (10a)

This is unconditional completion in the Euler-safe range, with an actual
convergent construction rather than only density inferred from a zero theorem.
It does not reach b=1/2.

Conditional on RH, Littlewood's bound used in Section 4 gives
|m(x)|<=C_epsilon x^(-1/2+epsilon). For every b>1/2 and
0<epsilon<b-1/2, the same argument proves

    ||f_(b,N)-h_b||_2 <=
       K_b C_epsilon (N+1)^(1/2-b+epsilon)/sqrt(2b-1-2epsilon).
                                                               (10b)

Thus the proposed approximants really do fill each positive-shift domain
under the classical RH input. Neither (10b) nor its constants are available
unconditionally in the critical range. The b=1/2 case cannot be obtained by
setting b=1/2 in this expression: its denominator condition fails, and the
uncut input is genuinely not in H, by Section 5.

## 3. Quantitative cost of a missing domain direction

Let rho=beta+i gamma be ANY nontrivial zero with beta>b; write lambda=rho-b
and alpha=Re lambda>0. From (6), Lf(lambda)=0. Let e=f-h_b. It is supported
in [T,infinity), belongs to H, and

    Le(lambda)=-1/rho^2.

Cauchy--Schwarz on that support gives

    |1/rho^2| <= ||e||_2 [exp(-2alpha T)/(2alpha)]^(1/2).

**CD26.T2 (remote tail lower bound).** Consequently

    ||f_(b,N)-h_b||_2^2 >=
         2(beta-b)(N+1)^(2(beta-b))/|rho|^4.                    (11)

The same theorem holds for ANY f in M_b which agrees with h_b on [0,T]; it is
not special to the proposed inverse coefficients. No phase can cancel this
single evaluation. Formula (11) is a lower bound under a hypothetical zero,
not an assertion that an off-critical zero exists.

### Full multiplicity strengthening

If rho has multiplicity m, (6) makes Lf vanish through derivative m-1.
Let e_T(u)=e(T+u). For 0<=j<m, set

    R_j(T)=sum_(k=0)^j binom(j,k)(2alpha)^k/k!
             *sum_(l=0)^k binom(k,l)T^(k-l)
                    (-1)^l(l+1)!/rho^(l+2).                   (12)

Then

    ||e||_2^2 >= 2alpha exp(2alpha T)
                         sum_(j=0)^(m-1) |R_j(T)|^2.          (13)

Indeed psi_j(u)=sqrt(2alpha) exp(-conj(lambda)u)L_j(2alpha u) are orthonormal.
This is the ordinary Laguerre orthogonality, obtained from its Rodrigues
formula by j integrations by parts. The derivatives of Le_T at lambda are
those of -exp(Tz)/(z+b)^2 through order m-1. Expanding L_j and using
integral u^k exp(-lambda u)e_T(u)du=(-1)^k (Le_T)^(k)(lambda)
gives <psi_j,e_T>=-sqrt(2alpha)exp(Tlambda)R_j(T). Bessel's inequality proves
(13). In particular

    liminf_(T->infinity) exp(-2alpha T)T^(-2m+2)||e||_2^2
       >= (2alpha)^(2m-1)/[((m-1)!)^2 |rho|^4]                 (14)

for any such family. This is a lower bound, not an equality for the full norm.

## 4. A precise sufficient completion theorem: only subpower output is needed

At b=1/2 define

    G_N=max(1, ||f_(1/2,N)||_2).

**CD26.T3 (growth criterion).** The following are equivalent:

(a) RH;
(b) for every epsilon>0, G_N=O_epsilon(N^epsilon);
(c) liminf_(N->infinity) log(G_N)/log(N+1)=0.

The direction (b)->(c) is immediate. If (c) held with a zero beta>1/2,
(11) and ||f||>=||f-h||-||h|| would give

    liminf log(G_N)/log(N+1) >= beta-1/2>0,

a contradiction. Reflection excludes left-of-line zeros too. This proves
(c)->(a), without a finite zero prefix, zero simplicity or a zero-density
estimate. Even one prescribed unbounded subsequence with subpower norm suffices.

For (a)->(b), import the classical Littlewood implication
RH -> M(x)=sum_(n<=x)mu(n)=O_epsilon(x^(1/2+epsilon)). Partial summation and
sum mu(n)/n=0 then give m(x)=O_epsilon(x^(-1/2+epsilon)). Thus (9)-(10) yield
||v_(1/2,N)||=O_epsilon(N^epsilon), and Young's inequality with K_(1/2)=6 proves
(b). This classical converse is credited, not an unconditional estimate.

A bounded output subsequence would also suffice by a different argument:
weak compactness in H, the exact agreement (7), and weak closedness of M_b
would put h_b in M_b. Its outerness then makes M_b=H. Boundedness is stronger
than needed for the zero-exclusion argument.

**Neither (b) nor (c) is proved here.** This is a source-explicit
Nyman--Beurling-type criterion, not a new proof of RH or a claim of reduced
arithmetic difficulty. The actual map at each positive a becomes globally
isometric if RH is obtained, by the predecessor's domain theorem.

## 5. The compact inverse input is necessarily unbounded at the critical line

**CD26.T4.** S_N in (10) tends to infinity, unconditionally.

Otherwise the increasing sequence S_N would be bounded, and
v_infinity(t)=exp(t/2)m(exp(t)) would lie in H. In Re z>1/2 its transform is

    Lv_infinity(z)=1/[(z-1/2)zeta(z+1/2)].                      (15)

This follows from absolute integration of the step source. Analytic uniqueness
would identify its H2 transform with the meromorphic continuation of (15).
Take a critical-line zero rho=1/2+i gamma, whose existence is a classical
unconditional input. If its order is m>=1, (15) grows like a nonzero constant
times delta^(-m) at z=delta+i gamma as delta decreases to zero. H2 evaluation
instead bounds it by ||v_infinity||/sqrt(2delta). Contradiction. Possible
interior poles would already contradict H2 and are not assumed absent.

Thus a proof relying on a uniformly bounded INPUT norm for this exact critical
inverse family cannot work. This does NOT refute bounded or subpower OUTPUT
norms; convolution is not boundedly invertible at boundary zeros.

An exact finite identity shows the unresolved signed term:

    S_N=(N+1)m(N)^2 -2sum_(n<=N)mu(n)m(n)+sum_(n<=N)mu(n)^2/n.  (16)

It follows by telescoping n[m(n)^2-m(n-1)^2]. The last term is at most
1+log N. Dropping the middle signed term or the first endpoint term is invalid.
The elementary identity sum mu(n)floor(N/n)=1 implies |m(N)|<=1, hence only
S_N<=N and ||f_(1/2,N)||<=6sqrt(N) by this route. That bound does not prove (b).

## 6. An all-real tail formula for each finite candidate

For y>=1 put

    S(y)=sum_(k<=y)log(y/k)=floor(y)log y-log(floor(y)!).

Stirling normalization and one periodic-Bernoulli integration give

    S(y)=y-(log y)/2-(log(2pi))/2+epsilon(y),
    |epsilon(y)|<=1/(6y).                                     (17)

Here is the complete remainder argument. Let B_1({u})={u}-1/2 and
B_2(v)=v^2-v+1/6. The derivative of the remainder away from integers is
-B_1({y})/y; S and B_2 are continuous across the integer knots. Stirling's
formula makes epsilon(y)->0. Therefore

    epsilon(y)=integral_y^infinity B_1({u})du/u
       =-B_2({y})/(2y)+(1/2)integral_y^infinity B_2({u})du/u^2.

Since |B_2|<=1/6, this proves (17). The first conditional integral is defined
by this convergent integration-by-parts formula. No omitted asymptotic tail
is treated as zero.

Let C=sum c_n, L=sum c_n log n, W=sum |c_n|n. For x>=X,

    A_N(x)=sum_(n<=X)c_n S(x/n)
          =-(C/2)log x+(L-C log(2pi))/2+r_N(x),
    |r_N(x)|<=W/(6x).                                        (18)

The term proportional to x cancels EXACTLY by P_N(1)=0. Define

    A=-1-C/2,   B=(L-C log(2pi))/2.

For Y>=X set Z=A log Y+B. The squared norm of the main error tail is

    I_b(Y)=Y^(-2b)[Z^2/(2b)+2AZ/(2b)^2+2A^2/(2b)^3],

and the squared norm of its remainder is at most

    J_b(Y)=W^2 Y^(-2b-2)/[36(2b+2)].                           (19)

Thus the FULL tail squared error is between

    [max(0,sqrt(I_b)-sqrt(J_b))]^2
    and [sqrt(I_b)+sqrt(J_b)]^2.                              (20)

The source coefficients are finite, but the tail bounded by (20) is infinite.

On x in [k,k+1], write A_N(x)-log x=a log x+d, where

    a=sum_(j<=k)a_N(j)-1,    d=-sum_(j<=k)a_N(j)log j.

At b=1/2 the exact integral is obtained from

    integral (a log x+d)^2 dx/x^2
      =-[(a log x+d)^2+2a(a log x+d)+2a^2]/x.                  (21)

Equations (17)-(21), with directed logarithms, are the entire analytic
contract used by certificate.py. No zero, zeta or gamma evaluation enters it.

## 7. Finite achieved results and the remaining gap

The prescribed eight candidates N=1,2,4,8,16,32,64,128 were certified at b=1/2.
Their normalized squared error means ||f-h||^2/2, not an optimized distance
and not the earlier exponential-target epsilon_N. Every nonzero integer cell
through Y=32768 and the complete infinite tail are included.

In particular the N=64 candidate gives

    ||f_(1/2,64)-h_(1/2)||^2/2 < 1/19000.                     (22)

The same enclosure proves strict increases from N=16 to 32 and from 64 to128.
Neither monotonicity nor convergence is inferred. These finite approximants
are new explicitly computed evidence in the stated source; no external
novelty claim is made for the classical combinatorial/Hardy mechanism.

The global subpower output bound (b), even only along one unbounded sequence,
remains unproved. Local exactness, admissibility, and a complete tail formula
are supplied; domain completion is not.
