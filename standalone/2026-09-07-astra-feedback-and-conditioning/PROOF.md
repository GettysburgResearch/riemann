# FC26: a fixed-feedback obstruction and a source-specific finite-section bound

Status: proposed component theorems with complete arguments; independent review required.
**This is NOT a proof of RH.** The growing-horizon, varying-controller upper bound
in Section 8 is unproved. No independent-review verdict is claimed.

Frozen predecessor: PR #812 at cc5277c34fdbc48787cf650b4627a44a77862f1d.
The predecessor's fixed-horizon error certificate and compact-input realization
are preserved. This note tests an additional proposed composition of them.
All transforms are causal Laplace transforms; inner products are conjugate-linear
in the first slot. H2 norms on a vertical boundary use dt/(2*pi).

## 0. Literal source and the attempted complete proof

Use the same source and target as FR26:

    d(t)=exp(-t/2)[floor(exp t)(1-t)+log(floor(exp t)!)],
    D(z)=(z-1/2)zeta(z+1/2)/(z+1/2)^2,
    h(t)=t exp(-t/2),  H(z)=1/(z+1/2)^2.

Here d is in L1 intersect L2, ||d||_1<=6, and ||d||_2^2<=5.
The apparent product at z=1/2 has its analytic removable value. The closed
causal source space M is the Hardy space B H2 generated by D. Its Blaschke
zeros are rho-1/2 for zeta zeros Re rho>1/2, with multiplicities.

For clarity, the absence of a singular inner factor is not assumed from RH.
The Cayley image A(w)=w*zeta(1/(1-w)) is analytic across every boundary point
except w=1. An inner singular measure can therefore only be supported at 1;
ordinary finite-order boundary zeros contribute no singular atom. On the real
radius A(r)->1. A nonzero singular mass at 1 would decay as exp(-c/(1-r)),
which cannot be offset by the H2 outer factor's pointwise bound
||O||/sqrt(1-r^2). Thus that mass is zero. This is the classical inner/outer
factorization argument, also given in the source-domain predecessors.

The attractive proposed iteration was: obtain one source output f close to h,
matching h before T>0; define the relative residual

    G(z)=F(z)/H(z), R(z)=1-G(z),
    F_m(z)=H(z)(1-R(z)^m).                             (0.1)

If R were a causal Schur multiplier, this would give uniformly bounded source
outputs matching the target before mT, and hence an RH proof. Sections 1-4
show precisely why this premise fails for the actual finite constructions.
Sections 5-7 separately improve finite conditioning. They do not repair that
failure or establish the remaining varying-controller bound.

## 1. FC26.1: what a valid feedback completion would prove

Suppose f is in M, f=h before T, and R in (0.1) is a bounded analytic function
on Re z>0 with ||R||_infinity<=1. Then each F_m belongs to M, since

    F_m=F sum_(j=0)^(m-1)R^j

and each multiplier in this finite sum is H-infinity. Also

    ||F_m||_H2 <=2||H||_H2.                             (1.1)

The causal distribution r with transform R is supported in [T,infinity).
Indeed r=delta_0-(partial_t+1/2)^2 f, and h is the fundamental solution of
(partial_t+1/2)^2. Thus F_m corresponds to h-h*r^(*m), which agrees with h
before mT. Causality/support here can alternatively be obtained from the
Laplace support theorem. No strict bound less than one is needed.

A bounded sequence in the Hilbert space has weakly convergent subsequences.
Every such limit agrees with h on every fixed compact interval, hence equals
h. Since M is a closed linear subspace, it is weakly closed; therefore h is
in M. Its transform H never vanishes in Re z>0, so D has no interior zero.
The functional-equation reflection of nontrivial zeta zeros gives RH.

This is a COMPLETE CONDITIONAL implication. It does not establish its Schur
premise. Strong convergence of R^m on the boundary is not asserted: an inner
R can have modulus one a.e. The weak-compactness argument avoids that error.

## 2. FC26.2: the predecessor's ideal second iterate leaves L2

FR26's ideal correction, BEFORE replacing it by a compact input, is

    f_*=f_0+S_T p(R_0)d,  T=log2,
    f_0=d*(exp(t/2)1_[0,T)(t)),
    R_0(z)=(z-1/2)/(z+1/2),
    p(w)=(4284-1839w-1199w^2-742w^3-371w^4)/10000.

The notation R_0 for the all-pass filter is distinct from the relative
residual R in (0.1). Crucially,

    p(1)=133/10000 !=0.                                (2.1)

Write s=z+1/2. The EXACT relative output is

    G_*(z)=zeta(s)[1-2^(1-s)
             +(s-1)2^(1/2-s)p(1-1/s)].                (2.2)

The second Newton output is F_2=2F_*-F_*^2/H. Its causal distribution has an
atom at t=2log2 of mass

    -p(1)^2 = -17689/100000000.                         (2.3)

Therefore this second iterate is not even locally L2. The predecessor's
small L2 error does NOT authorize iterating its relative residual.

Proof. F_0=zeta(s)(1-2^(1-s))/s^2, while the correction has transform
2^(1/2-s) zeta(s)(s-1)p(1-1/s)/s^2. In its square divided by H the rational
factor (s-1)^2 p(1-1/s)^2/s^2 has feedthrough p(1)^2. The delay is 2log2,
and zeta(s)^2 has the locally finite Dirichlet impulse expansion with first
coefficient one. Hence -F_*^2/H has the atom (2.3). Its other atomic terms
are at 2log2+log n, n>=2. The cross term with F_0 and the F_0-square term
are strictly proper rational factors times locally finite arithmetic impulse
sums, so yield ordinary local functions, not a cancelling atom. Also 2F_*
is an ordinary L2 function. This proves (2.3).

An independent transform check: on s=2+it, |zeta(s)|>=1/zeta(2)>1/2,
and p(1-1/s)->p(1). The last term in (2.2) grows linearly in |t|, while
the first two terms are bounded. Consequently |H(1-R^2)| is bounded below
by a positive constant for every sufficiently large |t| on that line.
It cannot be the Laplace transform of an L2 output.

This does NOT invalidate FR26's ideal fixed-horizon certificate or its
compact realization. Those did not assert that F_2 was admissible.

## 3. FC26.3: compact inputs repair the atom, but not fixed feedback

Consider ANY causal compact input v with the following regularity:

* v is piecewise absolutely continuous on finitely many intervals;
* its ordinary derivative on those intervals is in L1;
* it has finitely many jumps, including those from zero extension;
* v(0+)=1.

This class includes the actual FR26 compact input: its initial inverse
segment, finite all-pass polynomial applied to a box, shifted after log2,
and cut at the specified finite input time. The coefficients and input norms
need not be bounded uniformly in the chosen controller.

Let f=d*v, V=Lv, and G=F/H. Then

    G(z)=(s-1)zeta(s)V(s-1/2), s=z+1/2.                (3.1)

The pole at s=1 is removable. We prove the UNCONDITIONAL assertion

    liminf_(L->infinity) (1/(2L)) integral_(-L)^L
                      |G(it)|^2 dt = +infinity.       (3.2)

The same holds for R(it)=1-G(it). In particular neither G nor R belongs
to H-infinity on the right half-plane. This statement uses no zero table,
RH, prime number theorem, or new estimate for Mobius sums.

### 3.1 The frequency coefficients cannot all be cancelled by finite input jumps

Let the jump times be tau_j>=0, with tau_0=0, J_0=1. Distributional integration
by parts gives, on any fixed vertical line Re z>=0,

    z V(z)=sum_j J_j exp(-tau_j z)
                   + integral exp(-zt)v'_reg(t)dt.    (3.3)

On the line s=2+it the last integral tends to zero by the Riemann-Lebesgue
lemma. Thus (3.1), absolute Euler convergence there, and (3.3) show that G,
up to an o(1) term as |t|->infinity, has the absolutely convergent generalized
Dirichlet expansion

    sum_j sum_(n>=1) J_j exp(tau_j/2) (n exp(tau_j))^(-s).
                                                               (3.4)

There is a finite exceptional set of primes E: include a prime p if, for
some positive jump time, exp(tau_j)=p/b in lowest terms with b<p. For every
prime p outside E, the frequency log p in (3.4) has coefficient exactly 1.
Indeed n exp(tau_j)=p and tau_j>0 force exp(tau_j) to be rational with
reduced numerator p. Only tau_0=0 remains outside those finitely many cases.
No irrationality test on the actual jump times is required by this proof.

### 3.2 Moving a frequency mean to the critical boundary

For each fixed p outside E and every sigma in [1/2,2],

    lim_(L->infinity) (1/(2L)) integral_(-L)^L
       G(sigma-1/2+it) exp(it log p)dt = p^(-sigma).    (3.5)

At sigma=2 this follows termwise from (3.4). To move the line, apply Cauchy's
theorem to G(s-1/2)p^s on the rectangle with real endpoints sigma and 2.
The possible pole at 1 has already been cancelled. The elementary
Euler-Maclaurin representation, truncated at N=ceil(|t|), gives

    |zeta(s)|=O(sqrt(|t|)),
       1/2<=Re s<=2, |Im s|>=2,                       (3.6)

uniformly in this fixed strip. Specifically bound the Dirichlet sum by
2sqrt N and the fractional-part integral by |s|N^(-Re s)/(Re s).
Equation (3.3) is uniformly bounded on this strip after multiplication by z;
therefore G=O(sqrt(|t|)). The horizontal integrals divided by 2L tend to zero.
This proves (3.5), including sigma=1/2. All p here remain fixed during the limit.

### 3.3 Bessel's inequality in the long-interval mean

The functions exp(-it log p), with distinct primes p, have mean inner product
zero in the limit, and mean squared norm one. Expanding a finite squared
residual and using (3.5) yields, for every finite P subset of primes minus E,

    liminf_(L->infinity) (1/(2L)) integral_(-L)^L |G(it)|^2dt
                              >= sum_(p in P) 1/p.    (3.7)

The prime harmonic sum diverges. For completeness, convergence would make
product_(p<=x)(1-1/p)^(-1) bounded, using
-log(1-1/p)<=1/p+2/p^2 for p>=2. That product majorizes sum_(n<=x)1/n,
a contradiction. Removing finitely many primes does not affect divergence.
Let P increase to prove (3.2). Subtracting the constant 1 does not change any
nonzero prime frequency, so the same argument applies to R. End of proof.

The regularity conditions are part of the theorem. It is not a theorem about
all possible L2 inputs, infinitely many accumulating jumps, or varying inputs
as the horizon changes. It does cover every fixed, finite FR26 realization.

## 4. FC26.4: fixed-controller Newton refinement has superexponential cost

Assume additionally that f=d*v in Section 3 agrees with h before T>0. Define
F_m by (0.1), interpreted first as causal distributions. Then f_m agrees with
h before mT. But with +infinity assigned to a non-L2 output,

    liminf_(m->infinity) ||f_m||_2^(1/m) = +infinity.   (4.1)

The same holds for ||f_m-h||_2. Hence this PARTICULAR horizon-extension
mechanism cannot produce subexponential error, even if RH is true.

Proof. The distribution r=delta_0-(partial+1/2)^2f is supported after T.
It is a locally finite signed measure of exponential order in this class,
as is seen either from (3.3) and the Euler impulse series or by local
convolution. Its m-fold convolution has support after mT, proving the horizon.
The transform F_m is holomorphic to the right and has an inverse of exponential
order. This does not assert unweighted L2 membership.

By Section 3, the continuous boundary function R(it) is unbounded. For every
A>1 choose a finite interval I of positive length on which |R(it)|>=2A.
Since H(it) never vanishes, c_I=(integral_I |H(it)|^2dt/(2pi))^(1/2)>0.
If f_m is in L2, Plancherel and |1-R^m|>=|R|^m-1 imply

    ||f_m||_2 >= c_I A^m.

The same estimate, without the subtraction of one, applies to its error.
An infinite norm satisfies these bounds as well. Take m-th roots and then
let A increase. This proves (4.1). It does not assert that every finite m
has infinite norm, and is not a lower bound for the optimally re-chosen
controllers of OC26.

## 5. FC26.5: a stronger source-specific all-rank conditioning theorem

Keep R_0 and phi_j=R_0^j d, and G_K=(<phi_i,phi_j>)_(0<=i,j<=K). The
predecessor proves a root-exponential lower bound for every H2 source with
A(0)=1 and ||A||^2<=5. The LITERAL zeta source has the stronger asymptotic bound

    lambda_min(G_K) >= exp[-C(1+log(K+2))^2]            (5.1)

with an explicit absolute C. No claim of an optimal exponent or useful small
constants is made. An entirely rational version is given below.

### 5.1 A local small-value estimate proved from safe data

Put F(s)=(s-1)zeta(s), with its analytic value F(1)=1. On the closed disk
|s-(2+it)|<=7/4 the fractional-part integral gives

    |F(s)| <= M_t:=52(1+|t|)^2,
    |F(2+it)| >= (1+|t|)/4.                            (5.2)

The second inequality uses |zeta(2+it)|>=1/zeta(2)>1/2. For the first,
F(s)=s-s(s-1)integral_1^infinity {x}x^(-s-1)dx and Re s>=1/4, so
|F(s)|<=(|t|+4)(4|t|+13)<=52(1+|t|)^2.
Let L_t=log[208(1+|t|)]. Jensen's formula bounds the number, WITH MULTIPLICITY,
of F-zeros in the radius 13/8 disk by 14 L_t, since log(14/13)>=1/14.

Divide by their disk Blaschke factors, using outer radius 7/4. The quotient
g has modulus at most M_t on that disk and no zeros in the radius 13/8 disk.
The positive harmonic function log(M_t/|g|) is at most 25 L_t at radius
3/2 by Harnack, because (13/8+3/2)/(13/8-3/2)=25.
At a point at least delta away from every divided zero, each Blaschke factor
has modulus at least 4delta/13. Therefore at s=1/2+it,

    |zeta(s)| >= exp[-L_t(25+14log(13/(4delta)))]/(1+|t|),
                                                               (5.3)

provided its distance to every zero of F is at least delta, 0<delta<1/16.
The zeros were used only to describe an exceptional set; none is computed,
and no zero-free critical region is assumed. Boundary zeros and multiplicities
are included. A zero outside the divided disk does not affect the factor bound.

### 5.2 Removing an explicitly small exceptional set

Let n=K+1, k=ceil(log_2 n) (k=0 for n=1), and set

    T=16n, L=9+k,
    N_*=14(64n+3)(9+k), delta=1/(128n N_*).             (5.4)

There are at most N_* zeros within delta of the segment 1/2+i[-T,T]. To see
this, cover those zeros by radius 13/8 disks centered at 2+ij/2,
|j|<=2T+1. Their number is 4T+3, and every such zero lies in one disk:
its squared distance is at most (25/16)^2+(1/4)^2 < (13/8)^2.
Apply the preceding Jensen bound with log[208(T+2)]<9+k. Counting a zero
in several disks only increases the upper bound.

Under w=(it-1/2)/(it+1/2), normalized circle measure is

    dtheta/(2pi)=dt/[2pi(t^2+1/4)].                    (5.5)

Remove |t|>T, of measure less than 1/T, and the intervals of radius delta
about imaginary parts of the indicated zeros. The density in (5.5) is less
than one, so total removed measure is less than

    1/(16n)+2delta N_* = 5/(64n).                      (5.6)

On the remaining circle, (5.3) holds and |A(w)|=|zeta(1/2+it)|. Using
n<=2^k, 9+k<=2^(k+4), 416*938<2^19 and (8/3)^9>3744 gives

    log[208(1+T)] <9+k,
    log(13/(4delta)) <23+3k,
    log(1+T)<k+5.

Thus |A|>=exp(-E_k) there, with

    E_k=42k^2+726k+3128.                               (5.7)

A polynomial p of degree at most K obeys ||p||_infinity^2<=n||p||_H2^2.
The discarded set therefore holds at most 5/64 of its squared norm. Consequently

    ||Ap||_H2^2 >=(1/2)exp(-2E_k)||p||_H2^2.

Since e<4, the following explicit RATIONAL floor is valid for every K:

    lambda_min(G_K) >= 2^(-J_k),
    J_k=168k^2+2904k+12513.                            (5.8)

This proves (5.1). The constants are deliberately loose. The maximum of this
floor and FR26's older floor can always be used; the new estimate is not
claimed to be better at small ranks. It is asymptotically stronger and uses
actual zeta regularity rather than only a generic H2 norm constraint.

## 6. FC26.6: quasipolynomial source coverage for finite solves

Set d^S=d 1_[0,S]. The remainder d-d^S has norm at most e_S=(S+3)exp(-S/2), and
including all filtered future tails gives the predecessor's elementary bound

    ||G_K-G_K^S|| <=n(2sqrt5 e_S+e_S^2).               (6.1)

With k,J_k as above, the explicit cutoff

    S=4(J_k+k+10)                                    (6.2)

makes e_S<=2^(-J_k)/(12n), hence G_K^S >=2^(-J_k-1)I.
For the elementary integer verification, write x=J_k+k. The inequality
12(4x+43)<=2^(x+20), valid at x=0 and preserved by induction, proves the
claimed budget using e>2 and n<=2^k. This also ensures e_S<=1.

Asymptotically S=O(log^2(K+2)); the largest integer argument in the finite
factorial source is exp(O(log^2(K+2))). Filtered tails are still included.
Neither this cutoff nor (5.8) is a claimed practical resource recommendation;
no high-rank computation using the large constants was performed.

This improves finite solve conditioning and source coverage, not the
horizon-dependent projection error or the original infinite inverse.

## 7. Why even fast convergence to a limiting floor would not finish RH

For the synthetic disk source A_0(w)=1-2w, ||A_0||^2=5 and A_0(0)=1.
Its n-by-n orbit Gram has diagonal 5 and neighboring entries -2, hence is
bounded below by I for every n. Its determinant is

    det G_(n-1)=(4^(n+1)-1)/3.

The exact best squared error for approximating 1 is

    epsilon_(n-1)=3*4^n/(4^(n+1)-1)
      =3/4+3/[4(4^(n+1)-1)].                          (7.1)

This converges exponentially fast to a NONZERO floor despite uniformly
excellent conditioning. It does not contradict any statement about the actual
source. It exhibits the logical gap: convergence to the intrinsic model-space
cost and proof that the cost is zero are different assertions.

## 8. Reviewable end-to-end frontier

A sufficient genuine completion would construct T_j->infinity and individually
chosen compact inputs v_j, with f_j=d*v_j in the ORIGINAL source domain,

    f_j=h on [0,T_j],  ||f_j-h||_2^2 = exp(o(T_j)).     (FC26.OPEN)

A hypothetical rho=1/2+alpha+i gamma, alpha>0, would force, by delayed Hardy
evaluation,

    ||f_j-h||_2^2 >=2alpha exp(2alpha T_j)/|rho|^4.

Indeed F_j(rho-1/2)=0, H(rho-1/2)=1/rho^2, and the error starts at T_j.
Cauchy-Schwarz proves the bound with no simplicity assumption. This contradicts
FC26.OPEN. Reflection gives RH. This implication is complete; FC26.OPEN is not.

The finite conditioning and realization statements now have explicit, improved
bounds. They do not give FC26.OPEN. The attempted fixed-controller proof is
not merely missing a numerical check: Section 4 refutes that particular
composition even for the actual compact source class. A successful future
construction must vary its controller or use a different mechanism. The
explicit relative Schur criterion in Section 1 is sufficient but is impossible
in the finite-input class of Section 3, so it is not proposed there as an
unperformed finite test.

No proof of RH, the original uniform block gain, or a near-linear Mobius
quadratic estimate is contained in this manuscript. Independent reviewers
should assess the supplied component theorems and the feedback obstruction,
not be asked to invent the missing growing-horizon argument.
