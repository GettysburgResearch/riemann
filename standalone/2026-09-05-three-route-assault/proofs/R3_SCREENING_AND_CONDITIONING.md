## R3.9. A completely explicit screened quartet

Take `eta=1/sqrt(2)` on `[-1,1]`, zero elsewhere, with simple support

\[
 Z=\{-\epsilon,\epsilon,iy,-iy\},\qquad y>0,\ \epsilon>0.
\]

The positive span `V` is generated by `cos(epsilon t)`, `sin(epsilon t)`, and `cosh(yt)`. The odd horizontal vector is `sinh(yt)` up to sign. The first and third positive vectors are even, so only `sin(epsilon t)` screens it.

With the normalized integral norm `||h||^2=(1/2)int_(-1)^1 |h(t)|^2dt`, define

\[
 S(y)=\frac{\sinh(2y)}{4y}-\frac12,
\quad V(\epsilon)=\frac12\left(1-\frac{\sin(2\epsilon)}{2\epsilon}\right),
\]

\[
 C(y,\epsilon)=
 \frac{y\cosh y\sin\epsilon-\epsilon\sinh y\cos\epsilon}{y^2+\epsilon^2}.
\]

Direct integration gives the exact one-dimensional Schur complement

\[
 \boxed{h_N(y,\epsilon)=S(y)-C(y,\epsilon)^2/V(\epsilon)>0.}
 \tag{1}
\]

Strictness follows from linear independence of `sinh(yt)` and `sin(epsilon t)` on an interval.

Let `epsilon` tend to zero with `y` fixed. The normalized sine tends to the linear function, and

\[
 \boxed{
 h_N(y,0+)=S(y)-3\left(\frac{y\cosh y-\sinh y}{y^2}\right)^2
 =\frac{y^6}{1575}+\frac{y^8}{14175}+O(y^{10}).
 }
 \tag{2}
\]

In contrast, the raw odd norm is `S(y)=y^2/3+O(y^4)`. Thus two legitimate simple real neighbors can screen a quadratic horizontal signal down to sixth order.

There is also a single-parameter, nonconfluent version: choose `epsilon=y^2`. Then

\[
 h_N(y,y^2)=y^6/1575+O(y^8).
 \tag{3}
\]

To justify (3), divide `sin(epsilon t)` by `epsilon`; it is `t+O(epsilon^2)` uniformly. Along `epsilon=y^2` the induced change in the leading residual is `O(y^5)`, while the unscreened-after-linear-projection residual starts at order `y^3`. Its squared norm therefore has the same order-six coefficient. All four nodes are distinct for positive `y`.

## R3.10. General odd-polynomial screening law

Add `2m` simple real points at `±epsilon,...,±m epsilon`, keeping the single pair `±iy`. Their odd span is

\[
 \operatorname{span}\{\sin(k\epsilon t):1\le k\le m\}.
\]

As `epsilon` tends to zero, divided differences of these sine functions give an invertible change of basis converging to

\[
 \operatorname{span}\{t,t^3,\ldots,t^{2m-1}\}.
\]

This follows from the Vandermonde matrix in `k^2` after factoring `k epsilon` from each sine series. The relevant finite Gram matrices converge to a positive definite polynomial moment Gram, so their orthogonal projections converge.

Put `n=2m+1` and let `L_n=2^(-n) binom(2n,n)` be the leading coefficient of the Legendre polynomial `P_n`. After that first limit, expansion at `y=0` gives

\[
 \boxed{
 h_N(y,0+)=
 \frac{y^{2n}}{(n!)^2(2n+1)L_n^2}+O(y^{2n+2}).
 }
 \tag{4}
\]

**Proof.** The first surviving odd term of `sinh(yt)` is `y^n t^n/n!`. The monic residual after projection on lower odd powers is `P_n(t)/L_n`; its normalized squared norm is `1/((2n+1)L_n^2)`. This proves (4).

Formula (4) is an iterated-limit statement. No unproved uniformity in simultaneously growing `m`, shrinking `epsilon`, and varying `y` is assumed.

## R3.11. No displacement-only lower bound at fixed bandwidth

Fix `y>0`. The odd Taylor polynomials approximate `sinh(yt)` uniformly on `[-1,1]`. In particular, the degree-`(2m-1)` truncation has uniform error at most

\[
 e^y\frac{y^{2m+1}}{(2m+1)!}.
\]

By the subspace convergence above, for sufficiently small positive `epsilon` the real exponentials screen the odd vector with squared residual as close as desired to the polynomial residual. Thus, with one nonreal pair at a **fixed** positive displacement and an increasing real background, `h_N` can tend to zero.

Therefore there is no universal positive lower bound `h_N>=Phi(y)` depending only on nonzero horizontal displacement and fixed bandwidth, without an additional restriction on the real background or its conditioning.

This does not contradict `H>0` for every finite independent packet. It shows why finite strict positivity is not a uniform arithmetic gap.

## What this changes in the research programme

The horizontal surplus theorem succeeds, but the easiest proposed next step fails. A raw `sinh^2(y t)` penalty is not the retained penalty after projection. The exact target is the Schur complement, and any source/tail estimate must beat its potentially much smaller eigenvalues.

A useful continuation should derive a prime-side representation or bound for that Schur complement, or prove a source-specific frame condition that rules out the screening mechanisms above. A generic sampling assertion based only on the proportion of simple real zeros is insufficient.
