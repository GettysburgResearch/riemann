## Statement

Fix a finite-dimensional real feature space

\[
\mathcal Y=\mathbb R^n.
\]

A coordinate of `y in Y` is one exact primitive quantity: for example a real or
imaginary part of one sampled special-function value, a contracted prime block,
a recurrence value, or another finite quantity with unambiguous mathematical
semantics. Repeated appearances of the same primitive use one coordinate and one
feature identifier.

A **finite RH-admissible conic model** consists of:

1. exact affine scalar rows

   \[
   q_i(y)=b_i+a_i^{\mathsf T}y,
   \qquad i=1,\ldots,m;
   \]

2. exact affine Hermitian matrix pencils

   \[
   K_r(y)=K_{r,0}+\sum_{j=1}^n y_jK_{r,j},
   \qquad r=1,\ldots,R;
   \]

3. explicit logical gates proving that RH implies

   \[
   q_i(y)\ge0\quad\text{and}\quad K_r(y)\succeq0
   \]

   for the exact feature vector generated by the frozen finite object.

The corresponding RH-admissible feature region is

\[
\mathcal K_{\rm RH}
=
\{y:q_i(y)\ge0\ \forall i,\ K_r(y)\succeq0\ \forall r\}.
\]

An exact **dual witness portfolio** consists of nonnegative scalar weights
`lambda_i` and, for each matrix pencil, an exact positive-semidefinite multiplier
`W_r`. Its affine score is

\[
P(y)=
\sum_i\lambda_iq_i(y)
+
\sum_r\operatorname{tr}(W_rK_r(y)).
\]

Every such portfolio satisfies

\[
P(y)\ge0
\qquad\text{for every }y\in\mathcal K_{\rm RH}.
\]

For a proof-carrying certificate, represent each `W_r` by an exact finite Gram
sum

\[
W_r=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L_r}\alpha_{r,\ell}
 v_{r,\ell}v_{r,\ell}^{*},
\qquad \alpha_{r,\ell}\ge0,
\]

with rational, dyadic, or Gaussian-rational vectors. A numerical LP, SDP, or
eigensolver may nominate the weights and vectors, but the exact checker trusts
only the frozen exact data and direct algebraic reconstruction.

Given a rigorous nonempty uncertainty enclosure `C subset Y`, the portfolio is
an **uncertainty-robust conic separator** when

\[
\sup_{y\in C}P(y)<0.
\]

Its score moat is

\[
\mu=-\sup_{y\in C}P(y)>0.
\]

Conditional on every imported logical gate, such a certificate disproves RH.

## Definitions

- **Primitive feature:** a leaf-level finite mathematical quantity evaluated or
  enclosed once. Reuse does not create independent uncertainty.
- **Scalar row:** one affine inequality known to be nonnegative under RH.
- **Matrix cone:** one affine Hermitian matrix known to be PSD under RH.
- **Portfolio cone:** the product dual cone

  \[
  \mathbb R_+^m\times
  \mathbb S_+^{d_1}\times\cdots\times\mathbb S_+^{d_R}.
  \]

- **Score moat:** strict separation measured in the chosen affine score. It
  scales with positive rescaling of all portfolio weights.
- **Feature-repair moat:** a norm-dependent lower bound on how far the primitive
  feature vector must move before it can reach `K_RH`; L-5702 supplies the
  scale-invariant form.

## Motivation

The project now has many finite RH-valid constraints on shared data: scalar
positivity of `xi'/xi`, differential and Stieltjes localizers, secants, divided
differences, barycentric Pick forms, Loewner minors, fixed-vector Pick forms,
finite Weil matrices, and arithmetic comparison inequalities.

Treating each row or eigenvector as an isolated candidate discards their conic
structure. Two rows may each have an interval crossing zero, while a
nonnegative exact combination cancels their common uncertainty and is uniformly
negative. Likewise, one uncertain rank-one matrix direction may fail while a
higher-rank exact PSD multiplier survives.

The correct target is therefore not the most negative floating eigenvalue. It is
an exact dual functional separating the entire rigorous uncertainty set from
the finite RH-admissible cone.

## Proof or construction

For `y in K_RH`, every scalar term is nonnegative because
`lambda_i>=0` and `q_i(y)>=0`. For a matrix term, write the exact Gram sum:

\[
\operatorname{tr}(W_rK_r(y))
=
\sum_\ell\alpha_{r,\ell}
 v_{r,\ell}^{*}K_r(y)v_{r,\ell}\ge0,
\]

because `alpha>=0` and `K_r(y)` is PSD. Summing proves `P(y)>=0` on
`K_RH`.

If `sup_C P<0`, no point of `C` can belong to `K_RH`. The exact primitive
feature vector lies in `C` by the uncertainty-closure certificate. Hence it
violates at least one imported RH-valid scalar or PSD condition. Once the
route-specific implication gates are proved, RH is false.

## Analytic domain audit

D-5701 is finite-dimensional convex and linear algebra. It creates no analytic
continuation, contour deformation, branch choice, convergence exchange, or
zero-counting claim. Each imported scalar row or matrix cone retains its own
analytic-domain and normalization audit.

A portfolio cannot repair a bad analytic gate. A wrong sign, normalization,
admissibility statement, missing singularity exclusion, or invalid limiting
argument blocks the portfolio implication regardless of the numerical moat.

## Gap audit

- Portfolio weights must be nonnegative. Subtraction does not preserve RH-valid
  nonnegativity.
- A rounded PSD multiplier is not proof-grade. Use an exact Gram decomposition
  or another independently checkable PSD certificate.
- Shared primitives must not be duplicated as independent uncertainty
  coordinates.
- The row library is finite. Failure to find a separator proves nothing about
  RH or about a larger library.
- Raw score moat is not scale invariant.
- Strict separation is mandatory; a robust upper endpoint equal to zero is not
  a counterexample certificate.
- Matrix midpoint eigenvalues are nomination data only. Freeze exact Gram
  vectors and reevaluate the contracted score.

## Adversarial tests

1. Use `q1=-2/5+u`, `q2=-2/5-u`, `|u|<=1`. Each row is individually
   inconclusive, while the exact portfolio `(1,1)` equals `-4/5`.
2. Replace the shared `u` by independent copies and require the certificate to
   disappear.
3. Give one row a negative weight and require rejection.
4. Use `K(u)=diag(-1/2+u,-1/2-u)`. Each coordinate direction is inconclusive;
   the exact Gram multiplier `I` gives score `-1`.
5. Mutate one Gram vector or coefficient and require exact endpoint mismatch.
6. Multiply all weights by a positive rational. The score moat changes, while
   the feature-repair moat from L-5702 does not.

## Remaining uncertainty

The finite-dimensional implication is elementary. Practical strength depends on
constructing a useful finite row library without excessive coefficient
amplification and on retaining correlated primitive enclosures from the Arb,
Taylor-model, recurrence, or prime-sum producer.

Large SDP multipliers near a proper face may be difficult to rationalize. Low-
rank exact Gram families are preferable to opaque rounded dense matrices.
