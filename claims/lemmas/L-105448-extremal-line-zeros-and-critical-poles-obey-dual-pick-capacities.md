# L-105448 — Extremal-line zeros and critical poles obey dual Pick capacities

Claim ID: `L-105448`  
Status: **PROVED EXACT EXTREMAL-LINE STRUCTURE THEOREM**  
Created: 2026-08-24  
Depends on: `L-105442`, `L-105447`  
RH status: **not assumed**

## 1. Dual Pick functions at maximal zero height

Retain the notation of `L-105447`. At the maximal zero height `beta_r`, put

\[
f_\beta(w)
={\Xi^{(r)}(w+i\beta_r)
 \over
 \Xi^{(r+1)}(w+i\beta_r)},
\qquad
 g_\beta(w)=-{1\over f_\beta(w)}.
\tag{L-105448.1}
\]

Both are Pick functions in the upper half-plane.

The boundary poles of `g_beta` are the top-edge zeros of `Xi^(r)` and were
priced in `L-105447`. The boundary poles of `f_beta` are the top-edge
noncommon zeros of `Xi^(r+1)`.

## 2. Extremal critical points are automatically simple and correctly signed

Let

\[
c_j+i\beta_r
\]

be a zero of `Xi^(r+1)` for which `Xi^(r)` does not vanish. If its multiplicity
as a zero of `Xi^(r+1)` were greater than one, `f_beta` would have a boundary
pole of order greater than one. A Pick function can have only simple real
poles in its meromorphic boundary continuation. Therefore the critical point
is simple.

Write

\[
\rho_j
={\Xi^{(r)}(c_j+i\beta_r)
 \over
 \Xi^{(r+2)}(c_j+i\beta_r)}.
\]

The local expansion is

\[
f_\beta(w)={\rho_j\over w-c_j}+O(1).
\]

A real pole of a Pick function has negative residue. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\rho_j<0.
}
\tag{L-105448.2}

Thus a positive noncommon critical residue can never occur on the uppermost
zero line of its parent. Wrong residues, if they exist, are strictly below the
parent extremal boundary.

Common zeros remain in the confluent multiplicity ledger. They produce a
removable value of `f_beta`, not a noncommon critical pole.

## 3. Critical-pole Poisson capacity

Let `C_r^top` be the set of noncommon top-edge critical points and put

\[
w_j=-\rho_j>0.
\]

The Herglotz measure of `f_beta` has the atom `w_j delta_(c_j)`. Therefore, for
every `y>0`,

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{c_j\in C_r^{\rm top}}
 w_j{y\over c_j^2+y^2}
\le
\Im f_\beta(iy)
=U_r(\beta_r+y).
}
\tag{L-105448.3}

This is the exact dual of the top-zero packing theorem

\[
\sum_{a_j\in Z_r^{\rm top}}
 n_j{y\over a_j^2+y^2}
\le {1\over U_r(\beta_r+y)}.
\tag{L-105448.4}

## 4. Reciprocal capacity uncertainty

Define

\[
\mathcal Z_r(y)
=
\sum_{a_j\in Z_r^{\rm top}}
 n_j{y\over a_j^2+y^2},
\]

\[
\mathcal P_r(y)
=
\sum_{c_j\in C_r^{\rm top}}
 (-\rho_j){y\over c_j^2+y^2}.
\]

Multiplying (L-105448.3)--(L-105448.4) gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal Z_r(y)\mathcal P_r(y)\le1
\qquad(y>0).
}
\tag{L-105448.5
}

The two extremal boundary populations are not independently arbitrary: zeros
consume the reciprocal anchor budget while critical poles consume the anchor
budget itself.

## 5. Consequence for adjacent-rung height plateaux

If

\[
\beta_{r+1}=\beta_r
\]

and a zero of `Xi^(r+1)` occurs on that extremal line without being common
with `Xi^(r)`, then it is automatically simple and has the correct negative
residue by (L-105448.2). Thus equality of adjacent maximal heights cannot
create a positive-residue event at the extremal boundary.

This does not force strict height descent: a negative-residue critical point or
a common zero may still lie on the same line.

## 6. Scope

The reciprocal capacity inequality does not force either extremal set to be
empty. Continuous Herglotz measure generated by lower zeros may carry the
remaining source budget. The theorem is an exact constraint on any attained
maximal-height configuration, not a proof that `beta_r=0`.
