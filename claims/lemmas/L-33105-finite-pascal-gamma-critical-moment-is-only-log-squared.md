## 1. Finite size-biased child law

Let `K_n` have the exact finite size-biased Pascal law

\[
\mathbb P(K_n=k)=\frac{2k}{n(n+1)},
\qquad1\le k\le n,
\]

and put

\[
V_n=K_n/n.
\]

For every real `alpha`, finite summation gives

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[V_n^{-\alpha}]
=\frac{2n^{\alpha-1}}{n+1}
 \sum_{k=1}^n k^{1-\alpha}.
}
\tag{L-33105.1}
\]

## 2. Subcritical moments

If `0<=alpha<2`, integral comparison gives

\[
\sum_{k=1}^n k^{1-\alpha}
\le1+\int_1^n x^{1-\alpha}dx
\le1+\frac{n^{2-\alpha}}{2-\alpha}.
\]

Hence

\[
\boxed{
\sup_{n\ge1}\mathbb E[V_n^{-\alpha}]<\infty
\qquad(0\le\alpha<2).
}
\tag{L-33105.2}
\]

More precisely the Riemann-sum limit is

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[V_n^{-\alpha}]
\longrightarrow\frac{2}{2-\alpha},
}
\tag{L-33105.3}
\]

which is the `Beta(2,1)` moment.

## 3. Exact boundary moment

At `alpha=2`, (L-33105.1) becomes

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[V_n^{-2}]
=\frac{2n}{n+1}H_n,
\qquad
H_n=\sum_{k=1}^n\frac1k.
}
\tag{L-33105.4}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[V_n^{-2}]
<2(1+\log n).
}
\tag{L-33105.5}
\]

The corresponding continuum `Beta(2,1)` boundary moment diverges. At every finite endpoint, however, the divergence is only logarithmic.

## 4. Two-step finite Gamma state

Let `U_n,V_n` be independent copies of the finite Pascal fraction and define

\[
\boxed{
G_n=-4\log(U_nV_n).
}
\tag{L-33105.6}
\]

This is the exact finite analogue of the continuum Gamma state in `L-33102`.

For `eta>=0`,

\[
\mathbb E[e^{\eta G_n}]
=\left(\mathbb E[V_n^{-4\eta}]\right)^2.
\tag{L-33105.7}
\]

Thus, for every fixed

\[
0\le\eta<\frac12,
\]

the family is uniformly bounded:

\[
\boxed{
\sup_n\mathbb E[e^{\eta G_n}]<\infty.
}
\tag{L-33105.8}
\]

At the exact continuum boundary `eta=1/2`, equations (L-33105.4) and (L-33105.7) give the explicit finite value

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[e^{G_n/2}]
=\left(\frac{2n}{n+1}H_n\right)^2
<4(1+\log n)^2.
}
\tag{L-33105.9}
\]

So the finite Pascal target pays only a polylogarithmic cost at the critical exponential moment where the limiting Gamma law has an infinite moment.

## 5. Critical square-root propagation moment

The actual square-root scaling of one child uses the much smaller exponent `alpha=1/2`. From (L-33105.1),

\[
\mathbb E[V_n^{-1/2}]
=\frac{2}{\sqrt n(n+1)}\sum_{k=1}^n\sqrt k.
\]

Integral comparison yields a uniform constant bound, and

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb E[V_n^{-1/2}]\to\frac43.
}
\tag{L-33105.10}
\]

Therefore two finite Pascal generations have uniformly bounded ordinary square-root amplification. The only logarithmic growth appears at the much stronger exponential boundary corresponding to the continuum Gamma moment pole.

## 6. Why this matters for MPL

The old independent-convolution route required a positive residual law with exponential moments strictly below `1/2`; its limiting moment at `1/2` necessarily diverges.

The finite Pascal programme does not need to take that limit before paying the certificate. The exact state generated by two finite Pascal splits obeys

```text
subcritical eta<1/2:  uniform bounded cost;
critical eta=1/2:     O(log^2 n) cost.
```

Thus a finite state-resolved Martingale Pascal Lift is allowed a polylogarithmic boundary ledger without losing the `X^o(1)` target. This removes one apparent obstruction to the finite construction.

It does not construct the martingale lift itself and does not control the source-specific square-root dual pairing.
