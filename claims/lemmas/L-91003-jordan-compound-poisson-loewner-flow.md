## 1. Positive Jordan quotient

For `u>0` define the generalized Jordan totient

\[
\boxed{
J_u(n)=n^u\prod_{p\mid n}(1-p^{-u})\ge0.
}
\tag{L-91003.1}
\]

Euler products give, absolutely for `Re(s)>1+u`,

\[
\boxed{
R_u(s):=\frac{\zeta(s-u)}{\zeta(s)}
=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{J_u(n)}{n^s}.
}
\tag{L-91003.2}
\]

For `n>1`,

\[
J_u(n)
=\prod_{p^a\Vert n}
 e^{(a-1)u\log p}(e^{u\log p}-1),
\tag{L-91003.3}
\]

so every Taylor coefficient at `u=0` is nonnegative. In particular

\[
\boxed{
\Lambda_m(n):=\partial_u^mJ_u(n)|_{u=0}\ge0
}
\tag{L-91003.4}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
(-1)^m\frac{\zeta^{(m)}(s)}{\zeta(s)}
=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\Lambda_m(n)}{n^s}
\qquad(\Re s>1).
}
\tag{L-91003.5}
\]

For `m=1`, `Lambda_1=Lambda` is the ordinary von Mangoldt function.
Thus the safe-line prime source is the first member of an entire positive
Jordan tower.

## 2. Exact compound-Poisson characteristic exponent

Fix `sigma>1+u` and normalize on a vertical line:

\[
\Phi_{u,\sigma}(x)
=\frac{R_u(\sigma+ix)}{R_u(\sigma)}.
\tag{L-91003.6}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\log\Phi_{u,\sigma}(x)
=\sum_{p}\sum_{r\ge1}
 \frac{p^{-r\sigma}(p^{ru}-1)}r
 \left(e^{-ixr\log p}-1\right).
}
\tag{L-91003.7}
\]

All intensities are nonnegative and summable. Hence
`Phi_(u,sigma)` is the characteristic function of a compound-Poisson random
variable supported on the additive semigroup generated by the prime logs.

Differentiating the Lévy exponent at `u=0` recovers the prime logarithmic
derivative:

\[
\boxed{
\partial_u\log R_u(s)|_{u=0}
=-\frac{\zeta'(s)}{\zeta(s)}
=\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{n^s}.
}
\tag{L-91003.8}
\]

This explains why the finite-prime channel of `L-91002` has a canonical positive
feature space: it is the infinitesimal Lévy measure of a positive Jordan flow.

## 3. Completed horizontal transport

Define

\[
\boxed{
\Theta_u(s)=\frac{\xi(s-u)}{\xi(s)}.
}
\tag{L-91003.9}
\]

It obeys the exact cocycle law

\[
\boxed{
\Theta_{u+v}(s)
=\Theta_u(s)\Theta_v(s-u)
}
\tag{L-91003.10}
\]

and has infinitesimal generator

\[
\boxed{
\partial_u\log\Theta_u(s)|_{u=0}
=-\frac{\xi'(s)}{\xi(s)}
=\mathscr X(s).
}
\tag{L-91003.11}
\]

On the symmetry boundary

\[
\Re s=\frac{1+u}{2},
\]

the functional equation and conjugation give

\[
\boxed{|\Theta_u(s)|=1.}
\tag{L-91003.12}
\]

Indeed `xi(s-u)=overline{xi(s)}` there.

For `s=1+u+q`, (L-91003.9) becomes

\[
\frac1q\Theta_u(1+u+q)
=\frac1q\frac{\xi(1+q)}{\xi(1+u+q)},
\tag{L-91003.13}
\]

which is exactly the unconditional positive one-Green kernel of `L-9506`.
Thus `L-9506` is the safe, real-axis feature measure of the same horizontal
transport whose generator is the single-safe-line logarithmic derivative.

## 4. RH and the Schur flow

Assume RH. The denominator of `Theta_u` has no zero in

\[
\Re s>\frac{1+u}{2}.
\]

Together with (L-91003.12), the standard bounded-type/Phragmen--Lindelof
argument gives

\[
\boxed{
|\Theta_u(s)|\le1
\qquad
\left(\Re s>\frac{1+u}{2}\right).
}
\tag{L-91003.14}
\]

Hence the de Branges--Rovnyak kernel

\[
\boxed{
K_u(s,w)
=\frac{1-\Theta_u(s)\overline{\Theta_u(w)}}
 {s+\bar w-(1+u)}
}
\tag{L-91003.15}
\]

is positive semidefinite.

Conversely, if (L-91003.14)—or merely analyticity of `Theta_u` in its half-plane—
holds for every `u` in any interval `(0,u_0)`, then RH follows. Indeed an off-line
zero `rho` with `Re rho>1/2` lies in that half-plane for every sufficiently small
`u<2 Re(rho)-1`; cancellation by `xi(rho-u)` can occur only for a discrete set of
`u`, so some `Theta_u` has a pole.

Subject to the standard bounded-type detail, this yields the structural
criterion

\[
\boxed{
\mathrm{RH}
\iff
\{\Theta_u:0<u<1\}
\text{ is a Schur evolution in the moving half-planes.}
}
\tag{L-91003.16}
\]

## 5. Infinitesimal Pick kernel

Differentiate (L-91003.15) at `u=0`. Since `Theta_0=1`, the first nontrivial
kernel is

\[
\boxed{
K^{(1)}(s,w)
=-\frac{\mathscr X(s)+\overline{\mathscr X(w)}}
 {s+\bar w-1}.
}
\tag{L-91003.17}
\]

Under RH it has the zero-side Gram expansion

\[
K^{(1)}(s,w)
=\sum_\rho
 \frac{m_\rho}{(s-\rho)(\bar w-\bar\rho)}
\tag{L-91003.18}
\]

in the usual symmetrically regularized sense. Thus the completed logarithmic
derivative is literally the infinitesimal Pick generator of the horizontal
Jordan flow.

The radial Borel curvature of `L-91001` is a third-order differential localizer
of this infinitesimal kernel. The Hausdorff matrices of `T-91001` are therefore
finite Loewner tests of one and the same evolution.
