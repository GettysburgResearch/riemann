## 1. Abstract Selberg Riccati equation

Let `P0` be a fixed positive reference measure on `[0,infinity)` and let `nu` be a real signed measure. Suppose the exact distributional identity is

\[
\boxed{
 \mathscr L\nu+\nu*\nu=R,}
 \tag{1}

where

\[
 \mathscr L\nu=y\,d\nu+2\,dP_0*d\nu
 \tag{2}

and `*` is additive convolution in logarithmic coordinates.

For a test function `f` for which all pairings converge, the adjoint is

\[
\boxed{
 (\mathscr L^*f)(y)
 =y f(y)+2\int_0^\infty f(x+y)\,dP_0(x).}
 \tag{3}

Thus

\[
 \langle\nu,\mathscr L^*f\rangle
 +\iint f(x+y)\,d\nu(x)d\nu(y)
 =\langle R,f\rangle.
 \tag{4}

## 2. Positive Hankel gate

Assume the Hankel kernel

\[
 K_f(x,y)=f(x+y)
 \tag{5}

is positive semidefinite on the support under consideration. Equivalently, assume there is a Hilbert-space feature map `Phi_f` such that

\[
 f(x+y)=\langle\Phi_f(x),\Phi_f(y)\rangle.
 \tag{6}

Then

\[
\boxed{
 \iint f(x+y)\,d\nu(x)d\nu(y)
 =\left\|\int\Phi_f(x)d\nu(x)\right\|^2
 \ge0.}
 \tag{7}

Consequently, if

\[
 \mathscr L^*f=k,
 \tag{8}

then

\[
\boxed{
 \langle\nu,k\rangle
 \le\langle R,f\rangle.}
 \tag{9}

The sign is exact. No absolute value of the prime-pair term is taken.

## 3. Stop-loss target for one dyadic knot

The prime-ramp value at physical logarithmic scale `T` is generated by the stop-loss kernel

\[
 k_T(y)=(T-y)_+.
 \tag{10}

After subtracting the continuous reference ramp and inserting the explicit archimedean correction from `T-20208`, every dyadic knot margin has the form

\[
\boxed{
 \widetilde{\mathcal D}_r(T/r)
 =b_r(T)+\langle\nu,k_T\rangle,}
 \tag{11}

where `b_r(T)` is an explicit reference/archimedean scalar and `nu` is the centered prime measure in the shared PR #216 normalization.

Therefore any test `f_(r,T)` satisfying

\[
 \mathscr L^*f_{r,T}=-k_T
 \tag{12}

and the positive Hankel gate gives

\[
\boxed{
 \widetilde{\mathcal D}_r(T/r)
 \ge b_r(T)-\langle R,f_{r,T}\rangle.}
 \tag{13}

If the right side is `-exp(o(r))` uniformly for every endpoint and prime-power knot in `[2^r,2^(r+1)]`, then `T-20208` implies RH.

The opposite sign convention for `nu` replaces (12) by `L*f=k_T`; the production normalization must choose one convention and bind it globally.

## 4. Completely monotone sufficient class

A convenient sufficient condition for the Hankel gate is

\[
\boxed{
 f(u)=\int_0^\infty e^{-su}\,d\mu(s),
 \qquad \mu\ge0.}
 \tag{14}

Then

\[
 f(x+y)=\int_0^\infty e^{-sx}e^{-sy}\,d\mu(s)
 \tag{15}

and the square in (7) is literal.

The proof does not require this representation; finite positive Hankel matrices on the exact support also suffice. But a completely monotone adjoint solution would give a dimension-free cofinal theorem.

## 5. Explicit adjoint for the leading reference density

For the leading reference measure

\[
 dP_0(x)=e^{x/2}dx,
 \tag{16}

and a test supported in `[0,T]`, equation (3) becomes

\[
\boxed{
 (\mathscr L^*f)(y)
 =y f(y)+2e^{-y/2}
  \int_y^T e^{u/2}f(u)du,
 \qquad0<y<T.}
 \tag{17}

If `L*f=k`, put

\[
 J(y)=\int_y^T e^{u/2}f(u)du.
\]

Then

\[
 -yJ'(y)+2J(y)=e^{y/2}k(y),
 \qquad J(T)=0,
 \tag{18}

and hence

\[
\boxed{
 J(y)=y^2\int_y^T{e^{u/2}k(u)\over u^3}du.}
 \tag{19}

Thus the unique compactly supported adjoint solution is explicit. For the stop-loss kernel, its Hankel sign is not automatic; that sign is exactly the zeta-specific dual obstruction rather than a hidden functional-analysis gap.

## 6. Curvature-corrected alternative

The exact knot row may instead use the centered mass-square form of `L-20209`. In that case one may choose `k` to be the derivative/quantile test producing

\[
 A_j-H_r'(T_j)
\]

and use the quadratic term (7) directly to pay its square. A successful certificate must still retain the transport reserve `P_(r,j)`; bounding only the square proves no sign.

This gives two compatible proof interfaces:

```text
stop-loss adjoint:
  residual one-sided bound -> direct knot margin;

quantile adjoint:
  Selberg square -> centered mass penalty,
  transport reserve -> remaining orientation.
```

## 7. Exact remaining theorem

A complete Selberg closure of the dyadic route consists of a family `f_(r,T)` such that, uniformly over every required row,

1. `L* f_(r,T)` equals the exact stop-loss or quantile test;
2. `f_(r,T)(x+y)` is positive semidefinite on the complete support;
3. the exact Selberg residual obeys
   \[
   \langle R,f_{r,T}\rangle-b_r(T)
   \le e^{o(r)};
   \]
4. endpoint/source conventions match `T-20208`.

This theorem would imply RH. The present lemma proves only the duality and identifies the missing positive-Hankel adjoint.

## 8. Proof boundary

- Equations (3)--(9) are elementary exact convolution duality.
- The PR #216 Riccati identity must be independently normalized before composition.
- The compactly supported solution in (19) need not be completely monotone.
- No positive-Hankel solution or residual bound is proved here.
- An `L2` estimate for `nu` without the sign in (7) is insufficient.
