## 1. Arithmetic Lévy measure

For the ordered prime-power prefix of `T-20802`, write

\[
 q_r=p_r^{k_r},\qquad
 \tau_r=\log q_r=k_r\log p_r,
 \qquad
 w_r={\log p_r\over\sqrt{q_r}}.
 \tag{L-20810.1}
\]

Define

\[
 \boxed{
 \lambda_r={1\over k_r\sqrt{q_r}},
 \qquad w_r=\lambda_r\tau_r,}
 \tag{L-20810.2}
\]

and the finite Lévy measure

\[
 \boxed{
 \nu_j=\sum_{r\le j}\lambda_r\,\delta_{\tau_r}.}
 \tag{L-20810.3}
\]

Let `N_r` be independent Poisson random variables of means `lambda_r` and put

\[
 \boxed{X_j=\sum_{r\le j}\tau_r N_r.}
 \tag{L-20810.4}
\]

Then

\[
 \mathbb E e^{iuX_j}
 =\exp\left\{
  \int_0^\infty(e^{iut}-1)\nu_j(dt)
 \right\}.
 \tag{L-20810.5}
\]

Thus `X_j` is a finite compound-Poisson variable. Its first two cumulants are
exactly the two prime moments of `T-20802`:

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathbb E X_j=P_j,
 \qquad
 \operatorname{Var}(X_j)=Q_j.}
 \tag{L-20810.6}
\]

Indeed,

\[
 \int t\,\nu_j(dt)
 =\sum_{r\le j}{\tau_r\over k_r\sqrt{q_r}}
 =\sum_{r\le j}{\log p_r\over\sqrt{q_r}}=P_j,
 \tag{L-20810.7}
\]

and

\[
 \int t^2\nu_j(dt)
 =\sum_{r\le j}{k_r(\log p_r)^2\over\sqrt{q_r}}
 =\sum_{r\le j}{\Lambda(q_r)\log q_r\over\sqrt{q_r}}
 =Q_j.
 \tag{L-20810.8}
\]

This is the finite-prefix version of the classical compound-Poisson Euler
product measure. No convergence or RH assumption is involved.

## 2. Archimedean reference Lévy law

Put

\[
 \tau_1=\log2,
 \qquad p_0=A'(\tau_1),
 \qquad M_0=A(\tau_1)>0.
 \tag{L-20810.9}
\]

Let

\[
 \chi(p)=\arg\min_{t\ge\tau_1}\{A(t)-pt\}
 \tag{L-20810.10}
\]

be the minimizer map of `L-20808`. Hence

\[
 \chi(p)=\tau_1\quad(0\le p\le p_0),
 \qquad A'(\chi(p))=p\quad(p>p_0).
 \tag{L-20810.11}
\]

For `P>=0`, define a finite positive measure `nu_P^*` by

\[
 \boxed{
 \int_0^\infty f(t)\,\nu_P^*(dt)
 =\int_0^P {f(\chi(p))\over\chi(p)}\,dp.}
 \tag{L-20810.12}
\]

Equivalently, before truncation at cumulative mass `P`, the reference measure
has the boundary atom and density

\[
 {p_0\over\tau_1}\delta_{\tau_1}
 +{A''(t)\over t}\mathbf 1_{(\tau_1,\infty)}(t)dt.
 \tag{L-20810.13}
\]

Let `Y_P` be the compound-Poisson variable with Lévy measure `nu_P^*`. Since
`chi(p)>=tau_1`, the measure is finite. Its first two cumulants are

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathbb E Y_P=P,}
 \tag{L-20810.14}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{Var}(Y_P)
 =\int_0^P\chi(p)dp
 =A_+^*(P)+M_0.}
 \tag{L-20810.15}
\]

The final equality uses

\[
 (A_+^*)'(p)=\chi(p),
 \qquad A_+^*(0)=-A(\tau_1)=-M_0.
 \tag{L-20810.16}
\]

## 3. Exact variance-reserve identity

At a prime-power prefix, `X_j` and `Y_(P_j)` have the same mean. Their variance
difference is exactly the transport reserve beyond the fixed initial moat:

\[
 \boxed{
 M_j
 =M_0+\operatorname{Var}(X_j)-
       \operatorname{Var}(Y_{P_j}).}
 \tag{L-20810.17}
\]

Indeed, (L-20810.6) and (L-20810.15) give

\[
 M_0+Q_j-[A_+^*(P_j)+M_0]
 =Q_j-A_+^*(P_j)=M_j.
\]

Consequently the full theorem of `T-20802` is an equal-mean, second-cumulant
comparison between:

1. the arithmetic compound-Poisson law generated by all prime powers in the
   prefix; and
2. one explicit archimedean compound-Poisson law generated by the curvature of
   `A`.

The fixed reserve `M_0` is not an asymptotic error. It is the exact variance
credit inherited from the constrained endpoint `log 2`.

## 4. Stop-loss representation

For any positive Lévy measure `nu` with finite second moment, define its call or
stop-loss transform

\[
 \mathcal C_\nu(u)=\int_0^\infty(t-u)_+\nu(dt),
 \qquad u\ge0.
 \tag{L-20810.18}
\]

Tonelli gives

\[
 \boxed{
 2\int_0^\infty\mathcal C_\nu(u)du
 =\int_0^\infty t^2\nu(dt).}
 \tag{L-20810.19}
\]

For the arithmetic prefix,

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal C_j(u)
 =\sum_{r\le j}{(\tau_r-u)_+\over k_r\sqrt{q_r}}.}
 \tag{L-20810.20}
\]

For the reference law,

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathcal C_*(u;P)
 =\int_0^P{(\chi(p)-u)_+\over\chi(p)}dp.}
 \tag{L-20810.21}
\]

Therefore

\[
 \boxed{
 M_j=M_0+2\int_0^\infty
 [\mathcal C_j(u)-\mathcal C_*(u;P_j)]du.}
 \tag{L-20810.22}
\]

This is an exact positive-kernel attack surface. A sufficient cofinal theorem
may control the integrated stop-loss deficit directly, without locating the
Fenchel minimizer or differentiating a prime staircase.

## 5. Quantile form and relation to `L-20808`

The first-moment measures

\[
 t\nu_j(dt)=\sum_{r\le j}w_r\delta_{\tau_r}(dt),
 \qquad
 t\nu_{P_j}^*(dt)=\chi_*(dp|_{[0,P_j]})
 \tag{L-20810.23}
\]

both have total mass `P_j`. Let `tau_j^#(p)` denote the increasing arithmetic
quantile, constant and equal to `tau_r` on the mass interval
`(P_(r-1),P_r]`. Then

\[
 \boxed{
 M_j-M_0
 =\int_0^{P_j}[\tau_j^\#(p)-\chi(p)]dp.}
 \tag{L-20810.24}
\]

This is exactly the prime-minus-curvature transport identity of `L-20808`, now
identified as a variance comparison of infinitely divisible laws.

## 6. Relation to the probabilistic zeta literature

For `Re s>1`, the normalized Euler product is classically compound Poisson with
Lévy atoms `p^(-ks)/k` at `k log p`. Nakamura--Suzuki, arXiv:2306.08317, prove
that a different completed-zeta characteristic function is infinitely divisible
if and only if RH. The present lemma does not import that equivalence. It uses
only a finite prime prefix and identifies the exact second-cumulant transport
hidden in `T-20802`.

This suggests a concrete bridge: prove the reserve by an arithmetic
infinitely-divisible comparison, or show that a negative reserve creates the
anti-causal Lévy/Poisson defect isolated by `L-19810/L-19811`.
