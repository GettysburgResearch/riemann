## 1. Common function-space setting

Let `X` be a Hilbert or Banach function space continuously embedded in the
space of distributions on one fixed bounded interval `I`. For every simple
critical-line zero ordinate `gamma`, let

\[
 \mathcal E_\gamma:X\to\mathbb C
\]

be the corresponding Fourier-evaluation functional. Fix positive proof-grade
weights `m_gamma`.

Let

\[
 \mathcal K\subset X                                      \tag{L-18509.1}
\]

be a compact set such that

\[
 0\notin\mathcal K.                                      \tag{L-18509.2}
\]

For `f in mathcal K`, define the complete simple-line evaluation energy

\[
 \mathscr E_\infty(f)
 =\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal Z_0^{\rm simp}}
   m_\gamma|\mathcal E_\gamma f|^2.                      \tag{L-18509.3}
\]

Assume:

1. the series in (L-18509.3) converges uniformly on `mathcal K`;
2. its sum is continuous on `mathcal K`;
3. simple critical-line evaluations are a uniqueness family on `mathcal K`,
   meaning
   \[
   \mathscr E_\infty(f)>0\quad(f\in\mathcal K).           \tag{L-18509.4}
   \]

Condition 3 follows from `L-18507` whenever every member of `mathcal K` is a
nonzero compactly supported harmonic lift. Conditions 1--2 are quantitative
tail hypotheses, not consequences of uniqueness.

## 2. Uniform infinite-frame floor

Compactness and continuity give

\[
 \boxed{
 \sigma_\infty^2
 :=\min_{f\in\mathcal K}\mathscr E_\infty(f)>0.}         \tag{L-18509.5}
\]

This is the first uniform step: pointwise nonvanishing becomes a positive
minimum because the normalized family is compact.

## 3. One finite zero block frames the whole compact family

Enumerate the simple critical-line zeros as

\[
 \gamma_1,\gamma_2,\ldots
\]

and define

\[
 \mathscr E_N(f)
 =\sum_{n=1}^N m_{\gamma_n}
  |\mathcal E_{\gamma_n}f|^2.                            \tag{L-18509.6}
\]

Each `mathscr E_N` is continuous on `mathcal K`, and

\[
 \mathscr E_N(f)\nearrow\mathscr E_\infty(f)             \tag{L-18509.7}
\]

pointwise. Since the limit is continuous and the convergence is monotone,
Dini's theorem gives uniform convergence. Hence there is a finite `N_0` such
that

\[
 \sup_{f\in\mathcal K}
 |\mathscr E_\infty(f)-\mathscr E_{N_0}(f)|
 <\frac12\sigma_\infty^2.                                \tag{L-18509.8}
\]

Therefore

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathscr E_{N_0}(f)
 \ge\frac12\sigma_\infty^2
 \quad(f\in\mathcal K).}                                 \tag{L-18509.9}
\]

Thus one finite proof-grade set of simple critical-line zeros frames **every**
member of the compact family with a common lower bound.

## 4. Packet consequence

Let `W_j` be finite-dimensional harmonic complements with metrics `G_j`, and let
`J_j` be their harmonic lifts. Suppose their normalized lift spheres satisfy

\[
 \mathcal S_j
 =\{J_jw:\ w\in W_j,\ \|w\|_{G_j}=1\}
 \subset\mathcal K.                                      \tag{L-18509.10}
\]

Let `K_(N_0),j` be the selected-zero Gram generated by the finite block from
Section 3. Then (L-18509.9) gives, simultaneously for all `j`,

\[
 \boxed{
 K_{N_0,j}|_{W_j}
 \succeq\frac12\sigma_\infty^2G_j|_{W_j}.}               \tag{L-18509.11}
\]

In particular,

\[
 \boxed{
 \Sigma_j\ge\frac12\sigma_\infty^2}                     \tag{L-18509.12}
\]

uniformly along the packet sequence.

If complete one-sided omitted-zero budgets and radical endpoints satisfy

\[
 B_j+2\epsilon_j
 <\frac12\sigma_\infty^2                                \tag{L-18509.13}
\]

cofinally, then `L-18508` supplies the explicit threshold

\[
 \beta_j
 =\epsilon_j+
  \frac{\frac12\sigma_\infty^2-B_j}{2},                 \tag{L-18509.14}
\]

and therefore

\[
 \epsilon_j<B_j+\beta_j
 <\frac12\sigma_\infty^2\le\Sigma_j,                    \tag{L-18509.15}
\]

with `epsilon_j<=beta_j/2`.

## 5. A concrete compactness adapter

A sufficient way to produce `mathcal K` is the following. Suppose all lifts are
supported in one fixed bounded interval and, for some `s>0`,

\[
 \|J_jw\|_{H^s(I)}\le C\|w\|_{G_j},                     \tag{L-18509.16}
\]

\[
 \|J_jw\|_{L^2(I)}\ge c\|w\|_{G_j},                     \tag{L-18509.17}
\]

with constants `C<infinity` and `c>0` independent of `j`. Rellich compactness
shows that the closure in `L2(I)` of the normalized lift spheres is compact,
and (L-18509.17) excludes the zero function.

To use Fourier evaluations, one additionally needs enough regularity for those
evaluations and the zero-sum tail in (L-18509.3) to be continuous uniformly on
the closure. A uniform `H^s` bound with sufficiently large `s`, or a common
Hardy/form envelope, is one proof-facing route.

## 6. Why this is the exact missing topology

Finite-dimensional compactness at each separate support proves only
`Sigma_j>0`. It gives no common lower bound because the compact set changes with
`j`.

The present theorem shows that the missing upgrade is **collective
compactness** of all normalized visible lifts in a topology controlling the
complete simple-line evaluation energy. Once that is supplied, qualitative
critical-line uniqueness automatically becomes the uniform quantitative frame
needed by the harmonic theorem.

Conversely, a growing sequence containing localized approximants to an off-line
Xi-cardinal difference cannot satisfy all of (L-18509.10), uniform evaluation-
tail continuity, and (L-18509.17): its real-zero evaluations tend to zero while
its normalized form defect remains nonzero. Thus the compactness hypothesis is
load bearing rather than formal.

## 7. Proof boundary

- The compactness, minimum, Dini, and finite-frame implications are exact.
- No current production theorem proves the uniform graph/Hardy bound
  (L-18509.16) on the complete growing harmonic complements.
- If the natural frequency scale grows, an unrescaled `H^s` bound generally
  fails; one must prove a rescaled profile compactness theorem together with the
  corresponding moving-zero sampling limit.
- The theorem does not by itself prove the required cofinal moat or RH.
