## Exact relation to the parabolic error

Let

\[
f(u)=1-u^2.
\]

Then the parabolic error of `T-9503` is

\[
\mathcal E_1(x)=\int_0^1f(u)d\sigma_x(u).
\tag{L-9511.5}
\]

Since `f(1)=0`, Stieltjes integration by parts gives

\[
\mathcal E_1(x)
=-\int_0^1f'(t)T_x(t)dt
=2\int_0^1tT_x(t)dt.
\]

A second integration by parts yields

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal E_1(x)
=2\left[U_x(1)-\int_0^1U_x(t)dt\right].}
\tag{L-9511.6}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
|\mathcal E_1(x)|
\le2\sqrt2\,\mathfrak A(x)^{1/2},}
\tag{L-9511.7}
\]

where

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak A(x)
=|U_x(1)|^2+\int_0^1|U_x(t)|^2dt.}
\tag{L-9511.8}
\]

The quantity `A(x)` is nonnegative by construction.

## Positive kernel representation

For `u in [0,1]`, define the feature vector

\[
\Phi(u)=
\left(
1-u,
\ t\mapsto(t-u)_+
\right)
\in\mathbb R\oplus L^2([0,1]).
\tag{L-9511.15}
\]

Then

\[
U_x(1)=\int(1-u)d\sigma_x(u),
\]

and

\[
U_x(t)=\int(t-u)_+d\sigma_x(u).
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak A(x)
=\iint_{[0,1]^2}K_*(u,v)
 d\sigma_x(u)d\sigma_x(v),}
\tag{L-9511.16}
\]

with the positive-semidefinite kernel

\[
\boxed{
K_*(u,v)
=(1-u)(1-v)
+\int_0^1(t-u)_+(t-v)_+dt.}
\tag{L-9511.17}
\]

Equivalently, with `m=max(u,v)`,

\[
\boxed{
K_*(u,v)
=(1-u)(1-v)
+\frac{(1-m)^3}{3}
+\frac{|u-v|(1-m)^2}{2}.}
\tag{L-9511.18}
\]

Thus `A(x)^(1/2)` is exactly the maximum mean discrepancy of `sigma_x` in the
RKHS generated by `K_*`.

The kernel is a reflected released once-integrated Brownian covariance plus one
rank-one boundary feature. Its positivity is an identity, not an RH assumption.

## Why the boundary feature is load-bearing

The raw integrated profile `U_x` alone does not determine the parabolic error:
`U_x(1)` appears separately in (L-9511.6). Omitting the rank-one feature
`(1-u)(1-v)` would lose this boundary channel.

This is the positive-energy analogue of the endpoint term that repeatedly
appears in the Jordan/Volterra and Schur programs.
