## 1. Herglotz representation

Let `f` be a Pick function with the real-symmetric representation

\[
f(z)=\alpha z+\beta+
\int_{\mathbb R}
\left({1\over t-z}-{t\over1+t^2}\right)d\mu(t),
\qquad
\alpha\ge0,
\quad \mu\ge0.
\tag{L-105451.1}

Put `z=a+ih`, `h>0`, and define the differential microscope

\[
\mathcal C_f(a,h)
={1\over2}\left[h\Re f'(a+ih)-\Im f(a+ih)\right].
\tag{L-105451.2}

## 2. Exact cancellation of the affine carrier

Write

\[
D_t(a,h)=(a-t)^2+h^2.
\]

The Herglotz formula gives

\[
\Im f(a+ih)
=\alpha h+\int{h\over D_t(a,h)}\,d\mu(t),
\tag{L-105451.3}

\[
\Re f'(a+ih)
=\alpha+\int{(a-t)^2-h^2\over D_t(a,h)^2}\,d\mu(t).
\tag{L-105451.4}

Substitution yields the exact identity

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C_f(a,h)
=-h^3
\int_{\mathbb R}
{d\mu(t)\over((a-t)^2+h^2)^2}.
}
\tag{L-105451.5}

Both the real constant and the nonnegative affine carrier disappear. The
microscope sees only the Herglotz measure.

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal C_f(a,h)\le0
}
\tag{L-105451.6}

with strict inequality unless `mu=0`. Equality for the affine Pick maps is the
only global equality case.

## 3. Six-dimensional Newton form

Put

\[
\mathcal W_f(a,h)={\mathcal C_f(a,h)\over h^3}.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal W_f(a,h)
=-
\int_{\mathbb R}
{d\mu(t)\over((a-t)^2+h^2)^2}.
}
\tag{L-105451.7}

After the axisymmetric lift to `R x R^5`, this is the negative Newton
potential of the axis measure `mu`. Distributionally,

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_{\mathbb R^6}
\widetilde{\mathcal W}_f
=4\pi^3\,\mu\otimes\delta_0^{(5)}.
}
\tag{L-105451.8}

Thus the Herglotz measure and the six-dimensional Gauss source are literally
the same object.

For a meromorphic Pick ratio `F/F'`, an atom of mass `-rho_c` at a critical
point `c` gives

\[
\mathcal W_c={\rho_c\over((a-c)^2+h^2)^2},
\]

recovering `L-105446--L-105450`.

## 4. Complete monotonicity in scale

At every fixed `a`, put `q=h^2`. Equation (L-105451.7) gives, for every
`n>=0`,

\[
\boxed{
(-1)^n{d^n\over dq^n}
\left[-\mathcal W_f(a,\sqrt q)\right]
=(n+1)!
\int_{\mathbb R}
{d\mu(t)\over((a-t)^2+q)^{n+2}}
\ge0.
}
\tag{L-105451.9
}

The scale profile of the good microscope is therefore a Stieltjes-completely
monotone function. The one inequality `C<=0` sits inside an infinite source
hierarchy generated by the same measure.

## 5. Exact Fourier multiplier

Use the Fourier convention

\[
\widehat g(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{-ia\xi}g(a)\,da.
\]

The classical transform

\[
\int_{\mathbb R}
{h^3e^{-ia\xi}\over(a^2+h^2)^2}\,da
={\pi\over2}(1+h|\xi|)e^{-h|\xi|}
\tag{L-105451.10}

turns (L-105451.5) into

\[
\boxed{
\widehat{\mathcal C_f}(\xi,h)
=-{\pi\over2}
(1+h|\xi|)e^{-h|\xi|}
\widehat\mu(\xi).
}
\tag{L-105451.11
}

Hence the multiplier isolated independently in `L-105444` is exactly the
Fourier slice of the six-dimensional Newton kernel. The scale-flow, Hardy
phase and Gauss-law programmes are not analogies; they are three transforms of
one measure.

## 6. Schwarz–Pick interpretation

The Schwarz–Pick inequality for the upper half-plane gives

\[
h|f'(a+ih)|\le\Im f(a+ih).
\]

Therefore

\[
h\Re f'(a+ih)\le\Im f(a+ih),
\]

which is (L-105451.6). Formula (L-105451.5) identifies the exact slack rather
than merely bounding it.

## 7. Scope

The theorem assumes `f` is Pick. For Xi, proving that the lifted Gauss source
is nonnegative and axis-supported is exactly the unresolved critical-sign
problem. The representation does not prove that hypothesis; it identifies the
measure which every successful proof must construct.
