## 1. General additive observable

Let

\[
 g:\mathbb Z_{\ge1}\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}
\]

be completely additive:

\[
 g(mn)=g(m)+g(n),
 \qquad g(1)=0.
 \tag{L-90202.1}
\]

Equivalently, choose arbitrary prime costs `a_p=g(p)>=0` and put

\[
 g(n)=\sum_p v_p(n)a_p.
 \tag{L-90202.2}
\]

For real prime parameters `x_p in [-1,1]`, retain

\[
 f_x(d)=\prod_{p\mid d}x_p
 \quad(d\text{ squarefree}),
 \qquad b_x=\mu^2f_x,
\]

and define the generalized prime extraction

\[
 \boxed{
 \Lambda_{x,g}(n)
 =(b_x*g)(n)
 =\sum_{d\mid n}\mu^2(d)f_x(d)g(n/d).
 }
 \tag{L-90202.3}
\]

At the Liouville point `x_p=-1`, this is

\[
 \Lambda_g(n):=(\mu*g)(n).
 \tag{L-90202.4}
\]

## 2. Exact positive Bernstein formula

Let

\[
 \mathcal P(n)=\{p:p\mid n\},
 \qquad r=\omega(n),
 \qquad y_p=\frac{1+x_p}{2}\in[0,1].
\]

Then

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 \Lambda_{x,g}(n)
 ={}&2^{r-1}
 \sum_{q\mid\operatorname{rad}(n)\atop q\ {\rm prime}}
 g(q)\prod_{p\mid n,\ p\ne q}y_p\\
 &\quad+2^r\bigl[g(n)-g(\operatorname{rad}(n))\bigr]
 \prod_{p\mid n}y_p.
 \end{aligned}}
 \tag{L-90202.5}
\]

Every coefficient and monomial is nonnegative because

\[
 g(n)-g(\operatorname{rad}(n))
 =\sum_{p^a\Vert n}(a-1)g(p)\ge0.
 \tag{L-90202.6}
\]

### Proof

Complete additivity gives, for squarefree `d|n`,

\[
 g(n/d)=g(n)-\sum_{q\mid d}g(q).
\]

Therefore

\[
 \Lambda_{x,g}(n)
 =g(n)\prod_{p\mid n}(1+x_p)
 -\sum_{q\mid n\atop q\ {\rm prime}}
 g(q)x_q\prod_{p\mid n,\ p\ne q}(1+x_p).
 \tag{L-90202.7}
\]

Substitute `1+x_p=2y_p` and `x_q=2y_q-1`. The full-product terms combine into the second line of (L-90202.5); the remaining terms give the first line. ∎

## 3. The Liouville slice is exact prime-power extraction

The ordinary Liouville slice satisfies

\[
 \boxed{
 \Lambda_g(n)=
 \begin{cases}
 g(p),&n=p^a,\\
 0,&\omega(n)\ge2.
 \end{cases}}
 \tag{L-90202.8}
\]

This follows either from finite inclusion-exclusion or by setting every `y_p=0` in (L-90202.5).

Consequently

\[
 \boxed{
 \Lambda_{x,g}(n)\ge\Lambda_g(n)
 \qquad(n\ge2,\ x_p\in[-1,1]).
 }
 \tag{L-90202.9}
\]

Thus Liouville pointwise minimizes not one generalized-prime sequence but the entire cone generated by the valuation observables `v_p` with nonnegative prime weights.

## 4. Simultaneous nonnegative-weight theorem

Let `W(n)>=0` be finitely supported. Then

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{n\ge2}W(n)\Lambda_{x,g}(n)
 \ge
 \sum_{p^a}W(p^a)g(p).
 }
 \tag{L-90202.10}
\]

The inequality holds simultaneously for every choice of the nonnegative prime-cost vector `(g(p))_p`.

Equivalently, for each prime `p`, the coefficient of `g(p)` on the left is at least

\[
 \sum_{a\ge1}W(p^a).
 \tag{L-90202.11}
\]

This is a vector-valued domination theorem: the complete generalized-prime measure of every real multiplicative cube point dominates the ordinary prime-power measure coordinatewise after any nonnegative observation.

## 5. Ordered-algebra extension

The proof uses only addition, multiplication of distinct prime coordinates, and positivity of the Bernstein variables. Therefore (L-90202.5)--(L-90202.10) remain valid when the scalars `y_p` are replaced by commuting positive contractions in any ordered commutative algebra.

In particular, a finite family of commuting positive-semidefinite matrices `0<=Y_p<=I` produces a positive-semidefinite difference

\[
 \Lambda_{Y,g}(n)-\Lambda_g(n)I\succeq0.
 \tag{L-90202.12}
\]

No noncommutative ordering claim is made.
