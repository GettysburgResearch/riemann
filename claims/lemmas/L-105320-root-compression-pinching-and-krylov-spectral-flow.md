## 1. Root matrix and the first derivative as a compression

Let

\[
p(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(z-x_j),
\qquad
x_1<\cdots<x_n,
\qquad n\ge2,
\]

and put

\[
D=\operatorname{diag}(x_1,\ldots,x_n),
\qquad
e={1\over\sqrt n}(1,\ldots,1)^T,
\qquad
P=I-ee^*.
\]

On the Hilbert space

\[
\mathcal H=e^\perp
\]

define

\[
C=PDP\big|_{\mathcal H},
\qquad
\bar x=e^*De={1\over n}\sum_jx_j,
\qquad
b=PDe\in\mathcal H.
\tag{L-105320.1}
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\det(zI_{\mathcal H}-C)={p'(z)\over n}.
}
\tag{L-105320.2}
\]

Moreover the exact Schur complement is

\[
\boxed{
{p(z)\over p'(z)}
={1\over n}
\left[
 z-\bar x-b^*(zI_{\mathcal H}-C)^{-1}b
\right].
}
\tag{L-105320.3}
\]

### Proof

In the orthogonal decomposition `span{e} plus H`, the root matrix is

\[
D=
\begin{pmatrix}
\bar x&b^*\\
b&C
\end{pmatrix}.
\]

On the other hand,

\[
e^*(zI-D)^{-1}e
={1\over n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}{1\over z-x_j}
={1\over n}{p'(z)\over p(z)}.
\]

Block inversion says that the same scalar is

\[
\left[z-\bar x-b^*(zI-C)^{-1}b\right]^{-1}.
\]

This proves (L-105320.3). Taking determinants in the same block decomposition
and cancelling `p(z)` proves (L-105320.2).

## 2. Critical residues are the spectral weights of one vector

Let

\[
c_1<\cdots<c_R,
\qquad R=n-1,
\]

be the zeros of `p'`, and let `E_j` be the rank-one spectral projection of
`C` at `c_j`. Define

\[
\rho_j={p(c_j)\over p''(c_j)},
\qquad
a_j=-\rho_j>0.
\tag{L-105320.4}
\]

Taking the residue of (L-105320.3) at `c_j` gives

\[
\boxed{
b^*E_jb=na_j.
}
\tag{L-105320.5}
\]

Equivalently, if `v_j` is a normalized eigenvector of `C`, then

\[
|\langle b,v_j\rangle|^2=na_j.
\]

There is also a direct electrostatic verification. The vector

\[
r_j=\left({1\over c_j-x_1},\ldots,{1\over c_j-x_n}\right)^T
\]

belongs to `H` because `p'(c_j)=0`, satisfies `Cr_j=c_jr_j`, and obeys

\[
\langle b,r_j\rangle=-\sqrt n,
\qquad
\|r_j\|^2=\sum_{s=1}^{n}{1\over(c_j-x_s)^2}={1\over a_j}.
\]

Now let

\[
\operatorname{Pin}_C(X)=\sum_{j=1}^{R}E_jXE_j
\]

be spectral pinching onto the commutative algebra generated by `C`. The
critical-residue multiplication operator from `L-105300`, transported to the
symmetric compression model, satisfies

\[
\boxed{
-U_p={1\over n}\operatorname{Pin}_C(bb^*).
}
\tag{L-105320.6}
\]

Indeed both sides are diagonal in the `C` eigenbasis and have eigenvalues
`a_j`.

In particular,

\[
\sum_ja_j={\|b\|^2\over n}
={1\over n^2}\sum_{s=1}^{n}(x_s-\bar x)^2,
\tag{L-105320.7}
\]

recovering the first residue ledger without a contour.

## 3. Residue coherence is an inverse participation ratio

Put

\[
q_j={a_j\over\sum_sa_s}
={b^*E_jb\over\|b\|^2}.
\tag{L-105320.8}
\]

Then `q_j>0` and `sum q_j=1`. The residue coherence is exactly

\[
\boxed{
\mathfrak C(p)
={\left(\sum_ja_j\right)^2\over R\sum_ja_j^2}
={1\over R\sum_jq_j^2}.
}
\tag{L-105320.9}
\]

Thus coherence is the normalized inverse participation ratio of the physical
root-to-critical coupling vector `b` in the derivative eigenbasis.

Choose the signs of the eigenvectors so that

\[
\beta={b\over\|b\|}
=\sum_j\sqrt{q_j}\,v_j,
\qquad
h={1\over\sqrt R}\sum_jv_j.
\tag{L-105320.10}
\]

If `theta` is the angle between `beta` and `h`, then

\[
\boxed{
\sin^2\theta
\le
\min\left(1,{1-\mathfrak C(p)\over\mathfrak C(p)}\right).
}
\tag{L-105320.11}
\]

To prove this, put `y_j=Rq_j`. Since `|sqrt(y)-1|<=|y-1|`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\|\beta-h\|^2
&={1\over R}\sum_j(\sqrt{y_j}-1)^2\\
&\le {1\over R}\sum_j(y_j-1)^2
=R\sum_jq_j^2-1
={1-\mathfrak C\over\mathfrak C}.
\end{aligned}
\]

For unit vectors, `sin theta<=||beta-h||`.

## 4. The second derivative is a uniform compression

In the eigenbasis of `C`, let

\[
C_h=P_hC\big|_{h^\perp},
\qquad P_h=I-hh^*.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\det(zI_{h^\perp}-C_h)
={p''(z)\over n(n-1)}.
}
\tag{L-105320.12}
\]

Indeed, with `q(z)=det(zI-C)=p'(z)/n`,

\[
\det(zI_{h^\perp}-C_h)
=q(z)\,h^*(zI-C)^{-1}h
={q'(z)\over R}.
\]

Thus the actual zeros of `p''` are obtained by compressing the derivative-root
matrix in the **uniform derivative-eigenbasis direction** `h`.

By contrast, define

\[
C_\beta=P_\beta C\big|_{\beta^\perp}.
\tag{L-105320.13}
\]

Since `beta` is the normalized vector `b`, this is exactly the two-step
physical Krylov compression of the original root matrix:

\[
\boxed{
C_\beta
\simeq
P_{\{e,b\}^\perp}D\big|_{\{e,b\}^\perp}.
}
\tag{L-105320.14}
\]

The equality is unitary equivalence of the compressed quadratic forms.

## 5. Coherence controls the exact adjacent-derivative spectral mismatch

Let

\[
d_1\le\cdots\le d_{R-1}
\]

be the zeros of `p''`, and let

\[
k_1\le\cdots\le k_{R-1}
\]

be the eigenvalues of the Krylov compression (L-105320.14). Put

\[
\operatorname{diam}(C)=c_R-c_1.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
\max_{1\le j<R}|d_j-k_j|
\le
2\operatorname{diam}(C)
\sqrt{
\min\left(1,{1-\mathfrak C(p)\over\mathfrak C(p)}\right)
}.
}
\tag{L-105320.15}
\]

### Proof

Let `W` be the plane rotation taking `h` to `beta` and acting identically on
the orthogonal complement of their span. Then

\[
\|W-I\|=2\sin(\theta/2),
\qquad
W(h^\perp)=\beta^\perp.
\]

Center `C` at the midpoint of its spectral interval, obtaining `A` with
`||A||=diam(C)/2`. Pulling `C_beta` back to `h^perp` and applying Weyl's
eigenvalue inequality gives

\[
\begin{aligned}
\max_j|d_j-k_j|
&\le\|W^*AW-A\|\\
&\le2\|A\|\|W-I\|\\
&=2\operatorname{diam}(C)\sin(\theta/2)\\
&\le2\operatorname{diam}(C)\sin\theta.
\end{aligned}
\]

Insert (L-105320.11).

## 6. Meaning and scope

This theorem makes three previously separate coordinates identical:

```text
critical-residue moments       = spectral weights of b;
residue coherence              = inverse participation of those weights;
next derivative roots          = uniform compression of the critical matrix.
```

High coherence says that the actual derivative direction `h` is close to the
physical root-coupling direction `beta`; consequently the true next derivative
is close to the two-step Krylov compression of the original root cloud.

The theorem is exact finite linear algebra for a real-rooted polynomial. It
does not cross the first derivative level at which the parent polynomial has
nonreal roots, does not establish low-order Xi coherence, and does not prove
RH. Its conclusion-facing use is to convert a residue-coherence estimate into
an adjacent-derivative spectral-flow estimate without a contour or individual
critical-point matching.
