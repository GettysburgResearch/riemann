## Boundary scattering symbol

For `omega>0`, define

\[
 \Theta_\omega(t)=
 \frac{\xi(\frac12-\omega-it)}
      {\xi(\frac12+\omega-it)}
 \tag{L-15418.1}
\]

on the real boundary away from removable zero coincidences. The functional
equations give

\[
 \boxed{|\Theta_\omega(t)|=1\quad\text{a.e.}}
 \tag{L-15418.2}
\]

without assuming RH.

Let

\[
 L^2(\mathbb R)=H^2_+\oplus H^2_-
 \tag{L-15418.3}
\]

be the Hardy decomposition, with orthogonal projections `P_+` and `P_-`.
Boundary multiplication by `Theta_omega` is a unitary operator on `L2`.
Define

\[
 T_\omega=P_+M_{\Theta_\omega}|_{H^2_+},
 \qquad
 \mathsf H_\omega=P_-M_{\Theta_\omega}|_{H^2_+}.
 \tag{L-15418.4}
\]

## Exact positive defect identity

For every `f in H^2_+`, unitarity and orthogonal projection give

\[
 \|f\|_2^2
 =\|\Theta_\omega f\|_2^2
 =\|T_\omega f\|_2^2
  +\|\mathsf H_\omega f\|_2^2.
 \tag{L-15418.5}
\]

Hence

\[
 \boxed{
 I-T_\omega^*T_\omega
 =\mathsf H_\omega^*\mathsf H_\omega
 \succeq0.}
 \tag{L-15418.6}
\]

The right side is the complete positive anti-causal defect. It is not a
finite-section approximation and contains every phase channel.

## Innerness equivalence

The following are equivalent for one `omega`:

1. `Theta_omega` is the boundary value of an inner function in the upper
   half-plane;
2. multiplication by `Theta_omega` maps `H^2_+` into itself;
3. `mathsf H_omega=0`;
4. `T_omega` is an isometry.

Thus

\[
 \boxed{
 \Theta_\omega\text{ inner}
 \iff
 \mathsf H_\omega^*\mathsf H_\omega=0.}
 \tag{L-15418.7}
\]

Suzuki's zero-free-half-plane criterion then yields

\[
 \boxed{
 \mathrm{RH}
 \iff
 \mathsf H_\omega=0
 \quad\text{for every }\omega>0.}
 \tag{L-15418.8}
\]

It is enough to use a dense countable family of positive rational `omega`,
because any off-line zero has a nonempty interval of offsets to its left.

## Finite Blaschke/pole packet

Suppose, for one offset, the meromorphic continuation of `Theta_omega` has a
finite upper-half-plane pole set `p_1,...,p_N` after all exact cancellations.
Let

\[
 B_P(z)=\prod_{j=1}^N
 \left(\frac{z-p_j}{z-\overline{p_j}}\right)^{m_j}.
 \tag{L-15418.9}
\]

Multiplication by `B_P` removes the poles while preserving boundary modulus.
The finite anti-inner component lies in the model space generated by `B_P`; its
reproducing kernel is

\[
 \boxed{
 K_{B_P}(z,w)=
 \frac{1-B_P(z)\overline{B_P(w)}}
      {2\pi i(\overline w-z)}.}
 \tag{L-15418.10}
\]

For simple rational principal parts, the Gram of the corresponding Hardy
kernels is exactly the Cauchy matrix of `T-15407` after the upper-half-plane and
right-half-plane coordinates are identified. Hence the pole Gram and the
positive Hankel defect are the same finite object up to a change of basis.

## Why boundary convergence is insufficient

A pole and its nearby compensating zero may approach one another as
`omega` varies. The boundary symbol can converge to `1` pointwise and in every
finite `Lp` norm while the finite-rank anti-Hardy channel remains nonzero.
Therefore none of the following proves (L-15418.8):

- pointwise convergence `Theta_omega -> 1`;
- convergence in boundary `L2`;
- smallness of finitely many Fourier coefficients;
- compactness separately at each fixed `omega`.

A positive proof needs exact causality or a **collective** compactness/norm-zero
theorem for the complete family.

## Collective-compactness interface

Embed the ranges of `mathsf H_omega` in one lower-Hardy space. A sufficient
positive theorem is:

1. `mathsf H_omega f -> 0` for every member of one dense exact arithmetic core;
2. the union
   \[
    \bigcup_{0<\omega\le1/2}
    \mathsf H_\omega\{f:\|f\|\le1\}
   \tag{L-15418.11}
   \]
   is relatively compact;
3. the operator family is continuous between pole-crossing offsets.

The strong-plus-collectively-compact theorem would then force norm convergence.
To obtain exact zero rather than merely a vanishing limit, one must also use the
monotone zero-free-half-plane index: a pole persists for every smaller offset.
A false-RH pole necessarily breaks either collective compactness or the index
closure.

## Relation to the Hardy phase energy

`L-15416` expresses the filtered logarithmic-derivative norm as the Dirichlet
energy of the phase of `Theta_omega`. Equation (L-15418.6) expresses the same
failure of innerness as a positive operator defect. These are respectively the
local differential and global Hardy-space realizations of the pole Gram in
`T-15407`.
