# L-91318 — The mixed Brownian reflection form is nonnegative between any two monotone positive tests

Claim ID: `L-91318`  
Status: **PROVED EXACT MIXED-CONE THEOREM; UPPER CROSS-CORRELATION CONTROL OPEN**  
Created: 2026-08-12  
Depends on: `L-91310`, `L-91314`  
RH status: **unproved**

## 1. Two monotone tests

Fix

\[
 0<a<\frac12.
\]

Let `F,G:R->R` be locally absolutely continuous and satisfy

\[
 F,G\ge0,
 \qquad
 F',G'\ge0
\]

almost everywhere, with sufficient completed-zeta tilt integrability.

Let `B_a(F,G)` denote the Hermitian polarization of the Brownian reflection
quadratic:

\[
 \mathcal R_a(F)=B_a(F,F).
\]

For real tests, its real cross term is generated by the symmetrized observable
below.

## 2. Symmetrized reflection observable

For two BPY copies `Z_1,Z_2`, put

\[
 \Delta=Z_1-Z_2.
\]

Define

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal A_{F,G}^{\rm sym}(Z_1,Z_2)
 =\frac12\int_{-Z_2}^{Z_1}
 \big[&F(y)G(y-\Delta)\\
      &+G(y)F(y-\Delta)\big]dy.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{L-91318.1}
\]

The integral is oriented when `Z_1+Z_2<0`.  Polarization of the reflection
observable gives

\[
 \mathcal A_{F+G}
 =\mathcal A_F+\mathcal A_G
  +2\mathcal A_{F,G}^{\rm sym}.
\tag{L-91318.2}
\]

## 3. Coordinate derivative for positive orientation

Differentiating in `Z_1` and integrating the derivatives of the second factors
by parts gives

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
 \partial_{Z_1}\mathcal A_{F,G}^{\rm sym}
 ={}&\frac12\big[
 F(-Z_2)G(-Z_1)+G(-Z_2)F(-Z_1)
 \big]\\
 &+\frac12\int_{-Z_2}^{Z_1}
 \big[
 F'(y)G(y-\Delta)\\
 &\hspace{35mm}+G'(y)F(y-\Delta)
 \big]dy.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{L-91318.3}
\]

Similarly,

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
 \partial_{Z_2}\mathcal A_{F,G}^{\rm sym}
 ={}&\frac12\big[
 F(-Z_2)G(-Z_1)+G(-Z_2)F(-Z_1)
 \big]\\
 &+\frac12\int_{-Z_2}^{Z_1}
 \big[
 F(y)G'(y-\Delta)\\
 &\hspace{35mm}+G(y)F'(y-\Delta)
 \big]dy.
\end{aligned}}
\tag{L-91318.4}
\]

If `Z_1+Z_2>=0`, every displayed term is nonnegative.

## 4. Reversed orientation

Assume `Z_1+Z_2<0`.  Put

\[
 K(y)=F(y)G(y-\Delta)+G(y)F(y-\Delta).
\]

Then

\[
\begin{aligned}
 K'(y)={}&F'(y)G(y-\Delta)+G'(y)F(y-\Delta)\\
 &+F(y)G'(y-\Delta)+G(y)F'(y-\Delta),
\end{aligned}
\tag{L-91318.5}
\]

and all four terms are nonnegative.

The forward integral from `Z_1` to `-Z_2` of either pair of terms in
(L-91318.5) is at most

\[
 K(-Z_2)-K(Z_1).
\]

Since

\[
 K(-Z_2)
 =F(-Z_2)G(-Z_1)+G(-Z_2)F(-Z_1)
\]

and

\[
 K(Z_1)
 =F(Z_1)G(Z_2)+G(Z_1)F(Z_2),
\]

using the reversed orientation in (L-91318.3)--(L-91318.4) gives

\[
\boxed{
 \partial_{Z_1}\mathcal A_{F,G}^{\rm sym}
 \ge\frac12[
  F(Z_1)G(Z_2)+G(Z_1)F(Z_2)
 ]\ge0,
}
\tag{L-91318.6}
\]

\[
\boxed{
 \partial_{Z_2}\mathcal A_{F,G}^{\rm sym}
 \ge\frac12[
  F(Z_1)G(Z_2)+G(Z_1)F(Z_2)
 ]\ge0.
}
\tag{L-91318.7}
\]

Thus the mixed observable is coordinatewise nondecreasing for every
`(Z_1,Z_2)`.

## 5. Mixed Fisher covariance

Under the completed tilt `P_u tensor P_u`, the copies remain independent.
Applying the one-coordinate covariance identity conditionally gives

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{Cov}_u^{(2)}
 (\mathcal A_{F,G}^{\rm sym},Z_1+Z_2)
 \ge0
 }
\tag{L-91318.8}
\]

for every `0<=u<=a`.

Polarizing the integrated Fisher identity of `L-91310` yields

\[
 \boxed{
 \operatorname{Re}B_a(F,G)
 =M(a)^2\int_0^a
  \operatorname{Cov}_u^{(2)}
  (\mathcal A_{F,G}^{\rm sym},S)du
 \ge0.
 }
\tag{L-91318.9}
\]

Hence every pair of positive monotone tests has a nonnegative Brownian/Xi
reflection cross term.

## 6. Consequences

For any finite collection `F_1,...,F_N` in the monotone cone, the matrix

\[
 [\operatorname{Re}B_a(F_i,F_j)]_{i,j}
\]

has nonnegative entries, and every nonnegative coefficient combination is
positive:

\[
 \mathcal R_a\left(\sum_jc_jF_j\right)\ge0
 \qquad(c_j\ge0).
\]

The mixed theorem is stronger than the latter diagonal statement because it
identifies the instantaneous Fisher cross-covariance before integration.

## 7. Why this still does not prove full PSD

Entrywise nonnegativity of a Hermitian matrix does not imply positive
semidefiniteness.  A negative direction may use coefficients of opposite phase
to exploit an excessively large positive cross term.

To close the full Pick matrix one needs an upper Cauchy--Schwarz-type bound

\[
 |B_a(F,G)|^2
 \le B_a(F,F)B_a(G,G)
\]

for arbitrary generators of a spanning cone, or an explicit common Hilbert
feature map.  The present theorem supplies the favorable sign but not that
upper correlation bound.
