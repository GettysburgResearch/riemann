## 1. Continuum size-biased split

Let `R` be uniform on `(0,1)`. Given `R=r`, choose one of the two child fractions

\[
r,\qquad1-r
\]

with probabilities `r` and `1-r`, respectively. Call the selected fraction `V`.

For every bounded measurable `f`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E f(V)
&=\int_0^1
\big[r f(r)+(1-r)f(1-r)\big]dr\\
&=2\int_0^1v f(v)dv.
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{V\sim\operatorname{Beta}(2,1).}
\tag{L-33102.1}
\]

Equivalently,

\[
-\log V\sim\operatorname{Exp}(2).
\tag{L-33102.2}
\]

Thus if `U,V` are the two independent `Beta(2,1)` variables of `L-33101`, they may be interpreted as the size-biased child fractions from two independent uniform binary splits.

## 2. Gamma target as two size-biased entropy increments

Since each `-log U` and `-log V` is `Exp(2)`,

\[
-4\log U,\ -4\log V
\]

are independent exponentials of rate `1/2`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
G=-4\log(UV)
\sim\operatorname{Gamma}(2,1/2).
}
\tag{L-33102.3}
\]

This gives a fragmentation interpretation of the sharp Gamma law used by the carry programme: it is four times the sum of the logarithmic losses along two independent size-biased uniform binary splits.

Conditionally on a split ratio `R=r`, the expected logarithmic loss of the size-biased child is

\[
-r\log r-(1-r)\log(1-r)=H(r),
\]

the binary entropy. Averaging over uniform `R`,

\[
\boxed{
\int_0^1H(r)dr=\frac12,
}
\tag{L-33102.4}
\]

which is the same entropy calibration appearing in the atomized carry Mellin identity.

## 3. Exact finite Pascal analogue

Fix an integer `n>=1`. Choose `J` uniformly from

\[
\{0,1,\ldots,n\}.
\]

Given `J=j`, choose the child of size `j` with probability `j/n` and the child of size `n-j` with probability `(n-j)/n`. Let `K` be the selected child size.

For every `1<=k<=n`, there are exactly two contributing parent splits, `J=k` and `J=n-k`, with the natural coincidence at the endpoints interpreted by multiplicity. Directly,

\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(K=k)=\frac{2k}{n(n+1)}.
}
\tag{L-33102.5}
\]

Indeed the two contributions are each

\[
\frac1{n+1}\frac{k}{n}.
\]

The weights sum to one because

\[
\sum_{k=1}^n\frac{2k}{n(n+1)}=1.
\]

Thus `K/n` is the exact finite size-biased uniform-split law. As `n->infinity` it converges to the `Beta(2,1)` density `2v dv` without introducing an external probability model.

## 4. Exact entropy relation

For a fixed split `j+(n-j)=n`, if a unit of parent mass is selected uniformly and one follows the child containing it, the expected logarithmic size loss is

\[
\frac jn\log\frac nj
+
\frac{n-j}{n}\log\frac n{n-j}.
\tag{L-33102.6}
\]

Its continuum limit is `H(j/n)`. Averaging over `j` is therefore the finite size-biased path version of the same binary-entropy calibration that drives the averaged-binomial carry row.

This gives a direct dictionary:

```text
uniform Pascal split
    -> size-biased child
    -> discrete Beta(2,1) law
    -> logarithmic entropy increment
    -> two independent increments
    -> Gamma(2,1/2) target.
```
