# L-24512 — Prime-power cube budget and bounded consecutive runs

Claim ID: `L-24512`  
Status: `PROPOSED — complete elementary arithmetic`  
Scope: descending primitive correction bookkeeping  
Issue: #245

For an integer `n>=2`, let

\[
P_3(n)=\sum_{p^a\mid n}(p^a)^3,
\tag{L-24512.1}
\]

where the sum runs over every prime power divisor `p,p^2,...,p^{v_p(n)}`.

## 1. Non-prime-power cube budget

If `n` is not a prime power, then

\[
\boxed{
P_3(n)\le\frac{35}{216}n^3.
}
\tag{L-24512.2}
\]

Equality holds only at `n=6`.

### Proof

Write the distinct prime-primary components as

\[
n=x_1x_2\cdots x_r,
\qquad x_i=p_i^{a_i},
\qquad r\ge2.
\]

For one component,

\[
S_3(p,a)=p^3+p^6+\cdots+p^{3a}.
\]

If the smallest component is at least `4`, the crude bounds

\[
\frac{S_3(2,a)}{2^{3a}}<\frac87,
\qquad
\frac{S_3(p,a)}{p^{3a}}\le\frac{27}{26}\quad(p\ge3)
\]

already give, for the two smallest components `x>=4`, `y>=3`,

\[
\frac{P_3(xy)}{(xy)^3}
\le\frac8{7y^3}+\frac{27}{26x^3}
<\frac{35}{216}.
\]

If the smallest component is `2`, the other component is an odd prime power
`y`. Directly,

\[
\frac{P_3(2y)}{(2y)^3}
=\frac1{y^3}+\frac1{8}\frac{S_3(y)}{y^3}.
\]

At `y=3` this is `(8+27)/216=35/216`. For `y>=5` the bound
`S_3(y)/y^3<=125/124` gives a strict smaller value; for `y=3^a`, `a>=2`,
the displayed geometric sum is also strictly smaller than its value at `y=3`
after the additional factor `1/y^3` is included.

Adding any further coprime prime-primary component multiplies the existing
ratio by at most `1/5^3` and adds at most

\[
\frac{125}{124}\frac1{(2\cdot3)^3},
\]

so it cannot recover the `n=6` value. This proves (L-24512.2).

## 2. Proper divisors of a prime power

If `n=p^a` is itself a prime power, then its proper prime-power divisors obey

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{1\le k<a}(p^k)^3
<\frac1{p^3-1}n^3
\le\frac17n^3.
}
\tag{L-24512.3}
\]

Thus after the adjacent prime power `q=n` is treated inside its local run, all
remaining prime-power divisors of `n` satisfy the stronger constant `1/7`.

Combining the two cases, if the full value `n` is excluded whenever `n` is a
prime power, then

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{\substack{q=p^a\mid n\\q<n\text{ if }n\text{ is a prime power}}}q^3
\le\frac{35}{216}n^3.
}
\tag{L-24512.4}
\]

## 3. Consecutive prime-power runs

A run of four consecutive prime powers contains two even members. Every even
prime power is a power of `2`; the only two powers of `2` differing by `2` are
`2` and `4`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\text{the only length-four run is }2,3,4,5,
}
\tag{L-24512.5}
\]

and every run beginning at `7` or later has length at most three.

For a run of length `ell`, the local correction matrix generated by spikes at
the run indices is

\[
H_\ell=
\begin{pmatrix}
2&-1&&\\
-1&2&-1&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots\\
&&-1&2
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{L-24512.6}
\]

Its inverse is explicitly

\[
(H_\ell^{-1})_{ij}
=\frac{\min(i,j)(\ell+1-\max(i,j))}{\ell+1}>0.
\tag{L-24512.7}
\]

Therefore every nonnegative residual on a consecutive run has a unique
nonnegative local spike correction. Since `ell<=3` beyond the finite initial
run, all local inverse constants are absolute.

## 4. Application boundary

The cube budget is tailored to the exact spike identity

\[
\Delta v_q(F_j)
=F_j(\mathbf1_{q\mid j+1}-2\mathbf1_{q\mid j}+\mathbf1_{q\mid j-1}).
\]

After the adjacent prime powers `j-1,j,j+1` are grouped into one short run,
(L-24512.4) gives an absolute weighted budget for every remaining lower
prime-power destination dividing `j-1` or `j+1`.

This is sufficient to control balanced descending leakage in a positive cube
moment. It does not control the objective cost after mass cascades to very small
prime powers; the high-order mean-zero taper of `L-24503` is still needed for
that unbalanced channel.
