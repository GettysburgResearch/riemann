## 1. Butterfly cone

Let

\[
 \mathcal B_T
 =c_T^-a_{T-1}-a_T+c_T^+a_{T+1}
\]

be the adjacent endpoint butterfly, and let `K_but` be the cone generated by:

- nonnegative butterfly intensities;
- the declared nonnegative baseline endpoint row;
- the explicit finite boundary atoms and port rows admitted by the Route C model.

For a dual endpoint cost `y_T`, put `g_T=y_T/alpha_T`.  Exact moment cancellation gives

\[
\boxed{
 \langle y,\mathcal B_T\rangle
 =\alpha_T
 [\theta_Tg_{T-1}+(1-\theta_T)g_{T+1}-g_T].
}
\tag{L-91376.1}
\]

Thus the dual is nonnegative on every butterfly if and only if `g` is discretely convex on the ordered endpoint-state nodes.

## 2. Finite alternative

After every row, ordinary, detail, boundary, and port constraint is included, the Route C primal is one finite real linear system whose coefficients are explicit algebraic/logarithmic quantities.  Farkas duality gives exactly one of:

1. a nonnegative producer in `K_but`; or
2. a real dual certificate, including a convex normalized endpoint potential, which separates the requested packet from `K_but`.

For a proof artifact, every coefficient and inequality must be represented symbolically or by outward rational enclosures.  A strict primal or dual margin can then be certified by rational intervals; a zero-margin case requires an exact symbolic identity.  This is a fail-closed proof interface for the declared butterfly ansatz, but Farkas duality alone does not promise rational optimizer coordinates.

A dual separator proves

\[
 \text{requested packet}\notin K_{\rm but}.

\[
\boxed{

Conversely, a passing primal is an unconditional CFFP witness regardless of completeness.
