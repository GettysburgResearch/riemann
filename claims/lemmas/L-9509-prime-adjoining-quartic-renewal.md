## Finite-prime observables

Let

\[
w(u)=(1-u^2)^2\mathbf1_{0<u<1},
\qquad
I_w=\int_0^1w(u)du=\frac8{15}.
\tag{L-9509.1}
\]

For a finite prime set `P`, define

\[
F_P(n)=\prod_{\substack{p\in P\\p\mid n}}(1-p^{-1}),
\qquad
c_P=\prod_{p\in P}(1-p^{-2}),
\tag{L-9509.2}
\]

\[
Q_P(x)=\frac1x\sum_{n<x}F_P(n)w(n/x),
\tag{L-9509.3}
\]

and the centered discrepancy

\[
D_P(x)=Q_P(x)-c_PI_w
\qquad(x>0).
\tag{L-9509.4}
\]

For `0<x<=1`, the sum is empty and hence

\[
D_P(x)=-c_PI_w.
\tag{L-9509.5}
\]

## Exact prime-adjoining renewal

If `p notin P`, then

\[
\boxed{
Q_{P\cup\{p\}}(x)
=Q_P(x)-p^{-2}Q_P(x/p).}
\tag{L-9509.6}
\]

Since

\[
c_{P\cup\{p\}}=(1-p^{-2})c_P,
\]

the centered discrepancy obeys the homogeneous recursion

\[
\boxed{
D_{P\cup\{p\}}(x)
=D_P(x)-p^{-2}D_P(x/p).}
\tag{L-9509.7}
\]

### Proof

For `p notin P`,

\[
F_{P\cup\{p\}}(n)
=F_P(n)-p^{-1}F_P(n)\mathbf1_{p\mid n}.
\]

Write `n=pm` in the second term. Because `F_P(pm)=F_P(m)`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_{P\cup\{p\}}(x)
&=Q_P(x)-\frac1{px}
 \sum_{m<x/p}F_P(m)w(pm/x)\\
&=Q_P(x)-p^{-2}Q_P(x/p).
\end{aligned}
\]

Subtracting the exact means proves (L-9509.7), including the case `x/p<=1`
because of (L-9509.5).

## Full Euler-product limit

For the empty prime set,

\[
D_\varnothing(x)
=\frac1x\sum_{n<x}w(n/x)-I_w.
\tag{L-9509.8}
\]

Iterating (L-9509.7) over a finite set gives

\[
D_P(x)=
\sum_{\substack{d\ge1\\p\mid d\Rightarrow p\in P}}
 \frac{\mu(d)}{d^2}
 D_\varnothing(x/d).
\tag{L-9509.9}
\]

At fixed `x`, the limit over all primes converges absolutely in the tail after
using the constant value (L-9509.5) and the convergence of `sum mu(d)/d^2`.
The result is precisely

\[
\boxed{
\lim_{P\nearrow\mathbb P}D_P(x)
=\mathcal Q(x)-\frac{16}{5\pi^2}
=\mathcal E_2(x).}
\tag{L-9509.10}
\]

Thus the complete RH-equivalent error is the all-prime renewal of one elementary
lattice-quadrature seed.

## Explicit seed

`L-9508` gives, for `x>1`,

\[
\boxed{
D_\varnothing(x)
=-\frac1{2x}
-\frac4{3x^3}B_3(\{x\})
+\frac1{x^4}B_4(\{x\})
-\frac1{5x^5}B_5(\{x\}).}
\tag{L-9509.11}
\]

The leading `x^-1` mode is annihilated in the all-prime limit by the Euler

\[
\prod_p(1-p^{-1})=0.
\]

The surviving size is therefore determined by the action of the dilation
cascade on the bounded periodic remainder.

## Critical logarithmic normalization

Set

\[
r=\log x,
\qquad
H_P(r)=e^{3r/2}D_P(e^r).
\tag{L-9509.12}
\]

Then (L-9509.7) becomes

\[
\boxed{
H_{P\cup\{p\}}(r)
=H_P(r)-p^{-1/2}H_P(r-\log p).}
\tag{L-9509.13}
\]

Thus each prime acts by the critical high-pass filter

\[
\boxed{\mathcal W_p=I-p^{-1/2}T_{\log p},}
\tag{L-9509.14}
\]

where

\[
(T_aH)(r)=H(r-a).
\]

Formally, on a Mellin/Fourier mode `e^{itr}`, the all-prime multiplier is

\[
\prod_p(1-p^{-1/2-it})
=\frac1{\zeta(1/2+it)}.
\tag{L-9509.15}
\]

Equation (L-9509.13), rather than the divergent pointwise product, is the exact
physical-space definition.

## RH criterion in wavelet form

By `T-9502`, RH is equivalent to

\[
\boxed{
H_{\mathbb P}(r)=O_\varepsilon(e^{\varepsilon r})
\qquad(r\to\infty).}
\tag{L-9509.16}
\]

Equivalently, the critical prime-dilation cascade sends the explicit seed
(L-9509.11) to a subexponentially growing output.

This is the scalar physical-space analogue of the repository's escaping-mode,
complete-frame, and terminal-prime operator problems. A hypothetical off-line

## Gap audit

- The finite-prime recurrence is exact; the subexponential cascade bound is not proved.
- Pointwise boundedness of `1/zeta(1/2+it)` is false because known critical-line zeros create poles; any valid frame theorem must retain the causal seed and distributional cancellation.
