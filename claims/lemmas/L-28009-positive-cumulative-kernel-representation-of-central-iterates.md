## 1. Critical normalization

Let

\[
 F_0(t)=t e^{t/2}\mathbf1_{t\ge0}
\]

and let `mathcal U` be the continuum central operator

\[
 (\mathcal UF)(t)
 =\sum_{m\ge2}(-1)^mF(t-\log m).
\]

Put

\[
 F_j=\mathcal U^jF_0,
 \qquad
 G_j(t)=e^{-t/2}F_j(t).
\tag{L-28009.1}

Then

\[
 (\mathcal VG)(t)
 :=e^{-t/2}\mathcal U(e^{\cdot/2}G)(t)
 =\sum_{m\ge2}{(-1)^m\over\sqrt m}
  G(t-\log m).
\tag{L-28009.2}

## 2. Positive cumulative staircase

Define

\[
 \boxed{
 C(t)=\sum_{2\le m\le e^t}{(-1)^m\over\sqrt m}
 \qquad(t\ge0),
 }
\tag{L-28009.3}

with the empty sum equal to zero.

Every partial sum is strictly positive.  Pairing consecutive terms gives

\[
 (2k)^{-1/2}-(2k+1)^{-1/2}>0,
\]

and an even terminal term is also positive.

The limit is

\[
 \boxed{
 \rho=\lim_{t\to\infty}C(t)
 =1-\eta(1/2),
 \qquad0<\rho<1.
 }
\tag{L-28009.4}

The alternating-series remainder gives the global estimate

\[
 \boxed{
 |C(t)-\rho|\le e^{-t/2}
 \qquad(t\ge0).
 }
\tag{L-28009.5}

For `0<=t<log2`, this follows from `C(t)=0` and `rho<2^-1/2`; after the first
jump it is the ordinary alternating remainder bound.

## 3. Integration-by-parts form of the operator

Let `G` be causal, locally absolutely continuous, and satisfy `G(0)=0`.  Stieltjes
integration by parts gives

\[
 \boxed{
 (\mathcal VG)(t)
 =\int_0^tC(u)G'(t-u)du
 =(C*G')(t).
 }
\tag{L-28009.6}

Although the atomic derivative of `C` is signed, the integrated kernel in
(L-28009.6) is pointwise positive.

## 4. Exact convolution-power formula

Since `G_0(t)=t`, equation (L-28009.6) gives

\[
 G_1(t)=\int_0^tC(u)du,
\]

and

\[
 G_2(t)=(C*C)(t).
\]

Because `C` vanishes on a neighborhood of zero, differentiation and convolution
have no boundary distribution at the origin.  Induction yields

\[
 \boxed{
 G_j(t)=D^{j-2}(C^{*j})(t)
 \qquad(j\ge2).
 }
\tag{L-28009.7}

Thus every continuum stage is generated by one positive staircase.  The sign
defects arise from taking the growing derivative order, not from a negative
convolution kernel.

## 5. Laplace symbol

Initially for `Re(s)>1/2`,

\[
 \boxed{
 s\widehat C(s)
 =\sum_{m\ge2}{(-1)^m\over m^{s+1/2}}
 =1-\eta(s+1/2).
 }
\tag{L-28009.8}

Therefore

\[
 \widehat G_j(s)
 ={[1-\eta(s+1/2)]^j\over s^2},
\tag{L-28009.9}

in agreement with the central-cascade Dirichlet calculation.

## 6. Constant plus exponentially decaying error

Write

\[
 C(t)=\rho+E(t),
 \qquad |E(t)|\le e^{-t/2}.
\]

For `j>=2`, binomial convolution and differentiation give the exact expansion

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 G_j(t)={}&\rho^jt
 +j\rho^{j-1}\int_0^tE(u)du\\
 &+\sum_{k=2}^{j}
  {j\choose k}\rho^{j-k}
  D^{k-2}(E^{*k})(t).
 \end{aligned}}
\tag{L-28009.10}

The main term is positive and decreasing geometrically in the stage index.  All
sign changes lie in explicit convolution derivatives of one exponentially
small staircase error.

## 7. Correct analytic closing target

The parent DCCS proposal can now be replaced, on the continuum side, by the
concrete variation theorem

\[
 \boxed{
 \sum_{j\le\log_2X+O(1)}
 \int_0^{\log X}
 e^{-t/2}\,[ -G_j'(t)]_+dt
 =X^{o(1)}.
 }
\tag{L-28009.11}

A finite-lattice theorem must add the exact `-1` commutator, but may use
(L-28009.10) as its continuum ledger.

The product-six refutation says that the left side is not identically zero.
Equation (L-28009.10) says it is generated by a single exponentially decaying
error rather than by an arbitrary signed packet.

No estimate of the strength (L-28009.11) is proved here.  The eta Laplace symbol
shows that an all-stage subpower theorem remains RH-sensitive.
