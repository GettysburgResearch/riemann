## 1. The resonant budget

Let the test support length be `L`, so the imported explicit formula uses prime powers up to

\[
X=e^L.
\]

Suppose an aliased construction uses prime periods

\[
P_p=\log p,
\qquad p\le Y,
\]

and let `g_{p,r}` denote any nonnegative overlap weight attached to the exact resonance

\[
rP_p=\log(p^r).
\]

For every compact window supported in an interval of length `L`, the autocorrelation/overlap weight is bounded by its zero-shift mass; after the standard normalization it is enough here to assume

\[
0\le g_{p,r}\le L.
\tag{L-90403.1}
\]

The largest possible exact-resonant prime-power diagonal built from this bank is therefore

\[
D_{\mathrm{res}}(L,Y)
 :=\sum_{p\le Y}\sum_{r\ge1}
 \frac{\Lambda(p^r)^2}{p^r}g_{p,r}.
\tag{L-90403.2}
\]

## 2. Uniform upper bound

Since `Lambda(p^r)=log p`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
D_{\mathrm{res}}(L,Y)
&\le L\sum_{p\le Y}(\log p)^2\sum_{r\ge1}p^{-r}\\
&=L\sum_{p\le Y}\frac{(\log p)^2}{p-1}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{L-90403.3}
\]

For every prime `p>=2`, `(p-1)^{-1}<=2p^{-1}`. Hence

\[
\sum_{p\le Y}\frac{(\log p)^2}{p-1}
\le 2\sum_{p\le Y}\frac{(\log p)^2}{p}
\le 2\sum_{n\le Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\log n}{n}.
\tag{L-90403.4}
\]

The Chebyshev–Mertens estimate used upstream gives

\[
\sum_{n\le Y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\log n}{n}
 =\frac12(\log Y)^2+O(\log Y).
\tag{L-90403.5}
\]

Consequently

\[
\boxed{
D_{\mathrm{res}}(L,Y)
 \ll L(\log Y)^2.
}
\tag{L-90403.6}
\]

## 3. Comparison with the bandwidth-one main diagonal

The full Montgomery prime diagonal at support length `L` is

\[
D_{\mathrm{full}}(L)
 =\sum_{n\le e^L}\frac{\Lambda(n)^2}{n}(L-\log n)
 =\frac{L^3}{6}+O(L^2).
\tag{L-90403.7}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\frac{D_{\mathrm{res}}(L,Y)}{D_{\mathrm{full}}(L)}
 \ll \left(\frac{\log Y}{L}\right)^2.
}
\tag{L-90403.8}
\]

If

\[
\log Y=o(L),
\]

then

\[
\boxed{D_{\mathrm{res}}(L,Y)=o(L^3).}
\tag{L-90403.9}
\]

Thus any fixed finite bank, any fixed collection of Euler towers, and more generally any subpolynomial bank

\[
Y=X^{o(1)}
\]

cannot alter the leading two-trace constant through its exact resonant diagonal alone.

The dyadic, Q2, Q4, and factor-64 filters in the live full-RH branches use a fixed finite set of periods generated by `log 2`. In the zeta-23 proportion architecture, their exact resonances lie only on the tower `2^r`, whose total diagonal budget is `O(L)`, versus the `Theta(L^3)` Montgomery diagonal.

## 5. What would be large enough

To obtain a leading-order exact-resonant diagonal from prime periods, (L-90403.8) requires

\[
\log Y\asymp L,
\]

for example

\[
Y=X^\vartheta
\]

with fixed `vartheta>0`. Such a construction needs a growing bank involving exponentially many prime periods on the support scale. Its alias tax, finite-grid dimension, and off-diagonal prime interactions all become leading-order objects and must be analyzed together.

A gain cannot come from one clever fixed radix.

## 6. Scope firewall

This lemma bounds only the **exact resonant diagonal**. It does not rule out:

- near resonances within `O(1/H)`;
- coherent cross terms between different periods;
- cancellation between the smooth alias tax and the oscillatory prime density;
- nonstationary atoms;
- a growing bank with `log Y comparable to L`.

Any claim that all aliasing is useless would require those terms and is not made here.
