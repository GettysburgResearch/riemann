## 1. A rational test family with one safe pole

Fix

\[
 b>\frac43,
 \qquad
 w_b=b^{-1}<\frac34.
\]

Define the Möbius coordinate

\[
 z_b(\lambda)
 =\frac{\lambda(b-1)}{b-\lambda}.
\tag{L-91012.1}
\]

It maps `[0,1]` monotonically onto `[0,1]`. For `n>=0`, put

\[
 \boxed{
 R_{n,b}(\lambda)
 =T_n\bigl(2z_b(\lambda)-1\bigr),
 }
\tag{L-91012.2}
\]

where `T_n` is the Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind. Then

\[
 \boxed{
 |R_{n,b}(\lambda)|\le1
 \qquad(0\le\lambda\le1).
 }
\tag{L-91012.3}
\]

Therefore the universal beta background obeys

\[
 \boxed{
 Q_\nu[R_{n,b}]
 \le\nu([0,1])=\frac18.
 }
\tag{L-91012.4}
\]

The test is rational in `lambda`, with one pole at `lambda=b` of order `n`.

## 2. Exact amplification of an off-line depth

For a target pair of depth `0<y<1/2`, put

\[
 \lambda_y=\frac1{1-y^2}<\frac43<b.
\]

Then

\[
 X_{b,y}
 :=2z_b(\lambda_y)-1
 =2\frac{\lambda_y(b-1)}{b-\lambda_y}-1>1,
\tag{L-91012.5}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{
 R_{n,b}(\lambda_y)
 =\cosh\left(n\operatorname{arccosh}X_{b,y}\right).
 }
\tag{L-91012.6}
\]

The matching pair contribution to the rational quadratic form is exactly

\[
 \boxed{
 Q_{\rm pair}[R_{n,b}]
 =-4my^2(1-y^2)^{-3}
  \cosh^2\left(n\operatorname{arccosh}X_{b,y}\right).
 }
\tag{L-91012.7}
\]

Thus a single safe pole produces exponential target amplification while the positive `[0,1]` background remains uniformly bounded.

## 3. The sharp safe-boundary limit

Let `b` decrease to `4/3`. A direct calculation gives

\[
 \lim_{b\downarrow4/3}X_{b,y}
 =\frac{1+4y^2}{1-4y^2}
 =\cosh\left(2\operatorname{artanh}(2y)\right).
\tag{L-91012.8}
\]

Hence

\[
 \boxed{
 \lim_{b\downarrow4/3}
 \operatorname{arccosh}X_{b,y}
 =2\operatorname{artanh}(2y).
 }
\tag{L-91012.9}
\]

Against a background of size `ell_x`, the formal target degree is therefore

\[
 \boxed{
 n_{\rm rat}(x,y)
 \sim
 \frac{\log\ell_x}
 {4\operatorname{artanh}(2y)}.
 }
\tag{L-91012.10}
\]

For shallow pairs this is

\[
 n_{\rm rat}(x,y)
 \sim\frac{\log\ell_x}{8y},
\]

improving the optimal polynomial-Christoffel scale of `L-91011` by a factor two. As `y` approaches `1/2`, the rational amplification exponent diverges because the target pole approaches the safe Euler boundary itself.

## 4. Exact evaluation from one absolutely convergent Euler sample

Since `R_(n,b)^2` has only one finite pole, there are explicit coefficients `d_0,...,d_(2n)` such that

\[
 R_{n,b}(\lambda)^2
 =d_0+
 \sum_{j=1}^{2n}\frac{d_j}{(b-\lambda)^j}.
\tag{L-91012.11}
\]

For every moment functional generated by `A_x`,

\[
 \int(1-w\lambda)^{-j}d\mu_x(\lambda)
 =\frac1{(j-1)!}
 \frac{d^{j-1}}{dw^{j-1}}
 \left[w^{j-1}\mathcal A_x(w)\right].
\tag{L-91012.12}
\]

Therefore

\[
 \boxed{
 \begin{aligned}
 Q_x[R_{n,b}]
 ={}&d_0a_0(x)\\
 &+\sum_{j=1}^{2n}
 d_jb^{-j}\frac1{(j-1)!}
 \left.
 \frac{d^{j-1}}{dw^{j-1}}
 \left[w^{j-1}\mathcal A_x(w)\right]
 \right|_{w=1/b}.
 \end{aligned}
 }
\tag{L-91012.13}
\]

The square-root sample associated with `w_b` is

\[
 r_b=\sqrt{1-b^{-1}}>\frac12,
\]

so every derivative in (L-91012.13) is a finite algebraic combination of derivatives of

\[
 \mathscr X\left(\frac12+ix+r_b\right)
\]

and of the fixed sample at `3/2+ix`. Both lie in `Re(s)>1`; every finite rational test is therefore computable from absolutely convergent prime-power series plus explicit rational/polygamma terms.

This is a new way to spend safe Euler information: high nonlinear amplification without moving any zeta sample into the critical strip.

## 5. Why this is not already a proof

The safe margin

\[
 r_b-\frac12
\]

tends to zero as `b` decreases to `4/3`. High derivatives at `w_b`, or equivalently the partial fractions in (L-91012.13), become badly conditioned at exactly the rate needed to resolve a pole near the annular boundary.

Thus the construction supplies an exact accelerator and a finite safe-Euler certificate family, but not an unconditional sign theorem. A conclusion-producing argument must control the complete rational Pick quadratic uniformly as

```text
b -> 4/3 from above,
n -> infinity,
and x -> infinity
```

in a coupled regime.

The natural next theorem is a rational analogue of `T-91005` in a basis adapted to `z_b`, with estimates uniform up to the safe boundary. Zolotarev or de Branges-type rational extremals may improve on the one-pole Chebyshev family, but no such closure is asserted here.
