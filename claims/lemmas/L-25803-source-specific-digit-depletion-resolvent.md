## 1. Top source and nilpotent residual

Retain

\[
r_V=\varepsilon-\mathbf1*\mu_V
\]

and

\[
T_{K,V}=\Lambda*r_V^{*(K-1)}.
\tag{L-25803.1}
\]

Every nonzero coefficient of `r_V^K` is supported above `(V+1)^K`. Therefore,
coefficientwise for `n<=X<=V^K`,

\[
\boxed{
r_V*T_{K,V}
=\Lambda*r_V^{*K}=0.}
\tag{L-25803.2}
\]

This is the source-specific nilpotence used below.

## 2. Radix depletion

Fix an integer `Q>=2` and put

\[
a_Q=\mathbf1*(\varepsilon-\delta_Q).
\]

Define the depleted source

\[
\boxed{
Z_{K,V,Q}=a_Q*T_{K,V}.}
\tag{L-25803.3}
\]

Using `mu_V*1=epsilon-r_V`,

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mu_V*Z_{K,V,Q}
&=\mu_V*\mathbf1*(\varepsilon-\delta_Q)*T_{K,V}\\
&=(\varepsilon-r_V)*(\varepsilon-\delta_Q)*T_{K,V}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The term containing `r_V*T_(K,V)` vanishes by (L-25803.2). Hence

\[
\boxed{
(\varepsilon-\delta_Q)*T_{K,V}
=\mu_V*Z_{K,V,Q}
\qquad(n\le X).}
\tag{L-25803.4}
\]

Convolving with the pointwise-finite geometric inverse

\[
H_Q=(\varepsilon-\delta_Q)^{-1}
=\sum_{j\ge0}\delta_{Q^j}
\]

gives the exact recovery

\[
\boxed{
T_{K,V}
=H_Q*\mu_V*Z_{K,V,Q}
\qquad(n\le X).}
\tag{L-25803.5}
\]

This identity is unavailable for a generic source. It uses exactly one fact:
`r_V*T_(K,V)=0` on the finite coefficient range.

## 3. Reciprocal-free dipole

The elementary identity

\[
\mathbf1*\Lambda=\ell
\tag{L-25803.6}
\]

implies

\[
\boxed{
Z_{K,V,Q}
=(\varepsilon-\delta_Q)*\ell*r_V^{*(K-1)}.}
\tag{L-25803.7}
\]

Thus `Z` is a current-scale source minus a multiplicatively shifted copy of the
same source.

In Dirichlet-series notation, put

\[
R_V(s)=1-\zeta(s)M_V(s).
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
Z_{K,V,Q}(s)
=-(1-Q^{-s})\zeta'(s)R_V(s)^{K-1}.}
\tag{L-25803.8}
\]

There is no reciprocal zeta factor in (L-25803.8). Every nontrivial-zero pole
of the top source has been canceled before any estimate.

This does not make `Z` automatically small: its finite truncation boundary can
still carry the complete arithmetic obstruction. It makes the remaining target
a finite signed transport problem rather than a generic zero-free inverse.

## 4. Physical positive-kernel realization

Let `Q_T` be the square-root normalized logarithmic field generated by
`T_(K,V)` and a fixed compact safe window. Let `C_Q` be the positive kernel of
`L-25802`. Then

\[
\left(\partial+\frac12\right)(\mathcal C_Q*Q_T)
\]

is exactly the physical field generated by `Z_(K,V,Q)`.

Thus the depleted source has two simultaneous descriptions:

1. the reciprocal-free signed dipole (L-25803.7);
2. the derivative of a convolution with a nonnegative causal kernel.

The proposal uses the first for source transport and the second for the
reviewed two-frequency physical Gram.

## 5. Scale-adapted radix

Fix one constant

\[
0<\delta_0<\frac13
\]

and for each fixed packet order choose

\[
m_K=\lfloor\delta_0K\rfloor,
\qquad
Q=V^{m_K}.
\tag{L-25803.9}
\]

Then

\[
\log Q
=\left(\delta_0+O(1/K)\right)J.
\tag{L-25803.10}
\]

The negative member of the dipole is therefore at a fixed smaller logarithmic
scale. The geometric inverse has normalized norm

\[
{1\over1-Q^{-1/2}}=1+o_K(1)
\]

as `J` tends to infinity.

Recovery through `mu_V` costs at most

\[
\sum_{a\le V}{|\mu(a)|\over\sqrt a}
\le V^{1/2+o(1)}
\]

in amplitude, hence

\[
\boxed{
V^{1+o(1)}
=\exp\left[\left({1\over K}+o_K(1)\right)J\right]}
\tag{L-25803.11}
\]

in energy.

Consequently, a subexponential lower-scale estimate for the depleted source
would give the required vanishing `1/K` loss for the top source.
