## 1. Special finite matrix setting

Let `H_N` be the finite CCM Fourier space. Let

\[
 D=D^*
 \tag{L-15105.1}
\]

be the diagonal scaling operator, let `eta` be the Dirichlet-kernel boundary
vector, and let `Gamma` be the inversion-parity involution. Assume

\[
 D\Gamma=-\Gamma D,
 \qquad
 \Gamma\eta=\eta.
 \tag{L-15105.2}
\]

Let `Q=Q^*` commute with `Gamma` and satisfy the CCM special commutator identity

\[
 \boxed{
 [D,Q]=|\beta\rangle\langle\eta|
       -|\eta\rangle\langle\beta|}
 \tag{L-15105.3}
\]

for one vector `beta`. The finite Weil matrices have this structure in the
imported source normalization.

Choose any real shift `alpha` for which

\[
 B=Q+\alpha I\succ0.
 \tag{L-15105.4}
\]

Define

\[
 s=\langle\eta,B^{-1}\eta\rangle>0,
 \qquad
 c=s^{-1},
 \tag{L-15105.5}
\]

and

\[
 \boxed{
 \xi=\frac{B^{-1}\eta}{s},
 \qquad
 T=B-c|\eta\rangle\langle\eta|.}
 \tag{L-15105.6}
\]

Then:

1. `T` is positive semidefinite;
2. `ker T=span{xi}`;
3. `⟨eta,xi⟩=1`;
4. `xi` is even;
5. `T` remains a CCM special matrix, with

   \[
   \boxed{
   [D,T]=|\beta-cD\eta\rangle\langle\eta|
          -|\eta\rangle\langle\beta-cD\eta|.}
   \tag{L-15105.7}
   \]

Consequently, under the imported CCM finite special-matrix theorem, the
Fourier--Mellin transform of `xi` is entire and all its zeros are real.

No simplicity assumption on the lowest eigenvalue of `Q` is required.

## 2. Proof

### Positivity and kernel

For every `x`, Cauchy--Schwarz in the `B` inner product gives

\[
 |\langle\eta,x\rangle|^2
 =|\langle B^{-1/2}\eta,B^{1/2}x\rangle|^2
 \le s\langle x,Bx\rangle.
\]

Therefore

\[
 \langle x,Tx\rangle
 =\langle x,Bx\rangle-c|\langle\eta,x\rangle|^2
 \ge0.
 \tag{L-15105.8}
\]

Equality in Cauchy--Schwarz holds precisely when

\[
 x\in\mathbb C B^{-1}\eta.
\]

Thus `T` has one-dimensional kernel, generated by `xi`. The normalization in
(L-15105.6) gives `⟨eta,xi⟩=1`.

### Parity

Because `Q` commutes with `Gamma`, so do `B` and `B^{-1}`. Since `eta` is even,
`xi` is even.

### Special commutator

The shift commutes with `D`, and

\[
 [D,|\eta\rangle\langle\eta|]
 =|D\eta\rangle\langle\eta|
  -|\eta\rangle\langle D\eta|.
\]

Subtracting this from (L-15105.3) proves (L-15105.7). The imported CCM theorem
now applies to the positive semidefinite special matrix `T` with one-dimensional
even kernel and boundary normalization one. QED.

## 3. Why the construction is unconditional at every finite level

The finite Weil matrix is lower bounded. Any directed lower bound

\[
 Q\succeq-LI
\]

allows the exact choice

\[
 \alpha>L.
\]

Thus one obtains a real-rooted finite transform at every finite
`(lambda,N)` without:

- assuming RH;
- assuming `Q` is positive;
- identifying its natural lowest eigenvector;
- proving a natural spectral gap;
- relying on a floating eigensolver.

The vector is obtained by one positive-definite linear solve followed by an
exact sharp rank-one subtraction.

## 5. Resolvent diagonal criterion for RH

Let

\[
 \xi_j
 =\frac{(Q_{\lambda_j}^{N_j}+\alpha_jI)^{-1}\eta_j}
        {\langle\eta_j,
        (Q_{\lambda_j}^{N_j}+\alpha_jI)^{-1}\eta_j\rangle}
 \tag{L-15105.9}
\]

be the vectors above. Every `hat xi_j` has only real zeros.

Suppose there are nonzero real scalars `a_j`, parameters

\[
 \lambda_j\to\infty,
 \qquad N_j\to\infty,
 \qquad \tau_j\nearrow\frac12,
\]

such that, for the exact Hermite radical target `k` of `L-15101`,

\[
 \boxed{
 \|a_j\xi_j-k\|_{\tau_j}\longrightarrow0.}
 \tag{L-15105.10}
\]

Then the Hardy-strip estimate and Hurwitz argument of `T-14301` imply RH.

This criterion has no natural-ground or gap hypothesis. Its sole missing theorem
is **boundary-resolvent selection**: choose the scalar regularization `alpha_j`
so that the normalized boundary resolvents converge to the `Xi` radical.

## 6. Continuum interpretation

At fixed support, the finite vector is the normalized Riesz representer of the
boundary functional in the shifted Weil inner product:

\[
 \langle (Q+\alpha I)\xi,f\rangle
 =c\langle\eta,f\rangle.
 \tag{L-15105.11}
\]

The large-support target is therefore not a natural isolated ground state. It is
a boundary-selected resolvent direction. This is potentially robust against the
near-zero clustering caused by the infinite-dimensional global Weil radical.

The right analytic question is whether a continuum shifted resolvent, after a
suitable scalar normalization and a support/shift diagonal limit, converges to
`k=E(h)`.

## 7. Gap audit

- The real-zero conclusion imports the exact scope of the CCM special-matrix
  theorem. That theorem and the identification of `eta` with boundary evaluation
  require independent normalization review.
- Positivity of `B` is essential. Merely invertible indefinite `B` does not make
  the sharp rank-one subtraction positive.
- The construction produces many unconditional real-rooted functions. It does
  not identify their limit.
- Sending `alpha` to zero can amplify every near-radical direction. The boundary
  functional may select a different radical vector from `k`; this is the main
  unresolved selection problem.
- A scalar shift is only one regularization parameter. More general diagonal
  operators commuting with `D` preserve the special commutator, but target
  fitting must not be confused with a proof of positivity.
- No claim that (L-15105.10) holds is made here. Therefore this lemma does not
  prove RH.
