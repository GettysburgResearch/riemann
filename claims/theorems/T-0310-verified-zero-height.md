# T-0310 — RH verified through height \(3\cdot10^{12}\)

## Statement

Let
\[
 \rho=\beta+i\gamma
\]
be a nontrivial zero of the Riemann zeta function.  Platt and Trudgian proved,
using rigorous interval arithmetic, that
\[
 0<\gamma\le 3\cdot10^{12}
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \beta=\frac12,
\]
and that every zero in this range is simple.

By conjugation, the same conclusion holds for
\[
 0<|\gamma|\le 3\cdot10^{12}.
\]

Consequently, no rectangle disjoint from the critical line and contained below
this height can contain a nontrivial zeta zero.

## Source

Dave Platt and Tim Trudgian, *The Riemann hypothesis is true up to
\(3\cdot10^{12}\)*, Bulletin of the London Mathematical Society 53 (2021),
792--797, DOI 10.1112/blms.12460, arXiv:2004.09765.

Inspection level: the publisher abstract and arXiv abstract were inspected.
They explicitly state the interval-arithmetic rigor, the height, the critical-
line conclusion, and simplicity.

## Motivation

This theorem is a hard search boundary.  It prevents agents from spending
resources on direct off-line reconnaissance in a region already rigorously
excluded.  Combined with T-0308, it also rules out every nonhyperbolic zeta
Jensen polynomial of degree at most \(9\cdot10^{24}\), for every shift.

## Proof status

Imported certified-computation theorem; the computation and certificate were
not independently reproduced in this repository.

The extension from positive to negative ordinates is elementary: because
\(\zeta(\overline{s})=\overline{\zeta(s)}\), a zero
\(\beta-i\gamma\) is the complex conjugate of a zero \(\beta+i\gamma\).
Simplicity is preserved by conjugation.

## Gap audit

- This theorem does not prove RH.
- It does not justify extrapolating beyond \(3\cdot10^{12}\).
- A new direct search below this bound is useful only as an independent
  reproduction or software-validation exercise, not as counterexample
  discovery.
- A box whose vertical range crosses the endpoint must treat the portion above
  the endpoint separately.
- The repository has not audited the original interval implementation.
