## Fixed universal window

Let `F_*` be the nonnegative `C_c^infinity(2,4)` convolution-square window of
`L-15405`. Its Laplace transform is

\[
 \widehat F_{*,L}(z)
 =e^{-2z}
 \prod_{j=1}^\infty
 \left({1-e^{-2^{-j}z}\over2^{-j}z}\right)^2,
 \tag{T-15403.1}
\]

and

\[
 \widehat F_{*,L}(z)\ne0
 \qquad(\operatorname{Re}z>0).
 \tag{T-15403.2}
\]

Put

\[
 c_*=\widehat F_{*,L}(1/2)>0.
 \tag{T-15403.3}
\]

For `a>=2`, define the single terminal prime statistic

\[
\boxed{
 D_*(a)=
 \sum_{e^{2a-4}\le n\le e^{2a-2}}
 {\Lambda(n)\over\sqrt n}F_*(2a-\log n)
 -c_*e^a.}
 \tag{T-15403.4}
\]

Only prime powers in the displayed fixed-ratio terminal annulus occur.

## Main theorem

The following are equivalent.

1. The Riemann Hypothesis is true.
2. The one real function `D_*` is bounded:
   \[
    \boxed{\sup_{a\ge2}|D_*(a)|<\infty.}
    \tag{T-15403.5}
   \]
3. The same boundedness holds only along all real `x=2a>=4` translations of the
   one fixed window `F_*`.

### Proof

RH implies boundedness by `T-15402`.

Conversely, let `mathcal T` be the pole-subtracted prime distribution from
`T-15402`, and write

\[
 Y(x)=\langle\mathcal T,F_*(x-\cdot)\rangle.
 \tag{T-15403.6}
\]

For `x>=4`, `Y(x)=D_*(x/2)`. Boundedness on the right implies that the Laplace
transform of `Y` is holomorphic on `Re z>0`. On the initial domain
`Re z>1/2`, the convolution theorem gives

\[
\boxed{
 \mathcal L Y(z)=
 \widehat F_{*,L}(z)
 \left[
  -{\zeta'\over\zeta}\left(z+{1\over2}\right)
  -{1\over z-1/2}
 \right].}
 \tag{T-15403.7}
\]

The first factor is zero-free in `Re z>0`. Therefore the bracket extends
holomorphically to that half-plane. It has a pole at every
`z=rho-1/2` with `Re rho>1/2`; hence no such zero exists. The functional
equation rules out the reflected half and proves RH. QED.

## Explicit RH-valid bound

The criterion can be made fail-closed rather than merely qualitative. Under RH,
`T-15402` gives

\[
 D_*(a)=
 -\sum_\rho e^{2ia\gamma}
 \widehat F_{*,L}(i\gamma)
 +E_{triv}(a),
 \tag{T-15403.8}
\]

where `rho=1/2+i gamma` and the trivial-zero term decays exponentially for
`a>=2`.

Let `N_0(T)` be any proved upper bound for the number of nontrivial zeros with
`|gamma|<=T`, counted with multiplicity. Define

\[
\boxed{
 B_*=B_{triv}+
 \sum_{k=0}^\infty
 \bigl(N_0(k+1)-N_0(k)\bigr)
 \sup_{k\le|t|\le k+1}
 |\widehat F_{*,L}(it)|.}
 \tag{T-15403.9}
\]

The series converges effectively because `L-15405` gives

\[
 |\widehat F_{*,L}(it)|
 \le\exp[-c(\log(2+|t|))^2]
 \tag{T-15403.10}
\]

for one explicit `c>0`, while every standard zero-count majorant is
`O(T log T)`.

Thus RH implies the uniform computable inequality

\[
\boxed{|D_*(a)|\le B_*\qquad(a\ge2).}
 \tag{T-15403.11}
\]

A directed finite value whose interval lies outside `[-B_*,B_*]` is an
unconditional RH-disproof certificate after independent review of the explicit
formula, zero-count majorant, profile evaluator, and prime manifest.

The bound need not be sharp. Under false RH, `D_*` is unbounded because the
zero-free transform cannot cancel any right-half-plane pole; therefore every
finite valid `B_*` is eventually violated.

## Wider meaning

This theorem reduces the universal quantifier in `T-15402` to one explicit,
positive, smooth, compact window. The remaining statement is no longer an
abstract operator convergence condition:

```text
one fixed multiplicative prime annulus,
one fixed weight profile,
one pole subtraction,
one uniform scalar bound.
```

It is still equivalent to RH, not a proof of that bound.

## Gap audit

- The bounded-convolution Laplace theorem requires a precise distributional
  statement and is a priority for independent review.
- `B_*` depends on a rigorously cited zero-count majorant and outward evaluation
  of the infinite product.
- A large midpoint outside a heuristic bound is not a certificate.
- Boundedness on an integer subsequence of `a` is not the same as boundedness for
  all real translations unless a separate interpolation theorem is proved.
- The theorem offers a one-window positive/negative resolution target. It does
  not establish the uniform bound and therefore does not prove RH.
