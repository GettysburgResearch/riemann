## 2. Sharp class-restricted supply: the \(\mathcal H\)-narrowing computed (PROVED via pinned literature)

Hall's theorem (GS Decay (1.5)): \(f(p)\in D\) convex \(\ni1\) \(\Rightarrow\) \(\tfrac1x|\sum_{n\le x}f(n)|\ll\exp(-\kappa(D)\sum_{p\le x}\tfrac{1-\mathrm{Re}f(p)}p)\), and **κ(D) is optimal for every D** ("it cannot be replaced by any larger value"). For \(\mathcal H\), \(D=[-1,1]\): \(\kappa=0.32867416320\ldots\) (GS Decay, Example 1, \(m=2\)).

**Consequences.** (i) The sharp distance-uniform floor over \(\mathcal H\) is \((\log x)^{-2\kappa+o(1)}=(\log x)^{-0.65735+o(1)}\) at the distance cap — *polylog with a hard exponent \(<1\)*: restricting from \(\{|f|\le1\}\) to the real class \(\mathcal H\) makes the sharp distance-uniform exponent **worse** than the complex one-log scale (real f cannot exploit \(t\)-rotations; conjugation symmetry repels \(n^{it}\)-pretenders, and Hall's optimality bars any further distance-level gain). (ii) *Pretender-exclusion arithmetic:* granting free exclusion of all characters/\(n^{it}\) to maximal distance moves the complex primary term to \((1+2LL)e^{-2LL}\asymp LL/\log^2x\), but the class floor stays \((\log x)^{-0.657}\) (Hall, sharp) and the GHS secondary term \(LL/\log x\) stays regardless: **no Siegel-type narrowing of \(\mathcal H\) shrinks the deficit below a power; measured deficit vs the \(\varepsilon=0.1\) demand: \(4.5\times10^1\) at \(10^6\), \(7.1\times10^3\) at \(10^{12}\), \(8.1\times10^6\) at \(10^{20}\), \(5.1\times10^{14}\) at \(10^{40}\) — i.e. \(X^{0.4-o(1)}\), and \(X^{1/2-o(1)}\) as \(\varepsilon\to0\).** Stage 1 proved membership needs no Siegel condition; Stage 2/3 proves a Siegel condition would also buy nothing: the lever exists but has nothing to lift.

2. *(signed assembly neutral)* exploiting the sign structure changes demand by \(O(\log^2X)\) additive (moat \(+\) floors) and bounded constants (coherence, §3b); its class-uniform use is refuted at power scale (§3b/c);
