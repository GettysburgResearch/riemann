## 1. Normalized safe-line sequence

For the scalar hierarchy of `T-90903`, put

\[
a_k(x)=\frac{\mathfrak S_k(x)}{(k+2)!}.
\tag{T-91001.1}
\]

Under RH, the zero expansion becomes

\[
\boxed{
 a_k(x)
 =\sum_{\gamma}
 \frac{2m_\gamma(\gamma-x)^2}
      {(1+(\gamma-x)^2)^3}
 \left(\frac1{1+(\gamma-x)^2}\right)^k.
}
\tag{T-91001.2}
\]

Hence, for each real `x`, `(a_k(x))` is the moment sequence of the positive
measure

\[
\boxed{
\nu_x
=\sum_\gamma
 \frac{2m_\gamma(\gamma-x)^2}
      {(1+(\gamma-x)^2)^3}
 \delta_{(1+(\gamma-x)^2)^{-1}}
}
\tag{T-91001.3}
\]

supported on `[0,1]`.

## 2. Hausdorff criterion

A real sequence is a Hausdorff moment sequence exactly when all forward
differences have the alternating sign

\[
(-1)^m\Delta^m a_k\ge0
\qquad(k,m\ge0).
\tag{T-91001.4}
\]

Therefore

\[
\boxed{
\mathrm{RH}
\iff
(-1)^m\Delta^m a_k(x)\ge0
\quad
(k,m\ge0,\ x\in\mathbb R).
}
\tag{T-91001.5}
\]

The forward implication is (T-91001.2). The reverse implication already follows
from the case `m=0`, namely `a_k(x)>=0` for every `k,x`, by `T-90903`.
Rational centres suffice by continuity.

This formulation adds genuinely new finite constraints. Under RH, for every
`d,q>=0`, the Hankel and `[0,1]` localizing matrices

\[
\boxed{
H_d^{(q)}(x)=(a_{i+j+q}(x))_{0\le i,j\le d}\succeq0,
}
\tag{T-91001.6}
\]

\[
\boxed{
H_d^{(q)}(x)-H_d^{(q+1)}(x)
=(a_{i+j+q}(x)-a_{i+j+q+1}(x))_{i,j}\succeq0
}
\tag{T-91001.7}
\]

by the exact Gram identities

\[
 c^*H_d^{(q)}c
 =\int_0^1 r^q\left|\sum_{j=0}^dc_jr^j\right|^2d\nu_x(r),
\]

\[
 c^*(H_d^{(q)}-H_d^{(q+1)})c
 =\int_0^1 r^q(1-r)
 \left|\sum_{j=0}^dc_jr^j\right|^2d\nu_x(r).
\]

## 3. Ordinary generating function and the forbidden pole

Define

\[
G_x(z)=\sum_{k\ge0}a_k(x)z^k.
\tag{T-91001.8}
\]

Under RH,

\[
\boxed{
G_x(z)=\int_{[0,1]}\frac{d\nu_x(r)}{1-rz},
\qquad |z|<1,
}
\tag{T-91001.9}
\]

so `G_x` is a Markov--Stieltjes function. Its singularities lie on the real ray
`[1,infinity)` and all its diagonal Padé approximants are generated by positive
Jacobi matrices.

If RH is false and `(t,y)` is a terminal pair, then at `x=t` its contribution to
`a_k` is

\[
-\frac{4my^2}{(1-y^2)^3}
 \left(\frac1{1-y^2}\right)^k,
\tag{T-91001.10}
\]

so `G_t` has a negative-residue pole at

\[
\boxed{z_0=1-y^2\in(3/4,1).}
\tag{T-91001.11}
\]

Every nuisance pole has strictly larger real part by terminality. Thus false RH
is detected either by a negative scalar coefficient, by a finite Hausdorff
difference, or by a finite Hankel/localizing failure.

## 4. Exponential Borel criterion

Let

\[
\mathfrak B_x(u)
=\sum_{k\ge0}\mathfrak S_k(x)\frac{u^k}{k!}.
\]

By `L-91001`, under RH

\[
\mathfrak B_x(u)
=\sum_\gamma
 \frac{4m_\gamma(\gamma-x)^2}
      {(1+(\gamma-x)^2-u)^3}
\ge0
\qquad(0\le u<1).
\tag{T-91001.12}
\]

Conversely a terminal false-RH pair makes `mathfrak B_t(u)` negative near its
pole `u=1-y^2`. Hence

\[
\boxed{
\mathrm{RH}
\iff
\mathfrak B_x(u)\ge0
\quad(x\in\mathbb R,\ 0\le u<1).
}
\tag{T-91001.13}
\]

Equivalently, with `r=sqrt(1-u)`,

\[
\boxed{
\mathrm{RH}
\iff
-\Re\mathcal D_{r^2}\mathscr X(1/2+ix)\ge0
\quad(x\in\mathbb R,\ 0<r\le1).
}
\tag{T-91001.14}
\]

The expression is explicitly Euler-computable for `r>1/2`; the interval
`0<r<=1/2` is the exact RH-bearing continuation.

## 5. Finite arithmetic witnesses

Every `a_k(x)` is an absolutely convergent prime-power series at
`3/2+ix`, plus explicit rational/polygamma terms. Therefore every finite
Hausdorff difference and every finite matrix in (T-91001.6)--(T-91001.7) has an
elementary directed prime tail.

Consequently

```text
false RH
 -> terminal pair
 -> rational centre x
 -> finite order or finite matrix size
 -> finite prime-power cutoff
 -> strict negative scalar/eigenvalue interval.
```

No negative Riemann-data instance is claimed.

## 6. Why this is more than another scalar criterion

The previous hierarchy asked for infinitely many unrelated inequalities
`mathfrak S_k(x)>=0`. The Hausdorff formulation says they must be the moments of
one positive measure on one compact interval. It therefore couples all orders by:

```text
all finite differences;
all Hankel minors;
all [0,1]-localizing minors;
Stieltjes continued fractions;
positive Jacobi parameters;
Padé poles constrained to [1,infinity).
```

A proof may now target one compact moment problem rather than one sign at a time.
The missing theorem remains RH-equivalent.
