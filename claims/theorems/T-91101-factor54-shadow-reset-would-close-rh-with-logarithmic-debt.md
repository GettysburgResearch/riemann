## 1. The conclusion-producing score

For an endpoint \(X\), let
\[
 \mathfrak L_X(d)
 =J_\Lambda(X)-\mathcal S_X(d)
\]
be the signed seed-score loss of a nonnegative ordinarily feasible carry packing \(d\).

PR #352 proves:
\[
\boxed{
 F_\Lambda(X)\le\mathfrak L_X(d),
}
\tag{T-91101.1}
and
\[
\boxed{
 \mathfrak L_X(d)=o(\log^2X)
 \Longrightarrow
 \mathrm{RH}.
}
\tag{T-91101.2}

Thus it is enough to construct one family of feasible packings with logarithmic score loss.

## 3. Abstract reset hypothesis

A **factor-\(54\) shadow reset** consists, for every sufficiently large \(X\), of:

1. an integer reset scale
   \[
   K_X\le c_0X+C_0;
   \tag{T-91101.4}
   \]
2. a nonnegative endpoint/carry packing \(d_X^{\rm out}\);
3. a residual state at endpoint \(K_X\), denoted \(\mathcal R_X\);
4. a collar score charge \(E_X\).

It must satisfy the following exact properties.

### H1. Source-faithful outer feasibility

The outer packing is nonnegative and consumes no more than the original target. After subtracting it, the residual column capacities dominate the same canonical target at scale \(K_X\), with the residual two-state source \(\mathcal R_X\) lying in the positive cone generated by the equality and reserve states of `L-91108`.

Equivalently, a feasible packing at scale \(K_X\) may be lifted through the reset and added to \(d_X^{\rm out}\) without violating any column at scale \(X\).

### H2. Coefficient-one score recurrence

For every feasible inner packing \(d_{K_X}\), the lifted packing \(d_X\) satisfies
\[
\boxed{
 \mathfrak L_X(d_X)
 \le
 \mathfrak L_{K_X}(d_{K_X})+E_X.
}
\tag{T-91101.5}

No factor larger than one may multiply the inherited score loss.

### H3. Bounded or sublogarithmic collar debt

For some constants \(C_1,A\),
\[
\boxed{
 E_X\le C_1(1+\log\log(3X))^A.
}
\tag{T-91101.6}

The strongest intended theorem has \(E_X=O(1)\). Any fixed polylogarithm of \(\log X\) suffices.

### H4. Finite base

Every endpoint below a fixed \(X_0\) has an explicitly certified feasible packing with finite score loss.

## 5. Why the hypotheses are now finite and local

The preceding lemmas remove the former global ambiguities:

- `L-91101`: all inherited endpoint geometry has rank two;
- `L-91102`: adjacent butterflies are the exact convex-order coordinates and have positive score;
- `L-91105`: every butterfly is a positive two-node seed with a three-integer divisor stencil;
- `L-91106`: the equality correction is positive outside one explicit inner core;
- `L-91107`: the equality density is positive with fixed margin throughout that window;
- `L-91108`: the SHARP output is the positive functional \(L+2R\) of a positive two-state renewal.

Therefore H1--H3 ask only for a finite collar map repeated self-similarly. They do not ask for global positivity of the reciprocal-zeta equality factor, global SHARP, a generic spectral gap, or an unbounded list of independent inequalities.

## 6. Preferred concrete reset

The intended construction is:

```text
1. take the exact equality correction on T >= K_X;
2. express its positive outer part in adjacent butterfly / two-node seed coordinates;
3. repair the finitely many quotient collars by a state-dependent shadow map;
4. recombine equal divisor destinations before taking positive parts;
5. leave one residual (L,R) state at scale K_X;
6. charge only the finite collar score, then recurse.
```

Because one butterfly touches four endpoint rows and three consecutive divisor integers, the collar map has bounded local width. The quotient depth in one reset is at most \(54\). This nominates an exact finite interval/linear certificate rather than an asymptotic prime estimate.
