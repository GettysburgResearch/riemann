## 1. Explicit finite producer

For

\[
w_X(q)=q^{-1/2}\log(X/q),
\]

\[
f_{X,0}=w_X
\]

and iterate the exact finite central residual operator

\[
\boxed{
 f_{X,j+1}(q)
 =\sum_{k\ge1}
 \left[
 f_{X,j}(2kq-1)-f_{X,j}((2k+1)q)
 \right],
}
\tag{T-24508.1}
\]

where all values beyond the current finite endpoint are zero.  The support
halves at each step, so

\[
f_{X,L}=0
\qquad
(L\le1+\lceil\log_2X\rceil).
\tag{T-24508.2}
\]

Define

\[
A_X(n)=\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}
 [f_{X,j}(n)-f_{X,j}(n+1)].
\tag{T-24508.3}
\]

`L-24523` proves exactly

\[
\sum_{n=q}^{X}A_X(n)\chi_n^{\rm c}(q)=w_X(q)
\qquad(2\le q\le X).
\tag{T-24508.4}
\]

Thus every object in the proposal is generated by a terminating elementary
recurrence.  There is no existence oracle.

## 4. Central Bottom-Mass Recurrence (`CBMR`)

A production `CBMR` certificate consists, for every sufficiently large `X`, of
finite data

\[
Y_1,...,Y_R\le\left\lfloor{X+1\over2}\right\rfloor,
\qquad
\theta_1,...,\theta_R\ge0,
\]

and an exact signed source ledger proving

\[
\boxed{
|\mathcal D(X)|
 \le C(1+\log X)^A
    +\sum_{r=1}^{R}\theta_r|\mathcal D(Y_r)|,
}
\tag{CBMR.1}
\]

with absolute constants

\[
\boxed{
\sum_{r=1}^{R}\theta_r\le\theta_*<1.
}
\tag{CBMR.2}
\]

The ledger must be source-bound.  It must explicitly contain:

1. every stopped endpoint and reciprocal-cell boundary;
2. the complete monotone/Hausdorff tree layer cake;
3. every finite Euler/Peano jet and its top remainder;
4. both eta parity siblings before the dyadic filter;
5. the `q=1` term `log X`;
6. every lower endpoint `Y_r`;
7. the exact equality which produces the coefficients `theta_r`;
8. the first `2/3` Mertens mutation.

No ambient triangle inequality may replace this source ledger.

## 5. `CBMR` gives a polylogarithmic scalar

Let

\[
M(X)=1+\max_{2\le Y\le X}|\mathcal D(Y)|.
\]

Equations (CBMR.1)--(CBMR.2) imply

\[
M(X)
 \le C'(1+\log X)^A
    +\theta_*M((X+1)/2).
\tag{T-24508.8}
\]

Iterating at most `O(log X)` times yields

\[
\boxed{
M(X)=O((1+\log X)^{A+1}).
}
\tag{T-24508.9}
\]

Indeed the geometric coefficient sum is bounded by `(1-theta_*)^-1`, and the
logarithm decreases along the recursion.

Thus `CBMR` gives

\[
\mathcal D(X)=O(\log^{A+1}(2X)),
\]

which is stronger than (T-24508.7).

## 6. Completion to RH

By (T-24508.6), the Mellin transform of `mathcal D` is initially

\[
{1\over z^2\zeta(z+1/2)}.
\]

The polylogarithmic estimate makes it holomorphic in `Re(z)>0`.  A nontrivial
zeta zero with real part greater than `1/2` would produce a genuine pole in that
half-plane.  Hence no such zero exists.  Functional-equation symmetry puts all
nontrivial zeros on the critical line.

Therefore

\[
\boxed{\mathrm{CBMR}\Longrightarrow\mathrm{RH}.}
\tag{T-24508.10}
\]
