# R-101100 — Matrix/composition firewalls

## 1. Unsigned norms do not populate the matrix

The absorption proof uses the signed quantity

\[
 b_i=\sum_j\int K_{ij}f_j.
\]

Replacing it by `sum ||K_ij f_j||_1` destroys the cancellation and can turn a
subcritical owner budget into a critical or power-sized estimate.  Thus a
source-blind large sieve is not a valid substitute for either matrix row.

## 2. Spectral radius one is not enough

If `M=(1)` and the error is zero, the inequality `n<=Mn` holds for every
`n>=0` and proves nothing.  In two channels, equality
`bc=(1-a)(1-d)` is equally non-closing.  Strict Perron contraction is a
load-bearing condition.

## 3. A bridge identity is not a sign theorem

The identity

\[
 3G_\beta=C_2JP_2\delta_0-JP_2\mathcal A_\beta-JP_2(D-1)E_2
\]

preserves all source terms, but it does not sign either channel.  Estimating
them separately by absolute values loses the purpose of the bridge.

## 4. Horizon-dependent completion is not a moving Landau detector

A cutoff chosen as a function of `Y` may be used inside a proof of a bound for
the fixed scalar `G_beta`.  It may not itself be declared eventually
nonnegative and inserted into Landau.  L-101102 records the only permitted
composition.

## 5. RH-equivalence is not a reason to discard a node

An RH-equivalent node may still reveal a useful factorization or cross-channel
contraction.  What is forbidden is treating equivalence as proof.  T-101100
retains the quadratic and wavelet nodes precisely because their *interfaces*,
not their individual terminal statements, are complementary.
