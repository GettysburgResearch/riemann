# R-23803 — Direct boundary-charge transport overloads at the root

Claim ID: `R-23803`  
Status: **EXACT REFUTATION OF BCT AS STATED IN T-23802**  
Scope: direct no-double-spend parent-capacity allocation from negative squarefree children to positive parents  
RH status: **unproved**

## 1. Setup

Retain the seed

\[
h_0(t)=\left(e^t-\frac78e^{t/2}-\frac3{16}t e^{t/2}\right)\mathbf 1_{t\ge0}
\]

and the exact parent map of `L-23808`.

For a negative squarefree node `n`, the proposed local routing splits its complete negative trajectory among parents `n/p` with weights

\[
\frac{\log p}{\log n},\qquad p\mid n.
\]

The BCT statement in `T-23802` additionally requires that every favorable parent be charged by no more than the positive trajectory mass actually generated by that parent.

## 2. Prime children force all demand onto the root

Take `n=p` prime. Then `mu(p)=-1` and the only parent is

\[
p/p=1.
\]

The canonical weight is

\[
\frac{\log p}{\log p}=1.
\]

Thus at multiplicative horizon `X=e^t`, the positive root trajectory `h_0(\log X)` must, under BCT as stated, pay at least

\[
\boxed{
D_1(X)=\sum_{p\le X} h_0\!\left(\log\frac Xp\right).
}
\tag{R-23803.1}
\]

No alternative parent or sibling allocation exists for a prime child under the declared edge rule.

## 3. Exact lower bound on root demand

`L-23808` proves the elementary seed reserve

\[
h_0(\log y)\ge \frac y8\qquad(y\ge1).
\]

Hence, restricting (R-23803.1) to primes `p<=sqrt(X)`,

\[
\boxed{
D_1(X)
\ge \frac X8\sum_{p\le\sqrt X}\frac1p.
}
\tag{R-23803.2}
\]

Euler's elementary theorem that the sum of reciprocal primes diverges gives

\[
\sum_{p\le\sqrt X}\frac1p\to\infty.
\]

Therefore

\[
\frac{D_1(X)}X\to\infty
\]

along an unbounded sequence (indeed monotonically through the prime reciprocal partial sums).

## 4. Root capacity is only linear

The available root trajectory is

\[
C_1(X)=h_0(\log X)
=X-\frac78\sqrt X-\frac3{16}\sqrt X\log X
< X.
\tag{R-23803.3}
\]

Combining (R-23803.2) and (R-23803.3), for every sufficiently large `X` with

\[
\sum_{p\le\sqrt X}\frac1p>8,
\]

one has

\[
\boxed{D_1(X)>C_1(X).}
\tag{R-23803.4}
\]

Thus the root is necessarily overspent.

## 5. Consequence

The global no-double-spend Boundary Charge Transport theorem of `T-23802` is false as stated. The obstruction already occurs in the first generation of negative prime children; no deep composite family is needed.

What fails is not the exact local identity

\[
1=\sum_{p\mid n}\frac{\log p}{\log n},
\]

but the attempt to pay every negative trajectory independently from positive parent trajectories before sibling/generation recombination.

A viable boundary argument must therefore preserve cancellation between alternating generations. In particular it must allow the positive semiprime and higher even-parity trajectories to reimburse the common root channel before any capacity inequality is imposed.

## 6. Surviving scope

This refutation does **not** show that the actual prime-shift state

\[
G(t)=\sum_{n\le e^t}\mu(n)h_0(t-\log n)
\]

ever becomes negative. It does not refute GCF or FGCM.

It refutes only the specific direct parent-capacity/no-double-spend mechanism in `T-23802`.

## Required pivot

Do not attempt to repair BCT by increasing the root capacity with an unsigned bound: the excess is exactly generated by taking prime children before the positive semiprime/higher-generation cancellation.

The next admissible mechanisms are:

1. signed generation-netted boundary transport;
2. finite-horizon FGCM, where the boundary layer may absorb unresolved alternating descendants;
3. signed parabolic defect-to-slack transport of PR #254.
